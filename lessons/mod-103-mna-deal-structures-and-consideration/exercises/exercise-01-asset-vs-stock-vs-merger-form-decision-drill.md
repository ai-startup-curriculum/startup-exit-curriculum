# exercise-01: Asset vs. Stock vs. Merger Form Decision Drill

**Estimated effort:** 3 hours

## Objective

Take five hypothetical M&A transactions with distinct fact patterns and choose the corporate form (asset purchase, stock purchase, reverse-triangular merger, forward-triangular merger, forward merger, or Reverse Morris Trust) that best fits each. For each choice, write down (a) which form you picked, (b) the specific fact-pattern reasons that pushed toward that form, (c) the buyer-vs-seller tension the choice resolves and what the disfavoured party gives up, (d) the specific first-pass counter-proposal the disfavoured party is likely to make, and (e) the tax, corporate, and consent consequences that flow from the choice. By the end you should be able to defend the form choice at the first working-group call, not the last one.

## Background

This exercise covers material from:

- [Chapter 1 — Deal-Structure Form Selection](../01-deal-structure-form-selection.md)

Chapter 5 (IRC §368 / §338(h)(10)) and chapter 8 (§1202 QSBS) are supporting inputs — the corporate-form decision cascades into tax structure and QSBS-preservation analysis. Chapter 2 (consideration mix) is the immediate next-decision downstream; some of the fact patterns here will have consideration constraints that back-propagate into the form choice.

## Prerequisites

- You have read chapter 1 in full.
- You have skimmed chapter 5 (tax reorg types) and chapter 8 (QSBS preservation) enough to know that the form choice interacts with tax and QSBS.
- Optional: familiarity with the Cooley / WSGR / Gunderson practitioner memos on structure selection (see resources.md).

## The five fact patterns

Work each one independently. Do not read ahead — the patterns are ordered so that later ones build tension against earlier ones and it is easier to think clearly about each in isolation.

### Fact pattern 1 — venture-backed SaaS to public-strategic

- **Target.** Delaware C-corp, 9 years old. 4 co-founders each hold ~8% of common stock originally issued at incorporation (basis ≈$0, current fair value ≈$18M each). ~40 employees with vested options totaling ~15% of fully-diluted. Series A, B, C, D preferred with $180M preference stack; 1x non-participating.
- **Financials.** $52M ARR, growing 45% YoY, GAAP-negative but roughly cash-flow-neutral.
- **Business.** Vertical B2B SaaS in a compliance-adjacent market. No product-liability history. Clean tax and IP diligence expected.
- **Acquirer.** Publicly-traded strategic ($40B market cap, S&P 500 constituent). Cash-rich; recent M&A history mixes cash and stock.
- **Headline.** LOI proposes $520M — 60% cash / 40% acquirer stock. Buyer's tax director wants amortisable goodwill on the intangible portion. R&W insurer has cleared the deal with no undisclosed-liability flags.
- **Timeline.** Signing target 90 days; closing 60 days after that (or on HSR clearance, whichever is later).

### Fact pattern 2 — S-corp founder-owned services business

- **Target.** Delaware S-corporation, 12 years old. Two co-founder owners each hold 50% of the S-corp stock; no outside investors, no preferred stack. Founders acquired stock at incorporation with minimal basis.
- **Financials.** $28M revenue, $8M EBITDA (adjusted), growing 20% YoY, cash-generative.
- **Business.** Managed-services / consulting adjacent to enterprise cybersecurity. Long customer contracts with change-of-control consent requirements in ~40% of the customer base. Modest tax exposure noted in diligence (state nexus questions). No litigation.
- **Acquirer.** Mid-market financial buyer (PE fund, $2B fund size, in year 5 of a 10-year fund). Cash-only preference; the fund's LPA does not permit acquirer-stock consideration.
- **Headline.** LOI proposes $72M all-cash. Buyer wants asset-deal-tax treatment. Founders' tax counsel has raised the §338(h)(10) election as a compromise.
- **Timeline.** Signing target 60 days; closing 45 days after.

### Fact pattern 3 — carve-out of a business unit from a large public parent

- **Target.** A wholly-owned business unit of a $30B market-cap public parent. The business unit generates $220M revenue and $45M EBITDA. It is one of several business units in the parent's segment reporting and has been non-strategic for 3 years. The business unit is operated inside a subsidiary of the parent.
- **Acquirer.** Publicly-traded competitor in the same broad industry, $8B market cap.
- **Headline.** LOI proposes $600M. Acquirer would prefer a tax-free structure that avoids parent-level corporate tax on the divestiture; parent's tax director has raised a Reverse Morris Trust (RMT) as a candidate. Alternative: taxable stock sale of the subsidiary that holds the business unit.
- **Timeline.** Signing target 6 months; closing 6–12 months after (regulatory-driven).
- **Regulatory.** HSR clearly reportable. EU Merger Regulation and China SAMR filings likely required.

### Fact pattern 4 — foreign-strategic acquirer of US AI startup

- **Target.** Delaware C-corp, 6 years old. Founders hold significant §1202 QSBS positions (three founders each with ~$14M gain on qualifying stock, holding period > 5 years). Series A/B/C preferred with $95M preference stack; participating preferred on Series A. ~30% of the target's employee headcount is on H-1B or O-1 visas.
- **Financials.** $22M ARR, growing 100% YoY. Products used in enterprise AI workflows; some products handle sensitive-personal-data inputs (customer data, geolocation for a subset of customers).
- **Acquirer.** Publicly-listed European strategic in AI-adjacent enterprise software. Cash-rich with limited US-M&A history.
- **Headline.** LOI proposes $340M — 80% cash / 20% acquirer stock (foreign parent stock).
- **CFIUS.** Target's technology includes items on the Commerce Control List. CFIUS mandatory-filing analysis is live.
- **Timeline.** Signing target 90 days; closing conditional on HSR + CFIUS + EU antitrust filings (expected 6–9 months post-signing).

### Fact pattern 5 — LLC / drop-down target with C-corp holding company

- **Target.** Delaware LLC operating company (partnership-taxed) with a C-corp Delaware holding company on top. Holding company holds 100% of the LLC. Founders hold C-corp common (§1202 QSBS if issued at qualifying time — analysis pending); options are on C-corp stock; VC investors hold preferred at the C-corp level with a $60M preference stack.
- **Financials.** $18M ARR, growing 75% YoY. Some product-liability exposure historically (one settled customer claim; one active reservation-of-rights letter noted in diligence).
- **Acquirer.** Mid-market strategic ($5B revenue diversified industrial-tech company). Buyer's tax director wants a step-up in asset basis; buyer's M&A counsel wants to isolate the reservation-of-rights liability. R&W insurer will not cover the specific product-liability item.
- **Headline.** LOI proposes $140M — 100% cash. Tax structuring open: buyer's counsel has proposed an F-reorg drop-down pattern to reconcile the buyer's step-up requirement with the sellers' preference for stock-form / QSBS-preserving treatment.
- **Timeline.** Signing target 75 days; closing on HSR clearance.

## Tasks

For each fact pattern, produce a **1–1.5 page form-selection memo** with the following sections:

### A. Form choice

Name one of the six modal forms (asset / stock / reverse-triangular merger / forward-triangular merger / forward merger / RMT). If you propose a variant or hybrid (e.g., "F-reorg drop-down followed by stock deal with §338(h)(10) election"), name it specifically.

### B. Fact-pattern reasons

3–6 specific reasons from the fact pattern that pushed toward this form. Be concrete — "the target is a C-corp with material QSBS at the founder level" is defensible; "the target prefers stock" is not.

### C. Buyer-vs-seller tension resolution

- What does the disfavoured party (the one whose first-pass preference is not being adopted) give up?
- What is the specific first-pass counter-proposal the disfavoured party is likely to make?
- What price / structural concession from the favoured party addresses the disfavoured party's loss?

### D. Tax consequences

- **Target level.** Does the target recognise gain? If yes, one level of tax or two?
- **Shareholder level.** How is the consideration taxed to each shareholder cohort (founders, early employees, preferred investors)?
- **Buyer level.** Does the buyer get a step-up in asset basis? What is the amortisation profile of the intangible portion under §197?
- **§1202 QSBS.** Preserved, tacked (via §368 reorg), or forfeit?

### E. Corporate and consent consequences

- **Board and shareholder approvals.** What consents are needed on each side?
- **Third-party consents.** Customer contracts, licenses, permits — what specifically triggers?
- **Regulatory filings.** HSR, CFIUS, foreign antitrust — what specifically is triggered?
- **Change-of-control implications.** For debt facilities, commercial contracts, and equity plans.

### F. Risks and open questions

3–5 specific items that need further diligence or counsel-level analysis before the form choice can be finalised. Be honest — some fact patterns above are underspecified deliberately; note where you would ask for more information.

## Cross-pattern synthesis

After completing all five fact patterns, write a **½–1 page synthesis** answering:

1. Which patterns pushed *hardest* on which form? (e.g., "pattern 1 pushed hardest toward reverse-triangular merger because…") What was the specific driver in each case?
2. Where did the form choice *cascade* into other structural decisions (consideration mix, escrow, tax election)? Give one specific example from your five memos.
3. Which fact pattern felt underspecified — where would you have wanted materially more diligence before finalising the form? What specifically?
4. Which fact pattern would you expect the *most* dispute at first pass between buyer and seller counsel? Why?

## Starter guidance

Anti-patterns to watch for:

- **Defaulting to reverse-triangular merger for everything.** RTM is the modal form for venture-backed C-corp deals, but not universally correct. Pattern 2 (S-corp) and pattern 3 (carve-out) push in different directions; pattern 5 (LLC with C-corp holdco) has a specific F-reorg drop-down pattern that is more targeted than a simple RTM.
- **Ignoring the tax-cost of an asset deal to the seller.** For a C-corp target with material §1202 QSBS, the seller's real cost of an asset deal is not the corporate tax on the sale — it is the QSBS forfeit at the shareholder level. That cost has to be quantified and translated into a price concession, or the buyer is negotiating against a deal that will not sign.
- **Assuming §338(h)(10) is available.** §338(h)(10) requires an S-corp or a corporate subsidiary of a consolidated group. For a C-corp target with venture-backed cap table, §338(h)(10) is not available at first pass — the F-reorg drop-down is the pattern that achieves similar buyer-side economics.
- **Treating regulatory-filing exposure as a separate track.** For patterns 3 and 4, CFIUS and multi-jurisdictional antitrust filings materially affect the form choice (particularly around outside-date, interim covenants, and reverse-termination fees). The form memo should surface these interactions rather than deferring them.

## Acceptance criteria

You can demonstrate that:

- Each of the five fact patterns has a completed 1–1.5 page form-selection memo covering sections A–F.
- The form choice is *defensible* against the disfavoured party's first-pass counter-proposal — you can articulate what the disfavoured party gives up and what would compensate them.
- Tax, corporate, and consent consequences are named specifically rather than generically.
- The cross-pattern synthesis (½–1 page) has been written.
- You have identified at least one fact pattern where you would want materially more diligence before finalising, and named specifically what you would want to see.

## Reflection

Add a short reflection:

1. Which of the five form choices was hardest to make, and why? What tipped the decision?
2. For pattern 1 (venture-backed SaaS to public-strategic), if the buyer's tax director insisted on a §338(h)(10) election (impossible for a C-corp target, but assume they push anyway), what specifically would you tell them, and what alternative structure achieves the buyer's step-up objective?
3. If you carried a specific transaction through mod-101 and mod-102 (a fixed company profile, buyer map, and pre-marketing artefacts), which of the five patterns most resembles your transaction? What does that suggest for the form choice you should carry forward through the rest of mod-103?
4. Where in these fact patterns did you find yourself wishing you knew more about IRC §368 (chapter 5) or §1202 QSBS (chapter 8) than the current-chapter framing provides? Note this for when you get to those chapters.

## Stretch goals

- **Pattern 3 RMT depth.** Read the §355 spin-off requirements in detail (Treas. Reg. §1.355-2 and following) and write a one-page analysis of whether pattern 3 could plausibly satisfy the RMT requirements. If not, name the specific requirement that fails.
- **Pattern 4 CFIUS scoping.** Draft a one-page CFIUS-scoping memo for pattern 4: what makes this a TID US business, what is the mandatory-vs-voluntary analysis, what mitigation commitments might CFIUS require, and how does that back-propagate into the form choice?
- **Pattern 5 F-reorg depth.** Read the F-reorg drop-down mechanics in a practitioner memo (Cooley, WSGR, Wachtell) and write a one-page mechanical description of the specific steps in pattern 5's structure — the pre-transaction restructuring, the closing transactions, and the post-close entity structure.
- **Ask a specialist.** If you have access to sell-side M&A counsel (Cooley, Wilson Sonsini, Gunderson, Fenwick, Latham, Orrick, or others), share your five memos with a partner or senior associate for a 30-minute review. Note where their intuition diverged from yours and what fact-pattern signal they weighted differently.
