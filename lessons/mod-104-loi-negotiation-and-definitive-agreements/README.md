# mod-104 — LOI, Negotiation & Definitive Agreements

**Estimated effort:** 26 hours
**Stage:** GROWTH / MATURE (installed when a transaction is imminent — modular reading is useful earlier, but the drafting and negotiation disciplines here are exercised inside a live process)
**Seat:** founder-CEO, CFO, GC, SVP-Corp-Dev, M&A counsel, R&W insurance broker, investment-banking VP / Director

This is the drafting-and-negotiation module of the sell-side / buy-side M&A arc. mod-101 framed the endgame decision. mod-102 built the market-structure and pre-marketing apparatus that surfaces the buyer set. mod-103 designed the deal-structure shape (form, consideration mix, earn-out, escrow, tax, regulatory filings). mod-104 sits between the moment a bidder puts a written indication of interest on the table and the moment a definitive agreement is signed. It installs: the binding-vs-nonbinding architecture that separates a nonbinding IOI from a binding LOI from an executed definitive agreement; the exclusivity / no-shop / standstill / expense-reimbursement / break-up-fee / matching-rights package that shapes negotiating leverage after LOI signing; the auction choreography that maintains competitive tension without triggering signalling risk; the definitive-agreement architecture (SPA / Merger Agreement / APA) with its reps-and-warranties layering, interim-operating covenants, closing conditions, and MAC / MAE definition; the indemnification package (survival, cap, basket, tipping, de-minimis, sole-and-exclusive-remedy, sandbagging language); the R&W insurance decision against the traditional-indemnification alternative; and the deal-scale negotiation canon (Freund, Fisher / Ury / Patton, Voss) applied to real M&A conversations.

The module owns the *drafting-and-negotiation* layer of transaction execution. It does not re-teach deal-structure design (mod-103), the sell-side data room or diligence-workstream response (mod-105), or transaction-governance and fiduciary-duty analysis at deal-scale (mod-109). It explicitly does not re-teach early-stage venture term-sheet negotiation — [`startup-finance-fundraising-curriculum`](https://github.com/ai-startup-curriculum/startup-finance-fundraising-curriculum) mod-108 owns that domain, and this module treats "term sheet" and "LOI" as the *transaction-side* documents that follow a completed sale process, not the priced-round preferred-stock term sheets a founder negotiates with a lead investor.

## Learning objectives

By the end of this module you should be able to:

- Author a sell-side or buy-side term sheet / LOI with the correct binding-vs-nonbinding architecture — the nonbinding indication of interest (IOI) at round 1, the binding LOI at round 2 that names purchase price / structure / consideration and creates process obligations, and the definitive-agreement drafting that follows.
- Negotiate the exclusivity / no-shop / standstill / expense-reimbursement / break-up-fee / matching-rights package against ABA Deal Points Study benchmarks — the standard 30–60 day exclusivity window, the fiduciary-out carve-out required for a public target or a target with a fiduciary-duty-triggering board (Revlon), the standstill duration (typically 12–24 months) and its acquirer-defence purpose, and the expense-reimbursement-plus-break-up-fee ladder against WSGR / ABA benchmarks.
- Manage an auction — single-track vs. dual-track vs. limited-round vs. broad auction, indication-of-interest and best-and-final round mechanics, information-parity discipline across bidders, and the timing choreography that maintains competitive tension without triggering signalling risk.
- Draft and negotiate the definitive-agreement architecture — SPA / Merger Agreement / Asset Purchase Agreement — with the reps-and-warranties layering (fundamental reps vs. general reps vs. tax reps vs. environmental reps vs. compliance-with-law reps vs. IP reps vs. privacy reps vs. AI-model reps), the covenants (interim-operating covenants, non-compete, non-solicit, cooperation), the closing conditions (bring-down of reps, HSR expiration, no MAC / MAE, third-party consents), and the MAC / MAE definition negotiation.
- Design the indemnification package — survival period (typically 12–24 months for general reps, 3–7 years or the statute of limitations for fundamental / tax reps), overall cap (typically 10–20% of purchase price for general reps, up to purchase price for fundamental / tax reps), basket type (deductible vs. tipping / first-dollar), tipping / basket size, de-minimis threshold, sole-and-exclusive-remedy language vs. fraud / IP / tax carve-outs, sandbagging / anti-sandbagging language.
- Structure R&W (representations-and-warranties) insurance — the market-conditions read from Marsh / Aon / Lockton / Liberty GTS, the underwriter diligence process (call, review of legal-and-financial diligence memos, exclusions negotiation), the retention (typically 1% of enterprise value dropping to 0.5% after 12 months), the buyer-side vs. seller-side placement decision, and the negotiation trade-off vs. a traditional indemnification package.
- Apply negotiation canon at deal-scale — the Freund pattern (*Anatomy of a Merger* — the sell-side sequencing, the buyer-side pattern), the Harvard Negotiation Project pattern (Fisher / Ury / Patton — interests behind positions, BATNA discipline), the Voss pattern (calibrated questions, mirroring, tactical empathy) — mapped onto real M&A conversations at LOI, definitive-agreement red-line, MAC / MAE debate, indemnification negotiation, and closing-condition brinkmanship.
- Recognise the boundary — early-stage venture term-sheet negotiation is owned by `startup-finance-fundraising-curriculum` mod-108 and never re-taught here; this module owns the M&A / transaction-side term-sheet and definitive-agreement work.

## Ownership boundary

This module owns the drafting-and-negotiation layer. It does *not* re-teach:

- **Early-stage venture term-sheet negotiation** — priced Series Seed / A / B / C term sheets with a lead investor, NVCA-model preferred-stock economics, protective provisions design, board-composition negotiation — [`startup-finance-fundraising-curriculum`](https://github.com/ai-startup-curriculum/startup-finance-fundraising-curriculum) mod-108 owns those. This module addresses only *transaction-side* term sheets / LOIs — the documents signed when someone is buying the company, not investing in it.
- **Deal-structure design** — corporate form (asset / stock / reverse-triangular / forward-triangular / forward / RMT), consideration mix (cash / stock / mixed / CVR / rollover), earn-out design, escrow / holdback / working-capital adjustment mechanics, IRC §368 / §338(h)(10) / §280G / §409A / §1202 tax structuring, HSR / CFIUS / foreign-antitrust filing assessment — mod-103 in this track owns those. This module *negotiates and drafts* the structure mod-103 designed; it does not choose the structure.
- **Sell-side data room, quality-of-earnings, and diligence-workstream response** — mod-105 owns those. This module treats diligence outputs as *inputs* to the definitive-agreement disclosure schedules and to R&W-insurance-underwriter conversations; the workstream mechanics themselves are mod-105.
- **Transaction governance and Delaware fiduciary-duty analysis at deal-scale** — Van Gorkom / Unocal / Revlon / Corwin / MFW analysis, special-committee construction, fairness-opinion procurement, Rural Metro banker-conflict analysis, DGCL §262 appraisal exposure, stockholder-litigation defence — mod-109 in this track owns those. This module *drafts* the fiduciary-out carve-out that Revlon requires and *notes* that a fairness opinion supports the price defensibility — the underlying fiduciary framework is mod-109.
- **Transaction communications (employee / customer / press choreography, MIP design, WARN Act compliance)** — mod-110 owns those.
- **Post-close integration mechanics — escrow release choreography, earn-out achievement operations, working-capital true-up execution** — mod-111 owns those.

What this module *adds* on top: the binding-vs-nonbinding architecture that separates an IOI from an LOI from a definitive agreement, the exclusivity-and-standstill package that shapes leverage after LOI signing, the auction choreography that maintains competitive tension into a best-and-final round, the definitive-agreement drafting toolkit with the reps-layering discipline, the covenants-and-conditions apparatus with the MAC / MAE negotiation, the indemnification package benchmarked against ABA Deal Points, the R&W insurance mechanic and its trade-off against traditional indemnity, and the deal-scale negotiation canon (Freund, Fisher / Ury, Voss) mapped onto real M&A conversations.

## How to work through this module

Carry a specific hypothetical transaction through the module — the transaction you constructed for mod-101 / mod-102 / mod-103 is ideal because the buyer map, the deal structure, the earn-out design, and the QSBS / §280G analysis all feed the LOI and the definitive-agreement drafting here. Freeze the target profile, the acquirer profile, and the headline structure at the start.

Chapters 1–3 install the front-end mechanics — term-sheet architecture, exclusivity-and-standstill package, auction choreography — that shape the negotiating environment from IOI through LOI signing. Chapters 4–6 install the definitive-agreement drafting toolkit — architecture and reps layering, covenants and conditions with MAC / MAE, indemnification package. Chapter 7 installs R&W insurance as the modern alternative / complement to a traditional indemnification package. Chapter 8 installs the deal-scale negotiation canon.

The exercises are ordered to be worked in sequence, but the definitive-agreement exercises (4, 5, 6) are the ones a corp-dev VP or GC will exercise most often in practice and should be worked to a high level of finish. Exercise 8 (negotiation role play) is best done with a partner or an advisor playing the other side; a self-play version is workable but less realistic.
