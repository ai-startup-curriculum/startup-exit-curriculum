# exercise-01: Sell-side LOI authoring with fiduciary out

**Estimated effort:** 3 hours

## Objective

Author a sell-side-friendly Letter of Intent for a specific hypothetical transaction. The finished artefact is a redlined term sheet / LOI (typically 8–15 pages of drafting plus a 2–3 page cover memo) that a sell-side General Counsel or outside counsel would put in front of the target's board for review before sending back to the buyer. You should end the exercise able to defend every binding-versus-nonbinding boundary in the document, every carve-out in the exclusivity provision, and every silence you left deliberately in the drafting.

## Background

This exercise covers material from:

- [Chapter 1 — Term-Sheet Architecture — IOI, LOI, and the Path to Definitive](../01-term-sheet-architecture-ioi-loi-definitive.md) — the two-round pattern, binding-vs-nonbinding architecture, what an LOI actually locks in.
- [Chapter 2 — Exclusivity, No-Shop, Standstill, and the Break-Up-Fee Ladder](../02-exclusivity-no-shop-standstill-break-up-fees.md) — the fiduciary-out carve-out, matching rights, exclusivity extension mechanics.

The [chapter 8 negotiation canon](../08-deal-scale-negotiation-canon.md) is a supporting reference for the cover-memo defence of the drafting choices.

## Prerequisites

- The hypothetical transaction you have been carrying through mod-101 (endgame decision) and mod-102 (buyer map, banker engagement, pre-marketing document set). If you have not done those modules, freeze a plausible venture-backed C-corp target profile before starting — sector, ARR, growth rate, employee count, cap-table shape, board composition.
- The buyer profile you have selected as the round-2 winning bidder — strategic vs. financial, public vs. private, US vs. cross-border.
- The deal-structure shape from [mod-103](../../mod-103-mna-deal-structures-and-consideration/README.md) — corporate form (asset / stock / reverse-triangular / etc.), consideration mix (cash / stock / mixed / rollover / CVR), any specific tax elections (§338(h)(10), F-reorg, §368 reorg type).
- Access to a modern LOI template. Cooley GO, WSGR public model forms, or an equivalent practitioner template is fine as the starting form. Do NOT copy the template line-for-line — the exercise is to author, not to redistribute.
- The current ABA Private Target Deal Points Study and the SRS Acquiom deal-terms study for benchmark data on exclusivity duration, extension mechanics, and expense-reimbursement caps.

## Tasks

### 1. Set the deal one-pager

Write a one-page fact-set the LOI will be built against. This is the reference sheet every subsequent drafting decision points back to.

- Target legal name, jurisdiction of incorporation, fiscal year.
- Target headline financial profile — ARR, growth rate, gross margin, cash burn, cash on balance sheet.
- Target cap table at closing (Common, Preferred by series, options outstanding, ESPP participants, warrants, convertible notes).
- Board composition — number of directors, breakdown of investor / independent / founder seats, chair.
- Winning bidder legal name, jurisdiction of incorporation, public / private status, ticker if public.
- Round-2 headline number (from the winning bidder's IOI-round positioning, informed by mod-102 exercise-06's shape work).
- Deal structure shape (from mod-103 exercise-01).
- Consideration mix (from mod-103 exercise-02).
- Any known regulatory-filing complexity (HSR-only vs. CFIUS-triggering vs. foreign-antitrust vs. sector-specific approvals).

### 2. Author the LOI — section by section

Produce the actual LOI as a numbered-section document. Every section named in chapter 1's LOI section-order list should be present. The specific target sub-tasks by section:

**Section 1 — Background / Recitals.** Two to three paragraphs naming parties and general nature of the transaction. Deliberately vague on process context.

**Section 2 — Transaction Structure.** Name the corporate form, the treatment of vested and unvested equity (options, RSUs, ESPP, warrants, convertible notes), the treatment of the ESOP / option pool, and any specific tax elections proposed. Reference the mod-103 structure work.

**Section 3 — Purchase Price.** Enterprise-value or equity-purchase-price headline number. Name the specific adjustment mechanics (working-capital, closing-cash, closing-debt, transaction-expenses) at the concept level — the mechanics are negotiated in the definitive agreement (per mod-103 chapter 4). Escrow and holdback sizing at concept level. Explicitly note that the price is subject to definitive-agreement negotiation and diligence completion.

**Section 4 — Financing.** For a cash deal from a public strategic, name the cash-on-balance-sheet source. For a PE bidder, require equity-commitment letter and debt-commitment letter as annexes. For a stock deal, name the acquirer stock as the consideration source and note the SEC-registration or 4(a)(2) private-placement mechanic.

**Section 5 — Management-and-Employee Arrangements.** Sell-side-friendly retention pool sizing (aim for 4–6% of purchase price, sourced from buyer's transaction budget rather than seller proceeds). Note key-executive employment agreements as post-closing follow-on documents. Non-compete / non-solicit as concept placeholders. Treatment of vested and unvested options, RSUs, ESPP consistent with section 2.

**Section 6 — Diligence.** Scope, timing, cooperation commitment. Note the target's data-room readiness (see mod-105). Diligence must complete on a specified timeline.

**Section 7 — Definitive Agreement.** Commitment to negotiate a definitive agreement. Name the form (SPA / Merger Agreement / APA) consistent with section 2. Buyer's counsel delivers first draft on a specified timeline (aim for 10–15 days post-LOI). Definitive-agreement negotiation timeline (aim for signing within 45–60 days of LOI).

**Section 8 — Regulatory Approvals.** Anticipated HSR / CFIUS / foreign-antitrust filings. Both parties file; both parties bear their own filing fees (typical) or filing-fee allocation (negotiated). Cooperation commitment on regulatory response.

**Section 9 — Closing Conditions.** Concept-level list — HSR clearance, absence of injunction, delivery of specified closing documents. Detailed conditions negotiated in the definitive agreement. Deliberately do NOT include specific closing-condition mechanics (per chapter 1's warning against drifting into type-II preliminary-agreement territory).

**Section 10 — Binding Provisions.** This is the substantive drafting core. Cover:

- **Exclusivity** — length, extension mechanics, definition of Alternative Transaction, fiduciary-out carve-out with Superior-Proposal definition, matching-rights mechanic. Sell-side-friendly targets from chapter 2 (below in task 3 detail).
- **Confidentiality** — reference or incorporate the existing NDA.
- **Expense reimbursement** — sell-side-friendly cap (aim for none, or a de-minimis cap of $250K–$500K on the target-side; specifically no seller-side expense reimbursement to buyer for a target that walks in good faith).
- **Publicity** — mutual restriction; specific carve-outs for SEC-required disclosure (for a public acquirer or public target), employee communications after agreed choreography, litigation-response.
- **Choice of law and forum** — Delaware.
- **Notice, counterparts, severability** — standard boilerplate.

**Section 11 — Nonbinding Acknowledgment.** The explicit statement (per chapter 1) that everything except section 10 is nonbinding and does not create any obligation to close. Language must be unambiguous — no "best efforts to negotiate in good faith" language that could imply an enforceable duty to negotiate.

**Section 12 — Signature Blocks.** Target officer, buyer officer.

### 3. Draft the exclusivity + fiduciary-out package (deep detail)

The exclusivity provision is the load-bearing negotiating point of the LOI. Draft it with full attention to the sell-side targets:

**3.1 — Exclusivity length.** Sell-side target: 45 days as primary period, 60 days as fallback. Extension by mutual written agreement only, or by unilateral election of the target if the buyer is not prosecuting diligently. Automatic termination on buyer's material breach or material reduction of the offered price.

**3.2 — Definition of Alternative Transaction.** Sell-side target: threshold of 15% of equity or a material portion of assets (aligning with the poison-pill trigger concept per chapter 2). Explicit carve-out for ordinary-course financing, ordinary-course commercial licensing, and small-scale acqui-hire discussions in which the target is the acquirer.

**3.3 — Fiduciary-out carve-out.** Sell-side target: the board may, on determination that a proposal constitutes a Superior Proposal or would reasonably be expected to result in a Superior Proposal, engage with the third party subject to a substantially-similar confidentiality agreement. Draft the full carve-out. Draft the Superior Proposal definition — price threshold (aim for "materially higher") and closing-certainty threshold (aim for "comparable or better financing, regulatory certainty, timing").

**3.4 — Matching-rights mechanic.** Sell-side target: a 3-business-day match period (aim to hold — buyer's counsel will push for 5). Notification obligation (identity of the third party, material terms). Sell-side target: after buyer submits a revised proposal, the target's board evaluates and must in good faith determine that the revised proposal is at least as favourable as the Superior Proposal before the target is bound to continue with the buyer. Draft the language.

**3.5 — Standstill (if any at LOI stage).** For most sell-side transactions, the standstill lives in the NDA rather than the LOI. Confirm the standstill status of the executed NDA. If the NDA standstill needs to be modified for this LOI (e.g., to allow the buyer to purchase shares through the transaction), draft the amendment. If the buyer proposes an LOI-embedded standstill, draft a sell-side response — 12-month duration or the NDA-standstill duration, whichever is shorter.

### 4. Draft the cover memo

Write a 2–3 page cover memo to the target's board (or the target's outside counsel) that walks through the LOI drafting choices. The memo should cover:

- The specific binding-versus-nonbinding boundaries drawn in the document and the rationale.
- The exclusivity length chosen and the trade-off against the specific diligence-and-drafting timeline.
- The fiduciary-out language and its relationship to the fiduciary framework (mod-109).
- The matching-rights mechanic and how it interacts with a plausible Superior Proposal from the second-place round-2 bidder.
- The retention-pool sourcing and the specific reason for pushing back on any buyer proposal to source from seller proceeds.
- The publicity restrictions and how they interact with the employee-communications choreography (mod-110).
- Silences you left deliberately in the LOI (e.g., MAC / MAE, indemnification package, R&W insurance) and the specific reason to defer them to the definitive-agreement negotiation.

### 5. Anticipate the buyer's redline

Anticipate the buyer's counsel's most-likely redline positions on the LOI you have drafted. For each, name:

- The specific provision the buyer will red-line.
- The likely buyer counter-position.
- Your first-round response — accept, counter, or hold.
- Your fallback if the buyer holds hard.

Aim for at least 8 specific redline points. Candidates: exclusivity length (buyer wants longer), unilateral extension (buyer wants), fiduciary-out (buyer wants tighter), Superior Proposal definition (buyer wants higher price threshold), matching-rights period (buyer wants 5 days), expense reimbursement (buyer wants a cap and a break-up-fee ladder), retention-pool source (buyer wants seller proceeds), Alternative Transaction threshold (buyer wants lower or none), no-shop scope (buyer wants inclusion of representatives, advisors, and affiliates), publicity (buyer wants tighter).

## Starter guidance

Common first-drafter errors to avoid:

- **The un-redlineable ambiguity.** "The parties shall negotiate in good faith and use commercially reasonable efforts to enter into a definitive agreement" reads as boilerplate but has been interpreted by Delaware courts (see chapter 1's *SIGA v. PharmAthene* reference) as creating an enforceable duty to negotiate in good faith. If your LOI contains language of this shape, the "nonbinding" architecture is compromised.
- **The buried economic term.** Drafting the retention-pool source, the earn-out structure, or the CVR mechanics into the LOI in specific dollar terms rather than at concept level. This drifts the LOI toward type-II preliminary-agreement territory and can be later cited as evidence that the parties reached agreement on material terms. Chapter 1 warns against this specifically.
- **The single-shot fiduciary-out.** A fiduciary-out that requires the board to determine a "Superior Proposal" using the finalised Superior-Proposal definition without any prior "would-reasonably-be-expected-to-lead-to" engagement threshold. The sell-side draft needs both — the engagement threshold (lower) that allows the board to talk to a third party, and the Superior Proposal threshold (higher) that lets the board actually accept.
- **The missing matching-rights trigger.** A fiduciary-out without a matching right will be red-lined heavily; drafting one in from the sell-side draft, even if unfavourable, is the practitioner move.
- **The over-negotiated LOI.** Trying to resolve the definitive-agreement fights at LOI stage. Reps-and-warranties survival, cap, basket, sandbagging, MAC / MAE definition, indemnification package — these belong in the definitive agreement (chapters 4, 5, 6). If they appear in the LOI, the LOI has taken on definitive-agreement complexity without the definitive-agreement discipline.

## Acceptance criteria

You can demonstrate that:

- The one-page fact-set is written and referenced throughout.
- The LOI is drafted as a complete document with all sections in chapter 1's list present.
- Every binding provision is enumerated in section 10 and every nonbinding provision falls outside it.
- The nonbinding acknowledgment (section 11) is unambiguous and consistent with the section-10 enumeration.
- The exclusivity length, extension mechanics, definition of Alternative Transaction, fiduciary-out carve-out, Superior Proposal definition, and matching-rights mechanic are all drafted to sell-side targets and can be defended against ABA / SRS Acquiom benchmarks.
- The cover memo walks through the specific drafting choices and defends them.
- The buyer-redline anticipation includes at least 8 specific points, each with first-round response and fallback.
- Nothing in the LOI drifts into type-II preliminary-agreement territory (definitive-agreement mechanics buried in the LOI, "good faith" language that could imply an enforceable duty).

## Reflection

Add a short reflection at the end:

1. Which single sell-side provision are you most likely to lose in the buyer redline? What does that tell you about the specific structural leverage of the transaction?
2. If the second-place bidder actually surfaced a Superior Proposal during exclusivity, would your board be able to accept it under the LOI you drafted? Walk through the specific procedural path.
3. Which silence in the LOI is the one you are most nervous about? What is the plan to address it in the definitive-agreement negotiation?

## Stretch goals

- **Cross-border overlay.** If the target or buyer is non-US, add the specific cross-border considerations — foreign-antitrust (EU Merger Regulation, UK CMA, China SAMR, others), CFIUS-triggering ownership structures, cross-border tax structuring. Cite the primary regulator source.
- **Public-target overlay.** If the target is a public company (or the buyer is), redraft the LOI with the additional Revlon-triggering disclosures — go-shop-versus-no-shop, more-formal matching rights, break-up-fee ladder at 1–4% of transaction value per public-target practice, and the SEC disclosure choreography for the transaction announcement.
- **NDA-standstill audit.** Retrieve (or draft) the executed NDA between the parties. Audit the standstill for the specific carve-outs needed at LOI signing. Draft the amendment memo.
- **Chapter-8 frame overlay.** Annotate the cover memo with the specific Freund / Harvard / Voss frames deployed for each drafting choice. This is prep for exercise 8.
