# exercise-02: Buy-side LOI authoring with standstill

**Estimated effort:** 3 hours

## Objective

Author a buy-side-friendly Letter of Intent for the same hypothetical transaction used in exercise-01, this time seated on the buy-side. The finished artefact is a redlined LOI (typically 8–15 pages plus a 2–3 page investment-committee memo) that a corp-dev VP or a PE deal partner would put in front of an investment committee for approval before sending to the target. You should end the exercise able to defend the length of the exclusivity requested, the standstill scope, the expense-reimbursement ladder, and the specific first-draft anchoring positions on every substantive provision — and to explain the trade-offs when the target's counsel eventually pushes back.

## Prerequisites

- The same hypothetical transaction used in exercise-01. If you did exercise-01, use the same one-pager and swap seats.
- The acquirer profile from mod-102 (buyer map) that corresponds to the winning bidder — public strategic, private strategic, sponsor-controlled financial, sovereign, or family office.

## Tasks

### 1. Set the deal one-pager (buy-side lens)

Take the exercise-01 fact-set and rewrite it from the buyer's perspective. The specific additions:

- Buyer's specific rationale for the acquisition — strategic (name the specific integration thesis, synergy hypothesis, or competitive-response driver) or financial (name the specific platform-vs-add-on thesis, hold-period expectation, and exit thesis).
- Buyer's constraints — public-company disclosure obligations, board-approval process (special committee needed?), financing certainty status (cash-on-balance-sheet vs. debt commitment vs. equity commitment), regulatory-clearance profile.
- Buyer's alternative uses of capital — the buyer's BATNA. What else could the buyer do with this capital? An alternative target, a share buyback, an internal build, additional add-ons to an existing platform. Chapter 8's BATNA discipline applies.
- Buyer's diligence appetite — full-scope legal / financial / tax / commercial / technology / IP / HR / privacy / security / AI-model workstreams (typical for a strategic in a competitive process) vs. lighter workstreams (typical for an add-on to an existing platform where the buyer has substantial sector knowledge).
- Buyer's target closing date and the drivers behind it — regulatory-clearance timeline, integration-planning timeline, fiscal-quarter alignment.

### 2. Author the LOI — section by section, buy-side first draft

Every section named in chapter 1's LOI section-order list should be present. Draft the buy-side positions — deliberately buyer-friendly first-draft anchoring per chapter 8's buy-side sequencing:

**Section 1 — Background / Recitals.** Brief; identifies parties and the general nature of the transaction. Do not concede more here than necessary.

**Section 2 — Transaction Structure.** Buy-side-favourable structure per mod-103 chapter 1 — reverse-triangular merger or forward-triangular merger for a stock deal (asset deal if tax-driven and the buyer can accept the seller's tax preferences); §338(h)(10) election if the buyer wants asset-treatment on a stock deal with an S-corp target; specific F-reorg drop-down if the target is an LLC. Draft the treatment of vested and unvested equity, options, RSUs, ESPP, warrants, and convertible notes with an eye to minimising post-close employee-consideration surprises.

**Section 3 — Purchase Price.** Enterprise-value or equity-purchase-price number. Buy-side draft the adjustment mechanics — working-capital adjustment against a specific target (aim for the higher of trailing-twelve-month or 3-year-average, buy-side-favourable), closing-cash adjustment dollar-for-dollar, closing-debt adjustment dollar-for-dollar, transaction-expenses adjustment. Escrow and holdback sizing (aim for 10–15% escrow for a traditional-indemnification deal, 0.5–1% escrow with R&W insurance for the retention gap). Explicitly note that price is subject to diligence and definitive-agreement negotiation.

**Section 4 — Financing.** Confirm financing certainty in specific terms — cash on balance sheet with source, or debt-commitment-letter attached as annex (from named lenders), or equity-commitment-letter attached as annex (for a PE bidder). Buy-side-friendly: do NOT include a financing condition in the closing conditions. Buy-side-friendly if debt-financed: name the debt-commitment as a source of comfort rather than a condition.

**Section 5 — Management-and-Employee Arrangements.** Buy-side-friendly retention pool sourcing — 2–4% of purchase price sourced from seller proceeds (not from buyer's transaction budget). Named key-executive employment agreements as closing conditions. Post-closing non-compete (aim for 3–5 years for founders, 2 years for other key executives) and non-solicit (aim for 24 months). Reference the mod-103 §280G analysis if any executive is at or approaching a §280G-triggering payment level.

**Section 6 — Diligence.** Buy-side-friendly scope — full legal / financial / tax / commercial / technology / IP / HR / privacy / security / AI-model workstreams. Cooperation commitment from seller including access to key personnel, senior management interviews, and physical site visits as needed. Timing commitment aligned with definitive-agreement drafting timeline.

**Section 7 — Definitive Agreement.** Buyer's counsel delivers first draft within 10–15 days of LOI signing. Definitive-agreement negotiation timeline (aim for signing within 45–60 days of LOI, extendible by mutual agreement).

**Section 8 — Regulatory Approvals.** HSR / CFIUS / foreign-antitrust preparation. Cooperation obligation. Buy-side-friendly efforts standard — "commercially reasonable efforts" for regulatory cooperation (chapter 5 develops the efforts-standards ladder); avoid hell-or-high-water language.

**Section 9 — Closing Conditions.** Buy-side-friendly closing conditions concept-list — bring-down of reps and warranties, no MAC / MAE, HSR clearance, CFIUS clearance (if applicable), no injunction, delivery of specified closing documents, specific third-party consents (list the specific consents required — top 5 customer contracts, top 3 supplier contracts, material lender consent). Detailed mechanics negotiated in the definitive agreement.

**Section 10 — Binding Provisions.** The load-bearing drafting. Cover:

- **Exclusivity** — 60 days as primary period (buy-side aim; sell-side will push shorter). Extension mechanics — automatic extension if regulatory filings are pending or if any diligence workstream is materially incomplete due to seller delay. Definition of Alternative Transaction — broad (any transaction of any equity or asset portion above 10% of enterprise value). Fiduciary-out — narrow (only truly superior proposals with 5-business-day matching period).
- **Standstill** — 24 months (buy-side aim for the acquirer-defence purpose if the deal doesn't close; sell-side may push shorter or push to keep the NDA standstill only). Draft the standstill scope per chapter 2's list — no acquisition of target securities other than through the transaction, no tender offer or hostile action, no proxy solicitation, no group formation, no request to waive.
- **Confidentiality** — reference the NDA; extend if the NDA duration is less than 24 months.
- **Expense reimbursement** — buy-side ladder starting at $500K expense reimbursement cap if the target walks in circumstances other than buyer's material breach, escalating to a $1M–$2M break-up fee if the target accepts a Superior Proposal during exclusivity. Chapter 2's ABA / WSGR benchmarks anchor the ladder.
- **Publicity** — mutual restriction; buyer-favourable carve-outs for SEC-required disclosure and for competitive-response communications.
- **Choice of law and forum** — Delaware.

**Section 11 — Nonbinding Acknowledgment.** Same discipline as exercise-01 — unambiguous nonbinding statement outside the section-10 enumeration.

**Section 12 — Signature Blocks.**

### 3. Draft the standstill package (deep detail)

The standstill is the buy-side-specific load-bearing binding provision that exercise-01 did not develop in depth. Draft it with attention to:

**3.1 — Standstill duration.** Buy-side aim: 24 months from LOI signing. The sell-side will push for 12 months or for tying the standstill to the NDA duration. Prepare the buy-side defence: the standstill's acquirer-defence purpose (chapter 2) requires enough duration to prevent a "look inside and go hostile" pattern after a friendly negotiation fails.

**3.2 — Standstill scope.** Draft the full list of prohibited actions per chapter 2:

- No acquisition of target securities other than through the contemplated transaction (with a de-minimis carve-out for ordinary-course investment activity below a threshold — buy-side aim: 1% of outstanding equity, sell-side may push higher).
- No tender offer, exchange offer, merger proposal, or other business-combination proposal.
- No solicitation of proxies or consents from target stockholders.
- No formation or joining of a Rule 13d-5 group.
- No request to waive or amend the standstill.
- No public announcement of any of the above.

**3.3 — Standstill carve-outs.** Buy-side draft carve-outs for:

- Contemplated transaction (the standstill does not prevent the buyer from completing the LOI-contemplated transaction).
- Ordinary-course investment activity by the buyer's asset-management affiliates that do not receive target confidential information (Chinese-wall carve-out — important for financial buyers with public-market investment affiliates).
- Actions required by law or regulation (SEC-required disclosure of position, court-ordered disclosure).
- Response to unsolicited third-party approaches to the target — if the target's board publicly announces it is exploring a sale to a third party (e.g., through a proxy contest or a superior-proposal announcement), the standstill lifts automatically.

**3.4 — Standstill "top-up."** Buy-side-friendly: a provision that if the target enters into a definitive agreement with a third party during exclusivity, the standstill period restarts from the date of that definitive agreement. Sell-side will resist; hold as a first-draft anchor.

### 4. Draft the expense-reimbursement / break-up-fee ladder

Design the buy-side ladder:

**4.1 — Tier 1: expense reimbursement.** If the target walks for reasons other than buyer's material breach — expense reimbursement of buyer's out-of-pocket transaction expenses (legal, financial, tax, technical diligence, R&W insurance underwriting costs) up to a specified cap. Buy-side aim: $500K–$1M cap (scaled to transaction size).

**4.2 — Tier 2: enhanced expense reimbursement.** If the target walks and enters into an alternative transaction within a defined tail period — expense reimbursement without the cap, up to buyer's actual out-of-pocket transaction expenses.

**4.3 — Tier 3: break-up fee.** If the target accepts a Superior Proposal during exclusivity — a break-up fee of 1–2% of transaction value (aligned with the ABA / WSGR public-target break-up fee benchmarks per chapter 2; adjusted for the private-target private-equity context).

**4.4 — Reverse termination fee (if applicable).** For a buyer with material regulatory-clearance risk (CFIUS-triggering ownership, horizontal antitrust risk), the sell-side will often demand a reverse termination fee that the buyer pays if the deal does not close for regulatory reasons. Buy-side aim: no reverse termination fee (hold as first-draft anchor); if the sell-side holds hard, propose a limited reverse termination fee capped at 3–5% of transaction value with specific triggering conditions.

### 5. Draft the investment-committee memo

Write a 2–3 page memo to the buyer's investment committee (or the buyer's CFO / board for a strategic acquirer) that walks through the LOI drafting choices from the buy-side perspective. Cover:

- The strategic / financial rationale for the transaction.
- The specific first-draft anchoring positions and the rationale for each — how the buyer's negotiating leverage converges from the first-draft anchor per chapter 8's buy-side sequencing.
- The exclusivity length requested and its relationship to the buyer's specific diligence-workstream timeline.
- The standstill scope and duration, with the acquirer-defence rationale.
- The expense-reimbursement / break-up-fee ladder and its defence against ABA / WSGR benchmarks.
- The regulatory-clearance risk assessment and any specific carve-outs required in the closing conditions.
- The BATNA analysis — what happens if this deal does not close, and how that shapes the buyer's negotiating leverage.
- The specific fallbacks the buyer is prepared to accept on each substantive provision.

### 6. Anticipate the sell-side redline

Anticipate the target's counsel's most-likely redline positions on the LOI you have drafted. For each, name:

- The specific provision the sell-side will red-line.
- The likely sell-side counter-position.
- Your first-round response — accept, counter, or hold.
- Your fallback if the sell-side holds hard.

Aim for at least 8 specific redline points. Candidates: exclusivity length (target wants 45 days), unilateral extension (target rejects), fiduciary-out (target wants broader), Superior Proposal definition (target wants lower price threshold), matching-rights period (target wants 3 days), standstill duration (target wants 12 months), standstill scope (target wants narrower), standstill top-up (target rejects), retention-pool source (target wants buyer's transaction budget), expense reimbursement (target wants smaller cap), break-up fee (target rejects), reverse termination fee (target may demand for regulatory risk).

## Starter guidance

Common first-drafter errors from the buy-side chair to avoid:

- **The over-anchored first draft.** Loading the LOI with buy-side positions so aggressive that the sell-side reads them as bad-faith opening. Chapter 8's Freund frame calls for anchoring, not for provocation. The over-anchored first draft costs weeks of negotiation and can damage the working relationship for the definitive-agreement drafting.
- **The financing-condition creep.** Including a financing condition in the closing conditions after the LOI has represented "no financing condition." Sophisticated sell-side counsel catches this and it damages credibility.
- **The standstill over-reach.** Drafting a 36-month standstill with no carve-outs and no de-minimis threshold. Sell-side will red-line hard; the buyer looks unprepared.
- **The absent BATNA.** Approaching the LOI without a clear internal understanding of the buyer's BATNA. Every substantive provision the buyer holds on requires BATNA support; without BATNA discipline, the buyer concedes reactively and loses value.
- **The hell-or-high-water default.** Accepting hell-or-high-water antitrust language because it is the seller's default request, without stress-testing it against the specific regulatory-clearance-risk profile. Chapter 5's efforts-standards ladder is the right frame; if the transaction has genuine antitrust risk, the buyer must negotiate a moderated standard.

## Acceptance criteria

You can demonstrate that:

- Buy-side one-pager is written and includes the buyer's BATNA analysis.
- The LOI is drafted as a complete document with all sections in chapter 1's list present, with buy-side first-draft anchoring on each substantive provision.
- The standstill is drafted with duration, scope, and carve-outs consistent with the acquirer-defence purpose in chapter 2.
- The expense-reimbursement / break-up-fee ladder is drafted with all three tiers (or with a defended rationale for skipping a tier).
- The investment-committee memo walks through the strategic / financial rationale, the specific anchoring positions, and the specific fallbacks the buyer is prepared to accept.
- The sell-side redline anticipation includes at least 8 specific points, each with first-round response and fallback.
- The LOI does not drift into type-II preliminary-agreement territory.

## Reflection

Add a short reflection:

1. Which buy-side provision are you most likely to lose in the sell-side redline? Given that loss, does the transaction still meet the buyer's return threshold?
2. If the target's counsel refuses the standstill top-up, what specific alternative would you propose to preserve the acquirer-defence purpose?
3. What did drafting from the buy-side chair reveal that you did not see from the sell-side chair in exercise-01? Where did the two seats' interests actually align (chapter 8 interest-based frame)?

## Stretch goals

- **PE-buyer overlay.** If your winning bidder is a PE sponsor, redraft with the specific PE-buyer overlays — the equity commitment letter mechanic, the fund-diligence disclosures, the co-invest structuring, the specific portfolio-conflict disclosures.
- **Public-acquirer overlay.** If your buyer is a public strategic, redraft with the Regulation FD-driven disclosure choreography, the material-agreement 8-K trigger (Item 1.01), and the specific investor-communications timing.
- **Cross-border overlay.** If the buyer is non-US, add the CFIUS pre-filing analysis (mandatory-CFIUS vs. voluntary), the foreign-antitrust filing map, and the specific cross-border tax structuring in the LOI (repatriation, treaty analysis).
- **Two-track LOI.** If the deal is dual-track (M&A + IPO), draft the specific LOI provisions that manage the dual-track posture — the target's ability to continue IPO preparation activities, the specific S-1-draft-sharing choreography, the IPO-abandonment triggers.
- **Chapter-8 frame overlay.** Annotate the investment-committee memo with the specific Freund / Harvard / Voss frames deployed for each buy-side anchoring position. Prep for exercise 8.
