# exercise-05: MAC / MAE definition negotiation drill

**Estimated effort:** 3 hours

## Objective

Draft and counter-draft a Material Adverse Change / Material Adverse Effect (MAC / MAE) definition for three specific fact patterns that stress the definition in different ways. Then defend each definition against the *Akorn v. Fresenius* practitioner lesson and the specific carve-outs the *Akorn* framework validated. The finished artefact is three redlined MAC / MAE definitions (one per fact pattern), each with a 1-page defence memo, plus a 2-page cross-scenario synthesis on when a broad MAC / MAE serves the buyer, when a narrow one serves the seller, and where the negotiating middle-ground lies.

By the end you should be able to walk into a definitive-agreement red-line and hold the MAC / MAE definition position against a sophisticated counterparty — knowing which carve-outs are defensible, which are aggressive, and which are inconsistent with the case-law-driven practitioner discipline.

## Tasks

### 1. Anchor on the *Akorn* frame

Write a 1-page summary of the *Akorn v. Fresenius* practitioner lesson. Cover:

- The factual pattern: what Akorn's business did between signing and closing that Fresenius asserted was a MAC / MAE.
- The court's specific holding: what qualified as a MAC / MAE under the definition.
- The "durationally significant" requirement — the extent to which the adverse change must reasonably be expected to persist for a period measured in years.
- The "disproportionately affected" carve-out mechanic — how industry-wide changes are excluded but disproportionate impact on the target is captured.
- The specific quantitative benchmarks the court referenced (percentage EBITDA decline over specific periods).
- The specific evidentiary discipline the court applied — how much documentation the buyer needed to prove MAC / MAE.

This is the frame every subsequent drafting decision points back to.

### 2. Draft the baseline MAC / MAE definition

Before working through the three fact patterns, draft a "baseline" definition that is neither strongly buyer-favourable nor strongly seller-favourable — a modal, market-benchmarked definition. Cover:

- **The base MAC / MAE statement.** "Material Adverse Effect means any event, circumstance, occurrence, fact, or change that, individually or in the aggregate with all other events, circumstances, occurrences, facts, or changes, has had or would reasonably be expected to have a material adverse effect on the business, results of operations, financial condition, or assets of the Company and its subsidiaries, taken as a whole."
- **The carve-outs.** The standard list per chapter 5 — general economic conditions, industry conditions, changes in law, changes in GAAP, natural disasters, acts of war and terrorism, pandemic (post-COVID standard), general market changes, actions taken with buyer consent, actions taken pursuant to the agreement, failure to meet projections (with the "underlying causes" carve-in), stock-price changes (with the "underlying causes" carve-in).
- **The disproportionately-affected qualifier.** The carve-outs (except changes-in-law, natural disasters, pandemic) apply only to the extent the target is not disproportionately affected relative to comparable companies in the same industry.

This baseline is the reference against which the three fact-pattern-specific drafts diverge.

### 3. Fact pattern A — pandemic / macro-shock exposure

**Fact pattern:** The target is a B2B SaaS company selling to SMB customers with an ARPU concentrated in the hospitality and travel sectors. The transaction signs in a period of normalised macroeconomic conditions but with elevated recession signals. The exclusivity period is 60 days from signing to closing (regulatory-driven — HSR waiting period plus specific third-party consents). Between signing and closing, a sector-wide event materially reduces demand across hospitality-and-travel SaaS spend.

**Buyer position (aim):** A MAC / MAE definition that captures the sector-wide demand decline as a MAC / MAE and lets the buyer walk. Draft the specific language.

**Seller position (aim):** A MAC / MAE definition that carves out the sector-wide event as a general-industry-condition or pandemic exclusion and prevents the buyer from walking. Draft the specific language.

**Your task:**

- Draft the buyer's version.
- Draft the seller's version.
- Draft a middle-ground version — where the definition captures target-specific disproportionate impact but excludes the sector-wide baseline.
- Write a 1-page defence memo that walks through the *Akorn* frame's application to this fact pattern. What language does *Akorn* support for each position? Where is the middle ground?

### 4. Fact pattern B — target-specific product-safety event

**Fact pattern:** The target is a medical-device company. Between signing and closing, a routine FDA post-market surveillance report surfaces a specific product-safety signal on one of the target's core products. The signal does not yet trigger a recall or regulatory enforcement, but the target's counsel assesses a 30–50% probability of a recall within 12 months and a 60–80% probability of an FDA warning letter within 6 months. The specific product accounts for approximately 40% of the target's revenue.

**Buyer position (aim):** A MAC / MAE definition and closing-condition mechanic that lets the buyer walk on the specific product-safety signal.

**Seller position (aim):** A MAC / MAE definition that requires demonstrated durationally-significant financial impact — not just a probability assessment — before the buyer can walk.

**Your task:**

- Draft the buyer's version, including any specific product-safety trigger or specific-indemnity mechanic.
- Draft the seller's version, arguing the *Akorn* durational-significance requirement applies and that the buyer cannot walk on a probability-only signal.
- Draft a middle-ground version — perhaps a specific product-safety indemnity (chapter 6) funded from separate escrow that decouples the buyer's exposure from the walk-away decision.
- Write a 1-page defence memo covering the *Akorn* durational-significance analysis applied to the specific fact pattern, and the interaction between MAC / MAE and specific-indemnity mechanics.

### 5. Fact pattern C — regulatory-approval-driven timing pathology

**Fact pattern:** The transaction is a horizontal combination in a concentrated sector. HSR clearance requires a Second Request from the FTC / DOJ, extending the regulatory-clearance timeline from a nominal 30-day post-filing to 12–18 months post-filing. Between signing and 12 months later, general economic conditions have deteriorated, the target's specific revenue growth has slowed (from 35% year-over-year at signing to 15% year-over-year at 12 months post-signing), and the buyer's board has begun questioning the transaction's strategic fit.

**Buyer position (aim):** A MAC / MAE definition and outside-date mechanic that lets the buyer walk given the elongated timeline and the specific deterioration.

**Seller position (aim):** A MAC / MAE definition that captures the target's specific deterioration under the "disproportionately affected" qualifier but does not let the buyer walk on general-economic-conditions grounds.

**Your task:**

- Draft the buyer's version, including any specific timing-and-outside-date mechanic and any specific-EBITDA-decline threshold that would qualify as a MAC / MAE.
- Draft the seller's version, arguing that the 15% growth rate (materially positive) does not constitute a MAC / MAE even under the buyer's proposed threshold, and that the general-economic-conditions carve-out applies.
- Draft a middle-ground version — perhaps a specific bright-line quantitative threshold (X% EBITDA decline over Y months) that captures durationally-significant target-specific impact.
- Write a 1-page defence memo covering the *Akorn* framework's application to the timing-pathology fact pattern and the specific interaction with the outside-date termination right.

### 6. Draft the specific quantitative-threshold carve-outs

For each of the three fact patterns, consider whether to add a specific quantitative-threshold carve-out — a bright-line dollar or percentage below which the buyer cannot invoke MAC / MAE. Common formulations:

- **EBITDA-decline threshold.** MAC / MAE requires an X% year-over-year EBITDA decline over Y consecutive months (X often in the 20–40% range; Y often 4–8 months).
- **Revenue-decline threshold.** Similar formulation on revenue.
- **Financial-metric-and-durational-significance combined threshold.** MAC / MAE requires both a specific financial-metric decline and a durational-significance determination.
- **Specific-liability-dollar-value threshold.** MAC / MAE requires a specific dollar impact from the identified adverse event.

Draft the specific quantitative-threshold language for each fact pattern. Defend each threshold against the *Akorn* practitioner lesson (which validated a MAC / MAE finding on a substantial EBITDA-decline benchmark).

### 7. Draft the cross-scenario synthesis

Write a 2-page cross-scenario synthesis covering:

- **When a broad MAC / MAE serves the buyer.** The specific transaction facts that make a broad definition worth the negotiating cost.
- **When a narrow MAC / MAE serves the seller.** The specific transaction facts that make a narrow definition worth the negotiating cost.
- **The negotiating middle ground.** The specific carve-outs and qualifiers that a sophisticated counterparty would accept, and the specific carve-outs and qualifiers that a sophisticated counterparty would reject.
- **The interaction with the closing-conditions mechanic.** MAC / MAE is both a rep qualifier and a standalone closing condition; the interaction matters.
- **The interaction with the outside-date termination right.** For transactions with elongated regulatory-clearance timelines, the outside-date interacts with MAC / MAE in ways that shape the buyer's walk-away leverage.
- **The specific-indemnity alternative.** For known specific adverse events (product-safety signals, regulatory investigations, IP claims), a specific indemnity often serves both sides' interests better than a MAC / MAE walk-away right.

### 8. Chapter-8 negotiation-canon overlay

For each of the three fact-pattern MAC / MAE fights, name:

- The Freund sequencing frame — when in the negotiation does this fight happen? What does the trade-material look like?
- The Harvard frame — what is the buyer's underlying interest vs. position? The seller's? Where is the interest-based middle ground?
- The Voss frame — what calibrated questions would surface the other side's underlying concerns? What labelling defuses emotional charge?

## Starter guidance

Common MAC / MAE drafting errors to avoid:

- **The unqualified "material adverse effect" language.** Failing to include the "durationally significant" and "disproportionately affected" qualifiers exposes the seller to overly-broad walk-away rights that the *Akorn* framework does not support. Every well-drafted MAC / MAE definition includes these qualifiers.
- **The double-negative on carve-outs.** Adding a carve-out ("general economic conditions") without the disproportionately-affected qualifier lets the buyer argue that any target-specific impact on general economic conditions is inside the definition. The qualifier is essential.
- **The pandemic-specific carve-out ambiguity.** Post-COVID, the pandemic carve-out is standard but the specific drafting varies. Some drafts define pandemic broadly; some limit to specific named events. Consider the interaction with future pandemic events.
- **The failure-to-meet-projections carve-in trap.** The carve-out for "failure to meet projections" typically has an "underlying causes" carve-in — the underlying causes of the failure can be considered even if the failure itself cannot. Draft this precisely; imprecise drafting either creates loopholes or overwrites the carve-out.
- **The quantitative-threshold miscalibration.** A quantitative threshold set too low is a MAC / MAE that triggers on ordinary business variance; set too high, it is unreachable. Chapter 5's discussion of the *Akorn* benchmark is the practitioner reference.

## Reflection

1. Which of the three fact patterns produced the largest divergence between buyer and seller positions? What does that tell you about the MAC / MAE frame's limitations?
