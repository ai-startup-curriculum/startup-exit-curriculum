# Resources for mod-104-loi-negotiation-and-definitive-agreements

Primary source documentation, empirical deal-terms studies, R&W insurance market reports, Delaware Chancery and Supreme Court opinions, model agreements, practitioner writing, and the negotiation canon used by this module. Prefer these over secondary write-ups: the empirical studies refresh at least annually, the case-law citations are the authoritative statements of the doctrine, and the practitioner memos are the up-to-date market read.

> **Benchmarks refresh.** Numeric practitioner benchmarks cited in this module — exclusivity windows, standstill durations, indemnification caps, basket thresholds, R&W retention percentages, premium rates — are drawn from published studies that update annually. When an exercise or memo cites a specific number, follow the primary-source link, snapshot the current value with a date, and use that. Do not rely on numbers in secondary literature (including this document) for a live transaction.

> **Case law is state-specific.** The doctrinal references below are primarily Delaware (Delaware General Corporation Law and Delaware Chancery / Supreme Court decisions). Non-Delaware targets or acquirers invoke a different forum's law — California, New York, Texas, Washington, and the EU / UK each have their own M&A doctrine. The Delaware line is the modal reference because the substantial majority of venture-backed C-corps are Delaware-incorporated, but the applicable law is a function of the target's state of incorporation, the parties' choice-of-law clause, and the forum selected for any dispute.

> **This is education, not legal or tax advice.** A live transaction hands off to qualified M&A counsel. The intent of this document is to point to the materials a competent M&A lawyer or Corp-Dev VP would read; it is not a substitute for engagement of counsel.

## Empirical deal-terms studies

The two most-cited empirical sources on US private-target M&A deal terms. Each is referenced by name throughout this module ("ABA Deal Points," "SRS Acquiom").

- **ABA Business Law Section — Private Target Mergers & Acquisitions Deal Points Study.** The flagship empirical study of US private-target M&A deal terms, published periodically (typically every two years) by the ABA Business Law Section's M&A Market Trends Subcommittee. Covers rep-and-warranty scope, qualifiers, indemnification cap / basket / survival, MAC / MAE definitions, closing conditions, and R&W insurance usage trends. The study samples a few hundred recent transactions and reports distributions of specific contract terms. <https://www.americanbar.org/groups/business_law/resources/market-trends/>
- **ABA Business Law Section — Public Target Mergers & Acquisitions Deal Points Study.** The public-target counterpart to the Private Target study. Covers the Revlon / Corwin / MFW-shaped practice: fiduciary-out structure, go-shop clauses, break-up fee percentages (typical 2.5–4% of equity value in US public-target deals), matching rights, voting agreements, and tender-offer vs. one-step merger structures. Published periodically by the ABA M&A Market Trends Subcommittee. <https://www.americanbar.org/groups/business_law/resources/market-trends/>
- **ABA Business Law Section — Strategic Buyer / Private Equity Buyer Deal Points Study.** Compares deal-term distributions across strategic-buyer and PE-buyer transactions. Highlights the systematic differences — strategic buyers typically push for broader reps and larger caps; PE buyers more commonly use R&W insurance and narrower seller-indemnification packages. <https://www.americanbar.org/groups/business_law/resources/market-trends/>
- **ABA Business Law Section — Carve-Out Transactions Deal Points Study.** Deal-term distributions specific to carve-out / divestiture transactions (parent company selling a subsidiary or business unit), where the transition-services agreement, retained-liability, and reps-and-warranties scope have carve-out-specific patterns. <https://www.americanbar.org/groups/business_law/resources/market-trends/>
- **ABA Business Law Section — Canadian Private Target M&A Deal Points Study.** The Canadian-targets counterpart. Material differences in US vs. Canadian practice on survival periods and indemnification caps. <https://www.americanbar.org/groups/business_law/resources/market-trends/>
- **SRS Acquiom — M&A Deal Terms Study.** Annual study based on SRS Acquiom's role as shareholder representative on several hundred private-target transactions per year. Covers indemnification caps, baskets, survival periods, R&W insurance usage, escrow sizing, working-capital-adjustment outcomes, earn-out usage and achievement rates, and dispute frequency. Highly practitioner-oriented because SRS sees the actual claims experience post-closing. <https://www.srsacquiom.com/resources/>
- **SRS Acquiom — Buy-Side Representations & Warranties Insurance Study.** SRS Acquiom's analysis of R&W insurance claims across transactions where SRS acts as shareholder representative. Primary source on claim frequency, severity, denial rates, and specific exclusions-triggered disputes. <https://www.srsacquiom.com/resources/>
- **SRS Acquiom — Escrow and Indemnification Study.** Empirical data on escrow sizes, claim frequency, release timing, and the distribution of claim resolution outcomes. The single most useful empirical reference for sizing an escrow / holdback against a transaction's risk profile. <https://www.srsacquiom.com/resources/>
- **SRS Acquiom — Earn-Outs Study.** Deal-term distributions and achievement outcomes for M&A transactions with earn-out consideration. Supplements the mod-103 earn-out design work. <https://www.srsacquiom.com/resources/>
- **Houlihan Lokey / Cornerstone Research — M&A Study publications.** Various periodic studies on M&A deal volume, size, and premium trends. <https://www.cornerstone.com/insights/> <https://www.hl.com/insights/>

## Delaware Chancery and Supreme Court opinions — the doctrinal canon

The specific cases cited in the chapters. Each is cited by name throughout practitioner literature; read the actual opinions for the authoritative reasoning.

### MAC / MAE doctrine (chapter 5)

- **IBP, Inc. v. Tyson Foods, Inc., 789 A.2d 14 (Del. Ch. 2001).** The foundational Delaware MAC / MAE case. Vice Chancellor Strine held that a MAC / MAE must be "material when viewed from the longer-term perspective of a reasonable acquirer," establishing the "durationally significant" requirement that still governs the doctrine. <https://courts.delaware.gov/opinions/download.aspx?ID=24850> (search the Delaware Chancery opinion archive for the full text)
- **Hexion Specialty Chemicals, Inc. v. Huntsman Corp., 965 A.2d 715 (Del. Ch. 2008).** Vice Chancellor Lamb reinforced the high MAC / MAE bar in the context of the 2008 financial crisis. Held that a 20% decline in EBITDA was not sufficient to constitute a MAC / MAE under the specific definition at issue; buyer was required to close. <https://courts.delaware.gov/opinions/>
- **Akorn, Inc. v. Fresenius Kabi AG, 2018 WL 4719347 (Del. Ch. Oct. 1, 2018), aff'd, 198 A.3d 724 (Del. 2018).** The first and still only Delaware opinion to find a MAC / MAE sufficient to allow the buyer to walk from a signed deal. Vice Chancellor Laster held that Akorn's post-signing performance decline (ARR down ~25%, EBITDA down ~86%), combined with pervasive FDA data-integrity-rule violations, constituted both a MAC / MAE and separate breaches of regulatory and operating covenants. The opinion is the current practitioner reference for MAC / MAE drafting discipline. <https://courts.delaware.gov/opinions/download.aspx?ID=277290>
- **Channel Medsystems, Inc. v. Boston Scientific Corp., 2019 WL 6896462 (Del. Ch. Dec. 18, 2019).** Vice Chancellor Zurn reinforced the *Akorn* framework; held that undisclosed fraud by target's employee did not constitute a MAC / MAE because the target had already remediated and the business impact was not durationally significant. Illustrates the specific "durational significance" and "disproportionate effect" analysis in action. <https://courts.delaware.gov/opinions/>
- **Snow Phipps Group, LLC v. KCake Acquisition, Inc., 2021 WL 1714202 (Del. Ch. Apr. 30, 2021).** Vice Chancellor Slights held that pandemic-era declines did not satisfy the MAC / MAE condition and that the buyer's reliance on an "ordinary course" covenant breach was similarly unsuccessful. The opinion is cited by sellers in current practice as reaffirmation of the high MAC / MAE bar even in extreme circumstances. <https://courts.delaware.gov/opinions/>
- **AB Stable VIII LLC v. MAPS Hotels and Resorts One LLC, 2020 WL 7024929 (Del. Ch. Nov. 30, 2020), aff'd, 268 A.3d 198 (Del. 2021).** Hotel-sector pandemic case. Vice Chancellor Laster held that COVID-related operating changes constituted an "extraordinary" change outside the ordinary course of business, allowing the buyer to walk based on breach of the ordinary-course covenant rather than MAC / MAE. The opinion is the current practitioner reference for how the ordinary-course covenant and MAC / MAE interact. <https://courts.delaware.gov/opinions/>

### Type-II preliminary-agreement doctrine (chapter 1)

- **SIGA Technologies, Inc. v. PharmAthene, Inc., 67 A.3d 330 (Del. 2013).** Delaware Supreme Court affirmed that a term sheet with a "proposed" set of terms created an enforceable duty to negotiate in good faith and that the party that walked away was liable for expectation damages. The current practitioner reference for the drafting discipline on LOI "nonbinding" language. On remand: *PharmAthene, Inc. v. SIGA Technologies, Inc.*, 2015 WL 220484 (Del. Ch. Jan. 15, 2015). <https://courts.delaware.gov/opinions/>
- **Teachers Insurance and Annuity Association v. Tribune Co., 670 F. Supp. 491 (S.D.N.Y. 1987).** Judge Leval's canonical opinion distinguishing "type I" preliminary agreements (binding on all material terms, with details to be worked out) from "type II" preliminary agreements (binding to negotiate in good faith on a defined framework). The source of the type-I / type-II taxonomy in New York law.
- **Empro Manufacturing Co. v. Ball-Co Manufacturing, Inc., 870 F.2d 423 (7th Cir. 1989).** Judge Easterbrook's opinion holding that an LOI with "subject to definitive agreement" language was not binding. Still cited for the proposition that explicit nonbinding language is enforceable; contrast with *SIGA* on what "good faith" language can imply.
- **Global Asset Capital, Inc. v. Rubicon US REIT, Inc., 2016 WL 4091174 (Del. Ch. July 29, 2016).** Delaware application of the type-II framework in the LOI context. <https://courts.delaware.gov/opinions/>

### Fiduciary-duty-driven M&A doctrine (cross-referenced to mod-109; cited for the fiduciary-out drafting)

- **Revlon, Inc. v. MacAndrews & Forbes Holdings, Inc., 506 A.2d 173 (Del. 1986).** The source of the "Revlon duties" that trigger when a target's board enters change-of-control. The reason sell-side counsel must negotiate a fiduciary-out carve-out in the exclusivity provision. <https://courts.delaware.gov/opinions/>
- **Smith v. Van Gorkom, 488 A.2d 858 (Del. 1985).** The duty-of-care foundation; still cited for the proposition that a board must inform itself of all material information reasonably available before accepting a sale. <https://courts.delaware.gov/opinions/>
- **Omnicare, Inc. v. NCS Healthcare, Inc., 818 A.2d 914 (Del. 2003).** Held that a merger-agreement structure that locked up the deal with no fiduciary-out violated the board's fiduciary duties. The reason modern exclusivity provisions include a fiduciary-out. <https://courts.delaware.gov/opinions/>
- **Corwin v. KKR Financial Holdings LLC, 125 A.3d 304 (Del. 2015).** Established that a fully-informed, uncoerced stockholder vote cleanses breach-of-fiduciary-duty claims (absent a controller). Shapes the post-signing stockholder-approval choreography. <https://courts.delaware.gov/opinions/>
- **Kahn v. M&F Worldwide Corp. (MFW), 88 A.3d 635 (Del. 2014).** The controller-transaction cleansing framework: dual special-committee-plus-majority-of-minority process restores business-judgment-rule review. Shapes special-committee construction for controller transactions. <https://courts.delaware.gov/opinions/>

### Earn-out / covenant-breach doctrine (cross-referenced to mod-103)

- **Fortis Advisors LLC v. Johnson & Johnson, 2024 WL 2886559 (Del. Ch. June 7, 2024).** Recent earn-out dispute; useful for the "commercially reasonable efforts" standard and the specific operating-covenant discipline post-closing. <https://courts.delaware.gov/opinions/>
- **Shareholder Representative Services LLC v. Shire US Holdings, Inc., 2020 WL 6018955 (Del. Ch. Oct. 12, 2020).** Earn-out dispute case holding Shire liable for failure to use commercially reasonable efforts; recovery of ~$140M. <https://courts.delaware.gov/opinions/>
- **Winshall v. Viacom Int'l, Inc., 76 A.3d 808 (Del. 2013).** Delaware Supreme Court on earn-out interpretation. <https://courts.delaware.gov/opinions/>

### Indemnification-and-sandbagging doctrine (chapter 6)

- **Cobalt Operating, LLC v. James Crystal Enterprises, LLC, 2007 WL 2142926 (Del. Ch. July 20, 2007).** The leading Delaware authority on "pro-sandbagging" — a buyer who knows of a rep inaccuracy pre-closing can still bring an indemnification claim unless the agreement expressly provides otherwise. The reason modern sellers push for anti-sandbagging language. <https://courts.delaware.gov/opinions/>
- **Eagle Industries, Inc. v. DeVilbiss Health Care, Inc., 702 A.2d 1228 (Del. 1997).** Delaware Supreme Court on indemnification-claim mechanics. <https://courts.delaware.gov/opinions/>
- **ArchKey Intermediate Holdings Inc. v. Mona, 302 A.3d 975 (Del. Ch. 2023).** Recent application of the Cobalt line; sandbagging analysis in a specific-facts context. <https://courts.delaware.gov/opinions/>

### Banker-conflict and Revlon-process doctrine (cross-referenced to mod-109)

- **In re Rural Metro Corp. Stockholders Litigation, 88 A.3d 54 (Del. Ch. 2014).** Vice Chancellor Laster held that a financial advisor's undisclosed conflicts aiding and abetting a board's breach of fiduciary duty is actionable. Shapes the fairness-opinion-procurement discipline. <https://courts.delaware.gov/opinions/>
- **In re Toys "R" Us, Inc. Shareholder Litigation, 877 A.2d 975 (Del. Ch. 2005).** Chancellor Strine on no-shop and matching-rights provisions in the Revlon context. <https://courts.delaware.gov/opinions/>
- **In re Dollar Thrifty Shareholder Litigation, 14 A.3d 573 (Del. Ch. 2010).** No-shop and go-shop analysis in a specific Revlon-triggered process. <https://courts.delaware.gov/opinions/>

### DGCL and procedural statutes

- **Delaware General Corporation Law (DGCL).** The statutory framework. Note especially §203 (business-combinations with interested stockholders), §251 (merger procedure), §262 (appraisal rights), §271 (sale of all or substantially all assets), §280/281 (dissolution-and-winding-up — cross-referenced to mod-113). <https://delcode.delaware.gov/title8/c001/index.html>
- **DGCL §262 — Appraisal rights.** The statutory basis for stockholder appraisal claims; shapes the sign-and-close choreography and reserves. <https://delcode.delaware.gov/title8/c001/sc09/>

- **James C. ## Negotiation canon

The three bodies of writing that together form the deal-scale negotiation canon referenced in chapter 8.

### The Freund tradition

- **James C. Freund — *Anatomy of a Merger* (1975).** See the full citation above under M&A treatises. The specific chapters most useful for chapter 8 are the sequencing discussion (chapters 2–3 in the original edition) and the closing-negotiation discussion (chapters 7–8).
- **James C. Freund — *Smart Negotiating: How to Make Good Deals in the Real World* (Simon & Schuster, 1992).** The non-M&A-specific companion; distils the Freund negotiation-sequencing frame into general-commercial-negotiation vocabulary. <https://www.simonandschuster.com/>

### The Harvard Negotiation Project tradition

- **Roger Fisher, William Ury, and Bruce Patton — *Getting to Yes: Negotiating Agreement Without Giving In* (Houghton Mifflin / Penguin, 1981; 2nd ed. 1991; 3rd ed. 2011).** The foundational principled-negotiation text. The specific concepts most-cited in chapter 8: separating people from the problem, interests behind positions, invention of options for mutual gain, objective criteria, BATNA (Best Alternative to a Negotiated Agreement). <https://www.pon.harvard.edu/shop/getting-to-yes-negotiating-agreement-without-giving-in/>
- **William Ury — *Getting Past No: Negotiating in Difficult Situations* (Bantam, 1991).** The response to hard-bargaining tactics. The specific concept most-cited: the five-step "breakthrough" method (go to the balcony; step to their side; reframe; build a golden bridge; use power to educate). <https://www.williamury.com/books/getting-past-no/>
- **Roger Fisher and Daniel Shapiro — *Beyond Reason: Using Emotions as You Negotiate* (Penguin, 2005).** The emotional-dimension companion to *Getting to Yes*. The specific concepts: the five "core concerns" (appreciation, affiliation, autonomy, status, role) as leverage for productive conversation. <https://www.pon.harvard.edu/>
- **Robert H. Mnookin, Scott R. Peppet, and Andrew S. Tulumello — *Beyond Winning: Negotiating to Create Value in Deals and Disputes* (Harvard University Press, 2000).** Mnookin's extension of the principled-negotiation frame to the specific deal / dispute lawyer context. <https://www.hup.harvard.edu/>
- **Robert H. Mnookin — *Bargaining with the Devil: When to Negotiate, When to Fight* (Simon & Schuster, 2010).** On the specific question of whether to negotiate at all. <https://www.pon.harvard.edu/>
- **David A. Lax and James K. Sebenius — *3-D Negotiation: Powerful Tools to Change the Game in Your Most Important Deals* (Harvard Business School Press, 2006).** The "setup" dimension of negotiation (who, when, what sequence, what table). Complements the principled-negotiation tactical frame with a strategic-architecture frame. <https://www.hbsp.harvard.edu/>
- **Deepak Malhotra and Max H. Bazerman — *Negotiation Genius: How to Overcome Obstacles and Achieve Brilliant Results at the Bargaining Table and Beyond* (Bantam, 2007).** Harvard Business School practitioner-oriented negotiation text; especially useful on the specific-tactics vocabulary and the "mistakes-made-at-the-table" lens. <https://www.bantambooks.com/>
