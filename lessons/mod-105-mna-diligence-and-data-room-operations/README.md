# mod-105 — M&A Diligence & Data-Room Operations

**Estimated effort:** 24 hours
**Stage:** GROWTH / MATURE (the sell-side data-room construction and quality-of-earnings preparation begin 3–9 months before a process; buy-side workstream design and R&W underwriter negotiation are exercised live inside a transaction)
**Seat:** founder-CEO, CFO, GC, SVP-Corp-Dev, VP-FP&A, controller, VP-legal-operations, transaction M&A counsel, Q of E provider partner, R&W insurance broker

This is the diligence and data-room-operations module of the sell-side / buy-side M&A arc. mod-104 installed the LOI-and-definitive-agreement drafting-and-negotiation apparatus. Between LOI signing and definitive-agreement signing, the entire transaction runs on two artefacts and two workstreams: on the sell-side, a **defensible data room** and a **pre-transaction quality-of-earnings report** that pre-empts buyer findings; on the buy-side, a **diligence workstream plan** across ten to fifteen disciplines and a **diligence-findings memo** that either preserves the LOI price or drives a defensible price re-trade. On top of both sits the **R&W insurance underwriter**, whose diligence-review call and exclusions negotiation reshape what both sides actually need to inspect. Do these badly and the definitive-agreement negotiation stalls, exclusivity lapses, the deal price re-trades by five to fifteen percent, or a material item surfaces post-close that triggers an indemnification claim, an R&W policy exclusion, or a stockholder lawsuit. Do them well and the transaction closes on time at the LOI headline number.

The module owns the *transaction-side* diligence-and-data-room discipline. It does not re-teach the ongoing-company GTM infrastructure inspected during commercial diligence ([`startup-product-gtm-curriculum`](https://github.com/ai-startup-curriculum/startup-product-gtm-curriculum) owns that), the security-engineering depth of a SOC 2 / ISO 27001 / HIPAA control set or a penetration-test methodology ([`security-learning`](https://github.com/ai-infra-curriculum/security-learning) owns that), or the AI-safety-technical depth of a model-card audit, training-data-provenance investigation, or AI-Act GPAI-provider obligations ([`chief-ai-officer-learning`](https://github.com/ai-governance-curriculum/chief-ai-officer-learning) and [`head-of-ai-governance-learning`](https://github.com/ai-governance-curriculum/head-of-ai-governance-learning) own those). This module owns the workstream-design and diligence-response apparatus on top of those subject-matter disciplines.

## Learning objectives

By the end of this module you should be able to:

- Construct a sell-side data room that a diligence attorney can navigate without a guide — the fifteen-workstream architecture (corporate / financial / tax / commercial / customer / product-and-technology / IP / HR-and-comp / real-estate / privacy / security / AI-and-model / open-source / environmental / regulatory / litigation), file-naming discipline, folder-hierarchy defensibility, redaction discipline for PII / PCI / customer-confidential material, clean-team protocols for competitively sensitive information, and virtual-data-room selection (Intralinks / Datasite / Firmex / Ansarada / DealRoom) with access-controls-and-audit-log configuration.
- Prepare a sell-side pre-transaction quality-of-earnings report authored by a Big Four or QoE-specialist firm (Frank Rimerman, Riveron, Kroll, RSM, Grant Thornton) that pre-empts buyer findings — the EBITDA-normalisation adjustment discipline (owner comp, non-recurring items, run-rate revenue, deferred-revenue accounting) and the presentation architecture that survives a buyer-side rebuttal.
- Manage the sell-side diligence response — workstream owners, response-turnaround SLA (typically 24–48h for straightforward, 72h for complex), escalation-to-executive rules, the running Q&A tracker, the confidential-communication channel with the acquirer's diligence lead, and the pattern of proactive disclosure vs. answering only what is asked.
- Design a buy-side diligence workstream plan across financial / legal / tax / commercial / technology / IP / HR / privacy / security / AI-and-model / open-source, with the scope, provider, deliverable, and read-across-to-definitive-agreement mapping for each workstream.
- Author a buy-side diligence-findings memo (finding / evidence / impact / recommendation) and drive price renegotiation without collapsing the deal — the deal-team escalation for material findings, the price-adjustment mechanic (purchase-price reduction, specific-indemnity carve-out, escrow increase, deal-collapse trigger), and the discipline of using diligence to preserve deal certainty rather than kill the deal.
- Negotiate diligence scope with the R&W insurance underwriter — the underwriter's diligence-review call, the exclusions negotiation (cyber, wage-and-hour, tax positions in dispute, foreign-corrupt-practices, AI-model-training-data provenance), the underwriter-driven expansion of buy-side diligence, and the impact on retention / premium / coverage.
- Run AI-model and open-source-license diligence at the transaction-side level — training-data provenance, model-card documentation, GPAI-provider obligations under the EU AI Act, the AI-vendor-contract inventory, and the OSS-licence-obligation inventory (copyleft exposure, attribution obligations, patent-grant back) — while deferring the technical depth to the AI-governance and security tracks.
- Recognise the ownership boundary — the ongoing-company GTM infrastructure that commercial diligence inspects is owned by `startup-product-gtm-curriculum` and never re-taught; the security-engineering depth defers to `security-learning`; the AI-safety-technical depth defers to `chief-ai-officer-learning` and `head-of-ai-governance-learning`.

## Ownership boundary

This module owns the transaction-side diligence-and-data-room apparatus. It does *not* re-teach:

- **The ongoing-company GTM infrastructure inspected during commercial diligence** — pipeline hygiene, cohort retention instrumentation, customer-success motion, sales-productivity ratios, competitive-positioning materials, marketing-attribution methodology — [`startup-product-gtm-curriculum`](https://github.com/ai-startup-curriculum/startup-product-gtm-curriculum) owns those. Commercial diligence in this module *inspects* the GTM machine that the target has already built; it does not teach how to build it.
- **The security-engineering depth of a SOC 2 / ISO 27001 / HIPAA control set, a penetration-test methodology, or a threat-model** — [`security-learning`](https://github.com/ai-infra-curriculum/security-learning) owns those. Security diligence in this module *inspects* the security posture (the SOC 2 report, the pen-test report, the prior-incident disclosure, the control-set attestations) and translates findings into definitive-agreement rep language and R&W-policy exclusions; the security-engineering depth of what a control actually does defers to the security track.
- **The AI-safety-technical depth of a model card, training-data-provenance investigation, model-evaluation methodology, or AI-Act GPAI-provider obligations at the technical level** — [`chief-ai-officer-learning`](https://github.com/ai-governance-curriculum/chief-ai-officer-learning) and [`head-of-ai-governance-learning`](https://github.com/ai-governance-curriculum/head-of-ai-governance-learning) own those. AI-model diligence in this module *inspects* the AI-governance artefacts the target has produced (model cards, evaluation reports, training-data-source inventory, AI-vendor-contract stack, GPAI-provider filings) and translates findings into deal-side outcomes; the AI-safety-technical depth defers to the AI-governance track.
- **The ongoing-company financial infrastructure that a Q of E audits** — three-statement model, cohort unit economics, revenue-recognition posture under ASC 606, monthly close, controller function — [`startup-finance-fundraising-curriculum`](https://github.com/ai-startup-curriculum/startup-finance-fundraising-curriculum) owns those. This module runs the *transaction-side* Q of E as a marketing-and-defence artefact; it uses the underlying finance function as an input.
- **Deal-structure design (mod-103), definitive-agreement drafting (mod-104), transaction-governance and fiduciary-duty analysis at deal-scale (mod-109), transaction communications (mod-110), post-close integration (mod-111)** — those modules in this track own those disciplines. This module hands diligence findings across the seam into the definitive-agreement disclosure schedules (mod-104), into the definitive-agreement rep language and indemnification package (mod-104), and into the integration-planning input for the first-100-day plan (mod-111).

What this module *adds* on top: the data-room architecture and clean-team protocol that let a sell-side process withstand a competitor showing up on the bidder list; the pre-transaction QoE that pre-empts the buyer's finding; the sell-side response tracker and SLA discipline that hold the diligence period on schedule; the buy-side workstream plan that covers the ten-to-fifteen disciplines a modern venture-backed acquisition inspects; the findings-memo-and-price-renegotiation choreography that preserves deal certainty; the R&W-underwriter diligence negotiation; and the AI-model / OSS-licence transaction-side diligence that the last five years of practice have added to the standard workstream set.

## How to work through this module

Carry a specific hypothetical transaction through the module — the transaction you have been running from mod-101 forward. The workstream taxonomy in chapters 1 and 4 is only useful if you can populate it with the target's actual corporate documents, financial artefacts, customer contracts, technology stack, and workforce composition. If you are using a hypothetical, at minimum freeze the profile constructed in mod-101 exercise-01 plus the deal structure designed in mod-103 plus the LOI drafted in mod-104 exercises 01 / 02 — those become the inputs to the diligence work here.

Chapters 1–3 install the sell-side apparatus (data room, quality of earnings, diligence response). Chapters 4–6 install the buy-side apparatus (workstream plan, findings memo, R&W underwriter negotiation). Chapter 7 installs the AI-model and OSS-licence diligence that has become table-stakes in the last five years. The exercises run in sequence and produce artefacts you can use in a project deliverable — the sell-side pack (exercises 01–03) feeds `project-101-sell-side-m-and-a-execution-pack`; the buy-side pack (exercises 04–07) feeds any buy-side simulation you run against the same target.
