# exercise-02: Quality of earnings preparation drill

**Estimated effort:** 5–6 hours

## Objective

Author a full sell-side quality-of-earnings adjustment schedule for a specific hypothetical target, defend the adjustments against a simulated buyer-side rebuttal, and produce the presentation architecture (executive summary, adjustment bridge, methodology memo, and workpaper index) that a Big Four or QoE-specialist provider would deliver to a buyer's QoE team at LOI signing.

The finished artefact is a QoE-report package (10–20 pages of memo, plus a single-page waterfall exhibit and a workpaper index) that a CFO could present to the target's board, defend line-by-line against a buyer's QoE rebuttal, and hand to the winning bidder at LOI signing under a reliance letter.

You should end the exercise able to defend every adjustment (why this treatment, why this dollar amount, why this evidence pointer), pre-answer at least three specific rebuttal patterns the buyer's QoE is likely to raise, and articulate the working-capital-target and closing-cash / closing-debt bridges that feed the definitive-agreement purchase-price mechanic.

## Prerequisites

- The target-facts baseline from exercise 1 (or the mod-101 / mod-102 target). Extend it with:
  - Three years of historical financial summary (revenue, EBITDA, cash burn or generation).
  - The revenue-recognition posture — subscription, usage-based, hybrid, professional-services mix.
  - Any known non-recurring events in the historical period (aborted transaction, restructuring, one-time litigation, pandemic-related distortion, prior acquisition).
- A working knowledge of the ASC 606 five-step model. If unfamiliar, review the FASB codification introduction or a Big Four ASC 606 primer.
- A spreadsheet (Excel, Google Sheets) for the adjustment bridge and the working-capital analysis.
- The most-current SRS Acquiom or ABA Deal Points study for anchor data on typical EBITDA-adjustment prevalence and dispute rates.

## Tasks

### 1. Freeze the reported-EBITDA baseline

For the target, produce a reported-EBITDA-for-TTM (trailing twelve months) as of a specific reference date. Include:

- Revenue by category (subscription ARR-recognised, subscription non-ARR, usage, professional services, other).
- Cost of revenue.
- Gross margin.
- Sales-and-marketing.
- R&D.
- G&A.
- EBITDA.
- Any specific presentation choices (SBC treatment, capitalised software amortisation treatment).

Document your source assumptions (management-reporting basis, GAAP basis, adjustments already reflected). This is the anchor from which the QoE walks.

### 2. Draft the owner and executive compensation normalisation

Adjustment category 1 from chapter 2.

- Identify the founder-and-executive population.
- Benchmark each executive's compensation against a market source (Radford, Pave, Option Impact — or a defensible surrogate).
- Compute the adjustment (below-market comp normalises DOWN adjusted EBITDA; above-market comp normalises UP).
- Document the methodology in a workpaper.

### 3. Draft the non-recurring-items schedule

Adjustment category 2.

- List at least three non-recurring items in the target's historical period. Common candidates: transaction-preparation costs, aborted-prior-transaction costs, one-time litigation, restructuring, business-interruption events, executive-severance one-offs.
- For each: name the specific event, cite the supporting-evidence pointer (which document, which invoice, which board consent), quantify the dollar impact, defend the "non-recurring" classification (distinguish from variable-but-recurring).
- If any item is borderline, note both the aggressive and the conservative treatment and defend the choice.

### 4. Draft the run-rate-revenue adjustments

Adjustment category 3 — the most-disputed category.

- Full-period annualisation of customers signed mid-period.
- Removal of one-time revenue (migration fees, one-time professional services, perpetual-licence one-offs).
- Removal of revenue from churned customers.
- ASC 606 vs. contracted-revenue view where the target has multi-year contracts with recognition timing that does not reflect the economic activity.
- Document the methodology explicitly ("annualised value of active subscriptions as of the transaction reference date, with churn adjustments for customers who have provided termination notice"). Anchor the methodology, not the number.

### 5. Draft the deferred-revenue and revenue-recognition analysis

Adjustment category 4.

- Deferred-revenue rollforward (opening, additions, releases, ending) for the last 24 months.
- ASC 606 five-step-model posture memo covering the target's contract-identification, performance-obligation-identification, transaction-price, allocation, and recognition treatment. Note any nonstandard treatments (bill-and-hold, principal-vs-agent for reseller arrangements, right-of-return, variable consideration, contract-modification).
- FASB ASU 2021-08 acquisition-accounting posture — walk the buyer through the target's deferred-revenue balances and the acquirer's post-close revenue exposure under the updated treatment.
- Debt-like-item treatment for deferred revenue in the enterprise-value-to-equity-value bridge (partial or full — negotiated).

### 6. Draft the one-time-expenses and cost-basis-adjustments schedule

Adjustment category 5.

- Signing bonuses and executive-onboarding one-offs.
- Consulting engagements now concluded.
- One-time infrastructure migrations.
- Founder-vehicle / personal-expense / related-party items (with founder disclosure).
- Above-market related-party arrangements normalised to market.
- Stock-based-compensation treatment (with both views — excluded per practitioner default; included per some buyer-side treatments).

### 7. Draft the pro-forma adjustments for pending transactions

Adjustment category 6.

- Recent acquisitions — pro-forma contribution as if owned for the full historical period.
- Recent divestitures — pro-forma removal.
- Signed customer contracts with revenue not yet booked.
- Pending customer losses (received termination notice; contract not yet run out).

### 8. Build the adjustment-bridge waterfall

Produce a single-page adjustment-bridge waterfall (Excel or Google Sheets) with:

- Reported EBITDA on the far left.
- Each adjustment as a discrete waterfall bar with dollar value.
- Positive vs. negative adjustments colour-differentiated.
- Adjusted EBITDA on the far right.
- Every bar labelled and cross-referenced to a workpaper number.

Alongside the waterfall, produce a one-page narrative table with each adjustment's rationale, dollar amount, supporting-evidence pointer, and defensibility paragraph.

### 9. Draft the working-capital and closing-cash / closing-debt analysis

- Historical working-capital by month for 24 months, with seasonality and trend called out.
- Any one-time working-capital events that distort the historical picture.
- Recommended target-working-capital number (fixed dollar or formula — defend the choice).
- Deferred-revenue treatment in working capital (included, excluded, partial — defend).
- Closing-cash and closing-debt bridge with debt-like-items list (bank debt, venture debt, capital-lease liabilities, unpaid transaction bonuses, deferred-comp balances, unpaid earn-outs from prior acquisitions, unpaid sales-tax liabilities, environmental reserves, litigation reserves).

### 10. Author the executive summary and methodology memo

- **Executive summary** (1 page). Reported EBITDA, adjusted EBITDA, adjustment count by category, headline conclusion, working-capital target headline, debt-like-items headline. Written for the deal-team read.
- **Methodology memo** (2–4 pages). For each adjustment category, the methodology, the practitioner precedent, and the pre-answered rebuttal.

### 11. Simulate the buyer-side rebuttal

Pick three adjustments (typically owner-comp normalisation, one aggressive run-rate item, and the SBC treatment). For each, write a buyer-side rebuttal:

- The buyer's alternative treatment.
- The buyer's evidence.
- The buyer's proposed adjustment.

Then draft the sell-side counter-response for each. The exchange should be tight, evidence-anchored, and grounded in specific practitioner sources.

### 12. Draft the reliance-letter mechanism

Draft (or describe in operating detail) the reliance-letter mechanism through which the QoE-provider will permit the winning bidder's QoE provider to rely on the sell-side QoE. Note the engagement-letter language the target should have insisted on with its own QoE provider to preserve this.

## Starter guidance

Common QoE-preparation errors to avoid:

- **Add-back inflation.** Every EBITDA-augmenting adjustment that lacks specific evidence gets stripped by the buyer's QoE. Include only adjustments you can defend.
- **Omitting EBITDA-reducing adjustments.** A one-sided report loses credibility fast. Include the up-normalisation of above-market comp; include the pending cost the target has committed to but not yet incurred.
- **Methodology-thin reporting.** A methodology memo that says "we normalised owner comp to market" without naming Radford, Pave, or Option Impact is not defensible. The methodology matters more than the number.
- **Working-capital-target games.** Setting the target working capital materially below the historical average as a hidden buyer-favour is caught by the buyer's QoE and burns the seller's credibility.
- **Debt-like-items omission.** Failure to identify unpaid transaction bonuses, deferred-comp, unpaid earn-outs, sales-tax accruals, environmental reserves. Buyers surface these; sell-side omission reads as concealment.
- **Under-scoped QoE.** A "quick look" QoE that the buyer's QoE cannot rely on defeats the pre-empt purpose. Scope the engagement for a full report.

## Acceptance criteria

You can demonstrate that:

- Reported-EBITDA baseline is documented with source assumptions.
- All six adjustment categories are drafted with dollar amounts, evidence pointers, and defensibility narrative.
- Adjustment-bridge waterfall is produced (Excel / Google Sheets).
- Working-capital analysis includes 24-month history, seasonality, target-working-capital recommendation with defence, and deferred-revenue treatment.
- Closing-cash / closing-debt bridge is documented with a complete debt-like-items list.
- Executive summary (1 page) and methodology memo (2–4 pages) are drafted.
- Three buyer-side rebuttals are simulated with sell-side counter-responses.
- Reliance-letter mechanism is drafted or described in operating detail.

## Reflection

Add a short reflection:

1. Which single adjustment moved adjusted EBITDA by the largest amount, and would you defend it against a hostile buyer's QoE?
2. If your run-rate-revenue methodology were rejected by the buyer's QoE and the buyer proposed an ARR-annualised-in-place methodology instead, how large would the adjusted-EBITDA delta be?
3. For the working-capital target, would you prefer a fixed-dollar or a formula-based target for this specific target's seasonality profile? Why?
4. Which debt-like item on your list would you expect the buyer to press hardest, and what is your sell-side defence?

## Stretch goals

- **Full workpaper package.** Draft the full workpaper package supporting each adjustment — the source-data extraction, the calculation methodology, the reconciliation to source. This is what the buyer's QoE re-performs.
- **QoE-provider engagement letter.** Draft (or heavily annotate a template of) the QoE-provider engagement letter, with attention to scope, reliance letter, deliverable timing, and fees.
- **SaaS-metric-normalisation addendum.** For a SaaS-heavy target, produce a SaaS-metric normalisation addendum covering ARR, gross retention, net retention, gross margin, LTV/CAC, magic number, and payback — with methodology defended for each.
- **Broker-directed sell-side QoE conversation.** Reach out to a QoE-specialist firm's practice-development contact and request an introductory conversation. Note the specific scope-and-timeline advice they give against your specific target.
- **Buyer-side QoE simulation.** Swap seats — take your sell-side QoE and, from the buyer's chair, draft the buyer-side QoE that would result. Where do you push back? Where do you accept? Where do you add adjustments? The exercise reinforces the presentation-architecture disciplines from the sell-side side.
- **QoE-as-signing-diligence exhibit.** Structure the final QoE-report package as an exhibit to the seller's data room, cross-linked to the source workpapers and to the disclosure-schedule tax-and-financial items. This mirrors how the artefact actually lands in transaction operating practice.
