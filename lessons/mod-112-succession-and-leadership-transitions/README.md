# mod-112 — Succession & Leadership Transitions: Founder-CEO to Professional CEO, Board-Chair, Executive Bench

**Estimated effort:** 20 hours
**Stage:** MATURE (typically $50M+ ARR / 250+ headcount, or an approaching transaction that forces the question earlier)
**Seat:** founder-CEO, board chair, lead independent director, incoming CEO, president / COO, CHRO / VP-People, executive coach, executive-search partner

This module installs the **succession-and-leadership-transitions overlay** that sits on top of a company reaching the operating scale at which founder-fit-with-current-stage becomes a live board question — and on top of every transaction (M&A close, IPO, PE take-private, controller change) that forces the same question at a compressed timeline. It is the workstream that converts a founder-CEO's operating tenure into a durable leadership institution — a professional-CEO transition that lands and holds, a board-chair transition that separates chair from CEO with a defensible process, an executive bench that survives a top-of-house departure, and a board composition that graduates from venture-stage seats to growth-and-mature-stage seats.

The frame is *choreography of a small number of high-consequence transitions*, not "leadership development" in the ordinary-course HR sense. The Charan / Nadler / Bower / Wasserman canon on CEO succession is remarkably consistent on the empirical shape: **the majority of unplanned CEO transitions destroy value**, the *planned* transitions that succeed share a small number of specific disciplines (a board that started the conversation 12–18 months out, a founder who made peace with the transition before the search began, an executive-search partner selected against a specific role-scope, a 30/60/90/180-day onboarding programme with a named executive sponsor on the board), and the failures share a small number of specific pathologies (a founder who took the transition personally, a board that treated the search as a checkbox, an incoming CEO who inherited the founder's calendar without inheriting the founder's mandate). This module names those disciplines and pathologies concretely.

The module also owns the *adjacent* leadership-transition workstreams that founder-CEO succession pulls on and that a mature-stage governance apparatus depends on: board-chair transitions (the increasingly-common independent-chair pattern that separates chair-of-the-board from chief-executive), president / COO succession (the "heir apparent" pattern and its specific failure modes), executive-team succession bench (two-deep per critical role, nine-box calibration, internal-vs-external hire ratio, CEO-development track), and board-composition changes at growth-and-mature stage (sunsetting VC seats to observer, adding public-company directors 18–24 months pre-IPO, sunsetting the founder-seat if the founder departs, and the activism-defence composition patterns).

> Reminder: this module is education, not legal / executive-search / employment / executive-coaching advice. Every live succession engages a specific set of advisors — a specific retained executive-search partner, a specific executive coach for the outgoing founder and (separately) the incoming CEO, specific counsel for the founder-transition contract and the incoming-CEO employment agreement, and — for a board-chair transition on a public or pre-IPO company — specific corporate-governance counsel. Follow the counsel-imposed regime; this module describes the leadership-transition discipline that sits underneath it.

## Learning objectives

By the end of this module you should be able to:

- Read the **founder-CEO succession readiness signals** — the operating-scale threshold (typically $50M+ ARR and 250+ headcount, though sector-specific), the founder-fit-with-current-stage signal (do the tasks that dominate the seat still play to the founder's strengths?), the board-composition signal (has the board matured to include CEO-transition experience?), the funding-stage signal (is the next raise likely to demand a new CEO?), and the founder-personal-readiness signal (has the founder made peace with the transition?).
- Choreograph the **board conversation on succession** — the pre-conversation with the board chair or lead independent director, the confidentiality boundary (family, close friends, executive coach, and no one else), the timing rules (typically 12–18 months out from the intended handover), the executive-search partner selection (Heidrick & Struggles, Spencer Stuart, True Search, ON Partners, DHR, Daversa for tech-specialists, Bolster for fractional-first patterns), and the pattern of using a fractional or interim CEO as a bridge.
- Design the **CEO onboarding programme** — the 30/60/90/180-day plan for the incoming CEO, the founder-transition contract (title, board seat, equity, non-compete, communications channel), the direct-report onboarding waves, the customer-and-investor introduction sequence, and the pattern of the founder taking a 60–90-day off-ramp before returning in a Chair / Chief Innovation Officer / Chief Product Officer role.
- Design the **founder-transition-to-Chair / Chief Innovation Officer / Chief Product Officer role** — role scoping (executive vs. non-executive), decision-rights division (what the founder still decides, what the founder informs, what the founder is informed of), compensation architecture (Board Chair compensation vs. C-suite compensation vs. dual role), and the pattern of a formal 12–24-month founder-transition end-date.
- Manage **board-chair transitions** — the independent-chair vs. combined-chair-and-CEO decision (increasingly independent-chair post-Sarbanes-Oxley and post-Enron), the chair-selection process (from-within-the-board vs. new recruit), the chair-onboarding pattern (typically 2–3 board meetings of observer status), and the pattern of a Vice-Chair or Lead Independent Director as a bench role.
- Design **president / COO succession** — the president-as-heir-apparent pattern, the COO-as-scaling-lieutenant pattern, the multi-year handoff timeline, the direct-report onboarding sequencing, and the failure-mode pattern of president / COO seats installed without a clear promotion path.
- Design the **executive-team succession bench** — the two-deep-per-critical-role discipline, the annual talent-review calibration (nine-box grid), the internal-vs-external-hire ratio target, the CEO-development track (rotation, board exposure, external-network exposure), and the interim-CEO playbook for an unexpected departure.
- Manage **board-composition changes at growth / mature stage** — the sunsetting VC-seat conversion to observer (typically at Series-C or the first meaningful secondary event), the addition of public-company directors (18–24 months pre-IPO), the sunsetting founder-seat if the founder departs the company, and the activism-defence composition (independence-heavy, staggered-board consideration, poison-pill readiness).

## Ownership boundary

- **Day-to-day executive-team management, ongoing culture, ongoing comp architecture, ongoing talent-review process, ongoing leadership-development curriculum** is owned by `startup-operations-governance-curriculum` (level 50). This module never re-teaches how to run an ordinary-course nine-box calibration cycle, how to structure a standing leadership-development programme, or how to design an ordinary-course exec-comp philosophy. It owns the *transaction-and-transition overlay* — the specific choreography that fires when a founder-CEO is being succeeded, a chair is being separated from the CEO seat, an executive bench is being stress-tested against a top-of-house departure, or a board is being restructured for a growth-or-mature stage.
- **Board and committee construction, corporate-secretary machinery, ordinary-course independence maintenance, ongoing D&O programme** is owned by `startup-operations-governance-curriculum`. This module owns the *composition-change* dimension (sunsetting VC seats, adding public-company directors, sunsetting the founder seat) rather than the standing-committee machinery.
- **Transaction-governance and fiduciary duty for a change-of-control transaction** (Revlon, Corwin, MFW, special-committee construction, fairness opinions, banker-conflict analysis) is owned by mod-109. This module owns the *leadership-transition* choreography that runs alongside such a transaction, not the fiduciary-duty analysis of the transaction itself.
- **Post-close integration and the founder-inside-acquirer 100-day plan** is owned by mod-111. This module owns the deeper founder-CEO succession decision that a post-close-founder situation is one specific instance of — including the case where the founder-CEO is being replaced *by* the acquirer.
- **Transaction communications and stakeholder management** for an announced sale or IPO is owned by mod-110. This module owns the *internal-and-board-facing* communications choreography around a succession decision (which is often *not* transaction-driven and *not* under Reg FD or quiet-period discipline), not the wider transaction-communications workstream.
- **IPO-readiness governance graduation** (audit-committee independence, NYSE / Nasdaq listing-standard boards, SOX 404 committee-composition) is owned by mod-107. This module cross-references the timing (18–24 months pre-IPO board-composition changes) but does not re-teach the listing-standard depth.
- **Wind-down mechanics** are owned by mod-113. If a succession decision ends in "we cannot find a CEO and must wind down," the succession-through-decision arc is this module's territory but the wind-down execution is mod-113's.
- **Post-transaction personal-wealth planning** for the departing founder is owned by mod-114 and never re-taught here.

## How to work through this module

Pick a specific company — real (yours, a portfolio company, a client) or a realistic hypothetical you build for this module — at a specific succession moment: a founder-CEO at $50M ARR whose board is starting to ask the question, a founder-CEO 18 months pre-IPO whose board is planning the graduation, a founder-CEO 6 months post-close inside an acquirer whose seat is not working, or a founder-CEO in an unplanned-transition scenario (health, family, board loss of confidence). Freeze the profile at the start of exercise 01 and carry it through all seven exercises. The module is worth exactly what the concreteness of your target profile is worth; a hypothetical with vague numbers produces a vague succession plan.

Read chapter 1 and do exercise 01 first — the readiness signals are what determine whether the rest of the module is 24 months out or already overdue, and the audit you produce is the analytic core that every subsequent exercise sits on top of. Read chapter 2 next and do exercise 02 — the board conversation and search-partner selection are the entry point of the actual succession workstream; the search partner you pick shapes the candidate pool that shapes the onboarding plan. Read chapter 3 and do exercise 03 — the incoming-CEO onboarding plan is the operational spine that determines whether the search's output lands. Read chapter 4 and do exercise 04 — the founder-transition role scoping is the artefact that determines whether the founder's continuing presence *helps* the incoming CEO or *undermines* them. Read chapter 5 and do exercise 05 — the board-chair transition is often (though not always) coupled with the CEO transition, and the exercise is worth doing whether or not you are separating chair from CEO. Read chapter 6 as background — the president / COO patterns will inform your chapter-7 bench design, and the exercise for that bench design is exercise 06. Read chapter 7 and do exercise 06 — the executive-team bench and nine-box calibration are the standing discipline that makes an unplanned transition survivable. Read chapter 8 and do exercise 07 — the board-composition growth-to-mature plan closes the loop by matching the board to the company's forward stage.
