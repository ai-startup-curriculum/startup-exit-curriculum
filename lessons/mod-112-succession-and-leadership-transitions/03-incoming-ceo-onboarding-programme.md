# The Incoming-CEO Onboarding Programme — 30 / 60 / 90 / 180 Days

## Why this matters

A well-selected incoming CEO can still fail if the first 180 days are poorly choreographed. The specific transitional period between the announcement and the point at which the incoming CEO owns the seat as fully as the founder did is a window of specific fragility: the executive team is reading every early move for signals about their own futures, top customers and top partners are re-evaluating the relationship they had with the founder-CEO, the board is re-calibrating its own working rhythm, and the founder-CEO is executing (or failing to execute) their off-ramp. The specific pattern the Charan / Nadler / Bower canon documents across a very large number of CEO transitions is that the incoming-CEO's actual mandate is established or lost in the *first 90 days*, and their operating rhythm is set (well or badly) in the *first 180 days* — after which the incoming CEO is running the version of the company those first 180 days built, whether they intended to or not.

This chapter installs the specific 30 / 60 / 90 / 180-day onboarding programme that the search committee, the outgoing founder-CEO, the incoming CEO, and the board jointly execute. It also installs two adjacent artefacts that determine whether the programme lands: the specific **founder-transition contract** that names what the founder retains, gives up, and continues to be responsible for through the transition; and the specific **founder off-ramp** — the 60–90-day post-announcement window during which the founder is intentionally scarce from the company, allowing the incoming CEO to establish their own operating rhythm before the founder returns in a Chair / Chief Innovation Officer / Chief Product Officer role.

> Reminder: this module is education, not employment / executive-comp / employment-tax advice. Every founder-transition contract and incoming-CEO employment agreement is a specific negotiated instrument authored by specific counsel and reviewed by specific tax and executive-comp advisors. The specific structural elements below are a founder / board / incoming-CEO framework for scoping the negotiation, not a substitute for the specific counsel-mediated authoring.

## The onboarding programme's five constituencies

The onboarding programme is not one plan — it is five plans, one for each constituency the incoming CEO must establish credibility with, running in parallel:

1. **The board.** The specific relationship the incoming CEO builds with the board through the first 180 days determines their operating latitude for the rest of their tenure.
2. **The executive team.** The specific relationships with the CFO, CTO / VP-Engineering, CPO / VP-Product, CRO / VP-Sales, CHRO / VP-People, GC, and any other direct reports determine whether the executive team executes with the incoming CEO or against them.
3. **Employees below the executive layer.** The specific pattern of internal all-hands appearances, skip-levels, on-site visits, and internal-communications sets the tone that employees below the exec layer read.
4. **Top customers and top partners.** The specific choreography of executive-sponsor introductions, top-account visits, and partner-relationship handoffs shapes whether customer / partner retention holds through the transition.
5. **Investors and (post-IPO) public stakeholders.** The specific relationships with the investor-directors on the board, the wider investor base, and (for post-IPO companies) analysts / press / regulators shape the market's read of the transition.

Each constituency has a specific rhythm within the 30 / 60 / 90 / 180-day frame. The specific onboarding-plan artefact is a matrix — constituency on one axis, time window on the other — with named activities in each cell.

## The founder-transition contract

Before the onboarding programme goes live, the founder-transition contract must be signed. It is the specific instrument that names what the founder-CEO retains, gives up, and remains responsible for through the transition. Its specific elements:

**Title and role.**

- The founder's post-transition title. Common titles: **Founder & Chair** (executive or non-executive chair — see chapter 4), **Founder & Chief Innovation Officer**, **Founder & Chief Product Officer**, **Founder & Vice-Chair**, **Founder** (with no specific operating title). The specific title matters because it signals to the executive team, employees, customers, and market what the founder's continuing role is.
- The specific scope of the role. Chapter 4 develops the scoping question in depth. The founder-transition contract should reference a specific role-scope memo (chapter 4's artefact) rather than trying to author the scope inline.
- The specific reporting relationship. Does the founder-in-new-role report to the incoming CEO? To the board? Both? The specific answer shapes decision-rights.

**Board seat.**

- Does the founder retain a board seat? For most founder-CEO transitions where the founder remains at the company in some role, yes. The specific class of the seat (founder-seat, CEO-seat-that-transfers, independent-seat, observer) matters.
- What is the specific tenure of the board seat? Some founder-transition contracts specify a fixed term (2 years, 3 years, tied to the founder's operating role); others are open-ended.
- What committee assignments (or exclusions) attach to the seat? A founder-in-Chair role typically chairs no committee (chapter 5); a founder-in-CIO / CPO role typically has no committee seat.

**Equity.**

- Vesting continuation. The founder's specific existing equity — vested and unvested — needs to be treated in the transition. Typical patterns: (a) vested equity is fully owned by the founder and unaffected; (b) unvested equity continues to vest as long as the founder remains in a specified role (Chair, CIO, board seat) with a specific cliff or acceleration provision; (c) an additional retention grant is issued to align the founder with the transition period.
- Acceleration on specific events. Specific events (change of control, involuntary termination, resignation for good reason) may trigger acceleration of the founder's remaining unvested equity. The specific negotiation is typically single-trigger or double-trigger; single-trigger is more founder-friendly, double-trigger is more board-friendly.
- Secondary-sale allowances. The founder-transition contract sometimes includes a specific secondary-sale window (aligned with the next round or the next tender-offer window) that lets the founder achieve specific personal liquidity as part of the transition. Mod-106 develops the secondary mechanics; here the founder-transition contract simply references the specific window.

**Non-compete, non-solicitation, and non-disparagement.**

- Non-compete. The specific enforceability of non-compete clauses varies materially by jurisdiction (California and North Dakota broadly prohibit; the 2024 FTC final rule on non-competes is under specific ongoing legal challenge and its status should be verified with counsel at the time of the specific negotiation). Where enforceable, a founder-transition non-compete typically runs 12–24 months and covers a specific competitive scope.
- Non-solicitation of employees and customers. Typically 12–24 months, often more enforceable than non-competes.
- Non-disparagement. Both the founder and the company agree to a specific mutual non-disparagement covenant. This is typically less contested than the other three and is a specific instrument that shapes the founder's post-departure public voice.

**Communications channel.**

- The specific channel through which the founder and the incoming CEO communicate day-to-day and week-to-week. Common patterns: (a) a specific weekly 1:1 for the first 6 months, tapering after; (b) a specific board-and-CEO meeting cadence; (c) a specific project-review cadence for the founder's continuing product / innovation work.
- The specific escalation channel when the founder and the incoming CEO disagree on a specific matter. Some contracts route the escalation to the board chair or lead independent director; some route to a specific advisory-council structure; some (increasingly common) install a specific executive coach or facilitator who mediates on request.
- The specific external-communications discipline. Who speaks for the company externally? On what topics does the founder still speak? What is the founder's public-social-media / press / conference discipline?

**Compensation.**

- Chapter 4 develops the compensation architecture in depth. The founder-transition contract at minimum names the specific compensation package (cash, equity, benefits) for the founder's post-transition role.
- Specific attention to IRC §280G (golden-parachute) and IRC §409A (nonqualified deferred compensation) implications where the transition is coupled with a specific transaction (M&A close, IPO). Mod-103 and mod-110 develop the specific tax framework.

**Departure terms.**

- The specific circumstances under which the founder can be involuntarily terminated from the role. Typical protections: for-cause definition, notice-and-cure period, board super-majority requirement.
- The specific circumstances under which the founder can resign with acceleration and other departure protections. Typical patterns: for good reason (material role change, forced relocation, material compensation reduction).
- The specific separation payments and benefits that attach to each departure scenario.

**A specific structural principle.** The founder-transition contract is *bilateral* — it is not a founder-friendly instrument that the board is coerced into, and it is not a board-friendly instrument that the founder is coerced into. Its specific purpose is to make the *continuing relationship* durable through the transition period by naming what each side expects of the other. The specific negotiation is best conducted through counsel on both sides (with the founder's specific personal counsel — often a separate lawyer from the company's GC — for the founder's-side terms), with the founder's executive coach as a specific sounding board on the softer dimensions (the communications channel, the day-in-the-life feel of the post-transition role).

**A specific anti-pattern.** The founder-transition contract that is negotiated *after* the incoming CEO's offer is signed. The specific consequence: the incoming CEO comes in without a clear picture of what the founder's continuing role will be, the founder feels the terms are being written under time pressure with reduced leverage, and the specific ambiguity in the role produces friction in the first 90 days that the onboarding programme is not designed to absorb. The remedy is the specific discipline of negotiating the founder-transition contract *in parallel* with the incoming CEO's offer, with both signed at the same time.

## The 30-day plan

The first 30 days establish the incoming CEO's basic operating footprint — who they meet, what they read, what they explicitly do not do. It is often called a "listening period" but that framing understates what is happening: the incoming CEO is simultaneously listening, being read, and establishing the specific first-order operating rhythm the executive team will interpret as the new normal.

**The 30-day agenda across the five constituencies.**

- **Board.** Specific 1:1s with every board member (including the founder, who is technically a board member in most transitions). Specific attendance at the first board meeting or board-executive-session as CEO. Specific initial working relationship established with the board chair or lead independent director on cadence and channel. Specific meeting with the compensation committee to be briefed on any specific in-flight comp matters.
- **Executive team.** Specific 1:1s with every direct report (typically 2 meetings each in the first 30 days: one introductory, one deeper functional-review). Specific first executive-team meeting run by the incoming CEO. Specific decision on whether to make any changes to the existing exec-team meeting cadence in the first 30 days (typical answer: no changes yet — inherit the existing cadence and adjust after 60 days once patterns are clearer).
- **Employees below the executive layer.** Specific first all-hands appearance (typically in the first 2 weeks — some CEOs do it in the first week, some wait until they can speak from a position of some knowledge). Specific pattern of skip-level meetings with second-level managers (typically 6–10 in the first 30 days). Specific pattern of on-site visits to each material office, engineering site, and regional-sales hub the company has.
- **Top customers and top partners.** Specific choreographed introductions to the top 5–10 customers and the top 3–5 partners, typically executed jointly with the founder-CEO. Specific pattern of receiving inbound customer / partner communication through the CS / partnerships teams rather than direct.
- **Investors and public stakeholders.** Specific 1:1s with the largest 5–10 investors (including specific investors not on the board, if the cap table has them). For post-IPO companies: specific initial sell-side-analyst introductions (typically coordinated with IR); no specific press engagement in the first 30 days beyond the announcement.

**The 30-day "do-not-do" list.** Equally specific:

- **Do not make organisational changes in the first 30 days.** Any change — firing a direct report, restructuring a function, launching a new initiative — becomes the specific dominant narrative of the first 90 days and displaces the actual onboarding.
- **Do not commit to any specific strategic direction publicly.** Broad "here's my initial thinking" is fine; specific commitments ("we will move into vertical Y by Q3") are not.
- **Do not renegotiate any specific pricing, contract, or vendor arrangement.** Even where the incoming CEO thinks a specific arrangement is wrong, the specific fragility of the first 30 days makes it a bad window to move.
- **Do not schedule an off-site in the first 30 days.** Off-sites concentrate specific attention on the incoming CEO's agenda; too early they signal "the new CEO is already changing things" without the substance to back the signal. Typical off-site scheduling: month 3 or month 4 of the tenure.

**The 30-day artefact.** A specific written "first-30-days landing memo" (typically 3–5 pages) that the incoming CEO writes at day 25–30 and shares with the board and the executive team. Its specific content: what the CEO has learned about the company in the first 30 days, what specific questions they are still holding, what the CEO's specific intended focus is for the next 60 days, and what specific decisions the CEO expects to make (and not make) in the day-30-to-day-90 window.

## The 60-day plan

Days 30–60 shift from establishing basic operating footprint to running a specific deeper diagnostic. The incoming CEO now has enough context to ask more specific questions and to start forming specific views on the executive team, the specific operating cadences, and the specific strategic questions the board will want to see the CEO address.

**The 60-day agenda across the five constituencies.**

- **Board.** Specific formal update at the next scheduled board meeting on the first-60-days read. Specific working session with the board chair or lead director on any specific structural or personnel decisions the CEO is considering. Specific first exposure to the board's committee cadence (compensation-committee cycle, audit-committee cycle, nominating-and-governance-committee cycle).
- **Executive team.** Specific second round of 1:1s with every direct report, now with specific functional review — what is working, what is not, what the incoming CEO's intended next-90-day priorities for each function are. Specific first "state of the executive team" read for the CEO's own use: who is high-performing and well-fit, who is high-performing but potentially at risk of leaving, who is under-performing and needs specific attention, who is a specific talent for future promotion.
- **Employees.** Second all-hands with the incoming CEO's specific first-60-days read shared. Continued skip-level cadence. Specific engagement-survey pulse (if not already scheduled), typically administered at day 45–60 to give a specific baseline read of employee sentiment through the transition.
- **Top customers and top partners.** Specific ongoing customer-visit cadence, now with the incoming CEO leading the meetings the founder used to lead. Specific pattern of the founder handing off top-account executive-sponsor status (chapter 6 develops the direct-report-onboarding sequencing).
- **Investors and public stakeholders.** Specific update to the wider investor base (typically an "incoming CEO 60-day update" via investor letter or specific investor call). For post-IPO companies: specific initial-earnings-call preparation if the CEO transition lands close to an earnings window; specific IR-cadence establishment.

**The 60-day artefact.** A specific written "60-day priorities memo" (typically 5–8 pages) shared with the board that names the specific priorities the incoming CEO will pursue in the day-60-to-day-180 window, the specific decisions the CEO expects to make in that window, and the specific board-level asks the CEO has. This memo is often the specific instrument on which the board formalises the incoming CEO's mandate for the first year.

## The 90-day plan

Days 60–90 shift from diagnostic to specific initial-action. The incoming CEO has now built enough context and enough relationships to begin making the specific first-order decisions their mandate calls for. The specific pattern the Charan canon documents is that the "first 90 days" are the specific window in which the CEO's mandate is fixed — after which the mandate is either operating well or operating badly, but is not easily re-scoped without a specific costly re-alignment.

**The 90-day agenda.**

- **Board.** Specific 90-day board meeting or board-executive-session at which the incoming CEO presents the first-90-days read, the specific priorities, and the specific decisions the CEO intends to make. This is the specific moment at which the board formally confirms the mandate.
- **Executive team.** Specific first structural decisions may now be considered. Common patterns: (a) a specific under-performing direct report is put on a specific performance-improvement plan or given specific coaching support; (b) a specific role re-scoping decision is made (e.g., splitting a combined CTO / CPO role into two seats, or vice-versa); (c) a specific new role is added (Chief of Staff, VP-Strategy, VP-Business-Ops). Specific first off-site is often scheduled for month 4 to align the executive team on the incoming CEO's priorities.
- **Employees.** Specific first substantive strategic update, either at an all-hands or in a written CEO letter. Specific first pattern of the incoming CEO's own leadership style being visible to employees below the executive layer.
- **Top customers and top partners.** Specific baseline retention read. Specific pattern of customer-and-partner-facing executive-sponsor coverage now established with the incoming CEO in the specific top-account seat. Specific pattern of the founder's continuing customer-facing role established (chapter 4).
- **Investors and public stakeholders.** Specific 90-day update. For post-IPO companies: specific first earnings call as CEO (if the calendar lands there) or specific first analyst-day appearance.

**The 90-day founder-off-ramp landing.** By day 90 the founder off-ramp (below) has completed or is about to complete. The specific transition on this day is from "founder is off-ramping" to "founder is back in the new role" (Chair, CIO, CPO, or the specific negotiated role).

**The 90-day artefact.** A specific written "90-day plan" (typically 8–12 pages) that names the specific 12-month operating plan the incoming CEO is committing to, the specific board-level asks, the specific executive-team composition and priorities, and the specific customer / partner / investor plan. This is the specific operating spine for the incoming CEO's first year.

## The 180-day plan

Days 90–180 are when the incoming CEO's operating rhythm becomes the actual operating rhythm of the company. The specific decisions made in days 90–180 — executive-team composition, board-cadence adjustments, strategic-direction commitments, capital-allocation choices — set the specific pattern for years 2–3 of the incoming CEO's tenure.

**The 180-day agenda.**

- **Board.** Specific 180-day board review of the incoming CEO's first-half-year performance. Specific compensation-committee review if any specific comp-plan adjustments are appropriate. Specific pattern of the board's own working rhythm now stabilised with the incoming CEO.
- **Executive team.** Specific executive-team composition now largely finalised. Any specific senior hires the incoming CEO intended to make are typically named by day 180 (later hires start to feel like "we're still transitioning" rather than "we've transitioned"). Specific pattern of the executive-team operating cadence — weekly staff, monthly business review, quarterly off-site — now stable.
- **Employees.** Specific engagement-survey follow-up read at day 150–180 to give a specific "how is the transition landing" data-point. Specific first company-wide off-site or all-hands event with the incoming CEO's full team.
- **Top customers and top partners.** Specific baseline retention now fully readable — any customer / partner attrition that traced to the transition should have surfaced by this window.
- **Investors and public stakeholders.** Specific full-first-half-year investor / analyst read. For post-IPO companies: specific pattern of quarterly earnings calls now stable with the incoming CEO.

**The 180-day artefact.** A specific written "first-180-days retrospective" that the incoming CEO shares with the board, naming what worked, what did not, what the CEO intends to change in the next 6 months, and any specific board-level asks. This is the specific instrument that formalises the "transition is complete, the incoming CEO is now the CEO" moment.

## Direct-report onboarding waves

A specific sub-workstream within the onboarding programme deserves its own treatment: the specific choreography of the incoming CEO's relationship with each direct report over the first 180 days.

**Wave 1 — days 1–14.** Introductory 1:1 with every direct report. Specific format: an in-person 60–90-minute meeting, ideally over a meal or in a specific neutral setting, focused on the direct report's personal-and-professional story rather than the immediate business review. The specific purpose is establishing the specific personal relationship on which the working relationship will run.

**Wave 2 — days 15–45.** Specific functional deep-dive with every direct report — typically a 2–4-hour session (sometimes broken into two sittings) walking through the function's current state, the specific top priorities, the specific team structure, the specific under-the-hood issues the direct report wants the incoming CEO to know about.

**Wave 3 — days 45–90.** Specific working-rhythm establishment. The incoming CEO and each direct report align on the specific 1:1 cadence (typically weekly for CFO, CRO, CPO / CTO, CHRO; every-other-week for GC and other function heads), the specific decision-rights (what the direct report brings to the CEO, what they decide themselves), and the specific escalation channel.

**Wave 4 — days 90–180.** Specific performance-calibration establishment. The incoming CEO gives each direct report specific initial feedback on how the working relationship is running and what the CEO expects in the next 6 months. Specific compensation-plan and equity-plan adjustments (if any) for direct reports are typically actioned in this window.

**A specific structural pattern often overlooked.** The specific "founder's direct reports" vs. "incoming CEO's direct reports" distinction. In most successions, the incoming CEO inherits the founder's executive-team composition. The specific question is not whether the incoming CEO will change the composition — most do, over 6–18 months — but *when* and *in what order*. The specific pattern the Charan canon documents is: (a) do not change composition in the first 90 days except for specific acute cases; (b) make first composition changes in days 90–180 driven by specific fit-with-forward-strategy signals; (c) complete composition adjustments by day 365. Changes that leak past day 365 signal "the CEO is still trying to build their team," which is a specific board-level concern.

## Customer and investor introduction sequence

A specific sub-workstream that determines whether external stakeholders read the transition as well-choreographed or as a specific rupture:

**Customers — the top-30 tiering.**

- **Top 5 customers.** In-person meetings led by the founder-CEO and the incoming CEO jointly, typically in the first 30 days. The specific format is "founder introduces incoming CEO, incoming CEO listens, founder signals continuing commitment through the transition." The specific effect is that the top-5 customer relationship is formally transferred to the incoming CEO's executive-sponsor coverage with the founder's explicit endorsement.
- **Top 6–15 customers.** Virtual or in-person joint meetings in days 30–60. Specific format: shorter (30–60 minute) introductions, same choreography.
- **Top 16–30 customers.** Handled through the CS / sales-executive-sponsor structure in days 60–90, typically with an incoming-CEO video or written introduction rather than a specific meeting.
- **Broader customer base.** Specific customer-facing announcement (email, in-app, blog post) at the time of the transition announcement. Specific ongoing pattern of incoming-CEO customer-facing communication established over the first 180 days.

**Investors — the tiering.**

- **Board-seat investors.** Specific 1:1s in the first 30 days as part of the board choreography.
- **Non-board institutional investors** (typically 5–10 in a mature-stage private company; more in a public company). Specific 1:1s in days 30–60.
- **Angel and small-check investors.** Specific written-update pattern (typically an "incoming CEO 60-day update" investor letter).
- **For post-IPO companies:** specific sell-side-analyst introductions coordinated with IR, specific buy-side-investor introductions coordinated with IR, specific pattern of quarterly-earnings-call and investor-day appearances.

**Partners — the tiering.**

- **Top 3–5 strategic partners.** Specific joint-executive-sponsor introductions in the first 30–60 days.
- **Broader partner ecosystem.** Specific written and event-based communication over the first 180 days.

## The founder off-ramp

The specific pattern that most distinguishes well-choreographed successions from poorly-choreographed ones is the specific **founder off-ramp** — a specific 60–90-day period post-announcement during which the founder is intentionally scarce from the company, allowing the incoming CEO to establish their own operating rhythm before the founder returns in a Chair / CIO / CPO role.

**Why the off-ramp matters.** The specific pattern in the absence of an off-ramp: the founder remains present through the incoming CEO's first 60–90 days, the executive team reads the founder as the actual decision-maker, the incoming CEO's mandate is undermined by specific "should I check with the founder" hesitations across the team, and the incoming CEO ends up running a founder-dominated org rather than the incoming-CEO-led org they were hired for. The off-ramp is the specific structural instrument that prevents this pattern.

**Typical off-ramp shapes.**

- **The clean-break off-ramp.** Founder departs for a specific extended trip / sabbatical / personal-life-focus period of 60–90 days immediately post-announcement. No specific involvement in company operations during the window. Specific communications channel remains open (a specific weekly text-or-email check-in) but no meetings, no external representation, no employee-facing appearances. Founder returns at day 90 in the new (Chair / CIO / CPO) role.
- **The reduced-cadence off-ramp.** Founder does not fully depart but reduces to a specific minimal cadence — one 30-minute weekly meeting with the incoming CEO, one 60-minute monthly board-and-CEO session, no other operating involvement. Slightly weaker structurally than the clean-break but often necessary when the founder has specific unique-value work in-flight (a specific product-launch, a specific partnership deal, a specific ongoing investor conversation) that cannot cleanly be handed off.
- **The consultancy off-ramp.** Founder shifts from CEO to a specific external advisor for the transition window. Some founders prefer this because the specific advisor status signals "I'm no longer in the seat" more clearly than "on sabbatical." Legally more complex — often requires re-papering the founder's employment agreement mid-transition.

**Specific structural elements of the off-ramp.**

- **A specific end-date.** The off-ramp has a specific date on which the founder returns. Open-ended off-ramps signal "we don't know how this ends," which is corrosive.
- **A specific return-role.** The Chair / CIO / CPO role is scoped (chapter 4) before the off-ramp begins, so the founder's return is into a specific known seat.
- **A specific communications channel with the incoming CEO** through the window.
- **A specific communications channel with the board** through the window (typically a specific weekly-or-monthly board-chair-and-founder check-in).
- **A specific external-communications discipline.** The founder does not do press interviews, does not appear at conferences, does not post on social media about company matters through the off-ramp window.

**A specific pathology to name.** The founder who does not off-ramp — who remains fully present in the office, in the executive-team meetings, in the customer-facing sessions — through the incoming CEO's first 90 days. The specific consequence: the incoming CEO's mandate never lands, the executive team continues to run on the founder's authority, and the incoming CEO ends up either departing within 12–18 months (the "the founder never actually gave up the seat" pattern) or lasting but running a permanently-second-fiddle role that produces specific board and market frustration. The remedy is the specific structural discipline of naming and dating the off-ramp *in the founder-transition contract* rather than trying to negotiate it in the first 30 days when the founder is already present.

## Summary

The incoming-CEO onboarding programme is a specific 30 / 60 / 90 / 180-day plan across five constituencies — the board, the executive team, employees below the executive layer, top customers and partners, and investors and public stakeholders. The programme sits on top of a specific pre-signed **founder-transition contract** that names title, board seat, equity, non-compete, communications channel, and departure terms; and is choreographed alongside a specific **founder off-ramp** — a 60–90-day post-announcement window during which the founder is intentionally scarce, allowing the incoming CEO to establish their operating rhythm before the founder returns in a Chair / Chief Innovation Officer / Chief Product Officer role. The specific artefacts the programme produces — the day-30 landing memo, the day-60 priorities memo, the day-90 plan, the day-180 retrospective — are the specific instruments through which the incoming CEO's mandate is confirmed by the board and the transition is formally declared complete.

The chapter that follows turns to the specific role-scoping question for the founder's post-transition seat — the Chair / CIO / CPO scoping that determines whether the founder's continuing presence *helps* the incoming CEO or *undermines* them.
