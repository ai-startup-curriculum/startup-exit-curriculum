# mod-109 — Transaction Governance & Fiduciary Duty: Revlon, Corwin, MFW, Special Committees, Fairness Opinions

**Estimated effort:** 26 hours
**Stage:** MATURE
**Seat:** founder-CEO, board chair, lead independent director, GC / chief legal officer, special-committee counsel, transaction committee

This module installs the **Delaware transaction-governance overlay** that sits on top of every meaningful M&A deal, going-private transaction, controller-conflict transaction, and IPO-governance-graduation vote. It is the frame that determines whether a Chancery Court applies deferential business-judgement review or the punishing entire-fairness standard when a stockholder plaintiff — or (more commonly) a WeissLaw / Halper Sadeh / Brodsky Smith / Kaskela strike-suit — challenges the deal a year after signing.

The frame is *legal architecture that lives inside the board's calendar*. The Revlon change-of-control trigger, the Van Gorkom informed-decision standard, the Weinberger entire-fairness test, the Corwin business-judgement gate on a fully-informed uncoerced stockholder vote, the MFW / Kahn v. M&F Worldwide six-condition test for controller transactions, the Blasius compelling-justification standard for franchise interference, the Unocal proportionality test for defensive tactics — each one shapes a specific board meeting, a specific committee construction, a specific minute-book entry, a specific proxy disclosure, and a specific banker-engagement letter. A founder-CEO who thinks about fiduciary duty as "counsel's problem" is a founder who signs a proxy that gets challenged three months after closing.

The module also owns the *transaction-specific governance overlays* that sit adjacent to the fiduciary framework: special-committee construction, banker-conflict analysis (Rural Metro / Del Monte / El Paso), fairness-opinion procurement, appraisal-rights exposure (DGCL §262, Aruba, DFC Global, Dell), ISS / Glass Lewis engagement, forum-selection bylaws, D&O tail-policy purchase, and the plaintiff-firm engagement pattern that follows every announced deal.

> Reminder: this module is education, not legal advice. A live transaction engages qualified Delaware corporate counsel and specialist litigation counsel. Every case citation below is provided as a starting point for reading the actual opinion, not as a substitute for it — Delaware fiduciary law evolves through opinions, and a doctrine that reads one way in 2015 may read differently after a 2024 Court of Chancery decision. Verify against the current caselaw before relying on any specific formulation.

## Learning objectives

By the end of this module you should be able to:

- Apply the Delaware fiduciary-duty framework at deal-scale — the business-judgement rule as the baseline, the Van Gorkom informed-decision standard, the Revlon change-of-control fiduciary trigger, the Unocal defensive-tactics proportionality test, the Weinberger entire-fairness test for interested-director transactions, and the Corwin business-judgement gate on a fully-informed uncoerced stockholder vote of the disinterested majority.
- Apply the MFW / Kahn v. M&F Worldwide dual-protection framework for conflict transactions — the six-condition test that restores business-judgement review to a controlling-stockholder transaction.
- Apply the Blasius compelling-justification standard for board action that impairs a stockholder vote.
- Construct a special committee — composition rules (independence, disinterestedness, financial literacy, industry expertise), advisor selection (independent legal counsel, independent financial advisor, sometimes a separate valuation firm), meeting cadence and minute discipline, mandate scoping, and the Rural Metro / Del Monte / El Paso banker-conflict analysis that shapes the financial-advisor selection.
- Procure a fairness opinion — the banker vs. independent-financial-advisor decision, the valuation-methodology and discount-rate disclosure obligations in the proxy, the banker-conflict-of-interest disclosure (retainer / success-fee structure, past-and-current relationships with buyer and seller, stapled-financing arrangements), the impact of Rural Metro (2014) on the pattern.
- Assess appraisal-rights exposure — DGCL §262, class-of-stockholder entitlement, the pattern of dissenting-appraisal litigation (Aruba Networks / Verition Partners), the interest-rate-driven appraisal-arbitrage plaintiffs, and NVCA-voting-agreement drag-along mechanics that pre-empt individual dissents.
- Engage ISS and Glass Lewis for M&A-transaction and IPO-governance-graduation votes — the ISS Insight-M&A / Insight-Compensation methodology, the Glass Lewis methodology, the pre-engagement discipline, the response-to-recommendation letter, and the impact on the deal-approval vote at institutional-holder-heavy issuers.
- Design stockholder-litigation defence — the exclusive-forum bylaw / Delaware-forum bylaw / federal-forum bylaw for Section 11 IPO claims, the D&O tail-policy purchase (typically a 6-year run-off), the R&W-insurance interaction, and the plaintiff-firm engagement pattern (WeissLaw, Halper Sadeh, Brodsky Smith, Kaskela) that follows any announced sale.

This module never re-teaches how to seat a board, run a compensation committee, or maintain an ordinary-course D&O policy. It owns the *transaction-specific overlays* that sit on top of that machinery when a specific deal goes live.
- **Deal-structure design** (asset vs. stock, reverse-triangular, earn-out design, escrow, HSR / CFIUS) is owned by mod-103. This module assumes the structure is already chosen and analyses the fiduciary-duty and governance consequences of the chosen structure.
- **LOI drafting and definitive-agreement negotiation** is owned by mod-104. This module analyses the fiduciary implications of the deal-protection package (no-shop, fiduciary-out, matching rights, termination fee) rather than the pricing or drafting of it.
- **Bookbuild, allocation, roadshow, and pricing mechanics** for the IPO are owned by mod-108. This module owns the *governance* of the IPO — the pricing-committee delegation, the fairness-opinion-if-any, the ISS engagement on the IPO-governance-graduation vote, and the forum-selection bylaws adopted before pricing.
- **Post-close integration and 100-day plan** is owned by mod-111. This module ends at closing.
- **Communications strategy** (announcement press release, employee messaging, customer / partner choreography) is owned by mod-110. This module owns the *proxy-disclosure* dimension of communications (what must be said, in what form, under what SEC rule) but not the wider stakeholder-management workstream.

## How to work through this module

Pick a specific in-flight or hypothetical transaction — sell-side M&A, controller take-private, IPO with a pre-IPO recap, or dual-track — with a specific board composition, specific controller status (or not), specific cap-table composition, and specific buyer-universe shape. Freeze the profile at the start of exercise 01 and carry it through.

Read chapters 1–3 as a unit — they install the standards-of-review ladder that everything else hangs on. Chapter 4 then installs the special-committee construction that operationalises the ladder for controller and conflict transactions. Chapter 5 layers on the banker-conflict analysis that shapes the financial-advisor selection. Chapter 6 covers the fairness-opinion workstream. Chapter 7 covers the appraisal-rights exposure that shapes the deal-protection package. Chapter 8 covers proxy-advisor engagement and stockholder-litigation defence.

Do each exercise while the matching chapter is fresh. The exercises are compounding — the special-committee charter you draft in exercise 02 is what the fairness-opinion engagement in exercise 03 attaches to; the banker-conflict analysis in exercise 04 is what the fairness-opinion disclosure in exercise 03 must reflect; the appraisal exposure in exercise 05 shapes the deal-protection package that the ISS engagement in exercise 06 must defend.
