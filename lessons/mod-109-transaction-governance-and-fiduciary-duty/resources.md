# Resources for mod-109-transaction-governance-and-fiduciary-duty

Primary-source Delaware Chancery and Supreme Court opinions, DGCL statutory text, SEC and FINRA rules, ISS and Glass Lewis policy documents, leading corporate-law firm memoranda, and governance treatises used by this module. Prefer these over secondary write-ups: the opinions are the authoritative statements of the doctrine; the SEC rules are the operative disclosure regime; the practitioner memos are the up-to-date read of how Delaware courts and institutional stockholders are receiving the current cycle of cases.

> **Case law evolves.** Delaware fiduciary-duty doctrine advances through Chancery and Supreme Court opinions. A formulation that reads one way in a 2015 opinion may read differently after a 2024 affirmance. Every case citation below is a starting point for reading the actual opinion, not a substitute for it. Verify against the current caselaw before relying on any specific framing in a live transaction.

> **State-of-incorporation specificity.** The doctrinal references below are Delaware-specific. Non-Delaware targets invoke a different forum's law — California, New York, Nevada, Texas, Washington, and the UK / EU each have their own corporate-governance framework. The substantial majority of US venture-backed C-corps are Delaware-incorporated; the Delaware line is the modal reference for this module. The applicable law for any specific transaction is a function of the target's state of incorporation.

> **This is education, not legal advice.** A live transaction engages qualified Delaware corporate counsel (and specialist litigation counsel in the event of challenge). The intent of this document is to point to the materials a competent Delaware M&A lawyer or in-house general counsel would read; it is not a substitute for engagement of counsel.

## Statutory and regulatory framework

### Delaware General Corporation Law (DGCL)

The statutory framework for every Delaware-incorporated corporation. Each section cited below is the authoritative text; practitioner-annotated versions (Folk, Balotti & Finkelstein) are listed under *Treatises and practitioner references* below.

- **DGCL — full text.** The complete statute. <https://delcode.delaware.gov/title8/c001/index.html>
- **DGCL §102(b)(7).** Charter-based exculpation of directors (and, under the 2022 amendment, specified officers) from personal monetary liability for breach of the duty of care. The reason outside directors defending a sell-side process are generally protected from monetary damages. <https://delcode.delaware.gov/title8/c001/sc01/>
- **DGCL §109.** Bylaw adoption, amendment, and repeal. The statutory basis for board-adopted exclusive-forum bylaws under *Boilermakers v. Chevron*. <https://delcode.delaware.gov/title8/c001/sc02/>
- **DGCL §115.** Exclusive-forum selection for "internal corporate claims" (adopted 2015). The statutory authorisation for Delaware-forum provisions covering derivative actions, fiduciary-duty claims, and DGCL-based claims. <https://delcode.delaware.gov/title8/c001/sc02/>
- **DGCL §141.** Board authority, committee construction (§141(c)), director indemnification. <https://delcode.delaware.gov/title8/c001/sc04/>
- **DGCL §203.** Business-combinations with interested stockholders. The three-year moratorium on specified transactions with a 15%+ interested stockholder absent board pre-approval, 85% tender outcome, or two-thirds disinterested-stockholder approval. <https://delcode.delaware.gov/title8/c001/sc06/>
- **DGCL §251.** Merger procedure for domestic corporations. The §251(h) medium-form merger-after-tender-offer provision (adopted 2013) collapsed the back-end merger timeline for tender-offer-plus-back-end transactions. <https://delcode.delaware.gov/title8/c001/sc09/>
- **DGCL §253.** Short-form merger of a 90%+ owned subsidiary. Appraisal is the exclusive remedy under *Glassman v. Unocal Exploration Corp.* <https://delcode.delaware.gov/title8/c001/sc09/>
- **DGCL §262.** Appraisal rights. The statutory basis for stockholder dissent claims; shapes sign-to-close choreography, reserves, and voting-agreement / drag-along pre-emption. <https://delcode.delaware.gov/title8/c001/sc09/>
- **DGCL §271.** Sale of all or substantially all assets. The alternative to merger for a specific-structure transaction. <https://delcode.delaware.gov/title8/c001/sc09/>
- **DGCL §220.** Stockholder inspection of books and records. The specific pre-litigation discovery mechanism plaintiff firms use to specifically obtain the specific documentary record before pleading. <https://delcode.delaware.gov/title8/c001/sc06/>
- **Delaware State Courts.** The Court of Chancery and Delaware Supreme Court are the authoritative Delaware judicial forums. <https://courts.delaware.gov/chancery/> and <https://courts.delaware.gov/supreme/>

## Delaware Chancery and Supreme Court opinions — the fiduciary canon

The specific cases cited in the chapters. Read the actual opinions.

### Business-judgement rule foundation

- **Aronson v. Lewis, 473 A.2d 805 (Del. 1984).** The canonical articulation of the business-judgement rule and the pleading standard required to rebut it. Still the controlling formulation of the BJR presumption. <https://courts.delaware.gov/opinions/>
- **Stone v. Ritter, 911 A.2d 362 (Del. 2006).** Treated duty of good faith as a sub-component of the duty of loyalty rather than an independent duty. Shapes modern "conscious disregard" / *Caremark* oversight analysis. <https://courts.delaware.gov/opinions/>
- **Marchand v. Barnhill, 212 A.3d 805 (Del. 2019).** Reinvigorated *Caremark* oversight claims; also material on specific director-independence analysis in the controller / specifically-personal-friendship context. <https://courts.delaware.gov/opinions/>
- **In re Caremark International Inc. Derivative Litigation, 698 A.2d 959 (Del. Ch. 1996).** The foundational oversight-failure precedent. Cross-referenced for the ongoing-board context (owned by `startup-operations-governance-curriculum`); referenced here for the specific "conscious disregard" standard that resurfaces in *Lyondell* and *Dole Food*. <https://courts.delaware.gov/opinions/>

### Duty of care / informed decision

- **Smith v. Van Gorkom, 488 A.2d 858 (Del. 1985).** The foundational duty-of-care case. Set the "informed decision" standard that governs specific board-process discipline — advance materials, meaningful meeting time, written fairness opinion, documented alternatives. Shaped the subsequent §102(b)(7) exculpation statute. The specific minute-book discipline exercise-08 operationalises this standard. <https://courts.delaware.gov/opinions/>
- **Cede & Co. v. Technicolor, Inc., 634 A.2d 345 (Del. 1993).** Specific causation requirement for duty-of-care damages. <https://courts.delaware.gov/opinions/>

### Revlon change-of-control doctrine

- **Revlon, Inc. v. MacAndrews & Forbes Holdings, Inc., 506 A.2d 173 (Del. 1986).** The source of the "auctioneer" duty when a sale of control is inevitable. Still the controlling formulation of the specific change-of-control fiduciary trigger. <https://courts.delaware.gov/opinions/>
- **Paramount Communications, Inc. v. Time Inc., 571 A.2d 1140 (Del. 1990).** The limits of Revlon — Time's stock-for-stock combination with Warner did not trigger Revlon because the combined entity remained widely held. <https://courts.delaware.gov/opinions/>
- **Paramount Communications, Inc. v. QVC Network, Inc., 637 A.2d 34 (Del. 1994).** The three Revlon triggers — cash sale, break-up, change of control (fluid-to-concentrated). The controlling formulation of what counts as a change of control under Revlon. <https://courts.delaware.gov/opinions/>
- **Barkan v. Amsted Industries, Inc., 567 A.2d 1279 (Del. 1989).** The "no single blueprint" formulation of Revlon process compliance. <https://courts.delaware.gov/opinions/>
- **Lyondell Chemical Co. v. Ryan, 970 A.2d 235 (Del. 2009).** Revlon breach requires conscious disregard of the duties; a good-faith-but-suboptimal process is not a breach. The specific defensive posture for a seven-day-signed deal. <https://courts.delaware.gov/opinions/>
- **C&J Energy Services, Inc. v. City of Miami Gen. Emps.' & Sanitation Emps.' Ret. Trust, 107 A.3d 1049 (Del. 2014).** Revlon analysis in a specific stock-for-stock transaction with a change-of-control component. <https://courts.delaware.gov/opinions/>
- **In re Columbia Pipeline Group, Inc. Merger Litigation, 299 A.3d 393 (Del. Ch. 2023).** Recent Chancery application of Revlon and the specific CEO-conflict analysis. <https://courts.delaware.gov/opinions/>
- **In re Pattern Energy Group Inc. Stockholders Litigation, 2021 WL 1812674 (Del. Ch. May 6, 2021).** Specific controller-conflict / specific-committee-passivity analysis in a specifically Revlon-triggered sale process. <https://courts.delaware.gov/opinions/>

### Unocal defensive-tactics doctrine

- **Unocal Corp. v. Mesa Petroleum Co., 493 A.2d 946 (Del. 1985).** The two-part proportionality test for defensive measures in response to a hostile bid. <https://courts.delaware.gov/opinions/>
- **Unitrin, Inc. v. American General Corp., 651 A.2d 1361 (Del. 1995).** The preclusive / coercive refinement of the Unocal standard. <https://courts.delaware.gov/opinions/>
- **Moran v. Household International, Inc., 500 A.2d 1346 (Del. 1985).** Upheld the shareholder-rights-plan (poison pill) as a valid defensive measure. <https://courts.delaware.gov/opinions/>
- **Air Products and Chemicals, Inc. v. Airgas, Inc., 16 A.3d 48 (Del. Ch. 2011).** Chancellor Chandler specifically upheld a specifically-prolonged pill defence against a specifically-sustained hostile bid; the specific limits of the specific "just say no" defence. <https://courts.delaware.gov/opinions/>
- **In re The Williams Companies Stockholder Litigation, 2021 WL 754593 (Del. Ch. Feb. 26, 2021), aff'd sub nom. Williams Companies, Inc. v. Wolosky, 264 A.3d 641 (Del. 2021).** Specific pandemic-era pill-adoption case; specific limits on specific low-threshold, specific "acting-in-concert" pill design. <https://courts.delaware.gov/opinions/>

### Weinberger entire-fairness doctrine

- **Weinberger v. UOP, Inc., 457 A.2d 701 (Del. 1983).** The two-prong entire-fairness test — fair dealing (process) and fair price (substance) — for interested-director transactions. Still the controlling articulation of entire-fairness review. <https://courts.delaware.gov/opinions/>
- **Americas Mining Corp. v. Theriault, 51 A.3d 1213 (Del. 2012).** Specific application of entire-fairness review to a controller transaction with specific $2 billion damages award. <https://courts.delaware.gov/opinions/>

### Corwin cleansing doctrine

- **Corwin v. KKR Financial Holdings LLC, 125 A.3d 304 (Del. 2015).** A fully-informed, uncoerced stockholder vote of the disinterested majority restores business-judgement review (absent a controller). The post-signing stockholder-approval choreography that specifically cleanses non-controller transactions. <https://courts.delaware.gov/opinions/>
- **In re Volcano Corp. Stockholder Litigation, 143 A.3d 727 (Del. Ch. 2016), aff'd, 156 A.3d 697 (Del. 2017).** Extended Corwin cleansing to two-step tender-offer-plus-back-end-merger transactions under DGCL §251(h). <https://courts.delaware.gov/opinions/>
- **In re Saba Software, Inc. Stockholder Litigation, 2017 WL 1201108 (Del. Ch. Mar. 31, 2017).** The specific "fully informed" prong of Corwin — specific disclosure failures that break Corwin cleansing. <https://courts.delaware.gov/opinions/>
- **In re Solera Holdings, Inc. Stockholder Litigation, 2017 WL 57839 (Del. Ch. Jan. 5, 2017).** Specific Corwin-cleansing application. <https://courts.delaware.gov/opinions/>
- **City of Fort Myers General Employees' Pension Fund v. Haley, 235 A.3d 702 (Del. 2020).** Supreme Court specifically rejected Corwin cleansing where specific CEO-conflict in post-close-employment-negotiation was specifically not disclosed. Shapes specific disclosure discipline in exercise-03 and exercise-04. <https://courts.delaware.gov/opinions/>
