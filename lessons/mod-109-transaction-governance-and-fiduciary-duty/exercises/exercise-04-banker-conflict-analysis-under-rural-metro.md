# exercise-04: Banker Conflict Analysis under Rural Metro

**Estimated effort:** 4 hours

## Objective

Produce the **banker-conflict analysis** for the financial advisor the committee is engaging — the specific past-and-current-relationships schedule, the fee-structure analysis (retainer / opinion-fee / success-fee ratio), the stapled-financing analysis, the specific proxy-disclosure write-up, and a go / no-go decision memo. By the end you should be able to defend the advisor selection and the engagement structure against a *Rural Metro* / *Del Monte* / *El Paso* aiding-and-abetting attack and the proxy disclosure against a *Haley*-style disclosure challenge.

## Background

This exercise covers material from:

- [Chapter 4 — Special Committee Construction](../04-special-committee-construction.md)
- [Chapter 5 — Banker Conflicts under Rural Metro, Del Monte, and El Paso](../05-banker-conflicts-rural-metro-del-monte-el-paso.md)
- [Chapter 6 — Fairness Opinion Procurement and Disclosure](../06-fairness-opinion-procurement-and-disclosure.md)

*In re Rural Metro Corp. Stockholders Litigation*, 88 A.3d 54 (Del. Ch. 2014), *aff'd sub nom. RBC Capital Markets, LLC v. Jervis*, 129 A.3d 816 (Del. 2015) is the controlling precedent on aiding-and-abetting fiduciary breach by a financial advisor. The specific liability finding against RBC — $75.8 million in damages — was anchored on specific undisclosed self-dealing (steering the process to the specific private-equity buyer where RBC was simultaneously pursuing a stapled-financing engagement). *In re Del Monte Foods Co. Shareholders Litigation*, 25 A.3d 813 (Del. Ch. 2011) and *In re El Paso Corp. Shareholder Litigation*, 41 A.3d 432 (Del. Ch. 2012) are the precursor cases where Barclays' and Goldman's conflicts shaped the Court of Chancery's approach. Together they establish the specific due-diligence discipline a committee must run before engaging a financial advisor and the specific disclosure discipline the proxy must contain.

## Prerequisites

- The exercise-02 output (committee charter and shell financial-advisor engagement letter) and the exercise-03 output (fairness-opinion packet).
- The target advisor's candidate name and specific lead-banker identity, with specific proposed engagement scope.
- Access to the SEC EDGAR filings of the specific advisor firm (if a bulge-bracket, read specific recent `DEFM14A` filings disclosing fee structures and conflicts) and specific prior Delaware opinions naming the firm or lead banker.
- The *Rural Metro*, *Del Monte*, *El Paso*, *Dole Food*, *Zale*, and *Pattern Energy* opinions — read the actual opinions, not summaries.

## Tasks

### 1. Advisor-identity and relationship schedule

For the candidate financial advisor (and each specific proposed co-advisor), produce a specific relationship schedule covering the past 2 years:

- **Investment-banking engagements with the counterparty.** Specific advisory assignments, specific debt / equity financings, specific capital-markets work. Specific fees received. Specific timing.
- **Investment-banking engagements with the controller / sponsor** (if the counterparty is a controller, PE sponsor, or related party). Specific advisory, specific portfolio-company work. Specific fees. Specific timing.
- **Lending relationships.** Specific loans made to the counterparty / controller. Specific commitments outstanding. Specific syndicate participations.
- **Capital-markets research.** Specific coverage of the target (named analyst, specific rating history, specific target-price history). Specific coverage of the counterparty.
- **Principal-investment / proprietary-trading exposure.** Specific positions in the target or counterparty securities (as of a specific date). Specific merchant-banking / principal-investing fund exposure to either side.
- **Client-held positions.** Specific disclosed asset-management-arm holdings in either side (specific tranche sizes if available).
- **Prior fairness opinions.** Specific prior fairness opinions delivered in similar-structure transactions. Specific prior "yes" opinions on counterparty-side transactions.
- **Personal relationships.** Specific personal / familial / past-employment relationships between the lead banker and specific directors / officers / controllers of either side.
- **Prior Delaware opinions naming the firm or specific banker.** *Rural Metro* (RBC), *Del Monte* (Barclays), *El Paso* (Goldman Sachs), *Dole Food* (Deutsche Bank), *Zale* (Merrill Lynch), *Pattern Energy* (Goldman), and others — if the firm is named, note the specific finding and the specific banker involved.

For each item, note the specific firm response: nothing to disclose / specific disclosure / specific waiver / specific recusal.

The schedule is produced by the advisor; the committee's job is to interrogate it, challenge any suspicious absences, and require specific written confirmations of material lines.

### 2. Fee-structure analysis

Analyse the advisor's proposed fee structure:

- **Specific fee components.** Fixed retainer (amount), opinion fee (amount, often $1-5M), announcement fee (amount, if any), success / transaction fee (amount, usually % of transaction value or specific dollar), discretionary bonus (if any — rare and problematic).
- **Specific contingency ratio.** The ratio of contingent / success fee to non-contingent (retainer + opinion fee). *Rural Metro* highlighted the specific disincentive created when 90%+ of the fee was contingent on closing the specific transaction. A healthier ratio has specific non-contingent fees that are meaningful relative to the success fee (practitioner commentary varies on specific thresholds; the committee should articulate the ratio target and the specific rationale).
- **Specific "transaction completed" definition.** When does the success fee become earned — on signing, on closing, on specific milestone? A signing-based earn-out structure incentivises the advisor to recommend "yes" at signing; a closing-based earn-out ties the advisor to delivering the transaction through the process.
- **Specific "no transaction" compensation.** The committee should insist on specific compensation if no transaction occurs, so that the advisor can defensibly recommend "no" without foregoing meaningful compensation. Non-contingent compensation of $X-$Y should be meaningful relative to the lead-banker's / firm's typical opportunity cost on the engagement.
- **Specific fairness-opinion-fee treatment.** The opinion fee should ideally be paid regardless of outcome — this is the specific disincentive that would otherwise cause an advisor to shade the opinion to "fair" to secure the broader success fee. Specifically segregate opinion fee from transaction fee.
- **Stapled-financing arrangement.** If the advisor is also providing or committing to provide specific financing to the counterparty (debt syndication, bridge financing, equity co-investment), the specific fees from the financing engagement are a separate stream — specifically disclose them, specifically understand the committee's view of whether the stapled-financing creates an impermissible conflict. The practitioner consensus post-*Del Monte* and *Rural Metro* is that stapled financing by the sell-side advisor is often incompatible with the sell-side advisory role; alternative structures (specific second advisor or dedicated financing advisor) are preferred.

Produce a specific go / no-go recommendation on the fee structure, with specific proposed modifications if any.

### 3. Stapled-financing analysis

If the advisor's firm (or an affiliate) is providing or committing to provide financing to the counterparty, specifically document:

- **The specific stapled-financing arrangement.** Specific commitments, specific role (lead arranger, co-arranger, bookrunner), specific fees, specific timing of first discussion with the counterparty, specific prior relationship between the financing team and the counterparty.
- **The specific informational-wall structure between the sell-side advisory team and the financing team.** Specifically, who sits on each side, what information crosses, what review and approval must occur. The *Del Monte* court was unimpressed by specific informational walls that were specifically breached.
- **The specific committee determination.** Does the committee accept the stapled-financing? If so, with what specific disclosure, with what specific informational-wall discipline? Does the committee require the advisor to decline the stapled financing to preserve the integrity of the advisory role? Does the committee engage a specific second advisor to cross-check?

Produce a specific recommendation: accept stapled financing with specific disclosure and informational wall / decline stapled financing / engage second advisor as cross-check / disengage from the specific advisor and select a non-conflicted alternative.

### 4. Comparable-case stress test

For each of *Rural Metro*, *Del Monte*, *El Paso*, *Dole Food*, *Zale*, and *Pattern Energy*, produce a specific one-paragraph "if my advisor were in the specific posture of this case, what would the Court of Chancery say" analysis. Specifically:

- *In re Rural Metro Corp. Stockholders Litig.*, 88 A.3d 54 (Del. Ch. 2014). The RBC conflict: pursuing a sell-side-advisory engagement while simultaneously angling for a stapled-financing engagement with the specific private-equity buyer; the undisclosed-to-the-board nature of the specific financing-side pursuit; the specific advisor-manipulation of the DCF to support a specific-buyer outcome. What specific element of this fact pattern is present in your transaction? Specifically absent?
- *In re Del Monte Foods Co. S'holders Litig.*, 25 A.3d 813 (Del. Ch. 2011). Barclays' concurrent sell-side-advisory and stapled-financing-pursuit role; the specific director-communication about financing before board authorisation.
- *In re El Paso Corp. S'holder Litig.*, 41 A.3d 432 (Del. Ch. 2012). Goldman Sachs' specific pre-existing portfolio-of-Kinder-Morgan-stock exposure; the lead banker's specific personal-ownership position in Kinder Morgan.
- *In re Dole Food Co., Inc. S'holder Litig.*, 2015 WL 5052214 (Del. Ch. Aug. 27, 2015). The controller-and-management-side-advisor-together conflict and the specific $148M damages finding.
- *In re Zale Corp. S'holders Litig.*, 2015 WL 6551418 (Del. Ch. Oct. 29, 2015). Bank of America-Merrill Lynch's prior pitch to the specific buyer not disclosed to the Zale board.
- *In re Pattern Energy Group Inc. S'holders Litig.*, 2021 WL 1812674 (Del. Ch. May 6, 2021). The committee's specific inability to recognise the controller-aligned-advisor conflict and the specific failure to engage an independent-of-the-controller advisor.

For each, produce a 2-3 sentence "my transaction compared" statement.

### 5. Proxy-disclosure write-up

Draft the specific proxy write-up for the advisor-conflicts section of the "Opinion of the Financial Advisor" discussion. Required elements, in the specific Item 1015(b)(5) and (b)(6) framework:

- **Specific past and current advisory, financing, lending engagements with the counterparty / controller / specific affiliates.** For each, specific scope, specific role, specific fees received or expected, specific timing. The specific window is the past 2 years, but material prior engagements (within 5 years) are typically disclosed.
- **Specific stapled-financing or financing-side arrangement.** Specific financing scope, specific fees, specific informational-wall structure.
- **Specific proprietary-position disclosure.** Firm-affiliate principal-investment exposure to either side.
- **Specific individual-banker interest disclosures.** Any specific personal / familial / past-employment relationship.
- **Specific aggregate advisor-compensation.** Total transaction-advisory fees expected (retainer + opinion + success); total financing-side fees; firm aggregate expected revenue from the transaction.
- **Specific contingency disclosure.** The portion of compensation contingent on closing.

Compare the draft to the two most recent `DEFM14A` / `SC 13E3` filings in your sector — does the specific disclosure in your draft match the detail level of the comparables? If less, draft specific additional language. If greater, note why the detail is appropriate given the specific transaction facts.

### 6. Go / no-go recommendation memo

Produce a 2-page committee-decision memo on the financial-advisor engagement:

- **Specific recommendation.** Engage [specific advisor] on [specific terms] / engage with specific modifications / decline and select alternative advisor.
- **Specific reasons.** Expertise fit, deal-type-specific experience, specific relationships that are tolerable with specific disclosure, specific fee-structure fit.
- **Specific concerns.** The specific conflicts of concern, the specific disclosures and specific protections proposed.
- **Specific alternative considered.** The one or two alternative advisors specifically evaluated and the specific reason the committee did not select them.
- **Specific risk if a plaintiff challenges.** The specific *Rural Metro* / *Del Monte* / *El Paso* exposure the committee is specifically accepting and the specific defence.
- **Specific resolution.** The specific resolution the committee adopts to engage the advisor, specific signatures required, specific documents to be executed (engagement letter, specific disclosure letter, specific side-letter if any).

## Starter guidance

Three anti-patterns to avoid:

- **The "we don't need to look under the hood; they're a top bank".** Every bulge-bracket has specific past-and-current relationships that specifically overlap with any specific transaction. The question is not "does the bank have conflicts" (they do), it is "which specific conflicts, with what specific disclosure and specific protection, is the committee comfortable with." A specific-facts-tailored analysis is the only defence.
- **The "the stapled financing is just extra revenue; it doesn't change the advice".** *Del Monte* and *Rural Metro* both found that the stapled-financing engagement specifically shaped the advisor's advice, in both cases to the detriment of the target stockholders. The practitioner default post-2014 is to avoid stapled financing by the sell-side advisor; if the committee accepts it, the specific disclosure and specific protection must be designed into the engagement from the outset.
- **The "we'll handle disclosures in the proxy".** The specific conflict analysis is in the committee's record (minutes, counsel memos) and the specific disclosures are in the proxy. A committee without a documented contemporaneous analysis of the conflicts has given a plaintiff a specific *Van Gorkom*-style attack line.

## Acceptance criteria

You can demonstrate that:

- The relationship schedule has specific entries covering investment-banking, lending, research, principal-investment, client-held, prior fairness opinions, personal relationships, and prior Delaware opinions.
- The fee-structure analysis has specific numbers for retainer / opinion-fee / success-fee ratio with a specific rationale and specific proposed modifications if any.
- The stapled-financing analysis has specific informational-wall discipline or specific recommendation to decline the stapled financing.
- The comparable-case stress test addresses each of *Rural Metro*, *Del Monte*, *El Paso*, *Dole Food*, *Zale*, and *Pattern Energy* with a specific "my transaction compared" statement.
- The proxy-disclosure write-up is auditable against Item 1015(b)(5) and (b)(6).
- The go / no-go memo specifies the recommendation, the reasons, the concerns, the alternative considered, the risk, and the resolution.
- A critical reader (Delaware specialist counsel, peer general counsel, peer banker) could stress-test a specific conflict call and see the specific evidence you used.
