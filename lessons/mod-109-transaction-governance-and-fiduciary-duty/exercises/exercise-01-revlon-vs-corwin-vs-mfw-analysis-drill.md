# exercise-01: Revlon vs. Corwin vs. MFW Analysis Drill

**Estimated effort:** 4 hours

## Objective

Take a specific transaction profile — a sell-side M&A deal, a controller take-private, a tender-offer-plus-back-end-merger, or a strategic stock-for-stock combination — and produce a defensible **standards-of-review analysis memo** that identifies which Delaware standard the Court of Chancery will apply, maps each fiduciary trigger to the specific board action required to earn the most deferential available standard, and reduces the analysis to a one-page board-advisor summary. By the end of the exercise you should be able to answer, in a specific transaction, three questions in writing: (a) does Revlon apply, (b) can the board earn Corwin cleansing, and (c) if a controller is present, can the deal be configured for MFW business-judgement review?

## Tasks

### 1. Standards-of-review ladder analysis (12–18 pages)

Walk the specific transaction profile up the ladder, in order:

- **Business-judgement rule.** Would the BJR apply absent a Revlon trigger or an interested-director / controller conflict? Identify the specific *Aronson v. Lewis*, 473 A.2d 805 (Del. 1984) presumption. Address whether any pleaded fact (interested director, bad faith, uninformed decision) rebuts the presumption.
- **Van Gorkom informed-decision discipline.** Independent of the standard, articulate the *Smith v. Van Gorkom*, 488 A.2d 858 (Del. 1985) procedural checklist for this transaction — advance materials, meeting time, financial-advisor engagement, documented alternatives, written fairness opinion. Specific pages / hours / documents / meetings you would plan.
- **Revlon trigger analysis.** Walk the *Revlon*, 506 A.2d 173 (Del. 1986) and *Paramount Communications, Inc. v. QVC Network, Inc.*, 637 A.2d 34 (Del. 1994) framework. Determine whether the transaction is a cash sale, a break-up, or a change of control (fluid-public-stockholders-becoming-concentrated-controller). Address the *Paramount Communications, Inc. v. Time Inc.*, 571 A.2d 1140 (Del. 1990) counter-pattern. Articulate the specific QVC "best price reasonably available" obligation if Revlon applies.
- **Revlon process reasonableness.** If Revlon applies, map the specific *Barkan v. Amsted Industries, Inc.*, 567 A.2d 1279 (Del. 1989) "no single blueprint" formulation to the chosen process. Address *Lyondell Chemical Co. v. Ryan*, 970 A.2d 235 (Del. 2009) "conscious disregard" standard. Analyse the deal-protection package against *In re Toys "R" Us S'holder Litig.*, 877 A.2d 975 (Del. Ch. 2005), *In re Dollar Thrifty S'holder Litig.*, 14 A.3d 573 (Del. Ch. 2010), and *C&J Energy Services, Inc. v. City of Miami Gen. Emps.' & Sanitation Emps.' Ret. Trust*, 107 A.3d 1049 (Del. 2014).
- **Unocal defensive-tactic analysis** (if any pill, bylaw amendment, or defensive action is contemplated). Walk the *Unocal Corp. v. Mesa Petroleum Co.*, 493 A.2d 946 (Del. 1985) two-part test and the *Unitrin, Inc. v. American General Corp.*, 651 A.2d 1361 (Del. 1995) preclusive / coercive refinement.
- **Weinberger entire-fairness analysis** (if a controller or an interested director is on the other side). Walk the *Weinberger v. UOP, Inc.*, 457 A.2d 701 (Del. 1983) fair-dealing / fair-price test. Determine burden-shifting: who bears the burden of entire fairness absent cleansing?
- **Corwin cleansing analysis.** Walk the *Corwin v. KKR Financial Holdings LLC*, 125 A.3d 304 (Del. 2015) fully-informed-uncoerced-stockholder-vote framework and the *In re Volcano Corp. Stockholder Litigation*, 143 A.3d 727 (Del. Ch. 2016), *aff'd*, 156 A.3d 697 (Del. 2017) extension to tender offers. Address *In re Saba Software, Inc. Stockholder Litigation*, 2017 WL 1201108 (Del. Ch. Mar. 31, 2017) on what breaks "fully informed." Identify the specific disclosures the proxy must contain to secure Corwin cleansing. Note that Corwin does **not** cleanse controller transactions (that is MFW's job).
- **MFW six-condition analysis** (if the acquirer or any party on the other side is a controller). Walk the *Kahn v. M&F Worldwide Corp.*, 88 A.3d 635 (Del. 2014) six-condition test and the ab-initio timing rule as articulated in *Flood v. Synutra International, Inc.*, 195 A.3d 754 (Del. 2018). Address *Olenik v. Lodzinski*, 208 A.3d 704 (Del. 2019) on what counts as "substantive economic discussions" that would blow the ab-initio protection.
- **Blasius franchise analysis** (if any board action impairs or shapes the stockholder vote). Walk the *Blasius Industries, Inc. v. Atlas Corp.*, 564 A.2d 651 (Del. Ch. 1988) compelling-justification standard and the *Mercier v. Inter-Tel (Delaware), Inc.*, 929 A.2d 786 (Del. Ch. 2007) meeting-postponement refinement.

For each rung, write (a) which specific transaction fact triggers the rung, (b) which standard the court would apply, (c) which specific board action is required to clear the rung in the most deferential available posture, and (d) which specific proxy disclosure the analysis requires.

### 2. The "which standard governs" one-pager

Reduce the full ladder analysis to a one-page memo for the lead director. Format:

- **Transaction summary** (3 bullets).
- **Governing standard of review** — one of: BJR / enhanced-scrutiny-Revlon / enhanced-scrutiny-Unocal / entire-fairness / BJR-via-Corwin / BJR-via-MFW. Cite the specific case.
- **Why this standard applies** — the specific transaction fact that triggers the standard.
- **Specific board actions required to earn the most deferential available standard** — the top 5-7 operational items (special committee, fairness opinion, no-shop with fiduciary-out, majority-of-minority condition, Van Gorkom procedural discipline, specific proxy disclosure).
- **What breaks the posture** — the specific facts that would knock the deal down one rung on the ladder (controller participation blocks Corwin; pre-ab-initio economic discussions blow MFW; a preclusive deal-protection package pushes Revlon toward damages).

This one-pager is what the lead director reads before the board meeting that approves the engagement letter.

### 3. Deal-protection package stress test

Draft a 2-3-page memo analysing the deal-protection package against the governing standard. Address, specifically:

- **No-shop with fiduciary-out.** The specific drafting of the fiduciary-out and the specific "superior proposal" definition. Cite *Omnicare, Inc. v. NCS Healthcare, Inc.*, 818 A.2d 914 (Del. 2003).
- **Matching rights.** The specific window (3–5 business days) and the specific "last look" structure. Cite *In re Topps Co. Shareholders Litigation*, 926 A.2d 58 (Del. Ch. 2007).
- **Termination fee.** Specific percentage of equity value or enterprise value. Cite *In re Cogent, Inc. S'holder Litig.*, 7 A.3d 487 (Del. Ch. 2010) and the *In re Toys "R" Us* precedent on 3.75% as upheld. Articulate specific preclusive-fee risk if the fee is combined with expense reimbursement, matching rights, voting agreements.
- **Voting agreements.** Specific stockholders bound and specific percentage of outstanding bound. Cite *Omnicare* for the preclusive-combination risk.
- **Go-shop.** Specific window (30–50 days), specific reduced-fee if a superior proposal emerges from the go-shop. Cite *In re Lear Corp. S'holder Litig.*, 926 A.2d 94 (Del. Ch. 2007).

For each component, note (a) the specific drafting the deal has today, (b) the specific risk under the governing standard, (c) the specific drafting change you would recommend.

### 4. Corwin / MFW cleansing checklist

Produce a one-page operational checklist for the specific cleansing posture the board is targeting:

- **If Corwin:** majority-of-outstanding or majority-of-votes-cast approval requirement; specific "fully informed" disclosure items the proxy must include (banker-conflict disclosure, financial-advisor fee structure, specific valuation-methodology detail, specific board-process narrative in the "Background of the Merger"); specific "uncoerced" protections (no forced-into-vote structure, no coercive side payments to specific stockholders); specific *In re Saba Software* risk-of-break items.
- **If MFW:** the six specific conditions — (i) controller agrees to the transaction only if conditioned ab initio on special committee + majority-of-minority approval; (ii) special committee is independent and disinterested; (iii) special committee is empowered to select its own advisors and to say no; (iv) special committee meets its duty of care in negotiation; (v) vote of the minority is informed; (vi) minority is not coerced. For each, map the specific board action and specific document that evidences it. Cite the ab-initio timing rule from *Flood v. Synutra* and the "substantive economic discussion" line from *Olenik v. Lodzinski*.

Mark each item ready / in-flight / open with a specific owner (board chair, special-committee counsel, financial advisor, outside counsel).

### 5. Litigation-exposure brief

Draft a 2-page litigation-exposure brief addressed to the general counsel:

- **Pleaded claims the board can expect.** Specific causes of action a strike-suit firm (WeissLaw, Halper Sadeh, Brodsky Smith, Kaskela) will file post-announcement: breach of fiduciary duty, aiding-and-abetting breach by the financial advisor, breach of disclosure duty.
- **Specific defences available under the governing standard.** If BJR / Corwin / MFW: motion to dismiss on the pleadings. If enhanced scrutiny: deposition-and-document phase likely to be reached. If entire fairness: trial-track likely.
- **Specific damages exposure.** Nominal damages / disclosure-only settlement / "mootness fee" exposure / rescission / rescissory damages / monetary damages (benefit-of-the-bargain). Address *In re Trulia, Inc. Stockholder Litigation*, 129 A.3d 884 (Del. Ch. 2016) on disclosure-only settlements and the specific pattern of mootness-fee resolution post-Trulia.
- **Specific defensive positioning this exercise can set up.** The §102(b)(7) charter exculpation, the D&O programme with specific transaction endorsement, the forum-selection bylaw, the specific record-keeping discipline (chapter 8 exercise).

## Starter guidance

Three anti-patterns to avoid:

- **"Revlon applies, we're doomed."** Revlon is enhanced scrutiny, not entire fairness. A reasonable Revlon process survives Revlon. *Lyondell* reads Revlon as requiring good-faith substantial compliance, not perfection. The analysis is about identifying the specific process you need to run, not about running from the standard.
- **"Corwin always saves us."** Corwin requires a fully-informed, uncoerced vote of the disinterested majority. If there is a controller, Corwin does not cleanse — MFW does. If the proxy omits a material banker conflict, Corwin does not cleanse. If a specific side-payment coerces a specific stockholder bloc, Corwin does not cleanse. The analysis identifies the specific disclosures and specific vote architecture that earn Corwin, not a generic faith in it.
- **"MFW is a checklist."** MFW is six conditions, but each is live — the ab-initio rule, the empowerment of the committee, the duty-of-care discipline in the committee's negotiation, the quality of the proxy. *Olenik v. Lodzinski* taught the practice that "we talked about structure before adopting the MFW resolution" can blow the ab-initio protection. Treat each condition as its own process-engineering problem.

## Acceptance criteria

You can demonstrate that:

- The transaction profile is filled in with specific facts (not placeholder values).
- The ladder analysis walks every rung, cites the specific anchor case, and resolves each with a specific "applies / does not apply" answer tied to the transaction facts.
- The one-pager identifies a single governing standard and a specific list of actions required to secure the most deferential available posture.
- The deal-protection stress test addresses no-shop, matching rights, termination fee, voting agreements, and go-shop against the governing standard.
- The cleansing checklist is operational — a counsel reading it knows which document to draft, which meeting to schedule, which disclosure to include.
- The litigation-exposure brief is honest about the posture — it does not promise invulnerability and it does not catastrophise.
- A critical reader (board chair, outside counsel, peer general counsel) could disagree with a specific conclusion and see the specific evidence you used.
