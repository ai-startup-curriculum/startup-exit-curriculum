# exercise-02: Special Committee Construction and Charter Drill

**Estimated effort:** 5 hours

## Objective

Take the specific transaction profile from exercise-01 and construct the **special committee** that will govern it — the composition, the written charter, the independent legal and financial advisor engagements, the meeting cadence, and the minute-book discipline. By the end of the exercise you should have a complete committee-launch packet that a Delaware special counsel would accept as the opening evidence of a MFW-compliant / Revlon-defensible process and that would survive early Chancery discovery.

## Background

This exercise covers material from:

- [Chapter 1 — Delaware Fiduciary Duties at Deal-Scale](../01-delaware-fiduciary-duties-at-deal-scale.md)
- [Chapter 2 — MFW Dual Protection and Controller-Conflict Transactions](../02-mfw-and-controller-conflict-transactions.md)
- [Chapter 4 — Special Committee Construction](../04-special-committee-construction.md)

The special committee is the operating mechanism that turns the fiduciary-duty frame into a defended record. Exercise-01 identified the standard. This exercise builds the body that earns it.

## Prerequisites

- The exercise-01 output — in particular the one-page "which standard governs" memo and the cleansing checklist.
- The target's current board roster, each director's independence profile (NYSE / Nasdaq, Delaware functional-independence under *Beam v. Stewart*, 845 A.2d 1040 (Del. 2004); material-relationship analysis under the standard set in *In re Oracle Corp. Derivative Litigation*, 824 A.2d 917 (Del. Ch. 2003)), and each director's disinterestedness in the specific transaction.
- The target's current committee charters (audit, compensation, nominating / governance) as reference — the special-committee charter cross-references committee machinery that already exists.
- A list of candidate independent legal counsel (Delaware specialist firms — Richards Layton & Finger, Morris Nichols Arsht & Tunnell, Potter Anderson & Corroon, Young Conaway Stargatt & Taylor — plus the target's regular outside counsel, which typically **does not** represent the committee if conflict is present) and candidate independent financial advisors (banks or independent valuation firms — Houlihan Lokey, Duff & Phelps / Kroll, Lincoln International, Stout, Lazard independent-advisory, Evercore, Centerview, Moelis — tested for the specific banker-conflict analysis of chapter 5 and exercise-04).

## Tasks

### 1. Independence and disinterestedness screen

For each director on the target board, produce a short memo (½–1 page per director) addressing:

- **NYSE / Nasdaq independence.** The specific stock-exchange test (NYSE Listed Company Manual §303A.02; Nasdaq Rule 5605(a)(2)). Note that the ongoing-board independence profile is owned by `startup-operations-governance-curriculum` mod — do not re-establish it here, but confirm it in a specific sentence.
- **Delaware functional independence (duty-of-loyalty analysis).** Apply the *Rales v. Blasband*, 634 A.2d 927 (Del. 1993) standard and the refinement in *Beam v. Stewart*, 845 A.2d 1040 (Del. 2004) and *In re Oracle Corp. Derivative Litigation*, 824 A.2d 917 (Del. Ch. 2003). The question is whether the director's relationships (business, personal, familial, prior-service, charitable, co-investment) are "material" enough that the director cannot act independently of the controller / interested-party. Note the practical rule from *Marchand v. Barnhill*, 212 A.3d 805 (Del. 2019) and *In re Match Group, Inc. Derivative Litigation*, 315 A.3d 446 (Del. 2024) — the Delaware courts increasingly treat long-standing personal or professional friendships with the controller as material. Each director gets a specific finding: independent / not-independent-because-of-X / open-for-further-inquiry.
- **Disinterestedness in the specific transaction.** Separate from independence. Does the director receive something in the transaction that the general stockholders do not — a specific severance payment, specific vested-equity treatment, a specific go-forward board seat, specific indemnification scope, a specific consulting engagement? Apply *Orman v. Cullman*, 794 A.2d 5 (Del. Ch. 2002). Any director with a specific transaction-contingent benefit cannot sit on the committee.
- **Financial literacy and relevant expertise.** Specific industry experience, specific M&A-transaction experience, specific valuation-literacy to meaningfully engage with the financial advisor's work (not just to receive it).

Produce a one-page "committee candidate slate" — the directors who clear both independence and disinterestedness and who have the requisite financial literacy. The committee should typically be 3-5 directors. Odd numbers avoid tie votes; three is the practical floor; a two-member committee creates specific risk under *In re Trados Inc. Shareholder Litigation*, 73 A.3d 17 (Del. Ch. 2013).

### 2. Written special-committee charter

Draft the written charter the full board will adopt by resolution to constitute the committee. The charter is both an operating document and the first entry in the paper trail that a Chancery court will read if the transaction is challenged. Required elements:

- **Mandate scope.** Specific delegated authority — recommend only / recommend and negotiate / fully authorise. The strongest MFW posture gives the committee full authority to say no and to negotiate on behalf of the target without further board approval (apart from the specific statutory DGCL §141(c)(2) items reserved to the full board). Address *In re CNX Gas Corp. Shareholders Litigation*, 4 A.3d 397 (Del. Ch. 2010) on the empowerment-to-say-no requirement.
- **Specific authority.** Authority to engage own counsel, own financial advisor, own valuation firm (if separate), own tax / regulatory advisors. Authority to set its own meeting schedule. Authority to control its own agenda. Authority to communicate directly with the counterparty without management as intermediary. Authority to recommend against the transaction, to walk away, to propose alternative transactions. Authority to approve a specific transaction on specific terms without further board input.
- **Scope limitations.** Specific matters reserved to the full board (adoption of the merger agreement under DGCL §251(b); specific statutory determinations). The committee does not substitute for the full board on items the statute reserves to the full board — the committee *recommends*, the full board *adopts*. On a controller take-private configured for MFW, however, the ab-initio conditioning should give the committee veto power such that the full board cannot approve without committee recommendation.
- **Membership.** Named directors from the independence / disinterestedness screen in task 1. Specify chair. Specify a quorum rule (majority of members).
- **Meeting mechanics.** Minimum meeting cadence (weekly during active negotiation is typical); minimum meeting duration guidance; mandatory executive-session rule (committee meets without management, without controller representatives, without full-board directors who are not committee members); specific minute-taking convention (minutes drafted by committee counsel, reviewed by chair, circulated to committee).
- **Advisor appointment.** Named independent legal counsel (not the target's regular outside counsel if the regular counsel also represents the controller or any interested party; a Delaware specialist firm or a committee-independent full-service firm). Named independent financial advisor (banker-conflict analysis per exercise-04 before engagement). Named valuation firm (optional — needed if the financial advisor has specific banker-conflict issues that would benefit from a cross-check).
- **Compensation.** Specific per-meeting fee or specific flat retainer. The committee-service compensation is modest (typically $25,000–$150,000 depending on committee duration and transaction size), paid regardless of outcome, and specifically not contingent on the deal closing. Contingent compensation blows independence — never structure it this way.
- **Confidentiality and information-sharing.** Specific rule that committee materials and deliberations are shared with the full board only at specific inflection points (recommendation vote) and otherwise are committee-privileged. Specific rule that the controller / interested party is not an invitee to committee meetings and does not receive committee materials except through formal counterparty-counsel channels.
- **Reporting.** Specific reporting cadence to the full board (periodic status updates without substantive content; final recommendation with the detailed record).
- **Termination.** The committee is active until the earlier of (a) completion of the transaction, (b) termination of the transaction, or (c) resolution of the full board terminating the committee's mandate.

Attach the full board resolution adopting the charter and appointing the committee. The resolution should recite: the specific business purpose (consider and respond to the proposed transaction with the controller / third party / interested party); the specific MFW ab-initio conditioning if applicable (the controller agrees to proceed with the transaction only if, from the outset, approved by (a) a committee of independent directors and (b) a majority of the minority stockholders); the specific empowerment of the committee; and the specific limitation on the full board.

### 3. Independent-counsel engagement letter

Draft a short (2-3 page) engagement letter for the committee's independent legal counsel. Required elements:

- **Scope.** Representation of the special committee only, in connection with the specific transaction. Specific disclaimer that counsel does not represent the company, the full board, management, or any individual director in any other capacity.
- **Conflicts.** Specific representation of absence of conflicts with the controller, the counterparty, the other advisors. If any specific conflict waiver is needed (e.g., prior representation of a controller affiliate in an unrelated matter), the specific waiver and its scope.
- **Fee arrangement.** Specific hourly or flat-fee structure paid by the target. Confirm the fee is not contingent on the deal closing.
- **Scope of advice.** Delaware corporate law, M&A transaction counsel, specific regulatory specialists on the committee's demand.
- **Communications protocol.** Specific commitment that counsel reports to the committee, not to the full board, not to management, not to the controller. Specific commitment that counsel does not share committee privileged materials with any other party absent committee direction.
- **Reports.** Specific committed deliverables — process memos, standards-of-review analysis, minute-taking support, disclosure review, litigation-defence briefing.

### 4. Independent-financial-advisor engagement letter (shell)

Draft the shell of the committee's financial-advisor engagement letter. The full banker-conflict analysis is exercise-04; here, draft the structural shell:

- **Scope.** Financial advisor to the special committee in connection with the specific transaction. Specific disclaimer that the advisor is not advising management, the full board, or the controller.
- **Services.** Valuation analysis, fairness opinion, strategic alternatives analysis, counterparty engagement support, deal-structure analysis.
- **Fee structure.** Specific fixed component (opinion fee, retainer, work fee) that is **not** contingent on closing, plus optional specific transaction / success-fee component. The *Rural Metro* risk is in the ratio — too-high success-fee ratio shapes the advisor's incentive. State the retainer / opinion-fee / success-fee ratio you are targeting and the specific rationale.
- **Conflicts disclosure.** Specific requirement that the advisor deliver a written conflicts disclosure before engagement — all current and historical relationships with the counterparty, the controller, any specific stakeholder; all stapled-financing arrangements and specific intent (if any) to provide financing; all co-investment / fund-exposure to any specific party.
- **Scope of fairness opinion.** The specific recipient (committee / board), the specific target assertion (fairness from a financial point of view to the stockholders / to the minority stockholders / to the public stockholders), the specific scope limitation (not an opinion on the merits, not an opinion on the specific transaction choice, not an opinion on alternatives).
- **Follow-on-engagement non-contingency.** Specific commitment that the advisor's engagement for this transaction does not entitle it to any specific follow-on work (financing, follow-on advisory, specific private-side relationship).

### 5. Meeting cadence and agenda-first-six-meetings

Draft the specific calendar of the committee's first six meetings — the committee-constitution meeting through the hypothetical recommendation vote — with a specific agenda for each. Each meeting agenda should include:

- Specific advance-materials package the counsel will distribute, with specific lead time (24–72 hours typical).
- Specific topic / decision for the meeting.
- Specific executive-session rule (committee meets alone with counsel + banker; management / controller joins only for specific items).
- Specific minute-book treatment.
- Specific decision / resolution to be adopted, if any.

A plausible sequence for a controller-take-private configured for MFW: (1) constitution and ab-initio recital; (2) advisor engagement and scope setting; (3) initial valuation presentation and preliminary strategic-alternatives review; (4) counter-proposal to the controller; (5) negotiation round / stress-test; (6) fairness opinion and recommendation. Vary the sequence for the specific transaction.

### 6. Minute-book discipline note

Draft a one-page "minute-book discipline" memo for the committee counsel and the corporate secretary (if the secretary rather than the committee counsel takes minutes). Required elements:

- Minute-taking convention: narrative not transcript; specific decisions and specific votes recorded verbatim; specific "the committee discussed X" entries without purporting to summarise every statement.
- Specific disclaimer that the minutes are a confidential, attorney-privileged record of the committee's deliberations and may be produced in discovery.
- Specific rule that advisor presentations are referenced in the minutes with specific document identifiers, and the actual presentations are retained in a committee file.
- Specific rule that the minutes be drafted contemporaneously (within 48–72 hours) and approved at the next meeting.
- Specific rule that any specific objection or specific dissent by a committee member is recorded.
- Specific "do not" items — do not record specific legal advice verbatim (summarise with privilege reference); do not record speculative or informal commentary; do not record minutes that read as if the committee was going through the motions.

Chapter 8 covers the specific *Van Gorkom* minute-book discipline in more depth; exercise-08 is the full drill.

## Starter guidance

Three anti-patterns to avoid:

- **The "we'll figure out the charter after we start".** The charter is the first document a Chancery court reads to decide whether the committee was properly empowered. A committee that negotiates with a controller for three weeks under an informal mandate and then has the full board adopt a formal charter has blown the ab-initio protection under *Flood v. Synutra*. The charter is drafted and the resolution is adopted *before* any substantive negotiation.
- **The "the controller's lawyer is our lawyer too".** The regular outside counsel for a company with a controller typically has an existing relationship with the controller that compromises independent-counsel status for the committee. A specialist Delaware firm is the modal choice; a non-conflicted major corporate firm is acceptable if the specific conflict screening holds. *In re Rural Metro Corp. Stockholders Litigation*, 88 A.3d 54 (Del. Ch. 2014) is the aiding-and-abetting precedent on the financial-advisor side; the duty-of-loyalty discipline for legal counsel is tighter.
- **The "I'm independent, I'll serve".** Directors overestimate their own independence. *In re Oracle*, *Beam v. Stewart*, and the Match Group opinions all turned on specific long-term relationships that the directors treated as inconsequential. The committee's defensibility depends on a specific documented analysis of each candidate director's full relationship map with the controller.

## Acceptance criteria

You can demonstrate that:

- Each director on the current board has a specific independence / disinterestedness finding with evidence.
- The committee slate has 3-5 directors, no fewer than 3, with named chair.
- The written charter addresses mandate, authority, scope limitations, membership, meeting mechanics, advisor appointment, compensation, confidentiality, reporting, termination.
- The ab-initio protective recital is in the full-board resolution if the transaction is a controller transaction configured for MFW.
- The independent-counsel engagement letter addresses scope, conflicts, fee, communications protocol, and reports.
- The financial-advisor engagement letter shell addresses scope, services, fee structure (specific retainer / opinion-fee / success-fee ratio), conflicts disclosure requirement, fairness-opinion scope, follow-on non-contingency.
- The six-meeting calendar has a specific agenda for each meeting, specific advance-materials, specific decisions.
- The minute-book discipline memo is operational — a corporate secretary could follow it tomorrow.
- A critical reader (Delaware specialist counsel, peer general counsel) could stress-test a specific choice and see the specific evidence you used.
