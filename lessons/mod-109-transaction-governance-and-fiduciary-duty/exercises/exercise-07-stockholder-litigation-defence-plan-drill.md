## Starter guidance

Three anti-patterns to avoid:

- **The "we have exculpation; we're fine".** DGCL §102(b)(7) specifically exculpates specific duty-of-care breaches, specifically for outside directors, specifically from personal monetary liability. It does not exculpate duty-of-loyalty, bad-faith, or specific-benefit-based claims; it does not exculpate the corporation itself (which may face injunctive relief); it does not exculpate officers for specific stockholder-pursued matters pre-2022 amendment; and it does not exculpate specific aiding-and-abetting by the financial advisor. The exculpation is a defence, not a shield.
- **The "we'll just do a supplemental disclosure to make the lawsuit go away".** Specific post-*Trulia* Delaware Chancery scrutiny of specific disclosure-only settlements and specific SEC awareness of specific "mootness fee" patterns specifically reduce the specifically-silent-settlement option. Specific federal 14a-9 mootness-fee pattern specifically continues but specifically requires specifically-material supplemental disclosure to be defensible. A specific knee-jerk supplemental-disclosure response creates a specific pattern that specifically invites specific future filings.
- **The "we'll figure out D&O after signing".** The specific D&O tail procurement specifically requires specific binding at or before closing and specific coordination with specific pre-closing coverage. A specific board that specifically closes a transaction without a specific-bound tail specifically creates specific personal-exposure for specific directors post-closing and specific indemnification-liability for the specific corporation.
