# Post-Signing Employee Choreography and Retention

## Why this matters

Signing is not the moment the deal is done. It is the moment the *employee-communications workstream* becomes the highest-leverage lever on realised deal value. Between signing and closing (typically 30–150 days depending on regulatory clearances), the deal is publicly known — the press has a story, competitors have a recruiting target, employees have a decision to make. The specific pattern the corporate-development literature is consistent on: the top-20% of the retention-risk quartile (identified pre-close) leaves at 2–3x the ordinary attrition rate in the six months following announcement, unless a specific retention overlay is designed and executed. The engineering leader who leaves for a competitor takes a specific piece of institutional knowledge, a specific fraction of the code-review capacity, and — most damagingly — a specific network of engineers who now consider following. The customer-success leader who leaves takes a specific book of customer relationships that the acquiring company will spend two years rebuilding, sometimes unsuccessfully.

The post-signing choreography is not a series of speeches. It is a *specific run-of-show* — announcement-day sequenced to the hour, manager cascades sequenced to the minute, retention conversations pre-scripted and paired with a specific term sheet, benefits-transition FAQ pre-approved and pushed to a specific portal, integration-day-one messaging paired with a specific first-week programme. The CEO's job on announcement day is to execute a plan that was built weeks in advance, not to author the messaging live.

This chapter installs that choreography. The intent is to give the CEO / CFO / CHRO / GC a specific playbook they can adapt to their transaction, so that the announcement-day flow is deterministic — every manager knows what to say to their team at 11:03am, every employee knows where to find their benefits-transition detail at 11:15am, every top-20 retention target has a specific 1:1 booked for 11:30am, and the head of engineering does not learn about the deal from a Slack message.

> Reminder: this module is education, not legal / employment / tax advice. The specific compensation-related communications (RSU treatment, option acceleration, ESPP treatment, 280G tax exposure) are shaped by the specific plan documents, the specific transaction terms, and the specific jurisdiction rules. The messaging described below is the *choreography*; the substance is drafted by executive-compensation counsel and confirmed by tax counsel.

## The specific day-of sequencing — public target

For a **public target** (or a public acquirer of a private target where the acquirer's disclosure obligations dominate), the specific day-of sequencing is anchored to the SEC 8-K filing timeline. The pattern is:

**T–1 day (announcement eve).** Final board approval is obtained. Final signed definitive-agreement is filed as an exhibit to the 8-K (in draft form for filing on T-day). The comms team has finalised the press release, the internal all-hands script, the manager cascade materials, the FAQ, the customer email templates, and the analyst / investor / press call decks. The CEO reviews the run-of-show one last time. Retention conversations for the top-20 quartile are booked on the calendar of the CEO / CFO / CHRO for T-day and T+1. Nothing is sent to anyone outside the clean list.

**T-day, pre-market.** For a US-listed public target, the 8-K is filed *before market open* (typically 6:00–7:30am Eastern) so that market participants can price the news at the opening bell. The press release goes over the wire (Businesswire, PR Newswire, Globe Newswire) *simultaneously* with the 8-K filing to satisfy Reg FD.

**T-day, 30 minutes after press-release wire.** The **all-hands** is held. The timing convention is that the all-hands must happen within 30 minutes of the press-release wire — earlier is a Reg FD problem (selective disclosure to employees before the public), later is a retention problem (employees learn about the deal from journalists, from LinkedIn, from a comment from a friend at another company, and the CEO loses control of the framing). The 30-minute window is enforced by a specific pre-scheduled all-hands calendar invitation labelled "important company-wide announcement" or a similar non-specific title, sent 12–24 hours before the meeting.

**T-day, immediately post-all-hands: manager cascade wave 1 (VP / director level).** Within 15 minutes of the all-hands, VP-and-director-level managers are gathered for a 15-minute session led by the CEO / relevant executive, covering: what to say to their teams, what *not* to say, what questions to expect, where to route them, the specific timing of the wave-2 team cascades. VPs get the FAQ, the manager-only script, and a specific "boilerplate answer" for each expected question. They are on the clean list, but they were added in the past 24 hours specifically to lead this cascade.

**T-day, 30–60 minutes post-all-hands: manager cascade wave 2 (individual-team level).** VPs and directors run their own team meetings using the wave-1 script and the FAQ. This is the moment where the employee's specific direct manager — the person they trust most in the company — reinforces the framing and answers immediate questions. IC-level teams have their team-specific meeting within 60 minutes of the all-hands.

**T-day, 60–90 minutes post-all-hands: retention conversations begin.** The pre-identified top-20 retention-risk-quartile individuals have 1:1s on the calendar of the CEO, the CFO, the CHRO, and specifically named executive sponsors. Each conversation is 20–30 minutes and follows a specific script — see the retention-conversation-script section below. These are the specific conversations that determine whether the top of the retention risk quartile stays through the six-month mark.

**T-day, 90 minutes onwards: benefits-transition portal opens.** A specific benefits-transition portal (typically hosted on the intranet or on a specific microsite) is opened at the moment of the all-hands. It contains the FAQ, a specific benefits-transition landing page, a personalised equity-treatment page for each employee (accessible through SSO), a Q&A submission form, and a specific "office-hours" schedule for the CHRO / benefits lead / equity lead. The portal is the *self-service* mechanism that scales to the whole workforce.

**T-day, mid-day: customer, partner, and analyst calls.** In parallel with the internal cascade, the CRO and CS-lead are calling the specific top-ARR customers (see chapter 3), the head of partnerships is calling the specific top-tier partners, and the CFO / IR-lead is on the analyst call. These external-facing calls are pre-scripted and are covered in chapter 3.

**T-day, end-of-day: written follow-up.** The CEO sends a specific written follow-up to the workforce at the end of the day — typically an email or an internal post — that reinforces the day's framing, thanks employees for their work, previews the specific integration-planning cadence, and provides the specific channel for follow-up questions. The written follow-up is signed by the CEO personally and is the artefact that gets forwarded to spouses, saved in inboxes, and re-read in the first week.

**T+1 through T+30.** A specific cadence of follow-up communications runs — weekly written updates, weekly all-hands (in place of the ordinary-course cadence for the first 4–6 weeks), one specific "integration planning" all-hands at week 2, a specific "regulatory-timeline" update at week 4. Retention conversations continue with the second and third quartile of the retention-risk list. Manager 1:1s are structured to specifically address transaction questions during the ordinary weekly 1:1.

## The specific day-of sequencing — private target

For a **private target** where the acquirer is public (the more common pattern in mid-market M&A), the acquirer's 8-K obligation drives the timeline but the internal sequencing at the target has slightly more flexibility because the target does not have its own SEC-filing obligation. The pattern for the target:

**T–1 day.** Same as above.

**T-day, coordinated with the acquirer's 8-K.** The target's all-hands is held *simultaneously* with the acquirer's press release — typically the acquirer's press release and 8-K go over the wire at the moment the target's all-hands begins. There is no benefit to delay for a private target; delay creates the same "learn from LinkedIn" problem as at a public target.

**Manager cascade waves and retention conversations.** Same choreography as above.

**Customer, partner, analyst.** Same choreography, coordinated with the acquirer's IR / analyst outreach. For a private-target-being-acquired-by-a-public-acquirer, the target's customers get a specific joint communication (typically a joint letter from the target CEO and the acquirer CEO, or two coordinated letters); the target's press outreach is coordinated with the acquirer's PR team.

**Where the private-target pattern differs.** A private target with no SEC-filing obligation has the choice to hold the all-hands *immediately* — a specific 8:00am Pacific / 11:00am Eastern all-hands is common, timed to reach the whole workforce before the workday begins in the primary time zone. The private-target CEO also has more control over the announcement narrative because there is no requirement to file the definitive agreement as an exhibit — the acquirer's 8-K filing typically includes the agreement, but the target's internal messaging can be more curated.

## Manager cascade waves — the specific timing and content

The **manager cascade** is the moment where the CEO's messaging is translated into the employee's specific manager's voice. The specific waves are:

**Wave 1 — VP / director level, 15 minutes post-all-hands.** Covers:

- The specific "what we say" script — what the message is, what the framing is, what the specific talking points are.
- The specific "what we do not say" list — what has not yet been decided (org structure, specific role changes, specific relocations, specific compensation changes for the workforce), what cannot be discussed under Reg FD (specific financial detail beyond what is in the press release), what cannot be discussed under insider-trading rules (specific market-sensitive information).
- The FAQ — typically a 20–40-question document covering "why this deal, why now," "what happens to my job," "what happens to my equity," "what happens to my benefits," "what happens to the team," "what happens to the office," "when does this close," "what do I do if I have concerns."
- The escalation path — where to route questions the manager cannot answer, who is available for consult (typically CHRO's team, GC's team, benefits vendor's rep, equity administrator's rep).
- The retention-conversation coordination — which specific team members have been identified as top-20 retention risks, who is running their conversation, and when.

**Wave 2 — individual-team level, 30–60 minutes post-all-hands.** Each manager runs a specific 30-minute meeting with their team covering:

- Reading a specific opening statement (drafted from the wave-1 script).
- Reading the top-5 FAQ items relevant to their team.
- Opening Q&A with the manager taking notes (specifically, the manager captures questions that are not covered in the FAQ, which get routed back to the FAQ-owner for inclusion in the next update).
- Pointing the team to the benefits-transition portal.
- Reinforcing the availability of the manager 1:1 for follow-up.

**Wave 3 — 1:1 with each direct report over the next 5 business days.** Each manager schedules 1:1s with each direct report specifically to address transaction questions. This is the wave where the specific individual questions ("what happens to my visa status if my role moves to a different site," "what happens to my equity if my vesting cliff is next month," "what happens to my pending promotion") get addressed. The manager routes complex questions to the specific expert (CHRO for HR, GC for legal, benefits vendor for benefits, equity admin for equity, immigration counsel for visa).

**A specific anti-pattern.** The most common cascade failure is a wave-1 manager who does not attend the pre-cascade briefing and improvises the team meeting from the FAQ document, missing specific framing. The specific defence is a wave-1 attendance list that is enforced by the CEO's office, and a specific 24-hour follow-up review where each VP confirms their team was covered.

## The retention-conversation script and the top-20 retention-risk quartile

The **retention-risk quartile** is identified *pre-close*, typically 2–6 weeks before signing, by the CEO / CFO / CHRO in a specific working session that maps every employee against two axes: **value** (how critical the person is to the ongoing business or to the acquirer's specific integration thesis) and **flight risk** (how likely the person is to leave in the announcement-triggered attrition window). The top quartile — typically 10–30 individuals in a mid-size target — is the retention target.

For each individual on the retention list, the pre-close preparation includes:

- **Retention-package design.** A specific retention overlay is designed for each individual — typically a cash retention bonus payable in a specific schedule (e.g., 50% at close, 25% at 6 months post-close, 25% at 12 months post-close), possibly a specific accelerated-equity component, possibly an executive-role commitment from the acquirer. The retention package for executive-level individuals is negotiated as part of the definitive agreement (chapter 5 covers the MIP design that houses this).
- **The retention-conversation script.** A specific 20-minute conversation script drafted by the CEO / CHRO with input from executive-compensation counsel. Typical structure:
    - Opening: "You are one of the specific people we identified as critical to what we are building post-close. I want to have a specific conversation about your role."
    - Framing: "The transaction is [specific framing]. Here is what specifically will change for you [role, reporting line, mandate]. Here is what specifically will not change [team, product, mission-alignment]."
    - Specific offer: "As part of the transaction, we have a specific retention offer for you: [cash retention bonus amount], [equity treatment], [accelerated vesting if applicable], [role commitment from acquirer]. The specific terms are [documented in a term sheet you will receive today]."
    - Personal-consequence discussion: "Here is what this specifically means for you financially [walking through the specific numbers], here is how your equity treats, here is what your benefits transition looks like."
    - Ask: "I would like a specific commitment from you to stay through [specific date]. What questions do you have? What would you need from us to feel confident?"
    - Follow-up: "The specific term sheet will be in your inbox within an hour. We should talk again by end of week to answer any follow-up questions."
- **The named executive sponsor.** Each retention conversation is run by a specific executive sponsor — the CEO for direct-reports, the CFO for finance-team leaders, the CTO for engineering leaders, the CRO for sales / customer-success leaders. The sponsor is the same person who conducts the follow-up conversation, so there is a specific continuity of relationship.
- **The pre-scheduled time slot.** The retention conversation is on the executive sponsor's calendar for T-day between 60 and 180 minutes post-all-hands. It is a *pre-scheduled* meeting with a title like "1:1 with [name]," not an ad-hoc reach-out that day.

**Post-close retention monitoring.** The retention list is monitored specifically post-close — attendance in specific meetings, engagement in specific projects, response to specific check-ins by the executive sponsor. A specific retention-loss (a person on the list who resigns) is a specific escalation to the CEO and triggers a specific post-mortem: what did we miss, what should we do differently for the remaining individuals.

## The benefits-transition FAQ — the specific content

The **benefits-transition FAQ** is the specific document that covers what happens to every employee's benefits as of close. It is drafted by the CHRO with input from executive-compensation counsel, tax counsel, and the specific benefits-plan administrators. Every specific answer is confirmed by the acquirer's HR team so that the target's messaging does not commit to a specific benefit treatment the acquirer cannot deliver. Typical content areas:

**Health and welfare benefits.** What specifically happens to the current medical / dental / vision plan? When does the transition to the acquirer's plan happen? What is the specific coverage during the transition period? What happens to specific in-flight claims? What happens to specific supplemental benefits (fertility, mental health, pet insurance, etc.)? What happens to specific dependent coverage?

**Retirement benefits.** What specifically happens to the 401(k) plan? Does the plan merge into the acquirer's, terminate, or continue as a separate plan? What is the specific vesting treatment for the employer match (specifically, does the transaction accelerate vesting of unvested match)? What is the specific distribution / rollover option? What happens to any specific defined-benefit or profit-sharing plan?

**Paid time off.** What is the specific PTO balance treatment — carry over to the acquirer's plan, cash out at close, blend into the acquirer's PTO structure? What is the specific treatment for accrued but unused sick leave (which in some jurisdictions is a wage and must be paid out)? What is the specific treatment for parental leave, sabbatical accruals, and other specific leave types?

**Equity — RSU / option / ESPP / equity-incentive-plan treatment.** The most consequential and most misunderstood category:
- **RSU treatment.** What specifically happens to vested RSUs (typically settled into the transaction consideration — cash or stock — at close)? What specifically happens to unvested RSUs (varies widely — accelerated, converted to acquirer equity, cancelled, replaced with a new grant, subject to a specific retention agreement)?
- **Stock-option treatment.** What specifically happens to vested-and-in-the-money options (typically exercised or net-settled into transaction consideration)? What specifically happens to vested-and-out-of-the-money options (typically cancelled with no consideration if the exercise price exceeds transaction consideration)? What specifically happens to unvested options (varies — accelerated, converted, cancelled, replaced)? Specific to ISO treatment — does the transaction disqualify ISO status, converting to NSO treatment with different tax consequences?
- **ESPP treatment.** What specifically happens to the current ESPP offering period? Is the offering period terminated early with a specific true-up? What specifically happens to accumulated payroll deductions? What are the specific tax consequences?
- **Equity-incentive-plan treatment for the acquirer's plan.** What is the specific mechanism by which employees participate in the acquirer's future equity programme? What is the specific timing of the first grant? What is the specific vesting schedule? For a public-acquirer target, what is the specific 10b5-1 / trading-window regime the employees now sit under?

**Compensation and payroll.** What is the specific payroll transition — same payroll provider, transition to acquirer's provider, effective date? What is the specific base-salary treatment (typically no change, but must be confirmed)? What is the specific bonus treatment — accrued but unpaid bonuses, in-flight bonus programme prorated to close, or continuing under the ongoing programme? What is the specific commission treatment for sales employees? What is the specific expense-reimbursement transition?

**Location, role, and reporting.** What are the specific commitments (or non-commitments) on office location, remote-work policy, role structure, reporting lines, and organisational-integration timing? This is typically the section where the FAQ says "specific decisions on org structure will be communicated over the next 60–90 days" rather than committing prematurely.

**Immigration and visa.** For employees on work-authorisation visas (H-1B, TN, L-1, O-1, and international equivalents), what is the specific successor-employer process, what is the specific timing, what is the specific documentation the employee will need to provide? This is a specific compliance workstream that many deal teams under-invest in and that ends in a specific employee-visa expiration crisis 60 days post-close.

**Contact and follow-up.** Who specifically is the contact for each category (typically named individuals with email and calendaring link), what is the specific office-hours schedule, what is the specific escalation path.

The FAQ is a *living document* — new questions are added as they surface, revised answers are pushed to the portal, employees are notified of specific updates. The FAQ is not a legal document but is a factual-representation document that must be accurate; misrepresentations in the FAQ can support employee-side claims post-close.

## Integration-day-one messaging — the specific handoff to mod-111

The **integration-day-one messaging** is the specific communications programme delivered on the first day after closing — typically 30–150 days after signing, depending on regulatory clearance. Chapter 3 (customer) and mod-111 (integration mechanics) own most of this workstream, but the *employee-communications overlay* on day one is the responsibility of this module.

**Day-one all-hands (typically joint with acquirer leadership).** A specific joint all-hands is held on the first business day post-close, with the target CEO (or the acquirer's designated leader for the target unit) introducing the acquirer's leadership, previewing the specific integration timeline, and reinforcing the specific "what stays the same, what changes" framing. The joint all-hands is choreographed with the acquirer's comms team (their CEO / integration lead is often present, either in-person or via video). The specific messaging is drafted by both comms teams jointly.

**Day-one operational-change communications.** The specific operational changes that hit on day one — new email domain (or not), new Slack workspace (or not), new SSO login (or not), new expense system (or not), new performance-review cycle (or not), new benefits enrolment window (or not) — are each communicated in a specific message from the specific owner (IT for systems changes, HR for benefits and performance, Finance for expense, etc.).

**Day-one manager and team-leader briefings.** Each manager receives a specific day-one briefing covering the ongoing manager-cascade responsibility — the transition from "signing to close" messaging to "day one and beyond" messaging, the specific handoff of any pending questions, and the specific mechanisms for continuing to raise employee concerns during integration.

**Day-one integration-team introduction.** The specific integration-team members from the acquirer's side (typically an integration-management-office lead, a technology-integration lead, a functional-integration lead per function) are introduced to the target's leadership and to the specific workstreams. Each introduction includes the specific cadence of interaction (weekly / bi-weekly), the specific escalation mechanism, and the specific decision-rights division.

**Handoff to mod-111.** From day-two, the workstream is owned by the integration-management-office and by mod-111. The communications-workstream discipline continues (weekly updates from the target CEO, monthly all-hands, quarterly leadership town halls), but the specific integration-mechanic decisions (systems, org, culture, escrow-release, earn-out achievement) are mod-111's territory.

## Summary

The post-signing communications regime is a *specific choreographed run-of-show* that transforms a signed deal into a retained workforce. The specific sequencing is anchored to the announcement wire — 8-K filing at market open, press release simultaneously to satisfy Reg FD, all-hands within 30 minutes to prevent the retention-eroding "learn from LinkedIn" problem, manager cascade wave 1 within 15 minutes of the all-hands, wave 2 within 60 minutes, retention conversations for the top-20 retention-risk-quartile within 60–180 minutes, benefits-transition portal opened at the all-hands moment. The choreography is designed and rehearsed in advance — the CEO's job on announcement day is execution, not authoring. The retention overlay is designed 2–6 weeks pre-close through a specific value / flight-risk quartile analysis, with each top-quartile individual assigned a specific executive sponsor, a specific pre-drafted term sheet, and a specific pre-scheduled 1:1. The benefits-transition FAQ is a specific document covering health / retirement / PTO / equity / compensation / location / immigration, drafted by CHRO with input from executive-compensation counsel, tax counsel, and the acquirer's HR team, and pushed to a specific portal at the announcement moment. Integration-day-one messaging is the handoff to mod-111 and is choreographed jointly with the acquirer's integration team.

The specific chapter that follows turns to the customer, partner, and channel-communications workstream — the parallel workstream that runs alongside the employee choreography and that determines whether the top-ARR accounts renew through integration.
