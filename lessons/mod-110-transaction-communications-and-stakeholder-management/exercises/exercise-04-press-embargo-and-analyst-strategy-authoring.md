## Tasks

### 1. Draft the specific joint press release

Draft the specific joint press release the two sides will issue over the wire on announcement day. Follow the specific structure from chapter 4:

- Headline (one line).
- Sub-headline (one line, strategic rationale).
- Lead paragraph (2–3 sentences — parties, transaction structure, headline valuation, closing timeline, regulatory clearances).
- Buyer CEO quote.
- Seller CEO quote.
- Transaction rationale (3–5 paragraphs — combined-company positioning, product / geographic / customer synergies, financial synergies, transition commitments).
- Financial terms paragraph (consideration mix, per-share or aggregate value, accretion / dilution for public buyer, financing).
- Governance and management paragraph (post-close leadership, board composition, integration leadership).
- Timing and closing conditions paragraph (expected close date, regulatory approvals, stockholder-approval requirements, other closing conditions).
- Advisors paragraph (financial and legal advisors for each side).
- Investor and analyst call information (if applicable).
- Forward-looking-statements legend (PSLRA safe-harbour, specific risk factors, specific 10-K cross-reference).
- About-us paragraphs (one per side).
- Media contacts and investor contacts (named individuals with contact information).

Specifically annotate the draft with specific marginalia at each paragraph indicating:

- The specific negotiating-sensitivity flag — which specific paragraph is likely to require specific negotiation with the specific counterparty (headline phrasing, CEO quote-ordering, transition commitments wording, financial-term specificity).
- The specific Reg-FD-sensitive flag — specifically which claims in the specific release will constrain the specific CEOs' and specific CFOs' subsequent commentary (if the specific release omits specific synergy numbers, the specific CEOs cannot provide them in the specific analyst call either without triggering a specific supplemental 8-K).
- The specific litigation-sensitivity flag — specifically which claims will be inspected by specific plaintiff firms (specifically the strike-suit firms — WeissLaw, Halper Sadeh, Brodsky Smith) for specific disclosure-omission litigation.

### 2. Build the embargo grid with specific named outlets and reporters

Produce the specific embargo grid. The grid should list:

- The specific tier-1 outlets you are embargoing (typically 2–4 — WSJ, Bloomberg, FT, Reuters, plus specific sector outlets like TechCrunch, The Information, FierceBiotech, Variety, S&P Global, PitchBook, specific sector trade press depending on the specific target's specific industry).
- For each outlet: the specific reporter name (research actual sector-covering reporters at that outlet — WSJ Deals desk, Bloomberg's specific M&A reporters, FT Lex, Reuters Deals, TechCrunch's enterprise-SaaS or specific-sector beat reporter), the specific beat the reporter covers, the specific pre-briefing meeting type (phone call with CEO, phone call with CFO, group analyst-and-reporter call, text-brief with written-release-under-embargo), the specific embargo-lift time (coordinated with the specific wire release).
- The specific written embargo agreement template (1 page). Covers what information is being shared, when the embargo lifts, what commitments the reporter is making (no discussion with sources, no publication before embargo lift, no sharing with colleagues at other outlets, no social-media commentary pre-embargo), what the specific NDA basis is.
- The specific pre-briefing agenda for each specific reporter (specific transaction rationale, specific numbers permitted under Reg FD, specific colour, specific CEO / CFO quotes beyond the press release).
- The specific CEO / CFO time commitment for each pre-briefing (typically 30–60 minutes per outlet; cumulative 2–4 hours over the 12–24 hours before embargo-lift).

Specifically identify two or three specific outlets you considered but *excluded* from the embargo, and the specific reason (relationship risk, specific past embargo break, specific negative-coverage history, specific competitive-outlet-dynamic in the sector).

### 3. Build the analyst-call deck and scripted Q&A

Produce the specific analyst-call deck. The deck should include:

- Cover slide: transaction name, date, participants.
- Transaction overview (1 slide): parties, structure, consideration, expected close.
- Strategic rationale (2–3 slides): combined positioning, product / market / customer synergies.
- Financial detail (2–3 slides, buyer-perspective-dominant for a strategic acquirer / sponsor-perspective-dominant for a PE take-private): revenue synergies, cost synergies, expected accretion / dilution, expected deal financing, expected close P&L mechanics.
- Integration plan (1–2 slides): IMO structure, timeline, key milestones.
- Regulatory timeline (1 slide): expected clearances, specific jurisdictions, expected timing.
- Governance and management (1 slide): post-close leadership, board composition, retention commitments.
- Q&A (1 slide, cue card for the specific analysts in the call).

Draft the specific **scripted Q&A** — the 15–20 most-anticipated analyst questions with specific pre-approved responses. Expected categories:

- "What is the specific valuation multiple and how does it compare to specific recent comparable transactions?"
- "What specific revenue synergies drive the specific accretion case?"
- "What specific cost synergies drive the specific accretion case, and over what timeline?"
- "What specific role-consolidation is implied by the specific cost-synergy number?" (Specifically watch the Reg-FD boundary — the specific answer must be consistent with the specific joint-press-release framing and the specific workforce-communications messaging from exercise 02.)
- "What is the specific exit multiple assumed in the specific sponsor case?" (For a PE take-private.)
- "What is the specific earn-out structure, if any, and what are the specific achievement conditions?"
- "What is the specific regulatory-clearance risk?"
- "What is the specific break-fee structure, and under what specific circumstances is it payable?"
- "What is the specific stockholder-approval timeline?"
- "What specific guidance is the specific company providing for the specific interim period?"

For each specific question, flag the specific Reg-FD limit (what cannot be said without supplemental disclosure) and the specific insider-trading limit (what cannot be said without triggering specific MNPI concerns for the analyst audience).

## Starter guidance

Three anti-patterns to avoid:

- **"We'll let the PR firm draft the release."** The specific release is a specific legal document (filed as an 8-K exhibit, subject to Rule 14a-12 / Rule 425 as applicable), a specific commercial document (negotiated with the specific counterparty), and a specific communications document (shapes the narrative for years). The specific GC / CEO / CFO cannot delegate the specific drafting; the specific PR firm supports the drafting but does not own it.
- **"Embargoes are a formality — just send the release early."** The specific embargo is a specific contractual-and-reputational arrangement that specifically gives the sell-side specific control over the specific first stories written about the deal. A specific sloppy embargo (unclear lift time, specific overlap with uncovered outlets, specific pre-briefing of specific reporters not under embargo) specifically defeats the specific purpose and specifically burns specific outlet relationships for specific future deals.
- **"Short-sellers aren't our problem — they're the market's problem."** A specific short-seller report that goes unaddressed on announcement day specifically becomes the specific framing that specific sell-side analysts reference, that specific journalists cite, and that specific institutional investors remember. The specific IR discipline is to engage on specific factual errors, decline to engage on specific opinion, and specifically hold the specific commitment to the specific deal thesis.
