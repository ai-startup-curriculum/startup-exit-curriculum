# exercise-02: Post-Signing All-Hands Choreography Authoring

**Estimated effort:** 4 hours

## Objective

Author the specific **announcement-day run-of-show** that transforms the deal you perimeter-protected in exercise 01 into a retained workforce. Produce a specific minute-by-minute schedule anchored to the 8-K / press-release wire, draft the specific CEO all-hands script and VP cascade materials, build the specific top-20 retention-risk list and the specific pre-scheduled retention-conversation calendar, and author the specific benefits-transition FAQ that the whole workforce will read on their phones at 11:03am. By the end of the exercise, a critical reader should be able to execute the whole announcement day from the artefacts you produce — no CEO improvisation required.

## Background

This exercise covers material from:

- [Chapter 2 — Post-Signing Employee Choreography and Retention](../02-post-signing-employee-choreography-and-retention.md)
- [Chapter 1 — Pre-Signing Employee Communications](../01-pre-signing-employee-communications-and-need-to-know.md) — specifically the clean-list roster that governs who already knows and who is being briefed for the first time on announcement day.

Your artefacts in this exercise reference back to the clean list from exercise 01 (the people who have been *inside* the perimeter) and reference forward to the customer and partner communications (exercise 03) and the press cycle (exercise 04) that run in parallel on the same morning.

## Prerequisites

- The transaction profile frozen in exercise 01.
- Access to a specific recent 8-K announcement with the attached joint press release (search EDGAR for a specific comparable deal under Item 1.01 at <https://www.sec.gov/cgi-bin/browse-edgar>) — this anchors the specific tone and specific disclosure boundaries of the press release.
- Access to a specific recent **S-4 / DEFM14A** proxy that includes the specific "Interests of Our Directors and Executive Officers" section — this anchors the specific vocabulary around specific equity treatment and specific retention arrangements that your benefits-transition FAQ must be accurate against.
- Access to a specific set of SHRM, Mercer, or Willis Towers Watson post-merger retention-practice references (SHRM at <https://www.shrm.org>, Mercer at <https://www.mercer.com>, WTW at <https://www.wtwco.com>) for anchoring specific retention-package sizing conventions.
- Reg FD text at <https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-17/chapter-II/part-243> for anchoring the specific "simultaneous disclosure" discipline that drives the 30-minute all-hands timing.

## Tasks

### 1. Author the minute-by-minute announcement-day run-of-show

Produce a specific **T-day run-of-show** as a specific timeline document. Anchor every entry to a specific clock time (use the specific time zone of the target headquarters, and note the specific time-zone-conversion for the specific major international sites and the specific major customer regions). Cover:

- **T–1 day.** Each specific pre-day artefact that must be finalised and the specific owner of each. At minimum: final press release, internal all-hands script, VP cascade materials, FAQ, customer email templates, analyst / investor / press call decks, retention term-sheet packs for each top-20 individual, benefits-transition portal content.
- **T-day pre-market (00:00–06:30).** The specific board-approval timing (if a final sign-off is still outstanding), the specific EDGAR filing of the 8-K, the specific press-wire release, the specific internal-portal publishing. For a specific private target the pre-market steps are compressed; call out the specific private-vs-public-target differences for the specific transaction.
- **T-day 06:30–09:00.** The specific internal all-hands timing (within 30 minutes of the wire for a public target; immediately for a private target), the specific manager cascade wave 1 timing (15 minutes post-all-hands), the specific manager cascade wave 2 timing (30–60 minutes post-all-hands), the specific benefits-transition portal opening, and the specific tier-1 customer calls that happen in parallel.
- **T-day 09:00–12:00.** The specific retention conversations with the top-20 (named 1:1 slots on the CEO / CFO / CHRO calendar), the specific VP wave-3 1:1 cadence beginning, the specific additional customer and partner outreach, and the specific press pre-briefing follow-ups.
- **T-day 12:00–17:00.** The specific analyst / investor call (for public issuers), the specific partner-ecosystem written communications, the specific manager-to-direct-report 1:1 scheduling for the week ahead, and the specific "written follow-up" CEO note at end of day.
- **T+1 through T+30.** The specific weekly all-hands cadence that replaces the ordinary cadence for 4–6 weeks, the specific weekly written CEO note, the specific integration-planning all-hands at week 2, the specific regulatory-timeline update at week 4, and the specific moment the ordinary cadence resumes.

For each entry: name the specific owner, specific audience, specific channel (email, Slack, portal, in-person, Zoom, press wire), and the specific input the owner needs from the clean-list team (e.g., the specific signed benefits-FAQ from CHRO and the specific equity-treatment write-up from executive-compensation counsel).

### 2. Draft the specific CEO all-hands script (4–6 pages)

Draft the specific 15–20-minute all-hands script the CEO will read on announcement day. The script should include:

- Opening (90 seconds). The specific framing — "today we announced …," what the deal is, the specific transaction counterparty, the specific headline terms, the specific expected closing timeline.
- The specific "why this deal, why now" (3–5 minutes). The specific strategic rationale from the sell-side perspective. Keep it specifically consistent with the specific joint-press-release narrative; the specific CEO cannot preview buy-side synergy math that is not in the public release.
- The specific "what this means for employees" (5–8 minutes). The specific commitments on team-continuity, mission-continuity, product-continuity, and specific areas where decisions are still pending. The specific explicit guidance that the CEO *will not* commit to specific role-level decisions on announcement day. The specific redirect to the benefits-transition FAQ and the specific manager cascade.
- The specific "what happens next" (2–3 minutes). The specific regulatory-clearance timeline, the specific integration-planning cadence, the specific communication cadence over the next 60–90 days, and the specific specific-close-day expectations.
- The specific closing (90 seconds). The specific gratitude to the workforce, the specific commitment on availability, the specific reinforcement that managers have the FAQ and are the first stop for questions.

Produce the specific script in a specific Q&A format with the specific anticipated 10–15 employee questions and the specific pre-approved responses. For each anticipated question, flag the specific Reg FD / insider-trading limit (e.g., no specific financial-synergy numbers beyond the press release, no specific commitments on specific role-level decisions, no specific commitments on specific valuation beyond the specific definitive agreement).

### 3. Build the specific VP cascade materials (3–5 page pack)

Produce the specific pack that will be handed to every VP and director in the specific wave-1 15-minute cascade meeting 15 minutes after the all-hands:

- The specific **"what we say" script** (1 page). The specific talking points the manager uses to open their team meeting, the specific framing they reinforce, the specific hand-off to the FAQ and the benefits-transition portal.
- The specific **"what we do not say" list** (½ page). The specific decisions that have not yet been made (org structure, specific role changes, specific relocations, specific compensation changes), the specific Reg FD boundary (no specific financial detail beyond the press release), the specific insider-trading reminder.
- The specific **top-15 FAQ** (2 pages). The 15 most-anticipated employee questions and specific pre-drafted answers covering "why this deal, why now," "what happens to my job," "what happens to my equity," "what happens to my benefits," "what happens to the team," "what happens to the office," "when does this close," "what do I do if I have concerns."
- The specific **escalation path** (½ page). Who to route each category of question to — CHRO for HR, GC for legal, benefits vendor for benefits, equity administrator for equity, immigration counsel for visa.
- The specific **retention-conversation coordination note** (½ page). For each wave-1 VP, the specific list of their direct reports who are on the top-20 retention list (if any), who is running their retention conversation, and when. The specific note that the VP should **not** independently initiate retention conversations with their direct reports — these are centrally run by the CEO / CFO / CHRO / specific executive sponsor pattern.
- The specific **24-hour follow-up discipline**. Each VP confirms in writing (brief Slack / email note) that their team was covered by end of day, including specific unanswered questions that need FAQ-additions.

### 4. Build the top-20 retention-risk list and the retention term-sheet pack

Produce the specific retention-list artefact and the specific per-individual term-sheet pack:

- The specific **retention-risk quartile matrix** (table). For each specific executive-and-key-individual on the retention list (10–30 individuals in a mid-size target), capture: name / role, "value" score (criticality to ongoing business or to integration thesis) with specific rationale, "flight risk" score with specific rationale, quartile placement, assigned executive sponsor for the retention conversation, scheduled time slot for the T-day 1:1.
- The specific **retention-conversation script** (2 pages, parameterised per individual). Follow the chapter-2 template — opening, framing, specific offer, personal-consequence discussion, ask for commitment, follow-up. For each specific individual, parameterise the specific "what will change" and "what will not change" and the specific offer terms.
- The specific **per-individual retention term-sheet** (1 page per individual, 10–30 pages total). For each specific top-20 individual: cash retention bonus amount with specific vesting schedule (typical: 50% at close, 25% at 6 months, 25% at 12 months — but the specific numbers reflect the specific individual's specific base and specific flight-risk), equity treatment (acceleration triggers, specific unvested-equity disposition, specific new acquirer grants if applicable), severance protection (double-trigger window, severance months of base plus bonus), role commitments from the acquirer, non-compete and non-solicitation terms, 280G cutback or protection language.
- The specific **follow-up protocol**. For each specific individual, the specific "48-hour check-in" with the executive sponsor, the specific "week-2 commitment conversation," the specific "30-day sentiment check," and the specific escalation path if the specific individual signals they are not aligned with the specific transaction.

Cross-reference each specific term-sheet with the specific MIP / executive-retention design from exercise 05 — the specific per-individual terms should be internally consistent with the specific MIP framework you design in that exercise.

### 5. Author the benefits-transition FAQ (6–10 page document) and the portal content

Draft the specific benefits-transition FAQ that opens on the portal the moment the all-hands begins:

- **Health and welfare benefits.** Current plan vs. acquirer plan, specific transition date, specific coverage during the transition period, specific in-flight claim handling, specific supplemental benefits (fertility, mental health, pet insurance), specific dependent coverage.
- **Retirement benefits.** 401(k) plan treatment (merger / termination / standalone continuation), employer-match vesting treatment (specifically whether the transaction accelerates vesting of unvested match), distribution / rollover options, any defined-benefit or profit-sharing plan treatment.
- **Paid time off.** Current PTO balance treatment (carry over, cash out, blend into acquirer's structure), accrued but unused sick leave (in specific jurisdictions this is a wage and must be paid out — see California Labor Code §233 and specific state analogs), parental leave and sabbatical accruals.
- **Equity.** The specific most consequential and most read section:
  - RSU treatment — specific disposition for vested and unvested, cash vs. acquirer-stock conversion, specific tax consequences.
  - Stock options — specific treatment for vested-and-in-the-money, vested-and-out-of-the-money (cancelled with no consideration if exercise price exceeds consideration — flag this explicitly), unvested. ISO-to-NSO conversion risk under the specific transaction mechanics.
  - ESPP — specific offering-period treatment, specific accumulated-deductions handling, specific tax consequences.
  - Acquirer equity programme — specific first-grant timing, specific vesting schedule, specific 10b5-1 / trading-window regime for a public acquirer.
- **Compensation and payroll.** Payroll-provider transition, base-salary treatment, bonus-programme treatment (accrued but unpaid, in-flight prorated to close, continuing), commission treatment for sales employees, expense-reimbursement transition.
- **Location, role, and reporting.** The specific commitments made (and specifically not made) on office location, remote-work policy, role structure, reporting lines, and specific org-integration timing.
- **Immigration and visa.** Successor-employer process for H-1B / TN / L-1 / O-1 and international-equivalent visas, specific timing, specific documentation the employee must provide.
- **Contact and follow-up.** Named individuals for each category with specific email and calendaring link, specific office-hours schedule, specific escalation path.

Attach a specific **living-document protocol** — how new questions are added to the FAQ, how revised answers are pushed, how employees are notified of updates. Include a specific **accuracy-review attestation** confirming the FAQ was reviewed by executive-compensation counsel, tax counsel, benefits-plan administrator, and the acquirer's HR team before publication.

### 6. Author the integration-day-one messaging preview (1-page)

Draft the specific preview of day-one messaging that will be delivered on the first business day post-close. This is the specific hand-off to mod-111. Cover:

- The specific day-one all-hands format (joint with acquirer leadership, timing, agenda, duration).
- The specific operational-change communications on day one (email domain, Slack workspace, SSO, expense system, performance-review cycle, benefits enrolment window — specifying which actually change on day one versus which have a specific transition period).
- The specific manager-and-team-leader briefing on day one.
- The specific integration-team introduction and the specific weekly / bi-weekly cadence.
- The specific hand-off point where mod-111 (post-close integration) takes over — the specific day and the specific artefact that marks the transition.

## Starter guidance

Three anti-patterns to avoid:

- **"We'll figure out the manager cascade on announcement morning."** VPs who improvise from the FAQ miss the specific framing and plant the specific concerns that the CEO has deliberately not planted. The specific defence is a specific wave-1 attendance list enforced by the CEO's office and a specific 24-hour follow-up review.
- **"We shouldn't commit to specific retention terms until we see how the diligence lands."** The specific top-20 retention conversations are the specific lever that holds the critical quartile through close. A specific CEO who shows up on announcement day without a specific signed term sheet in hand loses the specific moment — the specific conversation becomes "we are thinking about retention" rather than "here is your specific offer." Design the specific terms in diligence, finalise before signing.
- **"The FAQ can be approximate — we'll update as we learn."** The specific benefits FAQ is a factual-representation document — misrepresentations in the FAQ can support specific employee-side claims post-close. Every specific answer must be accurate on day one; the specific "updates as we learn" discipline is for *new questions*, not for *correcting mis-statements*.

## Acceptance criteria

You can demonstrate that:

- The run-of-show is specific to the time zone, specific to the target headcount, and specific to the public-vs-private target status of the specific transaction.
- The CEO all-hands script is a specific usable artefact — a reader could execute the all-hands from the document.
- The VP cascade pack covers every element from the chapter and is specific enough that a wave-1 VP could run their team meeting using it.
- The retention list has specific named individuals with specific value / flight-risk rationale, each paired with a specific term sheet and a specific pre-scheduled 1:1.
- The benefits-transition FAQ is specific enough that an employee reading it on their phone at 11:03am gets a specific answer to each of the top questions, with a specific escalation path for anything not covered.
- A critical reader (CHRO, VP-People, executive-compensation counsel, deal-experienced GC) could disagree with a specific detail and see the specific judgement call that produced it.
