# exercise-03: Customer and Partner Communication Plan Drill

**Estimated effort:** 4 hours

## Objective

Build the specific **customer, partner, and channel-communications programme** that runs in parallel with the employee choreography on announcement day and through the first 100 days of integration. Take the frozen transaction profile from exercise 01, produce the pre-signing change-of-control-clause diligence matrix, build the top-ARR retention-call sequencing with pre-drafted scripts, tier the partner-communications programme, design the specific channel-reseller consent workflow, and produce the specific pre-warned reference-call plan for the customers sitting in the buy-side data room. By the end of the exercise you should be able to walk a critical reader through the specific customer whose contract requires consent, the specific hour the CEO is on the phone with that customer, and the specific written FAQ that lands in the customer's inbox 90 minutes later.

## Tasks

### 1. Build the change-of-control-clause diligence matrix

Produce the specific COC-diligence spreadsheet or structured table. One row per material contract, with the following columns:

- Contract name / counterparty / contract-value (annual and total) / contract-renewal date.
- Contract type (customer / supplier / channel reseller / technology integration / lease / government / specific regulated contract).
- COC category: (a) consent required, (b) notice required, (c) termination on COC, (d) silent.
- Specific clause text (the actual contract language).
- Specific timing requirement (consent received N days pre-close / notice delivered within N days post-signing / termination exercisable within N days post-notice).
- Defence language present (unreasonably-withheld qualifier, cure-period provision, deemed-consent-after-silence provision).
- Specific transaction-structure interaction (stock vs. asset deal — most assignments trigger specific additional consent requirements in an asset deal under UCC Article 9 and specific contract language).
- Specific action plan: who contacts the counterparty, when, with what specific script, what the specific fallback is if consent is withheld.
- Specific closing-condition impact: whether this contract is "material" enough that the definitive agreement conditions closing on specific consents being obtained.

Apply the matrix to the specific transaction's specific contract portfolio. At minimum populate the matrix with:

- The top 10–20 customer contracts by ARR.
- The top 5–10 supplier contracts by spend.
- Every real-estate lease.
- Every channel-partner and reseller agreement.
- Every material licensing / IP-in / open-source-inbound contract.
- Every specific regulated contract (government contracts, healthcare BAAs, specific DPAs under GDPR / CCPA, specific government-security-cleared contracts).

Attach a specific **summary memo** (2 pages) covering: how many contracts in each COC category, which specific contracts materially shape the deal (specific closing-condition candidates), which specific contracts have specific carve-out language that reduces risk, and which specific contracts need a specific pre-signing consent conversation versus a specific post-signing notice workflow.

### 2. Build the top-ARR retention-call sequencing

Produce the specific sequenced calling plan for the top-ARR customers. Deliverables:

- The specific **top-ARR customer list** (10–50 accounts in a mid-size company; top-5 in a highly-concentrated company). For each account capture: current ARR, current health (green / yellow / red), renewal date, expansion opportunity, specific primary contact (the person to call), executive sponsor (who at the sell-side owns the relationship), known concerns, in-flight negotiations, COC-clause implications from the matrix in task 1.
- The specific **sequenced calling plan** (table). For each account specify: calling owner (CEO for top-5, CRO for next tier, CSM for the long tail), target call window (hour of day in customer's time zone), joint-call participation (whether the acquirer CEO or specific relationship-owning executive joins), expected call duration (15 min for personal reach-out; 30 min for strategic account with specific concerns), scheduled follow-up (CS team written FAQ, specific executive follow-up at 48 hours and 2 weeks).
- The specific **time-zone propagation plan**. For a US-headquartered deal with international customers, sequence: APAC-based customers called first thing on announcement morning (their afternoon), Europe-based customers called mid-morning US time (their afternoon), US customers called mid-day and afternoon. Document the specific earliest call that happens pre-embargo-lift (specifically, the top-5 US customers called before 06:30 Eastern press-embargo-lift) and the specific latest call that happens on announcement day.

### 3. Author the specific customer-call scripts

Draft three specific call scripts, each 1 page:

- **CEO-to-top-5 script** (the deep personal call, 20–30 minutes, often joint with acquirer CEO). Opening, framing, specific commitments (product continuity, roadmap continuity, support continuity, account-team continuity, pricing continuity), specific-benefit framing (expanded capability, expanded footprint, specific integration with acquirer product), relationship commitment, ask for concerns, follow-up commitment.
- **CRO-to-tier-2 script** (the significant-relationship call, 10–15 minutes). Shorter version of the CEO script, with a specific hand-off to the account-team lead for ongoing coordination.
- **CSM-to-long-tail script** (the written-first-then-call reinforcement). The specific pre-written email that lands in the customer's inbox 90 minutes after announcement, with the specific CSM follow-up call template for the specific customer who responds with specific questions.

Each script should include a specific **anticipated-concerns section** with pre-drafted responses to the 8–10 most-common concerns: "will the product continue," "will my pricing change," "will my account team change," "will my data stay in [specific region]," "will my integration with [specific acquirer competitor] continue," "will the acquirer be my new vendor-of-record / contract counterparty," "when does this close," "what should I tell my boss."

Add a specific **escalation path**: for each category of concern, which specific executive owns the specific response, and the specific commitment on response time (CEO issues direct for existential concerns; CRO within 24 hours for commercial concerns; CS team within 72 hours for operational concerns).

### 4. Tier the partner-communications programme

Produce the specific partner-tier plan. One row per partner (or per partner-tier where the partner count is high):

- **Tier 1 — strategic partners.** The 5–20 partners whose relationship materially affects the target's business. Treatment: personal outreach from CEO or specific relationship-owning executive within hours of announcement, specific commitments, joint-call participation where relationship warrants.
- **Tier 2 — technology integration partners.** The partners whose product the target integrates with or vice versa. Treatment: written outreach same day or next business day, specific commitments on integration-continuity.
- **Tier 3 — channel-reseller partners.** The partners who resell the target's product. Treatment: written outreach, specific commitments on channel-programme continuity, specific coordination on COC-consent workflow (see task 5).
- **Tier 4 — referral / affiliate partners.** Lower-touch, templated email with FAQ.
- **Tier 5 — government / regulatory / association partners.** Specific outreach for specific regulatory or advocacy relationships, typically handled by GC or government-affairs lead.

For each tier, specify: owner, timing, communication channel, specific FAQ scope, specific escalation path for partner concerns, specific cadence of ongoing updates (weekly for tier 1, monthly for tier 2, event-triggered for lower tiers).

### 5. Design the channel-reseller consent workflow

For the specific transaction's channel-reseller agreements (identified in task 1), design the specific consent-collection workflow:

- The specific **consent-request letter template** (1 page). Drafted jointly by partnerships-lead and outside counsel. Covers transaction facts, specific consent request, specific ongoing-commitment language, specific timeline.
- The specific **tracker spreadsheet.** One row per reseller: contract value, COC category, consent-request-sent date, consent-response date, consent-granted or denied or conditional (with specific negotiated conditions), outstanding-items list.
- The specific **aggregation thresholds.** For a target with a large reseller footprint, specify the specific closing-condition threshold (e.g., 80–90% of consent-required contracts by revenue obtained before close). Document the specific definitive-agreement mechanic for the specific remainder (post-close cure period, specific indemnification, specific price adjustment).
- The specific **negotiation playbook for push-back.** For resellers who respond with specific demands (contract extension, specific exclusivity, specific pricing protection), the specific framework the partnerships-lead uses to triage — which demands are approved pre-negotiated (within the specific pre-signing authority), which escalate to the GC / CFO, which escalate to the CEO.
- The specific **termination-election response playbook.** For resellers who exercise a specific termination-on-COC right, the specific framework for either renegotiating a specific new agreement or accepting the specific loss of the specific reseller — with the specific revenue-impact analysis fed back to the deal-team for the specific definitive-agreement impact (specific MAC analysis, specific closing-condition analysis).

### 6. Author the pre-warned reference-call plan

Produce the specific pre-warning plan for the customers sitting in the buy-side data room during exclusivity:

- The specific **reference-list artefact.** The 5–15 named customers in the buy-side data room, with specific contact information, specific relationship-owner on the sell-side, and specific pre-warning status (not-yet-warned / warned on specific date / inbound-call expected within specific window).
- The specific **pre-warning call script** (½ page). The specific 5-minute call that explains the specific confidential process, specifically asks the customer to accept an inbound call from the specific buy-side diligence firm (if the sell-side can name the firm) within the next 1–2 weeks, specifically requests confidentiality until public announcement, and specifically requests the customer's willingness to participate.
- The specific **tracker spreadsheet.** One row per reference: customer, pre-warning call date, pre-warning call outcome (accepted / declined / requires-further-call), expected inbound-call window, actual inbound-call occurrence, sell-side post-call debrief (what was discussed, any specific concern that surfaced, specific sell-side response).
- The specific **leak-risk-mitigation protocol.** For a specific customer who is specifically declining the pre-warning or specifically signalling they are uncomfortable with the confidential process, the specific escalation to the GC and the specific decision — either accept the specific risk the customer may leak, remove the specific customer from the reference list and offer a specific substitute, or accept that the specific reference cannot be used.

### 7. Produce the integration-communications cadence (1-page summary)

Author the specific cadence of integration communications that runs through close and into the first 100 days:

- Weekly customer updates (for the top-ARR customers): specific owner, specific channel, specific template content.
- Monthly all-customer updates: specific channel (email newsletter or customer portal), specific content categories.
- Quarterly customer summits (post-close, for the top-ARR customers): specific format (in-person / virtual), specific target timing (60–120 days post-close), specific joint-acquirer-leadership participation.
- Partner ecosystem updates: analogous cadence per tier.
- The specific hand-off to mod-111: the specific day and the specific artefact that marks the transition to the acquirer-managed ongoing customer-communications programme.

## Starter guidance

Three anti-patterns to avoid:

- **"COC diligence is the lawyer's problem."** The specific COC clauses are the mechanism by which a specific customer or specific reseller re-opens the specific commercial conversation during the specific pre-close window — and specifically imposes the specific negotiating leverage that was closed when the specific contract was signed. The specific CRO and specific partnerships-lead must be reading the specific contracts alongside counsel; the specific communications programme is downstream of the specific legal analysis.
- **"We'll let the acquirer talk to customers."** The specific customers' trust is with the specific target's specific relationship-owner — not the specific acquirer. A specific customer who hears first from the specific acquirer (whose brand they do not know, whose relationship they do not have) receives the specific communication as impersonal and transactional. The specific sell-side CEO's specific personal call is the specific lever that preserves the specific relationship through the transition.
- **"Reference calls are the buyer's problem."** The specific reference customers who are called out of the blue by an unknown firm during exclusivity are the specific customers most likely to leak the specific deal — specifically because they have no specific confidentiality obligation unless they have been specifically asked for one. The specific pre-warning is the specific mechanism by which the specific customer becomes a specific protective participant in the specific confidential process.

## Acceptance criteria

You can demonstrate that:

- The COC diligence matrix is populated with specific contract-level detail, not a specific generic placeholder.
- The top-ARR retention-call sequencing is specific — specific accounts, specific owners, specific times (in the specific customer's time zone), specific joint-call participation decisions.
- The three customer-call scripts are specifically usable — a CEO reading the specific script could execute the specific call.
- The partner-tier plan covers the specific partners in the specific transaction's ecosystem, not a specific generic tier framework.
- The channel-reseller consent workflow has specific thresholds, specific aggregation mechanics, and specific decision frameworks for the specific push-back scenarios.
- The pre-warned reference-call plan has a specific tracker and a specific leak-risk-mitigation protocol.
- A critical reader (CRO, VP-Customer-Success, head of partnerships, deal-experienced GC) could disagree with a specific decision and see the specific judgement call that produced it.
