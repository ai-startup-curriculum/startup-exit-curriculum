## Starter guidance

Three anti-patterns to avoid:

- **"WARN is a 60-day notice — plan a 60-day pre-close window and we're fine."** Specifically wrong. State mini-WARN specifically extends the window (90 days in NY, NJ, and others). New Jersey specifically imposes mandatory 1-week-per-year severance *regardless* of notice. International works-council consultation specifically extends the window further (1–3 months in France, variable in Germany, 30+ days plus Enterprise Chamber challenge in Netherlands). The specific aggregate timing is often 3–6 months from signing to the specific "safe-to-close" moment, not 60 days.
- **"Works councils are a European-counsel problem — we'll let them handle it."** Specifically wrong. The specific pre-announcement-vs-post-announcement choreography decision is a specific CEO-level decision that specifically interacts with the specific US-headquartered announcement timing, the specific clean-list perimeter, the specific customer-communications workstream, and the specific press cycle. European counsel executes the specific consultation; the specific choreography is the specific deal-team's responsibility.
- **"The buyer will deal with WARN post-close — not our problem."** Specifically wrong in asset deals (the specific seller specifically retains liability for pre-close non-compliance under 29 CFR §639.4(c)) and specifically problematic even in stock deals (the specific pre-signing workforce-action modelling is specifically required for the specific accurate rep-and-warranty-insurance underwriting, and specific undisclosed WARN exposure is a specific common R&W-claim trigger). The specific seller-side deal team specifically owns the specific pre-close workforce-action identification.
