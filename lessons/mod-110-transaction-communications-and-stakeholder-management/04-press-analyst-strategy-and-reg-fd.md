## The public-company disclosure regime — Regulation FD

**Regulation FD** (17 CFR §§243.100–103), adopted by the SEC in 2000, prohibits a public-company issuer (and specific persons acting on its behalf) from **selective disclosure** of material non-public information (MNPI). If a specific selective disclosure is *intentional* (defined as a communication made with knowledge that the information was material and non-public), the issuer must publicly disclose the same information simultaneously. If the selective disclosure is *non-intentional* (accidental), the issuer must publicly disclose the same information promptly — defined as no later than the later of 24 hours or the start of the next trading day.

**Public disclosure methods.** Reg FD is satisfied by disclosure through a specific "recognised channel of distribution" — traditionally a Form 8-K filing with the SEC, a press release distributed through a wire service, or a press conference / conference call / webcast that has been publicly announced with adequate notice. Following the 2013 SEC guidance in the *In re Netflix* matter, disclosure through corporate social-media channels (Twitter/X, LinkedIn, Facebook) can satisfy Reg FD if investors have been advised in advance that the specific channel will be used for specific material information — though most public issuers do not rely on social-media disclosure alone for material transaction announcements.

**Who is covered.** Reg FD covers communications by senior officials (defined broadly — CEO, CFO, IR-lead, board members, and other senior executives) and specific persons who regularly communicate with securities-market participants (analysts, institutional investors, IR consultants). It specifically does *not* cover communications with employees for ordinary-course purposes (though the specific announcement-day all-hands communication of transaction-related MNPI would create a Reg FD issue unless the same information had been publicly disclosed — hence the specific 30-minutes-post-press-release timing for the all-hands from chapter 2).

**The specific exemptions.** Reg FD has specific exemptions for: communications to persons owing a duty of trust or confidence (attorney-client, accountant, banker under confidentiality agreement), communications in connection with a registered securities offering (which are governed by Section 5 / Rule 433 disclosure regime — see mod-108 chapter 2), and communications to credit-rating agencies. The specific banker / lawyer / accountant exemption is the specific reason the sell-side deal team can discuss the specific transaction with its specific advisors without triggering Reg FD.

**The specific transaction application.** For a public-target sell-side transaction, the specific MNPI attaches the moment the deal is contemplated seriously (typically no later than the CEO's specific engagement of the sell-side banker). From that moment until public disclosure, the specific Reg FD discipline applies to every specific communication by covered persons that could touch on the specific transaction. The specific practical consequence: every specific investor conversation, every specific analyst call, every specific press interview during the pre-announcement window must be scripted so that the specific covered person does not disclose the specific MNPI. Standard framing: "We do not comment on rumours or speculation. We remain focused on our current strategy and our customers." The specific script is prepared in advance and rehearsed with the CEO / CFO.
