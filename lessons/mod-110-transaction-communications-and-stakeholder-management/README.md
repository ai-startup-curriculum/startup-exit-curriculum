# mod-110 — Transaction Communications & Stakeholder Management: Employees, Customers, Press, MIP, WARN

**Estimated effort:** 20 hours
**Stage:** any (used in every deal — GROWTH / MATURE for M&A and IPO, any stage for shutdown / secondary)
**Seat:** founder-CEO, CFO, GC / chief legal officer, CHRO / VP-People, VP-Comms / IR, SVP-Corp-Dev, deal-team lead

This module installs the **stakeholder-communications overlay** that sits alongside every meaningful transaction — sell-side M&A, buy-side M&A, going-private, IPO, secondary tender, minority strategic — and turns a *legally-and-financially defensible* deal into a *humanly-and-commercially defensible* one. It is the workstream that determines whether the deal leaks and re-prices, whether the top ten employees stay through close, whether the top-twenty ARR accounts renew through integration, whether the Wall Street Journal reporter you ignored writes a hostile story on announcement day, and whether the CFO discovers 45 days before close that the company owes 60-day WARN notice to 78 employees at the Mountain View site.

The frame is *choreography*, not messaging. A single mis-timed all-hands, a single leaked codename, a single change-of-control clause the sell-side lawyer missed in the largest customer contract, a single 280G excise-tax number the CEO learns about at the disclosure schedule, a single WARN notice missed by three days — each of these has a specific cost measured in millions of dollars of deal value, tens of millions of dollars of litigation exposure, and years of leadership credibility. A founder-CEO who treats communications as "the comms team's problem" ends up with a deal announcement that surprises the head of engineering and a Slack channel full of resignation-decision conversations by lunch.

The module also owns the *transaction-specific compensation and workforce overlays* that sit adjacent to the communications workstream: management-incentive-plan (MIP) design in a PE take-private, executive retention agreements with single-vs-double-trigger acceleration, IRC 280G golden-parachute analysis and the 280G(b)(5) shareholder-vote mitigation, IRC 409A payment-timing constraints on deal bonuses, federal and state mini-WARN Act compliance in transaction-driven workforce reductions, and works-council and merger-control stakeholder notifications in cross-border deals.

> Reminder: this module is education, not legal / tax / employment / communications advice. Every live transaction engages qualified securities counsel, employment counsel, executive-compensation counsel (for the 280G / 409A workstream), and specialist communications and IR advisors. WARN and works-council obligations are jurisdiction-specific and change; the specific thresholds cited below are anchors for further reading, not substitutes for current statutory research.

## Learning objectives

By the end of this module you should be able to:

- Design the **pre-signing employee-communications choreography** — the need-to-know discipline, the deal-team clean list, the code-name-per-project convention, the virtual-data-room access-audit review, the executive-team-only briefing pattern, the strict deal-team-adds-only rule with legal and financial advisor briefings gated separately, and the leak-response playbook (a leak almost always compresses the timeline and depresses the price).
- Design the **post-signing employee-communications choreography** — the day-of sequencing (board-approval → SEC filing → all-hands within 30 minutes of the press release for a public target, immediate all-hands for a private target), the manager one-on-one waves (first VP-level, then IC-level within 60 minutes), the retention-conversation script for the top-twenty quartile of retention risk (identified pre-close), the benefits-transition FAQ (health, 401(k), PTO, equity, vesting, RSU / option treatment, ESPP), and the integration-day-one messaging.
- Design **customer / partner / channel-reseller communications** — pre-close notice-and-consent obligations under change-of-control clauses in commercial contracts, customer-retention-call scripts for the top-20%-ARR accounts, partner communications sequenced by tier, integration-communications cadence, and the reference-request timing (customer references sit in the buy-side data room and are called during exclusivity — the customer must be pre-warned).
- Design **press and analyst strategy** — press-release drafting against Regulation FD (fair-disclosure) rules, embargo choreography with tier-1 outlets (WSJ, NYT, Bloomberg, FT, Reuters, TechCrunch, The Information), the quiet-period compliance regime for an IPO issuer, activist / short-seller / journalist inbound handling, and the pattern of holding the sell-side story and the buy-side story separate.
- Design a **management incentive plan (MIP)** and executive retention package — the MIP-cash-vs-rollover-equity split for a PE take-private, executive retention agreements (retention bonus, single-trigger vs. double-trigger acceleration, IRC 280G gross-up policy — controversial post-Dodd-Frank), the 280G shareholder-vote process for private companies under IRC 280G(b)(5), and the interaction with IRC 409A payment-timing constraints.
- Manage **WARN Act compliance in transaction-related workforce reductions** — the federal 60-day notice for 50-or-more-affected employees at a single site (100+ headcount employer), state mini-WARN acts (California WARN's 75-employee threshold, NY WARN's 25-employee threshold and 90-day notice, NJ WARN's 90-day notice-and-severance rule as of 2023, WI WARN, MA WARN), the deal-team obligation to identify workforce actions pre-close, and the successor-liability pattern in an asset deal.
- Manage **works-council and national-security-review stakeholder notifications** — EU / French / German / Dutch works-council information-and-consultation obligations, EU EMR / UK CMA / Chinese SAMR merger-control communications, and CFIUS mitigation-agreement negotiation with the target's national-security-relevant stakeholders.

## How to work through this module

Pick a specific in-flight or hypothetical transaction — sell-side M&A to a strategic acquirer, PE take-private of a public company, IPO with a management-transition, or a cross-border deal with an EU works council — with a specific headcount, specific site footprint, specific top-customer concentration, specific executive team, and specific cap-table. Freeze the profile at the start of exercise 01 and carry it through.

Read chapters 1–2 as a unit — they install the employee-communications regime that everything else attaches to. Chapter 3 layers on customer and partner communications; chapter 4 layers on press and analyst strategy. Chapters 5–7 install the transaction-specific compensation-and-workforce overlays: MIP design, WARN compliance, and cross-border stakeholder notifications.

Do each exercise while the matching chapter is fresh. The exercises are compounding — the clean-team roster you draft in exercise 01 is what the announcement-day sequencing in exercise 02 depends on; the customer-tier map in exercise 03 is what the press embargo in exercise 04 references; the MIP design in exercise 05 is what the executive-team retention conversations in exercise 02 rely on; the WARN analysis in exercise 06 shapes the workforce-action messaging that lands in the same all-hands as the announcement.
