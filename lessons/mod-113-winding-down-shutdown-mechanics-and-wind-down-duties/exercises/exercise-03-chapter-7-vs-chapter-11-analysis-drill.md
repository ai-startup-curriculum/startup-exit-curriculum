## Starter guidance

Three anti-patterns to avoid:

- **"Chapter 11 preserves optionality."** Only if specific going-concern value exists that a specific plan can preserve. For venture-backed shutdowns with limited going-concern value, Chapter 11 is specifically expensive process for no specific preserved optionality beyond the specific §363 "free and clear" machinery — which is a specific benefit, not "optionality."
- **"Chapter 7 is simpler than Chapter 11."** Specifically true in a sense but specifically misleading. Chapter 7 is specifically simpler because the specific trustee runs the specific liquidation and the specific debtor-and-management are specifically displaced; the specific displacement is specifically a cost (specific trustee commission; specific loss of control; specific exposure to specific avoidance-action pursuit). The specific choice between Chapter 7 and Chapter 11 is specifically not a complexity choice but a specific control-versus-cost trade-off.
- **"ABC and Chapter 7 are equivalent."** Specifically wrong in multiple dimensions. ABC is state-law, no automatic stay, no "free and clear" §363(f) machinery, no court-appointed trustee investigation of insider transactions. Chapter 7 is federal, automatic stay, trustee-driven, with specific avoidance-action powers. The specific choice between them is a specific function of specific creditor-collection threats, specific IP-encumbrance shape, and specific pre-shutdown-transaction cleanliness.

Three operational suggestions:

- **Interrogate the specific "free and clear" requirement.** The specific §363(f) "free and clear" machinery is often the specific single reason to choose Chapter 11 over ABC. Specifically check with specific buyers whether they specifically require §363 title or whether ABC transfer is specifically acceptable. The specific answer determines the specific mechanism.
- **Walk the specific first-day motion economics.** For a Chapter 11, the specific first-day motions can specifically preserve specific operational continuity (specific critical vendors; specific employee wages; specific cash management). The specific gap between a specific prepared Chapter 11 filing and a specific scrambled one is specific weeks of specific operational disruption.
- **Read a specific recent Chapter 11 docket on PACER.** The specific docket for a specific recent technology-company Chapter 11 with a §363 sale (e.g., a specific SaaS company; a specific hardware startup) is specifically illuminating about the specific mechanics, the specific professional-fee pattern, and the specific creditor dynamics.
