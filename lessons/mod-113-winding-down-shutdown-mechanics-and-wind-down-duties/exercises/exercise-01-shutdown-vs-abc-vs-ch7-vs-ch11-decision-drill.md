# exercise-01: Shutdown vs. ABC vs. Chapter 7 vs. Chapter 11 Decision Drill

**Estimated effort:** 4 hours

## Objective

Take a specific shutting-down company — a portfolio company you are advising, a company you lead that is approaching the shutdown decision, or a realistic hypothetical you build for this module — and produce a defensible **shutdown-mechanism recommendation memo** that walks the five-mechanism framework (informal wind-down, sale-for-parts, Assignment for the Benefit of Creditors, Chapter 7, Chapter 11) against the six axes (cost, speed, creditor treatment, D&O exposure, employee outcomes, IP disposition), identifies the governing mechanism for the specific facts, and sets up the subsequent exercises in this module. By the end of the exercise you should be able to answer, in a specific scenario, three questions in writing: (a) which mechanism is the right one, (b) why the other four are not, and (c) which pre-shutdown workstreams must be completed before the mechanism can be executed.

## Background

This exercise covers material from:

- [Chapter 1 — The Shutdown Decision Framework](../01-shutdown-decision-framework.md)

You do not yet need the ABC execution depth (chapter 2), the Chapter 7 / Chapter 11 depth (chapter 3), or the WARN / employee / IP / investor / D&O / personal-liability detail (chapters 4-9). The point of this exercise is to install the specific framework before the specific mechanism choice is made, so that the subsequent exercises operationalise the specific recommendation this exercise produces.

## Tasks

### 1. The five-mechanism comparison matrix (6-9 pages)

Produce a 5x6 matrix — five mechanisms × six axes — scored for your specific profile. For each cell:

- The specific score (quantitative where possible: dollar amount, week count, percentage recovery estimate; qualitative where not: HIGH / MEDIUM / LOW with a sentence of reasoning).
- The specific fact in your profile that drives the score.
- The specific uncertainty (what would need to change about the profile for the score to move).

Axes to score:
- **Cost** — assignee / trustee / DIP-professional fees; counsel fees; court fees; officer-time (hours).
- **Speed** — weeks from decision to substantial completion; tail period for claims resolution.
- **Creditor treatment** — expected recovery percentage by creditor class.
- **D&O exposure** — qualitative LOW / MEDIUM / HIGH / UNACCEPTABLE with the specific exposure vector.
- **Employee outcomes** — WARN compliance; final-pay compliance; PTO payout; COBRA continuation; severance pattern.
- **IP disposition** — flexibility; speed; "free and clear" availability; insider-purchase feasibility.

### 2. The decision-tree walk (2-3 pages)

Walk the six-question decision tree from chapter 1 against your specific facts, in order:

1. **Is the company solvent?** Document the specific solvency analysis (balance-sheet and cash-flow). If solvent, consider informal wind-down. If not, informal wind-down is off the table.
2. **Is there going-concern value a Chapter 11 plan could preserve?** Document the specific going-concern analysis. For venture-backed shutdowns the answer is typically no; if yes, Chapter 11 reorganisation is on the table.
3. **Is there a specific §363-sale advantage that only Chapter 11 provides?** Document the specific buyer requirements and the specific "free and clear" need. If yes, Chapter 11 as §363 vehicle. If no, less expensive mechanisms are preferred.
4. **Are the pre-shutdown transactions clean enough for a self-managed mechanism?** Document the specific pre-shutdown transactions in the 90-day / 1-year / 2-year windows and the specific exposure.
5. **Is there a specific creditor constituency that requires the automatic stay?** Document the specific threatened collections and whether they can be managed informally.
6. **What is the jurisdiction?** Document the specific state ABC framework (California vs. Delaware vs. other), the specific bankruptcy-venue analysis, and the specific jurisdictional-advantage factors.

For each question, produce a specific "yes / no / tbd" answer tied to specific facts, and a specific implication for the mechanism ranking.

### 3. The recommendation memo (2-3 pages)

Reduce the matrix and the decision-tree analysis to a specific recommendation memo addressed to the specific board. The memo should contain:

- **Executive summary** (3-5 bullets): the recommended mechanism, the key reasoning in one sentence per bullet.
- **The specific recommendation** — one of: informal wind-down, sale-for-parts under ABC, sale-for-parts under Chapter 11 §363, ABC without sale-for-parts, Chapter 7, Chapter 11 reorganisation.
- **Why this mechanism** — the specific axis scores that favor the recommendation.
- **Why not the other four** — one paragraph per rejected mechanism explaining the specific reason the mechanism is not selected for these specific facts.
- **Pre-shutdown workstreams required** — the specific items that must be completed before the mechanism can be executed (D&O tail procurement; pay-order memo; pre-shutdown cleanup of specific insider transactions; sale-for-parts marketing timeline; secured-creditor consent discussions; employee-notice workstream per WARN; investor communications per the LP-report schedule).
- **Timeline** — the specific weeks-from-today schedule for the pre-shutdown workstreams and the specific mechanism execution.
- **Specific risks** — the specific risks of the recommended mechanism and the specific mitigation strategy.

### 4. The pre-shutdown workstream checklist (1 page)

Produce an operational checklist of the specific workstreams the recommendation memo identifies. Each item should have:

- Specific deliverable (document, meeting, decision).
- Specific owner (CEO, CFO, GC, outside counsel, broker, assignee candidate).
- Specific deadline (relative to the mechanism-execution date).
- Specific status (ready / in-flight / blocked / open).

The checklist is what the shutdown-workstream meeting reviews weekly.

### 5. The counsel-engagement letter (1 page outline)

Outline the specific engagement letter to the specific restructuring counsel. The outline should name:

- The specific counsel (name a specific firm with the specific restructuring practice for the specific jurisdiction).
- The specific scope (shutdown mechanism recommendation; execution of the recommended mechanism; related workstreams).
- The specific deliverables (pay-order memo; mechanism-specific execution documents; coordination with ABC assignee or trustee or DIP counsel).
- The specific fee structure (hourly; capped; retainer).
- The specific team (specific partner lead; specific associate; specific paralegal).
- The specific timeline.

## Starter guidance

Three anti-patterns to avoid:

- **"Chapter 7 is the formal way to close."** Chapter 7 is one of five mechanisms, and for a venture-backed company with modest residual assets and clean pre-shutdown transactions it is often the wrong one — ABC is typically faster, cheaper, and delivers more to creditors. Starting the analysis with "we file Chapter 7" skips the framework.
- **"We should preserve optionality with Chapter 11."** Chapter 11 is expensive process. The specific optionality it preserves is the ability to confirm a plan of reorganisation — which requires going-concern value most shutdown candidates do not have. Name the specific §363-sale advantage that specifically justifies Chapter 11 over cheaper mechanisms. "Optionality" without a specific preserved option is a reason to spend money that should go to creditors.
- **"Informal wind-down is cheapest so we should default to it."** Only if solvent. An informal wind-down of an insolvent company produces specific fiduciary-duty exposure, specific fraudulent-transfer exposure, and specific involuntary-bankruptcy exposure that specifically dwarfs the cost savings. Solvency is the gate; without it, the informal wind-down is not an option, not a cheaper option.

Three operational suggestions:

- **Freeze the profile.** The analysis changes materially with every $500K of residual assets, every ten employees, every month of expected timeline. If you let the profile drift during the analysis you will produce a defence of whatever recommendation you were leaning toward, not a defensible recommendation. Freeze the profile at the top and reference it as the specific data in each axis score.
- **Score the axes separately before combining.** A tempting failure mode is to pick the recommendation first and score the axes to support it. Score each axis independently (cost; speed; creditor treatment; D&O exposure; employee outcomes; IP disposition) on each mechanism, then observe which mechanism dominates and which trade-offs the recommendation is making.
- **Name the trade-off explicitly.** The recommendation is almost always a trade-off — ABC gives up the §363 "free and clear" machinery for speed and cost savings; Chapter 11 gives up speed and cost savings for the §363 machinery and the specific credibility of a court-supervised process. State the specific trade-off the recommendation makes and the specific facts that justify it.

## Acceptance criteria

You can demonstrate that:

- The shutdown profile is filled in with specific facts (not placeholder values).
- The matrix walks every mechanism against every axis with specific scores and specific reasoning.
- The decision-tree walk resolves each question with a specific answer tied to specific facts.
- The recommendation memo identifies a specific mechanism and defends it against the other four.
- The pre-shutdown checklist is operational — a counsel reading it knows which document to draft, which meeting to schedule, which decision to request from the board.
- The counsel-engagement outline is specific — a specific firm with a specific restructuring practice, a specific scope, specific deliverables.
- A critical reader (board chair, outside counsel, peer CFO) could disagree with a specific conclusion and see the specific evidence you used.

## Stretch goals

- **Mechanism-drift analysis.** Build a specific sensitivity analysis: for your profile, what change in facts (residual assets fall 50%; a specific buyer emerges; a specific creditor files suit) moves the recommendation to a different mechanism? Produce a 1-page sensitivity table.
- **Comparable-shutdown case study.** Find a specific public shutdown in the past 36 months — a public filing of a Chapter 7, a published Delaware §275 dissolution, an ABC announcement from Sherwood Partners / DSI / SierraConstellation / Armanino / FTI Consulting — and write a 1-page "what would our framework have predicted for them, and what did they actually do" memo.
- **Jurisdictional comparison.** If the company is multi-state or has operations in a specific non-California / non-Delaware jurisdiction, produce a specific comparison of the specific ABC framework (or specific state-law dissolution mechanism) in that jurisdiction.
