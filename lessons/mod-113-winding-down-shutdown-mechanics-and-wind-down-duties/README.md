# mod-113 — Winding Down: Shutdown Mechanics & Wind-Down Duties

**Estimated effort:** 22 hours
**Stage:** Any (the framework is installed once and re-read whenever the shutdown question becomes live — typically at a runway-cliff, a failed round, a failed sale process, or a covenant-breach)
**Seat:** founder-CEO, CFO, GC, board chair, lead independent director, restructuring counsel, restructuring advisor

This module installs the **shutdown-and-wind-down overlay** — the discipline that lets a founder-CEO, CFO, and GC face the end-of-company decision from a position of process rather than panic. Every venture-backed company runs a non-zero probability that the endgame it realises is the "responsible shutdown" leg of the [`mod-101`](../mod-101-endgame-strategy-and-optionality-design/README.md) frame rather than one of the acquisition / IPO / secondary / stay-independent legs. When that leg becomes live the founder-CEO and the board have four to twelve weeks to make a small number of high-consequence decisions — which shutdown mechanism (informal wind-down vs. sale-for-parts vs. Assignment for the Benefit of Creditors vs. Chapter 7 liquidation vs. Chapter 11 reorganisation), which assignee or trustee, which employees get notice on which timeline under WARN, how the intellectual-property estate is disposed of, how the D&O tail is procured, and which of the officer-personal-liability triggers (IRC §6672 trust-fund taxes, PAGA, WARN Act, FLSA / state wage-and-hour, ERISA) are being watched — and every decision closes doors that cannot be reopened.

The frame is *decision discipline under a compressed timeline with unforgiving liability rules*, not "how to run a restructuring practice." The practitioner canon here — the American Bankruptcy Institute materials, the Sherwood Partners / Development Specialists / SierraConstellation / Armanino / FTI Consulting published methodology on Assignments for the Benefit of Creditors, Cooley GO's "Winding Down Your Startup" and NVCA / Wilson Sonsini / Latham / Fenwick / Gunderson practitioner memos, the US Department of Labor WARN Act guidance and the state Employment Development Department mini-WARN references, the SEC filings archive of Chapter 11 debtors-in-possession, and the Delaware Court of Chancery and US Bankruptcy Court opinions that shape fiduciary-duty-of-a-zone-of-insolvency and Section 363 sale-process discipline — converges on a small number of concrete disciplines this module installs. The point is not to substitute for counsel; the point is to arrive at counsel *already knowing the shape of the decisions on the table* so that counsel's time is spent on judgment rather than education.

> Reminder: this module is education, not legal, tax, restructuring, employment, insurance, or accounting advice. Every live shutdown engages a specific set of advisors — a specific restructuring counsel, a specific ABC assignee or (for Chapter 7 / 11) bankruptcy counsel, a specific employment counsel on WARN and wage-and-hour, a specific insurance broker on the D&O tail, a specific tax advisor on the trust-fund-tax and cancellation-of-indebtedness questions, and a specific IP counsel on the disposition question. Follow the counsel-imposed regime; this module describes the decision discipline that sits underneath it.

## Learning objectives

By the end of this module you should be able to:

- Apply the **shutdown decision framework** — informal wind-down vs. sale-for-parts vs. Assignment for the Benefit of Creditors vs. Chapter 7 vs. Chapter 11 — comparing cost, speed, creditor treatment, director-and-officer exposure, employee outcomes, and IP disposition; and defend the choice against a specific company profile (residual assets, secured debt, employee count, jurisdiction, buyer interest).
- Execute an **Assignment for the Benefit of Creditors (ABC)** — the assignee-selection decision (Sherwood Partners in CA, Development Specialists Inc., SierraConstellation, Armanino, FTI Consulting), the assignment-agreement mechanics, the notice-to-creditors-and-stockholders choreography, the asset-disposition process, the priority-of-claims waterfall, the California CCP §493 interaction for California ABCs, the Delaware trust-law alternative, and the ABC-as-precursor-to-a-Section-363-sale pattern.
- Analyse **Chapter 7 vs. Chapter 11** — the automatic-stay-and-trustee-controlled-liquidation shape of Ch 7, the debtor-in-possession-plan-of-reorganisation shape of Ch 11, the pre-packaged Ch 11, the Section 363 sale process, and the Chapter 15 cross-border alternative — and defend the choice given the residual going-concern value and secured-creditor posture.
- Manage **WARN Act compliance in a shutdown** — the federal 60-day notice, the state mini-WARN variations (California, New York, New Jersey, Illinois, Minnesota, and others), the "faltering-company" and "unforeseeable business circumstance" exceptions and their tight construction, the successor-liability pattern in an asset sale, and the standard damages / settlement pattern.
- Manage **employee obligations in a shutdown** — final-pay obligations (state-specific), PTO payout (state-specific), COBRA administration, WARN notice, severance design, and the enforceability of non-competes and non-disclosures post-2024 FTC non-compete-ban and its litigation.
- Manage **IP disposition** — sell the IP to a strategic buyer, assign it to investors or founders under a pre-existing agreement, abandon it (with the cost implications of forfeited patent-maintenance fees), license it back to the founders or a spin-out, or transfer it to a special-purpose vehicle for later monetisation.
- Manage **investor communications** — the LP-report obligations of the VC firm, the distribution-vs-wind-up choice for remaining cash under the liquidation-preference stack, and the founders-and-investors alignment pattern on shutdown timing.
- Purchase **D&O tail coverage** — the 6-year run-off pattern, the tail-policy premium sizing, and the coverage that responds to post-close claims (securities fraud, breach of fiduciary duty, wrongful-termination, WARN, wage-and-hour) after the entity has dissolved.
- Manage the **officer-and-director personal-liability watch** — IRC §6672 trust-fund taxes, PAGA in California, WARN Act personal liability, FLSA / state wage-and-hour, ERISA fiduciary liability, and the "pay trust-fund taxes and wages first" pattern that applies even when general unsecured creditors will not be paid in full.

## How to work through this module

Pick a specific shutdown scenario — real (a portfolio company that recently wound down, a company you are advising through the decision, or a hypothetical you build against a real cap-table shape you have access to) or a realistic hypothetical you build for this module — and carry it through every exercise. Freeze the profile at the top of exercise-01 (jurisdiction, employee count and locations, secured-vs-unsecured-debt shape, residual assets including IP, remaining cash relative to preference stack, buyer interest if any) and do not let it drift. The module is worth exactly what the concreteness of your scenario is worth; a shutdown scenario with vague numbers produces a vague shutdown plan.

Read chapter 1 and do exercise-01 first — the shutdown decision framework is the analytic spine every subsequent chapter and exercise sits on top of. Read chapters 2 and 3 and do exercises 02 and 03 in parallel; the ABC mechanics and the Chapter 7 / Chapter 11 mechanics are the two most-used shutdown branches for venture-backed companies and both should be understood in their concrete detail before you commit to a recommendation. Read chapters 4 and 5 and do exercises 04 and 05; the employee-side obligations are the highest-frequency source of officer personal liability in a shutdown and the most likely source of a post-close plaintiff-firm inbound. Read chapter 6 and do exercise-06; the IP-disposition decision is often the highest-consequence single decision inside the shutdown because it is irreversible. Read chapter 7 as context for the investor-alignment conversation that runs alongside the whole workstream. Read chapter 8 and do exercise-07 as early as possible in the timeline — the D&O tail must be procured *before* the policy lapses at the entity's dissolution, and post-facto procurement is not available. Read chapter 9 and do exercise-08 last as the synthesis; the officer personal-liability watch is what makes the whole workstream a *personal* decision for the CEO / CFO / GC, not just a corporate decision.
