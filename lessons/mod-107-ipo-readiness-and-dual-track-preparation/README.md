# mod-107 — IPO Readiness & Dual-Track Preparation

**Estimated effort:** 28 hours
**Stage:** MATURE
**Seat:** founder-CEO, CFO, GC, SVP-Corp-Dev, audit-committee chair, IPO counsel

This module installs the transaction-execution work that sits between the mature-stage private company and the priced IPO. The company has cleared the readiness bar (or is running the assessment to see whether it clears); the board has authorised the workstream; and the specific execution — the S-1 registration statement, the PCAOB-registered audit, the SOX 404 internal-control programme, the JOBS Act Emerging Growth Company election, the dual-track M&A + IPO governance, the NYSE / Nasdaq listing-standard graduation, and the EDGAR filing choreography — is what this module owns. Once the S-1 is effective, mod-108 takes over the underwriter bake-off, roadshow, pricing, greenshoe, lock-ups, and IPO alternatives.

Every workstream in this module is intensely rule-bound. Regulation S-K (Items 10 through 512) governs S-1 disclosure; Regulation S-X (Article 5 and cross-referenced Articles) governs the financial-statement footprint; Section 404 of the Sarbanes-Oxley Act governs the internal-control programme; Section 6(e) of the Securities Act (as amended by the JOBS Act) governs the confidential-DRS submission and the 15-day-before-roadshow public-filing rule; SEC Rule 10A-3 governs audit-committee independence at listing; Nasdaq Rule 5000 series and the NYSE Listed Company Manual govern initial-listing standards. The CFO / GC who reads the workstream without reading the specific rulebooks behind each piece is a CFO / GC who signs an SEC comment letter later.

> Reminder: this module is education, not legal / tax / accounting / investment advice. A live IPO engages SEC-registered underwriters, PCAOB-registered auditors, qualified securities counsel, and specialist tax / SOX / IR / compensation advisors. The disclosure-liability exposures under Section 11 of the Securities Act, Section 12(a)(2), and Rule 10b-5 are personal-liability exposures for officers and directors, and they attach whether or not the founder read the rule.

## Learning objectives

By the end of this module you should be able to:

- Apply the going-public decision framework — the specific quantitative bar (roughly $150M+ ARR for SaaS with sector variation, 30%+ YoY growth, Rule of 40, gross-margin band, NRR, revenue concentration), the qualitative bar (Series C+ operating maturity, IPO-experienced CFO / GC, board and audit committee readiness, IR bandwidth), the market-window read (Meritech, Bessemer, PitchBook, Renaissance Capital), and the board choreography that turns the assessment into a documented decision.
- Author an S-1 registration statement across the Item 101 business, Item 105 risk factors, Item 303 MD&A, Item 402 executive-comp CD&A, Item 404 related-party transactions, Item 407 corporate-governance, and the Regulation S-X financial-statement footprint — with a defensible drafting workflow, a specific management-of-the-comment-letter cycle, and a legally-defensible risk-factor set that survives Section 11 diligence.
- Engage a PCAOB-registered auditor and prepare the 2-year audit history — the Big Four vs. next-tier selection decision, the Reg S-X independence rules, the audit-partner rotation, the SAS 100 interim review, the PCAOB inspection quality signal, and the audit-committee choreography that engages the auditor.
- Run a Sarbanes-Oxley internal-control readiness programme — Section 404(a) management assessment scoping, Section 404(b) auditor attestation scoping (deferred for non-accelerated filers and for EGCs), ICFR design and testing under COSO 2013, deficiency classification (deficiency / significant deficiency / material weakness), the remediation programme, and the interaction with the 5-business-day close discipline from `startup-finance-fundraising-curriculum` mod-111.
- Elect and manage JOBS Act EGC status — the definition, the 5-year on-ramp, the specific early-termination triggers, the scaled-disclosure elections (2-year audited financials instead of 3, reduced executive-comp disclosure, no SOX 404(b) auditor attestation, deferred adoption of new accounting standards under Section 107(b)), and the confidential-submission and testing-the-waters privileges.
- Structure the dual-track M&A + IPO process — the parallel-workstream governance (special-committee vs. full-board decision, banker-conflict analysis across M&A-banker vs. IPO-underwriter roles, price-signalling risk from a live sell-side alongside a public filing), the go / no-go decision points, and the pattern of using IPO-readiness leverage in a sell-side negotiation.
- Graduate to NYSE / Nasdaq listing-standard governance — the initial-listing standards (Nasdaq Rule 5000 series, NYSE Listed Company Manual), the corporate-governance rules (majority-independent board, fully-independent audit committee under SEC Rule 10A-3, independent comp committee, independent director selection for related-party-transaction approval), the insider-trading policy and 10b5-1 framework, the code of business conduct and ethics, and the whistleblower / clawback / ESG / cybersecurity-disclosure additions post-2023 SEC rules.
- Choreograph the EDGAR filing workflow — the confidential DRS submission under Section 6(e), amendments, public filing, pre-effective amendments, effectiveness determination, Form 8-A registration for Exchange Act reporting, and the post-IPO 10-K / 10-Q / 8-K / DEF 14A cadence.

## Ownership boundary

- **Ongoing-company finance-function design** — the month-end close, controller and accounting-manager staffing, R&D tax credit and sales-tax nexus, payroll-and-benefits, FP&A / rolling-forecast discipline, cap-table administration, ongoing-company treasury — is owned by `startup-finance-fundraising-curriculum` mod-111 and never re-taught here. This module owns the IPO-readiness transaction-execution work that sits on top of that functioning finance function. A company that arrives at the going-public decision without the finance function in place is running the transaction and the finance-function build in parallel, which is expensive and slow.
- **Roadshow, pricing, allocation, greenshoe, and IPO-alternatives execution** is owned by mod-108. This module gets the S-1 effective and the org through the listing-governance graduation; mod-108 takes over the marketing and pricing.
- **Transaction-governance and fiduciary-duty analysis for the IPO** — special-committee construction for a controlled-company IPO, fairness-opinion sourcing where required (rare in a straight IPO, common in dual-track deals converging on a sale), banker-conflict analysis under Rural Metro and Del Monte, ISS / Glass Lewis engagement — is owned by mod-109.
- **Transaction-communications strategy** — pre-and-post-filing employee messaging, customer / partner / press choreography, Reg FD compliance for the public-company launch, gun-jumping compliance during the offering window (covered at mechanics depth in mod-108 chapter 2) — is owned by mod-110.
- **Post-transaction personal-wealth planning** — QSBS preservation through the IPO under IRC §1202, Rule 10b5-1 trading plans for the newly-public founder, exchange-fund alternatives for lock-up-expiry diversification — is owned by mod-114.
- **AI-safety-technical content of an AI-usage disclosure** defers to `head-of-ai-governance-learning`; the **security-engineering content of a cybersecurity-disclosure** defers to `security-learning`. This module covers the specific SEC disclosure obligations under Item 106 of Reg S-K (cybersecurity risk management, strategy, governance, and incident disclosure) and the mechanics of the Form 8-K Item 1.05 material-incident disclosure, but not the security-engineering programme itself.

## How to work through this module

Pick a specific pre-IPO company (real, in-flight, or a realistic hypothetical you carry through the module) with a specific sector, ARR, growth rate, cap-table shape, and geographic footprint. Every exercise is worth what the specificity of the company is worth. Freeze the profile at the start of exercise 01 and carry it through.

Read a chapter, then do the matching exercise while the frame is fresh. Chapter 1 installs the decision framework; chapters 2–4 install the drafting / audit / SOX regimes that occupy the bulk of the pre-filing workload; chapter 5 installs the EGC scaled-disclosure elections that materially affect chapters 2 and 4; chapter 6 installs the dual-track structure; chapters 7–8 install the listing-governance and EDGAR mechanics that transition the company into the post-effective public-company regime.
