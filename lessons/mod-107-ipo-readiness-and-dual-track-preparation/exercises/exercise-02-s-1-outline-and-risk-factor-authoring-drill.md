# exercise-02: S-1 Outline and Risk-Factor Authoring Drill

**Estimated effort:** 5 hours

## Objective

Take the frozen pre-IPO company profile you carried through exercise-01 and produce a Section 11-defensible S-1 outline plus a specific set of drafted risk factors. By the end you should be able to hand an issuer-counsel senior associate an outline that maps every Regulation S-K item to a drafting owner, a specific source-and-cite plan, and a specific EDGAR calendar — and hand an underwriter-counsel partner a risk-factor draft that survives the specificity test on a due-diligence Q&A read-through.

## Background

This exercise covers material from:

- [Chapter 2 — S-1 Registration Statement Authoring — Reg S-K, Reg S-X, and the SEC Comment-Letter Cycle](../02-s-1-registration-statement-authoring.md)

You do not yet need the PCAOB auditor engagement mechanics (chapter 3), the SOX 404 programme design (chapter 4), the EGC scaled-disclosure elections in depth (chapter 5 — you will reference the EGC vs. non-EGC footprint but not the full election decision), the dual-track M&A + IPO structure (chapter 6), the listing-standard governance graduation (chapter 7), or the post-effective EDGAR filing cadence for 10-K / 10-Q / 8-K / DEF 14A / Form 4 (chapter 8). The Form 8-A Exchange Act registration is noted in the calendar but not re-taught here. The point of this exercise is to install the specific discipline of building the S-1 as a Section 11-defensible paper trail across every Reg S-K item, every Reg S-X requirement, and every SEC-staff comment vector — not to re-derive the substantive rules.

> Reminder: this module is education, not legal / accounting / tax / investment advice. A live S-1 is drafted under qualified issuer counsel with PCAOB-registered auditor sign-off and underwriter-counsel diligence, with each item-disclosure decision made against current SEC rule text and staff guidance.

## Tasks

### 1. Org-meeting-to-effectiveness workstream sequence

Produce a specific 4–9-month workstream calendar from board go-decision to effectiveness. Assume the org meeting kicks off 2–4 weeks after the go-decision from exercise-01.

- Org meeting attendee list with specific role and firm placeholder — issuer counsel (name the firm from the practitioner-canon list), underwriter counsel, PCAOB auditor (engagement partner, senior manager, technical-accounting specialist), lead-left ECM team and co-managers, and financial printer account executive.
- Drafting-assignment matrix — which working-group member owns first drafts of Business (Item 101), Risk Factors (Item 105), MD&A (Item 303), Executive Compensation (Item 402), Financial Statements (Reg S-X), and each material-contract exhibit under Item 601.
- Target dates — DRS submission, first SEC comment letter (assume ~25–30 calendar days post-submission), response and DRS/A rounds (assume 3–5 rounds), public flip (governed by JOBS Act §6(e)(1) 15-day rule for EGCs — see task 7), roadshow launch, pricing, closing.
- Recurring cadence — all-hands drafting sessions per week, working-group calls per day, printer-proof cycles per amendment.

### 2. Item-by-item Reg S-K coverage outline

Produce an outline of the S-1 with a specific Reg S-K item mapped to each section. For each item note (a) the specific drafting owner from task 1, (b) the specific source input (audited financials, internal SaaS-metrics workbook, HR system, cap-table administrator, litigation docket, board minutes), and (c) any specific EGC-scaling election that changes the footprint.

Sections to cover, with the specific item citation:

- **Prospectus Summary** and **Summary of Risk Factors** (two-page lead required by Item 105 where the risk-factor section exceeds 15 pages).
- **Risk Factors** — Item 105.
- **Use of Proceeds** — Item 504.
- **Dilution** — Item 506.
- **Capitalization**.
- **Selected Financial Data** and **MD&A** — Item 303, including results-of-operations driver analysis, reasonably-likely-trend disclosure under Item 303(b)(2)(ii), liquidity and capital resources, critical accounting estimates (call out ASC 606 revenue-recognition estimates and ASC 718 cheap-stock explicitly), and Reg G / Item 10(e) non-GAAP reconciliations with equal-or-greater-prominence GAAP presentation.
- **Business** — Item 101, including the five-year history and the Item 101(c)(2)(ii) human-capital-resources disclosure.
- **Management** — Item 401.
- **Executive Compensation** — Item 402, with an explicit **EGC-scaled vs. full** option (2-year Summary Compensation Table, top-three NEOs, CD&A omission for EGCs; full CD&A, 3-year table, top-five NEOs, and Item 402(v) pay-versus-performance for non-EGCs).

- **Description of Capital Stock** — Item 202, including anti-takeover charter and bylaw provisions and the exclusive-forum provision (Delaware Chancery for internal-affairs claims and, per **Salzberg v. Sciabacucchi**, federal district court for Securities Act claims where charter so provides).

### 4. Risk-factor authoring drill

Draft 15–25 principles-based, plain-English risk factors under Item 105 (2020 amendments) for your specific company. Group under specific descriptive headings, not the generic "Risks Related to Our Business" catch-all.

- Grouping — at minimum: **industry and market**, **business and operational**, **financial**, **regulatory and legal**, **intellectual property**, **cybersecurity** (with the Item 106 governance tie), **AI and model** where applicable, **governance** (controlled-company disclosure, dual-class or multi-class if applicable), and **common stock** (volatility, no-dividend intent, anti-takeover charter and bylaw provisions, DGCL §203 posture, exclusive-forum provision).
- Length — target a section that stays inside the Item 105 15-page soft cap. If your draft exceeds 15 pages, include a two-page **Summary of Risk Factors** lead per Item 105 and per Rule 421 plain-English drafting.
- Specific-facts discipline — every risk factor names the specific mechanism (specific competitor, specific hyperscaler dependency, specific regulator, specific customer-segment or margin impact) and ties to a source in the task 5 binder. Ban generic language: "we operate in a competitive market", "our business is subject to regulation", "we may not be able to attract and retain qualified personnel" are unacceptable without specific-facts qualification.
- Anti-generic checklist — produce a one-page checklist your drafting team runs each risk factor against before it gets circulated. At minimum the checklist should test for (a) specific-facts naming, (b) specific quantification where the underlying source supports it, (c) a specific tie to a source-and-cite entry, and (d) a rejected-generic-language flag.

### 5. Source-and-cite binder plan (Section 11(b) due-diligence defence)

Produce the binder plan supporting the Section 11(b) due-diligence defence under the **BarChris** framework — every material claim traces to an underlying source with a specific owner and a specific evidence pointer.

- Binder schema — for each material claim in the S-1: claim text, section reference, source type (audited statement, internal SaaS-metrics workbook, Gartner / IDC / Forrester report, database query, board minute, contract), source pointer (file path, report title and page, query name), source owner, review-and-tie-out status.
- Due-diligence Q&A schedule — a specific cadence of 2–4-hour sessions with management (CEO, CFO, GC, controller, sales leader, product leader) run by underwriter counsel, transcribed and preserved in the diligence file.
- Management representation letter — the specific letter management delivers to the underwriters at pricing, coordinated with the officers' certificate.
- Officers' certificate — the specific certificate delivered at pricing covering the accuracy of the S-1 at pricing.
- Comfort-letter interlock — the auditor's pricing and closing comfort letters providing negative assurance on interim-period changes and tick-and-tie on every S-1 number that traces to the accounting records; sequenced against the pricing and closing dates in task 1.
- Underwriter-facing coverage — call out the specific "watchdog" investigation posture the underwriters owe under BarChris beyond passive reliance on management representations.

### 6. Mock SEC first-round comment letter

Draft 12–20 plausible SEC Corporation Finance staff comments against your S-1 outline and risk-factor draft. Then draft response-letter language for a sample subset of 4–6 comments citing the specific disclosure change or the specific supplemental information provided.

Comment areas to cover (pick from these; you do not need every category, but cover at least six):

- **Cheap stock** — ASC 718 valuation of pre-IPO grants against the anticipated IPO price; incremental stock-compensation expense; disclosure in the critical-accounting-estimates and stock-compensation footnote.
- **Non-GAAP prominence** — Reg G and Item 10(e); equal-or-greater-prominence GAAP presentation; reconciliation completeness; prohibited-adjustment tests.
- **ASC 606 disaggregation** — revenue by product line, geography, contract duration, timing of recognition; interaction with the segment footnote under ASC 280.
- **MD&A driver quantification** — Item 303(b)(1)(i) requirement to quantify each material factor contributing to a line-item change.
- **Revenue concentration** — Item 101 10%+ customer disclosure; risk-factor specificity for the top customer.
- **Human-capital resources** — Item 101(c)(2)(ii) specificity beyond generic head-count disclosure.
- **Risk-factor specificity** — Item 105 principles-based standard; rejection of generic language.
- **Related-person transactions** — Item 404 completeness against the $120,000 threshold.
- **Segment reporting** — ASC 280 identification of the chief operating decision-maker and reportable-segment determination.

Response-letter format — numbered response to each comment, cross-reference to the marked S-1/A page and the specific added or revised disclosure, or the specific supplemental information provided.

### 7. EDGAR calendar

Produce a specific EDGAR calendar aligned to the workstream sequence from task 1.

- **DRS submission** under Section 6(e) of the Securities Act.
- **DRS/A rounds** — 3–5 iterations against the SEC comment cycle.
- **Public flip** — the transition from confidential DRS to publicly-filed S-1; for an EGC the flip must occur at least **15 days before the road show** under Securities Act §6(e)(1).
- **S-1/A pre-effective amendments** — post-flip amendments responding to remaining staff comments and to any interim-financial refresh under the Rule 3-12 staleness cliffs.
- **Section 8(a) acceleration request** — the specific acceleration request to the SEC staff, typically requesting effectiveness at a specific time (e.g., 4:00 PM ET the day before pricing).
- **Rule 424(b) prospectus** — the specific prospectus supplement filed at pricing under Rule 424(b)(1), (b)(3), or (b)(4) as applicable to the offering.
- **Form 8-A** — the Exchange Act registration effective at listing, filed alongside the S-1 effectiveness for exchange listing. Note the filing in the calendar; do not re-teach the mechanics (owned by chapter 8).

### 8. iXBRL tagging plan

Produce a specific iXBRL tagging plan under Reg S-T Rule 405.

- Taxonomy scope — the FASB **US-GAAP taxonomy** for the financial statements and the SEC's **DEI taxonomy** for cover-page tagging.
- Extension-element policy — the specific decision framework for when to use a taxonomy element vs. create a taxonomy extension, with accounting-and-taxonomy judgement documented for each extension.
- Printer coordination — the specific division of responsibility between the accounting team and the financial printer (DFIN / Toppan Merrill / Workiva) across taxonomy-element selection, tagging execution, validation, and pre-submission review.
- Validation cadence — pre-submission validation against the EDGAR Filer Manual and taxonomy-specific validation rules; tagging errors are a specific staff comment category and are worth defending against pre-submission.

## Starter guidance

Anti-patterns to avoid:

- **The generic-risk-factor default.** A risk section that reads "we operate in a competitive market", "we depend on key personnel", "we are subject to regulation" is a risk section a plaintiff-firm associate can rewrite as an omission theory in under an hour. Every risk factor names the specific mechanism, the specific counterparty or regulator, and the specific customer-segment or margin impact — or it is deleted.
- **The marketing-document business section.** A business section that reads like the roadshow deck without specific-source citation for every market-sizing claim, every "leadership" claim, and every customer statement is a business section that will collect Item 101 comments and, worse, a Section 11 claim if the aftermarket narrative diverges from the S-1 narrative. Every claim traces to a source in the task 5 binder.
- **The disclosed-but-not-explained MD&A trend.** An MD&A that flags a trend without the specific driver quantification required by Item 303(b)(1)(i) and without the specific reasonably-likely-trend analysis required by Item 303(b)(2)(ii) is the exact MD&A a plaintiff firm attacks post-drop. If management is discussing the trend in operating reviews, the trend is disclosed or the non-disclosure decision is documented in the diligence file.
- **The printer-owns-tagging fiction.** iXBRL tagging is not a printer clerical task; taxonomy-element selection and extension creation are accounting judgements. A tagging plan that treats the printer as the tagging owner without accounting-team review is a tagging plan that produces a Rule 405 comment.

- The additional company-profile fields are filled in with specific facts (no `TBD` or `TBC` in the frozen additions).
- The org-meeting-to-effectiveness calendar names a specific drafting owner for every Reg S-K section in the task 2 outline.

- The source-and-cite binder plan maps every material claim in the outline to a source, owner, and evidence pointer, and interlocks with the management representation letter, officers' certificate, and comfort-letter cadence.
