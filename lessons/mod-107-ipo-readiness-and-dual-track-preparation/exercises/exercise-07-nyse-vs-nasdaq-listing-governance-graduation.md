# exercise-07: NYSE vs. Nasdaq Listing-Governance Graduation

**Estimated effort:** 3 hours

## Objective

Take the frozen pre-IPO company profile you carried through exercise-01 and produce the specific listing-standard graduation package a CFO / GC / audit-committee chair would walk into a board meeting with 90-120 days before the anticipated S-1 effectiveness date. By the end you should be able to: (1) run the initial-listing-standards check against both Nasdaq Global Select Market (Rule 5405) and NYSE (Listed Company Manual §102.01A / §102.01B) with a specific PASS / MARGINAL / FAIL call per test, (2) deliver a defensible exchange recommendation with rationale, (3) show the majority-independent-board and Rule 10A-3 audit-committee build-out with a specific director-recruitment gap analysis, (4) present the corporate-governance policy suite (audit-committee charter, comp-committee charter, nom-gov-committee charter, insider-trading policy with 2024 disclosure, 10b5-1 framework, Rule 10D-1 clawback, code of ethics, related-party policy, whistleblower, cybersecurity), and (5) walk the ISS / Glass Lewis pre-effectiveness audit that flags proxy-recommendation exposure before the first post-IPO proxy season.

> Reminder: this module is education, not legal / accounting / tax advice. A live listing-governance graduation engages qualified securities counsel, the listing-exchange staff at NYSE Regulation or Nasdaq Listing Qualifications, PCAOB-registered auditor advisory input, specialist compensation counsel, and independent-director search firms.

## Background

This exercise covers material from:

- [Chapter 7 — NYSE / Nasdaq Listing-Standard Governance Graduation](../07-listing-standard-governance-graduation.md)

The exchange-selection and governance-graduation workstream interlocks tightly with the **Form 8-A** Exchange Act registration and the **Section 6(e)** confidential-DRS and public-filing mechanics covered in chapter 8 — the 8-A is the specific filing that formally registers the security for exchange trading once the S-1 is declared effective, and the exchange listing-application package is submitted and negotiated in parallel with the S-1 comment-letter cycle. This exercise does not re-teach chapter 8 EDGAR or Form 8-A mechanics — refer at the boundary. It also assumes the EGC decisions from chapter 5 (scaled disclosure, pay-vs-performance timing, SOX 404(b) deferral) are already frozen, and the SOX 404 programme from chapter 4 is running with a designated audit-committee-of-the-audit-committee owner.

The graduation is not a one-time deliverable. Every element authored here becomes a live artefact of the public-company operating system on day one: the audit-committee charter is filed as an S-1 exhibit and reviewed annually, the insider-trading policy is exhibited to the first Form 10-K under Item 408 of Reg S-K, the clawback policy is exhibited under Item 402(w), the 10b5-1 pipeline runs quarterly, and the ISS / Glass Lewis posture is retested at every proxy season.

## Tasks

### 1. Dual-exchange initial-listing-standards pro-forma check

Run the initial-listing-standards check against **both** Nasdaq Global Select Market and NYSE at the anticipated pricing-range midpoint. For each exchange, test each of the four financial-standards tests and the liquidity-and-distribution requirements. Deliver a specific **PASS / MARGINAL / FAIL** call per test with a specific numeric margin (headroom above / shortfall below the threshold).

**Nasdaq Global Select Market — Rule 5405** financial-standards tests:

- Earnings standard (aggregate pre-tax earnings threshold over prior three years; each of the two most recent years positive).
- Capitalisation-with-cash-flow standard (aggregate cash flow, average market cap, prior-year revenue thresholds).
- Capitalisation-with-revenue standard (average market cap and prior-year revenue thresholds).
- Assets-with-equity standard (total assets, stockholders' equity, market-cap thresholds).

Nasdaq liquidity-and-distribution requirements under Rule 5405:

- 1.25M+ publicly-held shares.
- $110M+ market value of publicly-held shares.
- 550+ round-lot holders OR 2,200+ total holders.
- $4.00+ minimum bid price.
- 3+ registered and active market makers.

**NYSE — Listed Company Manual §102.01A** general equity-listing standards financial tests:

- Earnings test (aggregate pre-tax earnings over three years; two most recent years positive).
- Global market capitalisation test.
- Assets and equity test.
- Affiliated-company test (for spin-offs — mark N/A with rationale for a standalone IPO).

NYSE §102.01A distribution and size requirements:

- 400+ round-lot holders.
- 1.1M+ publicly-held shares.
- $40M+ aggregate market value of publicly-held shares for IPOs.
- $4.00+ per-share price at listing.
- DMM assignment.

Note whether **NYSE §102.01B** IPO-specific variants (including the direct-listing accommodation) would apply to your specific listing structure, and mark accordingly.

Deliver a per-exchange summary: does the company clear each threshold with margin, marginally, or fail? Where the call is MARGINAL, describe the specific sensitivity (which pricing / share-count assumption would push the test into FAIL). Where you rely on a specific dollar or share threshold that you cannot cite from the live rulebook at authoring time, insert an inline `<!-- needs-research: confirm specific threshold under Rule 5405 / §102.01A as of the current rulebook -->` marker rather than inventing a number.

### 2. Qualitative exchange decision and recommendation

The financial-standards check is table stakes at the scale contemplated by this module; the decision is the qualitative overlay. Score each of the following on a specific 1-5 scale with rationale, then deliver a specific exchange recommendation.

- **Branding and identity narrative fit.** Which exchange's public identity aligns with the company's positioning as of the S-1 filing?
- **Sector-peer fit.** Map 10-20 comparable public peers (recent IPOs in the same sub-sector, plus the more mature reference set the sell-side coverage will benchmark you against) and their exchange listings. Which exchange has the specific peer density?
- **Initial and annual listing-fee schedule.** Compare the Nasdaq tiered fee schedule and the NYSE tiered schedule (including the NYSE cap for large listings) at the specific anticipated market-cap floor and float. Use `<!-- needs-research: specific fee schedule as of current effective date -->` markers where the specific dollar amounts are uncertain.
- **Opening-ceremony optionality.** NYSE trading-floor bell vs. Nasdaq MarketSite Times Square — a specific IR / brand asset for the first-day narrative.
- **Specific relationships with exchange leadership.** Does the CFO, board, lead underwriter, or IPO counsel have specific pre-existing relationships with named contacts at either exchange that materially affect execution?

Deliver a specific exchange recommendation (Nasdaq Global Select Market / NYSE / other) with a one-paragraph rationale. Note explicitly in the memo: founders who spend disproportionate board time on this decision have usually mis-prioritised — the recommendation should be defensible and specific but should not consume more board oxygen than the substantive governance-graduation and disclosure workstreams.

### 3. Majority-independent board build-out and Rule 10A-3 audit-committee construction

Assess the current board against the applicable exchange independence definition (**Nasdaq Rule 5605(a)(2)** or **NYSE §303A.02**). For each current director, deliver a specific independent / not-independent call with a specific rationale keyed to the look-back tests (prior employment, direct compensation >$120K, family-member relationships, outside-auditor affiliation, cross-compensation-committee interlock, material commercial or charitable relationships).

Compute:

- Current independent-director count and majority-independent status (Yes / No under **Nasdaq Rule 5605(b)** or **NYSE §303A.01**).
- Current independent audit-committee members satisfying both the exchange definition AND the **Rule 10A-3** heightened-independence tests (no compensatory fee other than director comp; no affiliation with the issuer or subsidiary; 10% beneficial-ownership safe harbour).
- Current members qualifying as **Item 407(d)(5) of Reg S-K** audit-committee financial experts (or a specific written explanation for the absence).
- Current members who cannot read and understand fundamental financial statements (a directional test — flag any concern for board-level remediation).

For any gap, plan the specific director-recruitment workstream to close it 12-18 months before IPO so majority-independence is achieved **AT listing** rather than under the newly-listed-issuer phase-in under **Nasdaq Rule 5615(b)** or **NYSE §303A.00** (available only as fallback). For each open seat produce:

- The target profile (audit-committee financial expert / compensation-committee independence / nom-gov specialist / sector-experience / diversity / geographic).
- The search-firm engagement plan (Heidrick / Spencer Stuart / Russell Reynolds / Egon Zehnder — specific firm choice with rationale).
- The specific timeline (approach → interview → offer → onboarding → committee assignment) mapped against the anticipated S-1 filing date.
- The interim fallback if the seat is not filled at effectiveness (specific reliance on **Rule 5615(b)** or **§303A.00** phase-in with the specific proxy-recommendation cost).

Design the audit committee: at least three members satisfying both the exchange independence definition and Rule 10A-3 heightened independence; at least one Item 407(d)(5) financial expert (or the specific disclosed rationale for absence); each member able to read and understand fundamental financial statements. Deliver the specific slate with committee-chair designation.

### 4. Audit-committee charter authoring

Draft the audit-committee charter enumerating the **Rule 10A-3** substantive responsibilities:

- (a) Direct appointment, compensation, retention, and oversight of the independent auditor — with the auditor reporting directly to the committee.
- (b) Pre-approval of all audit and permitted non-audit services with a specific de minimis exception threshold and a specific pre-approval workflow.
- (c) Procedures for receipt, retention, and treatment of complaints regarding accounting, internal accounting controls, or auditing matters — including a specific confidential, anonymous submission channel for employees.
- (d) Authority to engage independent counsel and advisors with issuer-provided funding at the committee's determination.

Add the exchange-level responsibilities from **Nasdaq Rule 5605(c)** or **NYSE §303A.07**:

- Review and discussion of the annual audited financial statements with management and the auditor.
- Review of major issues regarding accounting principles and financial-statement presentation.
- Review of earnings press releases and financial information provided to analysts and rating agencies.
- Risk-assessment and risk-management oversight (including the specific Item 106 cybersecurity oversight overlay).

Specify meeting cadence (minimum quarterly, plus executive sessions with the auditor, with management, and with the internal-audit lead), materials-distribution cadence, and the specific charter-review-and-approval cycle (annual). Note the charter is filed as an S-1 exhibit and posted publicly under exchange rules.

### 5. Compensation-committee and nom-gov-committee construction

**Compensation committee under Nasdaq Rule 5605(d) or NYSE §303A.05.** Confirm all members satisfy the applicable independence definition, apply the heightened-independence factors (source of compensation, affiliation with the issuer / subsidiary / affiliate). Draft the specific written charter covering CEO and other executive-officer compensation, incentive-compensation plans, and equity-based plans. Include the **compensation-adviser-independence assessment** framework — before engaging or receiving advice from any comp consultant, counsel, or other adviser, the committee documents consideration of the six specific factors (services provided to the issuer, fees as a percentage of the adviser's total revenue, conflict-of-interest policies, business or personal relationships with committee members, ownership of issuer stock, business or personal relationships with executive officers). Note the assessment is annual and is documented in committee minutes.

**Nominating and corporate governance committee under Nasdaq Rule 5605(e) or NYSE §303A.04.** If listing on NYSE, adopt an all-independent nom-gov committee with a written charter (no dispersed-independent-director alternative). If listing on Nasdaq, choose between an all-independent nom-gov committee and the dispersed-independent-director alternative under **Rule 5605(e)**, and document the choice with rationale. Draft the charter covering director qualifications, independence assessments, board composition, board evaluations, and corporate-governance guidelines.

For each committee, deliver the specific committee slate (chair + members), the meeting cadence, and the charter for filing as an S-1 exhibit.

### 6. Required corporate-governance policy suite

Draft (or produce a defensible outline with all substantive elements) each of the following. Each policy is either filed as an S-1 exhibit, exhibited to a subsequent Form 10-K, or maintained as a public-facing governance document — note the specific filing / posting requirement per policy.

- **Insider-trading policy** covering quarterly trading windows (specific window-open / window-close mechanics keyed to earnings), event-specific blackouts, pre-clearance procedures, prohibited transactions (hedging, pledging, short-selling), hardship-exception procedures, and specified insider populations. Note the **2024 exchange-amendment public-disclosure requirement** — the policy is filed as an exhibit to the periodic report under the 2022 SEC amendments to **Item 408 of Reg S-K** and reinforced at the listing-standard level by **Nasdaq Rule 5250** and the corresponding <!-- needs-research: confirm exact NYSE Listed Company Manual section for the 2024 insider-trading-policy amendment --> NYSE amendment.
- **Rule 10b5-1 plan framework** under the December 2022 SEC amendments (effective February 27, 2023): 90-day cooling-off for directors and officers (up to 120 days depending on quarterly-results disclosure timing), 30-day cooling-off for non-Section-16 employees, self-certification at plan adoption, prohibition on overlapping plans (with narrow sell-to-cover and successor-plan exceptions), one-plan-per-12-months limit on single-trade plans, quarterly disclosure under Item 408 on Form 10-Q and Form 10-K covering plan adoptions / modifications / terminations, and the Form 4 check-the-box indicating trades made pursuant to a 10b5-1 plan.
- **Rule 10D-1 clawback policy** consistent with **Nasdaq Rule 5608** or **NYSE §303A.14** covering incentive-based compensation received during the 3-year lookback if a Big-R or little-r restatement occurs, filed as an exhibit to the first Form 10-K under **Item 402(w) of Reg S-K**, with a "no indemnification" provision prohibiting indemnity or insurance premiums covering clawback recovery, plus integration into every executive compensation agreement (offer letters, equity award agreements, cash-bonus plans).
- **Code of business conduct and ethics** under **Nasdaq Rule 5610** or **NYSE §303A.10** applicable to directors, officers, and employees — conflicts of interest, corporate opportunities, confidentiality, fair dealing, protection of company assets, compliance with laws (with the specific intersection with the insider-trading policy), reporting of illegal or unethical behaviour, and the amendments-and-waivers public-disclosure workflow (Form 8-K Item 5.05 or website posting with a disclosed posting practice).
- **Related-party-transactions policy** consistent with **Item 404 of Reg S-K** disclosure — specific approval threshold, approval procedures (typically audit-committee or nom-gov committee), disclosure obligations in the S-1 and subsequent proxies.
- **Whistleblower policy** integrating the Rule 10A-3 audit-committee complaints procedure with the **Sarbanes-Oxley Section 806** anti-retaliation protections for employees / contractors / subcontractors / agents reporting suspected securities-law violations, plus a summary of the **Dodd-Frank Section 922** SEC whistleblower monetary-award programme (10-30% of monetary sanctions >$1M) and the direct federal-court private right of action for retaliation.
- **Item 106 cybersecurity disclosure procedure and Form 8-K Item 1.05 material-cybersecurity-incident workflow** (2023 SEC final rule) — the specific materiality-determination workflow involving the GC / CISO / CFO with audit-committee (or dedicated cyber / risk committee) escalation, the four-business-day filing infrastructure with pre-drafted 8-K templates, and the annual Item 106 disclosure covering the registrant's processes for assessing, identifying, and managing material cybersecurity risks and the board's oversight thereof. Note the underlying security-engineering programme is owned by `security-learning` — this exercise covers only the disclosure obligation and the governance overlay.

For each policy deliver: the specific responsible officer / committee, the filing / posting requirement, the annual-review cadence, and the S-1 exhibit or Form 10-K exhibit reference.

### 7. Controlled-company exemption analysis (conditional)

If the prerequisite frozen profile shows >50% voting power held by a specific person, entity, or group at IPO (e.g., a founder-controlled dual-class structure or a VC-controlled structure), run the controlled-company analysis under **Nasdaq Rule 5615(c)** or **NYSE §303A.00**:

- Identify which listing-standard governance requirements the exemption relieves: majority-independent board, fully-independent nom-gov committee, fully-independent compensation committee. Note explicitly that the **fully-independent Rule 10A-3 audit committee is NOT exempted** — Rule 10A-3 is an SEC rule under Section 10A(m), not an exchange rule, and applies to every listed issuer regardless of control structure.
- Draft the specific S-1 controlled-company disclosure identifying the controlling person / group, the voting-power calculation, and each requirement not complied with in reliance on the exemption.
- Run the vote-off analysis: for each exempted requirement, would you nonetheless voluntarily comply as a governance signal? Document the recommendation and rationale.
- Assess **ISS and Glass Lewis posture** on controlled-company reliance and dual-class structures — the specific pattern of vote-against recommendations on nom-gov-committee-chair re-election and on Say-on-Pay at controlled companies without meaningful sunset provisions.
- Track the specific voting-percentage trajectory over the next 3-5 years (founder secondaries, VC distributions, dilutive issuances) and identify the specific trigger date at which the exemption is expected to cease and the phase-in build-out under **Rule 5615(c)(3)** or **§303A.00** begins.

If the profile does not show a controlled-company structure, mark this task N/A with a one-sentence confirmation of the voting-power calculation and skip.

### 9. ISS / Glass Lewis pre-effectiveness alignment audit

Run the pre-effectiveness proxy-advisor audit against the current ISS Benchmark Policy and Glass Lewis Guidelines. For each dimension deliver a specific "aligned / friction / against" call with a specific action item to close friction before the first post-IPO proxy season.

- **Charter and bylaws — exclusive-forum provision.** Delaware Chancery exclusive forum for internal-affairs claims (see the *Boilermakers Local 154 v. Chevron* 2013 Chancery decision) plus federal-district-court exclusive forum for 1933-Act claims (upheld in ***Salzberg v. Sciabacucchi*** with subsequent 2020 amendments to **DGCL §115**).
- **Charter and bylaws — anti-takeover package.** Classified / staggered board, blank-check preferred authorisation, **DGCL §203** opt-out analysis, supermajority-vote requirements, prohibition on stockholder action by written consent, advance-notice bylaws, shareholder-rights-plan ("poison pill") posture (adopted at IPO vs. on-the-shelf).
- **Dual-class structure sunset.** If dual-class, is there a meaningful sunset provision (time-based, transfer-based, ownership-based)? ISS and Glass Lewis both disfavour dual-class without sunset.
- **Pay-vs-performance readiness.** Interlocks with the chapter 5 EGC decisions — as an EGC the issuer is generally exempt from **Item 402(v)** pay-vs-performance disclosure until EGC status ends, but the first post-EGC proxy triggers a specific catch-up. Identify the specific expected fiscal year at which the exemption ends and the specific pre-work required to be ready.
- **Board diversity.** ISS Benchmark Policy and Glass Lewis Guidelines include specific board-composition-and-diversity policies with vote-against consequences on nom-gov chair or full slate. Note the specific current threshold and confirm the board composition satisfies (or plan the specific remediation).
- **Over-boarding tests.** ISS and Glass Lewis apply specific limits on the number of public-company boards a director (particularly a CEO / active executive) may sit on. Test every current and prospective director against the applicable limit.

Deliver a specific action list: for each "friction" or "against" finding, the specific pre-effectiveness remediation (charter amendment, bylaw change, dual-class sunset addition, additional director search, disclosure enhancement) with a specific owner and a specific date relative to the anticipated S-1 filing.

## Starter guidance

Four anti-patterns to avoid:

- **The exchange-selection theatre.** Spending three board meetings on Nasdaq-vs-NYSE while the audit-committee-financial-expert seat is still unfilled and the clawback policy is a first draft. Either exchange delivers equivalent buy-side liquidity and regulatory compliance at the scale this module contemplates. The recommendation should be defensible and specific but should be the smaller, not the larger, workstream in the graduation package.
- **The phase-in dependency.** Treating the **Nasdaq Rule 5615(b)** or **NYSE §303A.00** newly-listed-issuer phase-in as the plan rather than the fallback. Companies that list with the majority-independent board and the fully-independent audit committee in place at effectiveness get a cleaner first proxy season and materially lower ISS / Glass Lewis exposure; companies relying on the phase-in show up in the proxy-advisor commentary and pay for it at Say-on-Pay and director-election votes.
- **The paper-policy fallacy.** Filing an S-1-exhibit clawback policy, insider-trading policy, and code of conduct that no operating executive has read and no committee has genuinely adopted. The policies are only load-bearing if the GC's office actually runs the pre-clearance workflow, the audit committee actually reviews the anonymous-complaint intake, and the compensation committee actually runs the clawback analysis when a restatement triggers it. Test each policy against the specific human owner and the specific workflow, not just the specific rule reference.
- **The controlled-company-and-dual-class silence.** Adopting a dual-class controlled-company structure at IPO without a specific written analysis of the ISS / Glass Lewis vote-against posture, the specific proxy-recommendation exposure at year 1 / year 2 / year 3, and the specific sunset provisions that materially move that exposure. Investors reading the S-1 read the exhibits; the working-group discipline is that the founder / board / audit-committee chair reads the exhibits with them.
