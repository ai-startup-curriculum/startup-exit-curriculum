# exercise-01: Going-Public Decision Framework Drill

**Estimated effort:** 3 hours

## Objective

Take a specific pre-IPO company (real or a realistic hypothetical you carry through the module) and produce a defensible readiness-assessment deliverable — a filled-in readiness memo covering the quantitative bar against sector benchmarks, the qualitative bar across organisation / board / audit committee / IR, the market-window read, the alternatives analysis, and a specific recommendation. By the end you should be able to walk the recommendation into a board conversation and defend it against a critical audit-committee chair, a critical VC director, and a critical outside advisor.

## Background

This exercise covers material from:

- [Chapter 1 — The Going-Public Decision Framework](../01-going-public-decision-framework.md)

You do not yet need the S-1 drafting mechanics (chapter 2), the PCAOB auditor selection (chapter 3), the SOX programme design (chapter 4), the EGC election framework (chapter 5), the dual-track structure (chapter 6), the listing-standard governance graduation (chapter 7), or the EDGAR filing choreography (chapter 8). Those install downstream execution work. The point of this exercise is to install the specific discipline of running the readiness assessment as a documented workstream against specific benchmarks rather than as a founder-CEO gut check.

## Prerequisites

- A specific pre-IPO company profile with: sector / sub-sector, ARR / revenue, YoY growth rate, gross margin, Net Revenue Retention (NRR), Rule of 40, customer concentration, cap-table shape (VC concentration, founder concentration, employee equity), current CFO / GC / VP-IR bench, current board composition (independent count, financial-expert count, audit-committee composition), and geographic footprint.
- Access to SEC EDGAR for specific-comparable-issuer S-1 filings.
- Access to the Meritech public-comps deck, the Bessemer Cloud Index or comparable sector-index reference, and the Renaissance Capital IPO Center for the market-window read. All three are publicly accessible.
- A specific reference-set of comparable-IPO issuers (typically 5–10 recent IPOs in the same sub-sector) whose readiness profile and aftermarket performance you can benchmark against.

## Tasks

### 1. Quantitative readiness scorecard

Construct a specific scorecard comparing the company against the sector-specific quantitative bar (chapter 1) and against 5–10 specific comparable-issuer IPOs from the past 24 months in the same sub-sector.

For each metric, produce:

- The company's current reading.
- The sector-benchmark reading (from Meritech / Bessemer / your own comp-set analysis).
- The specific comparable-issuer readings at their respective S-1 filings (sourced from SEC EDGAR).
- A **PASS / MARGINAL / FAIL** call with a specific rationale.

Metrics to cover:

- ARR / revenue level (against sector minimum).
- YoY growth (against sector minimum and against the deceleration slope of the comp set).
- Rule of 40 (against the "durability" bar).
- Gross margin (against sector median).
- NRR (against the enterprise-SaaS / SMB-SaaS / consumer bar as applicable).
- CAC payback and magic number (against practitioner-standard bars).
- Customer concentration (against the ≤10% single-customer threshold).
- Predictability — hit-rate on internal quarterly forecast over the trailing 8 quarters, stated as % variance.

Where a specific metric is not applicable to your sector (e.g., NRR for a non-recurring-revenue business), note the substitute metric you would use in its place.

### 2. Qualitative readiness assessment

Construct a specific assessment against the qualitative bar from chapter 1. For each dimension, produce a **READY / GAP-6-MONTHS / GAP-12-MONTHS+** call with a specific rationale and a specific remediation plan for any gap.

Dimensions:

- Series C+ operating maturity (documented sales methodology, formal deal-desk, QBRs, structured forecasting cadence, competent middle-management layer).
- IPO-experienced CFO (has the CFO taken a company public before or served as VP-Finance / deputy CFO at a public company; if not, what specific search and onboarding pattern will close the gap and by when).
- Public-company-experienced GC (same test).
- VP-IR / IR bandwidth (is the seat filled; if not, when will it be filled and from where).
- Board composition (majority-independent test under Nasdaq Rule 5605(b) / NYSE §303A.01, allowing for the 12-month post-IPO phase-in but preferring at-listing majority-independence).
- Audit committee (three independent directors satisfying both the Rule 10A-3 heightened-independence definition and the exchange listing-standard independence definition; at least one audit-committee financial expert under Item 407(d)(5) of Reg S-K).
- Compensation committee readiness (independent-director test under exchange rules; consideration of consultant-independence per exchange rules).
- Nominating and corporate governance committee readiness.
- Finance-function close discipline (calendar days from period-end to close; the specific reason a 15-day close cannot support the SOX 404(a) / quarterly-reporting cadence — see chapter 4).

### 3. Market-window read

Produce a specific market-window read for a 3-month, 6-month, and 12-month forward horizon.

- Meritech / Bessemer sector-multiple time-series: what does the specific sector's forward-revenue multiple look like against its 5-year history? Compressed, expanded, or normal?
- Renaissance Capital IPO Center: recent IPO pricings and aftermarket performance in the specific sector — deals priced at the top, mid, or bottom of the range; first-week performance; day-30 and day-90 performance.
- The specific window-closing and window-opening signal set from chapter 1: what specific signals are currently active?
- The specific sector-idiosyncratic factors: what specific macro / regulatory / sector-catalyst factors are moving the window for your sub-sector?
- A specific window read: is the window currently **OPEN / AMBIGUOUS / CLOSED** for your company's specific sub-sector at the current moment, and what specific price / range would you expect to price at?

### 4. Alternatives analysis

Draft an explicit analysis against the four alternatives from chapter 1.

- **Stay private with structured secondary liquidity.** What specific secondary-transaction pattern (founder secondary sleeve, employee tender, structured secondary, Nasdaq Private Market / Forge / EquityZen / Carta X trading) would provide the specific liquidity the company needs at the specific timeline? What growth-capital plan would replace the IPO proceeds? What per-share outcome does this produce for founders / employees / early investors over the same 3-year horizon compared with an IPO?
- **Sell to a strategic or financial acquirer.** What specific short-list of 5–10 strategic buyers would be plausible bidders? What specific short-list of 3–5 financial sponsors could execute a take-private at the target size? What per-share outcome — with specific mixed-consideration assumptions — does an M&A process plausibly produce?
- **Dual-track.** What specific structural design would run both processes in parallel (special-committee construction, banker-conflict management, price-signalling discipline)? What is the specific cost of running both workstreams? See chapter 6 for the mechanics.
- **Delay 12 months.** What specific quantitative or qualitative gap would close in 12 months (assume the current growth / R40 / gross-margin trajectory)? Would delay put the company on the good side of the sector multiple curve if the window compresses or expands? What is the specific opportunity cost of the delay to founders / employees / investors?

### 5. Readiness memo

Draft a 4–6-page CFO-authored readiness memo for the board (or for the transaction / readiness committee). The memo should be organised as:

- **Recommendation.** A specific one-paragraph recommendation among the alternatives: proceed to org meeting for a target Q_ 20__ filing; proceed to org meeting with concurrent dual-track authorisation; defer 12 months and revisit at the Q_ 20__ board meeting; pursue a specific alternative path.
- **Quantitative readiness.** The scorecard from Task 1, condensed to a specific-numbers-with-rationale summary.
- **Qualitative readiness.** The assessment from Task 2, condensed with a specific-remediation-and-owner section for any gap.
- **Market window.** The read from Task 3, with a specific window-signal set as of the memo date and a specific expected pricing range.
- **Alternatives.** The analysis from Task 4, condensed to a per-alternative one-paragraph recommendation-and-rationale.
- **Timeline and next steps.** If the recommendation is proceed, the specific pre-org-meeting workstream (auditor engagement, banker bake-off, IPO counsel selection, board-committee formation, readiness-gap remediation) with specific owners and specific dates.
- **Risks.** A specific short list of the top 3–5 execution risks to the recommendation with specific mitigation approaches.

The memo is drafted 4–8 weeks before the board meeting at which the decision is discussed; assume it is circulated to the board 2 weeks in advance.

### 6. Board / committee choreography

Draft a specific board-meeting or readiness-committee-meeting agenda and pre-read package for the meeting at which the recommendation is discussed:

- Pre-read package (memo from Task 5, plus specific supporting exhibits — quantitative scorecard, market-window deck, alternatives summary, comparable-issuer S-1 references).
- Agenda: CFO presentation, audit-committee-chair independent read, external-advisor input (specific bankers and counsel, in person or in written summary), specific Q&A time, executive session, specific vote.
- The specific board-resolution language authorising the org meeting (or the specific delay / alternative path).
- The specific follow-up documentation cadence (minute preparation, resolution filing in the corporate record, communications to specific external advisors).

## Starter guidance

Three anti-patterns to avoid:

- **The rear-view-mirror scorecard.** A scorecard that reads the company's last two years against sector benchmarks is only half the exercise. The other half is a specific forward-plan read against the same benchmarks — a specific company at $130M ARR growing 40% is a different company on the Q4 20__ filing calendar than on the Q4 20__ filing calendar.
- **The "market window is open" default.** The window is open somewhere for someone; the specific question is whether it is open at a specific pricing for your specific sub-sector. A specific window read that does not tie to a specific expected pricing range is not a read.
- **The alternatives-are-not-really-alternatives frame.** The alternatives section becomes theatre when the reader senses the recommendation was made before the alternatives were considered. The specific practitioner discipline is that the alternatives analysis produces a specific quantitative and qualitative comparison; the memo shows the reader why each alternative was rejected on specific grounds.

## Acceptance criteria

You can demonstrate that:

- The company profile is filled in with specific numbers (no `TBD` or `TBC` in the frozen profile).
- The quantitative scorecard has a specific PASS / MARGINAL / FAIL call for every metric with a specific rationale.
- The qualitative assessment has a specific READY / GAP-6-MONTHS / GAP-12-MONTHS+ call for every dimension with a specific remediation plan for gaps.
- The market-window read cites specific Meritech / Bessemer / Renaissance Capital data as of a specific date.
- The alternatives analysis produces a specific per-alternative comparative outcome (not just a description of the alternative).
- The readiness memo carries a specific recommendation the reader can either accept or specifically disagree with.
- A critical outside reader (an audit-committee chair, a VC director, an IPO-counsel partner) could challenge the recommendation and see the specific evidence the memo relies on.

## Reflection

Add a short reflection (½ page):

1. Which specific metric or dimension surfaced the biggest gap between the founder's / CEO's stated read and the specific benchmark read?
2. If the market window closes for 6 months during your target filing period, what specific decision changes in your recommendation? What specific workstream continues regardless?
3. Which specific alternative was hardest to argue against, and why? What specific piece of evidence would tip the recommendation the other way?

## Stretch goals

- **Two-scenario alternate memo.** Draft a specific alternate memo assuming the specific quantitative bar is missed by one metric (e.g., growth is 25% not 30%; ARR is $110M not $150M; NRR is 108% not 115%). What specific recommendation changes? What specific remediation plan closes the gap?
- **Board-conversation preparation.** Prepare the specific 8-slide deck you would present to the board at the meeting. Which specific slide is the audit-committee chair most likely to push back on, and what is your response?
- **Sector-comp cohort deep-dive.** Pick 3 specific recent IPOs in the same sub-sector (from EDGAR) and reconstruct their readiness profiles from their S-1s (revenue, growth, gross margin, NRR, R40, customer concentration, board and audit-committee composition at filing). What specific patterns emerge? Which of those companies would you have advised to proceed, delay, or pursue an alternative, and why does your recommendation for your specific company differ (or align)?
