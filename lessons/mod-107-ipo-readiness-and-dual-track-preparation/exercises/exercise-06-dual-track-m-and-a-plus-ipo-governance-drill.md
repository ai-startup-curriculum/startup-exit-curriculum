# exercise-06: Dual-Track M&A + IPO Governance Drill

**Estimated effort:** 4 hours

## Objective

Take the frozen pre-IPO company profile from exercise-01 and produce a defensible dual-track governance package — a specific full-board-vs-special-committee diagnosis, a committee charter, a banker-conflict remediation plan, a milestone-by-milestone go / no-go decision timeline, a Revlon / Basic-v-Levinson S-1 disclosure plan, a price-signalling and confidentiality choreography, an employee-communications posture, and a flip-execution playbook if the M&A path is chosen mid-S-1. By the end you should be able to walk the package into a full-board meeting and defend it against a critical independent director, a critical outside M&A counsel partner, and a critical securities-litigation partner.

## Background

This exercise covers material from:

- [Chapter 6 — Dual-Track M&A + IPO Governance](../06-dual-track-m-and-a-plus-ipo-governance.md)

The chapter installs the governance posture — when to run a dual track, when the process crosses into special-committee territory, how banker conflicts are managed across the underwriter and sell-side advisor roles, how the go / no-go decision sequences across S-1 milestones, how the S-1 discloses the M&A alternative under the **Basic v. Levinson** materiality threshold, and how the flip is executed under **SEC Rule 477** if forced.

Ownership handoffs — respect them and do not re-teach at depth:

- Sell-side M&A execution mechanics (banker engagement, teaser / CIM / management-presentation authoring, bid-round choreography, LOI negotiation, definitive-agreement drafting, diligence-and-data-room operations, secondary-transaction mechanics) is mod-101 through mod-105. Refer at the boundary; do not re-run the CIM.
- Fiduciary-duty case-law depth (full **Revlon** / **Corwin** / **MFW** / **Van Gorkom** / **Blasius** analysis; full special-committee construction; full banker-conflict remediation) is mod-109. This exercise applies the doctrine at the governance-decision level.
- Transaction-communications choreography (merger announcement, day-of scripts, customer / partner / press messaging, retention-programme rollout) is mod-110. Refer at the boundary.
- Gun-jumping / Reg FD mechanics during the offering window is mod-108 chapter 2. Refer at the boundary.

> Reminder: this module is education, not legal / tax / accounting / investment advice. A live dual-track engages qualified securities counsel, separate M&A counsel, separate financial advisors for each track, a PCAOB-registered auditor, and a documented board and committee record.

## Prerequisites

Carry forward the frozen pre-IPO company profile from exercise-01. Add — and freeze — the following dual-track-specific inputs at the top of your deliverable. If a field does not apply, note why rather than skipping.

- **Cap table with voting-power breakdown.** Fully-diluted ownership plus voting power for each holder above 5%. Note dual-class structure (Class A one-vote vs. Class B ten-vote or similar), founder supermajority protective provisions, and any VC that acquired majority voting power in a down-round recap. State the specific voting percentage held by (a) each founder, (b) each 5%+ VC / growth fund, (c) the employee equity pool, and (d) all other holders.
- **Incoming strategic-interest inbound log.** A specific 12-month log of parties who have signalled inbound interest. For each entry: party name, date, channel (banker intro / direct outreach / conference conversation), specificity level (generic "we should catch up" vs. specific price indication), and current status (dormant / active dialogue / under NDA / IOI received).
- **Planned founder-secondary sleeve, if any.** Specific dollar or share amount, specific participating founders / early holders, and whether the sleeve is being planned inside the IPO (mod-108 chapter 4) or a separate structured secondary.
- **Existing IPO underwriter bake-off shortlist.** Named lead-left candidates plus co-manager candidates (typically 6–10 firms across bulge-bracket, boutique, and sector-specialist). Note any incumbent bank relationships (prior private-round advisor, existing lender, prior sell-side pitch history).
- **Planned M&A financial advisors shortlist.** Named sell-side advisor candidates (typically 3–5 across bulge-bracket and boutique — Qatalyst, Centerview, LionTree, Allen & Company, Moelis, or bulge-bracket M&A groups).
- **Existing IPO counsel.** Named firm and lead partner.
- **M&A counsel.** Named firm and lead partner (typically a separate firm from IPO counsel, though occasionally the same firm with a separate deal team).
- **Board composition with independence testing.** For each director: name, seat type (founder / management / VC-designated / independent), affiliations disclosable under Item 404 of Reg S-K, and a preliminary independence call under Nasdaq Rule 5605(a)(2) or NYSE §303A.02 (as applicable to the anticipated listing exchange).
- **Current audit-committee membership.** Named directors, Rule 10A-3 independence status, and financial-expert designation under Item 407(d)(5) of Reg S-K.
- **Planned or existing readiness / transaction committee charter.** Attach or reference the existing charter (from chapter 1's IPO readiness committee) if one exists.

## Tasks

### 1. Full-board-vs-special-committee diagnosis

Diagnose the company's governance posture for the dual-track. Test each of the chapter 6 special-committee triggers against the frozen inputs and produce a specific yes / no recommendation with rationale.

- **Controller-involvement trigger.** Does the cap table concentrate voting power such that a controller exists under Delaware doctrine — a dual-class structure with Class B supermajority in founder hands, a founder retaining a supermajority protective provision, or a VC that acquired majority voting power in a down-round recap? Apply the **Kahn v. M&F Worldwide (MFW)** framework and its progeny. If a controller is present, does the controller's interest in an all-cash sale diverge from the minority's interest in continued IPO participation, or vice versa? Produce a specific yes / no on whether a controller-conflict analysis attaches.
- **Insider-participation trigger.** Does any insider (founder, executive, director) participate in the transaction consideration in a way that diverges from the non-participating common — the founder-secondary sleeve, a management-participating equity rollover into a sponsor buyer, a management-participation stake in an LBO? Apply the **In re Trados Inc. Shareholder Litigation** fact pattern — common-holder economics compromised by preferred-stack liquidation preference plus management-participating consideration. Produce a specific yes / no.
- **Insider-bidder trigger.** Is the inbound-log bidder set contaminated by insider-affiliated bidders — a director's PE firm on the shortlist, a controlling-stockholder-affiliated sponsor, a management-led buyout consortium? Produce a specific yes / no.
- **Recommendation.** Combine the three tests into a specific recommendation: (a) full-board oversight with a transaction / IPO readiness committee, (b) a fully-empowered special committee of independent, disinterested directors, or (c) a hybrid (special committee for the M&A track, full board for the IPO track). State the specific rationale referencing the specific facts of the frozen profile.

### 2. Committee charter construction

Based on the Task 1 recommendation, draft the specific committee charter.

**If Task 1 recommends a special committee:**

- Independence testing under both the applicable Delaware standard and the anticipated listing-standard independence definition (Nasdaq Rule 5605(a)(2) or NYSE §303A.02). Identify by name which existing independent directors qualify and which do not, and why. Note any recruiting need to reach 3+ qualifying independent directors.
- **Exclusive negotiating authority** vested in the committee, with the specific carve-out that the full board retains statutory approval under DGCL §141 and §251 for the merger vote itself.
- **Separate M&A-track counsel and financial advisor** retained by the committee (not by the company or by conflicted directors), with the specific engagement-letter language authorising the committee to select and dismiss.
- **Meeting cadence** — typically weekly through active negotiation with ad-hoc meetings at each go / no-go milestone.
- **Minute-taking discipline** — every meeting minuted, every advisor-input referenced, every alternative considered noted, every conflicted-party recusal documented.
- **The MFW front-loaded majority-of-minority stockholder vote structure** — the six *ab initio* conditions the transaction must satisfy from the outset to obtain business-judgment-rule review rather than entire-fairness review. State each of the six conditions in the charter and identify which are already in place vs. which require specific setup work.

**If Task 1 recommends full-board oversight:**

- Draft or amend the transaction / IPO readiness committee charter making explicit that the committee is **advisory not exclusive** — the full board retains all substantive decision authority, and the committee's role is execution coordination between full-board meetings.
- Identify the committee's membership, meeting cadence, and escalation-to-full-board triggers.
- Document the specific conflict-monitoring protocol — the standing agenda item at each meeting reviewing whether facts have developed that would flip the diagnosis into special-committee territory.

### 3. Banker-conflict analysis and remediation

For each candidate lead-left IPO underwriter on the frozen shortlist, assess whether the same firm can also serve as the sell-side M&A financial advisor. Apply the **In re Rural/Metro Corp. Stockholders Litigation**, **In re Del Monte Foods Company Shareholders Litigation**, and **In re El Paso Corp. Shareholder Litigation** doctrines.

For each candidate produce:

- **Fee-incentive conflict.** How do the underwriting-fee economics (7% gross spread, aftermarket-support economics) compare against the sell-side M&A-fee economics (tiered success fee on transaction value plus retainer)? Which incentive pushes the go / no-go decision at each milestone in which direction?
- **Existing buyer-relationship conflict.** Has the candidate firm run recent buy-side advisory for any party on the M&A inbound log? Is any such party a lender to the firm or a major client of the firm's other divisions?
- **Prior sell-side advisory history.** Has the candidate firm previously pitched or advised on a sale process for any inbound-log party? Has the firm previously advised the company on a sell-side pitch that did not close?
- **Staple financing.** Is the candidate firm positioned to offer staple financing to a sponsor bidder (the specific **Del Monte** and **Rural Metro** fact pattern)?
- **Individual-banker conflicts.** Do any senior bankers on the proposed team hold personal ownership in any inbound-log party, or have direct prior-employment relationships with any inbound-log party (the specific **El Paso** fact pattern)?

Produce a decision matrix: for each candidate, PASS (can serve dual role) / MARGINAL (dual role only with specific mitigations) / FAIL (must be either IPO or M&A, not both).

Then:

- Decide whether a separate M&A advisor must be retained; if yes, name the firm from the frozen shortlist and rationale.
- If any candidate is retained in a dual role, design the specific **information-barrier construction** — separate deal teams with named senior bankers, physical / systems separation, documented compliance-officer supervision, and prohibition on cross-staffing.
- Draft the specific **conflict-disclosure paragraph** for the special-committee (or transaction-committee) minutes and for the eventual proxy under Item 14 of Schedule 14A. The paragraph should specifically identify the conflict, the mitigation, and the board / committee's specific findings on adequacy.

### 4. Go / no-go decision-point timeline

Construct a specific milestone-by-milestone timeline mapping each S-1 milestone to the corresponding M&A-track state and the specific go / no-go decision available at that point. For each milestone, identify (a) which path can still be pivoted to and at what cost, (b) the specific board or committee approval required, and (c) the specific resolution language authorising the transition.

Milestones to cover:

- **Org meeting.** IPO working group kick-off with underwriters, counsel, auditor. M&A track: banker engagement letter signed, initial outreach list approved. Decision: commit to dual track vs. single track.
- **Initial DRS submission** under **JOBS Act Section 6(e)**. M&A track: initial outreach complete, IOIs received. Decision: proceed with both / narrow bidder list / pivot to M&A (pause DRS clock).
- **First-round SEC comments cleared.** M&A track: LOIs from high-conviction bidders, confirmatory diligence underway. Decision: keep both tracks live vs. narrow.
- **Subsequent-round SEC comments cleared.** M&A track: definitive-agreement drafting underway with one or two bidders. Decision: primary-path selection typically forced here.
- **Public flip.** Confidential-DRS conversion to public S-1, which under Section 6(e) must occur at least 15 days before roadshow. Pricing-anchor moment. Decision: commit to IPO (M&A wound down or held for topping bid) or commit to M&A (public flip either not executed or executed as a signal a strategic must beat).
- **Roadshow launch.** M&A track: definitive agreement signed or on the shelf. Decision: rare late pivot possible but highly disruptive.
- **T-1 pricing.** Both tracks converging to final decision at the eleventh hour. Decision: sign merger vs. price IPO, executed in hours not weeks.
- **Effectiveness.** IPO effective; M&A track wound down or reserved for post-IPO topping-bid regime.

For each milestone produce the specific board-resolution or committee-resolution language authorising the transition (e.g., "RESOLVED, that the Board hereby authorizes the Company to withdraw the confidential draft registration statement submitted to the Securities and Exchange Commission on [date] and to enter into the Agreement and Plan of Merger with [Buyer] on the terms presented to the Board on [date]"). Do not write a solution — draft the specific-resolution-language shell with fill-ins.

### 5. Revlon-becomes-reasonably-probable disclosure analysis

Identify the specific facts and events that cross the **Basic v. Levinson** (485 U.S. 224, 1988) probability-magnitude materiality threshold and trigger the Item 105 risk-factor and Item 303 MD&A disclosure obligation while the S-1 is still in confidential DRS.

For each of the following trigger events, produce a specific facts-and-circumstances judgment on whether the event crosses the threshold, with rationale referencing the probability-magnitude test and the **Revlon v. MacAndrews & Forbes** change-of-control-becomes-reasonably-probable perimeter:

- A specific bid at a defined valuation from a named strategic under NDA.
- Definitive-agreement drafting underway with one bidder.
- Executive-management access to bidder confirmatory diligence at the data-room level.
- Board resolution instructing advisors to negotiate final terms.
- Two or more competing bids under active negotiation.
- A signed LOI with a no-shop.

Then draft the specific risk-factor language (under Item 105 of Reg S-K) that would be added to the S-1/A upon crossing the threshold — a specific paragraph calibrated to disclose the possibility of an acquisition proposal without disclosing bidder identities, specific price, or specific terms that would compromise the M&A process. Also draft the specific MD&A trend-disclosure language under Item 303 (or the specific cross-reference to the risk factor if a full MD&A paragraph is not required).

State the specific board / committee approval workflow — who reviews the amendment, when it is filed, and how the disclosure decision is documented in the corporate record for **Corwin** and **Revlon** defensibility.

### 6. Price-signalling discipline and information-flow control

Design the specific price-signalling and information-flow choreography across the two workstreams.

- **Sequential vs. simultaneous marketing.** Are the M&A process letters going out **before** the public flip (the M&A track runs ahead so the primary-path decision is made before the S-1 goes public and the pricing anchor sets), **at** the public flip (a coordinated "the company is publicly on the market via both paths" signal), or **after** the public flip (the M&A track is reactive to inbound triggered by the public S-1)? Produce a specific recommendation with specific rationale referencing chapter 6's price-signalling analysis.
- **Clean-team protocols.** Draft the specific named deal-team clean-list. Identify by role which executives, directors, and outside advisors are read into both workstreams, which into one, and which into neither. Draft the specific NDA / clean-list-addition protocol for adding a person mid-process.
- **Disclosure-committee gating.** Design the specific disclosure-committee review protocol for the interaction of M&A information with the S-1 drafting cycle — how does the M&A track inform (or not inform) the risk-factor set, the MD&A trend discussion, the executive-comp CD&A (which touches on any change-of-control-triggered acceleration), and the related-party-transaction disclosure under Item 404?
- **Restricted-list mechanics.** Design the specific restricted-list protocol at each candidate underwriter and each M&A advisor — the internal-compliance restriction on the firm trading in company securities or affiliated securities during the dual-track window.
- **Underwriters' post-flip gun-jumping and Reg FD posture.** State the specific posture without re-teaching. (Gun-jumping mechanics are mod-108 chapter 2 — refer at the boundary.) The dual-track question is: how does the underwriter working group's post-flip publicity discipline reconcile with the M&A track's need to communicate the IPO's live-status posture to bidders?
- **Specific communication protocol to bidders about the IPO's status.** Draft the specific talking points a sell-side advisor uses when a bidder asks "what is the status of the IPO?" — before public flip, at public flip, and post public flip. The talking points must be truthful, must not disclose material non-public information selectively (Regulation FD applies to the extent the company itself is speaking), and must preserve the negotiating posture.

### 7. Employee-communications posture

Design the internal-communications regime reconciling the IPO-track publicly-facing narrative and the M&A-track need-to-know posture. Do not re-teach mod-110 transaction-communications mechanics — refer at the boundary.

- **Pre-signing deal-team clean-list.** Reference the Task 6 clean-list and add the specific employee-communications overlay — which named executives outside the deal team must be read in for operational reasons (the head of finance for close-cycle support, the head of legal ops for data-room support, the head of HR for retention-programme design)? Each addition requires a specific NDA and a specific documented business justification.
- **Disclosure-committee waivers of confidentiality for specific process participants.** Draft the specific approval protocol — who authorises adding a person to the clean-list, on what documented basis, and with what specific confidentiality obligations.
- **The "we are considering strategic alternatives" statement standard.** Draft the specific pre-approved holding statement the CEO, CFO, and GC will use if a leak reaches the broader organisation or the press before the primary-path decision. The statement must neither confirm nor deny a specific transaction, must not disclose material non-public information, and must preserve optionality across both paths.
- **Day-of choreography if the flip is triggered.** Sequence the specific internal-communications cadence for the day the M&A path is chosen and the IPO is withdrawn — the all-hands announcement, the manager-cascade, the FAQ, the retention-programme rollout. Refer to mod-110 for the mechanics; here, note the specific coordination requirement with the SEC Rule 477 withdrawal application and the 8-K disclosure.

### 8. Flip-execution sequence if the M&A path is chosen mid-S-1

Sequence the specific execution steps if the primary-path decision falls to M&A while the S-1 is in confidential DRS or is publicly filed. Coordinate with mod-109 for fiduciary-record discipline — the exercise applies the sequence at the governance-decision level.

- **S-1 withdrawal or hold-in-abeyance.** Draft the specific withdrawal application under **SEC Rule 477** under Regulation C — the withdrawal-request letter to the SEC staff, the specific reasons stated (typically "the Registrant has determined not to pursue the offering at this time in light of market conditions and its evaluation of strategic alternatives"), and the EDGAR filing mechanics. Alternatively, sequence the hold-in-abeyance approach where the DRS is paused without formal withdrawal.
- **Definitive-agreement signing.** Sequence the specific board approval, committee approval (if a special committee is in place), fairness-opinion delivery, and signing choreography. Do not re-teach mod-104 definitive-agreement mechanics — refer at the boundary.
- **Proxy or proxy-and-tender structure.** Determine whether the transaction structure requires a **DGCL §251** merger vote with a proxy filed on **Schedule 14A**, a two-step **Regulation M-A** tender offer under §251(h) top-up, or an exchange-offer structure. State the specific structure with rationale.
- **The required 8-K.** Sequence the **Form 8-K Item 1.01** (entry into a material definitive agreement) and **Item 5.02** (departure of directors or officers if applicable) filings and their four-business-day deadline.
- **SEC no-action or comment-cycle coordination.** If the S-1 has active SEC comments outstanding at the moment of the flip, sequence the specific staff-coordination approach — the withdrawal request, the response to any pending comments, and the notification to the staff of the transaction announcement.
- **Redacted-vs-clean disclosure of the M&A process in the eventual proxy.** Sequence the disclosure of the dual-track process in the proxy under **Item 14 of Schedule 14A** and the **background-of-the-transaction** narrative under **Item 1005 of Regulation M-A**. The narrative must fully describe the dual-track history — the initial IPO authorisation, the parallel M&A outreach, the bidders contacted, the IOIs and LOIs received, the committee deliberations, the go / no-go decisions at each milestone, and the final decision to sign. Identify which specific inputs to the background section come from which specific documents in the corporate record (board minutes, committee minutes, advisor engagement letters, banker-book analyses, LOI drafts). Coordinate with mod-109 for the fiduciary-record standard.

## Starter guidance

Anti-patterns to avoid:

- **The "we do not have a controller because no one holds 50%+" default.** Delaware controller doctrine reaches beyond bright-line majority ownership — a founder with Class B supermajority voting power, a VC with contractual protective provisions amounting to effective control, or a group acting in concert can trigger the controller analysis. Test each theory specifically rather than defaulting to the bright-line miss.
- **The dual-role-banker shortcut.** Retaining the same firm for both IPO and M&A because the firm has the best relationship or the best sector coverage — without documented information barriers, independent-advisor validation, and specific board findings — is the fact pattern **Rural Metro**, **Del Monte**, and **El Paso** litigated. The convenience is not worth the litigation record.
- **The silent-S-1 default on M&A disclosure.** The default that "we do not disclose the M&A process because it might compromise negotiations" is not a defensible disclosure posture once **Basic v. Levinson** materiality is crossed. Silence is Section 11 exposure; a calibrated risk-factor with GC and outside-counsel input is the inoculation.
- **The public-flip-then-decide plan.** Filing the public S-1 with the intent of "seeing whether a topping bid emerges" — without a specific plan for what happens if it does not, without a plan for what price-anchor damage the public flip does to the M&A negotiation, and without a specific board record supporting the decision — is a plan that produces the worst of both paths. The primary-path decision is typically forced by the public flip; if you are unwilling to decide, do not flip.
