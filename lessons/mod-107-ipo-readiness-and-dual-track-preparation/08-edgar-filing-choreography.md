# EDGAR Filing Choreography and the Post-IPO Disclosure Regime

## Why this matters

Every substantive act of public-company disclosure — the confidential DRS submission that starts the SEC comment cycle, the S-1/A amendments that clear it, the priced Rule 424(b) prospectus, the Form 8-A that registers the class for listing, the first 10-Q as a reporting issuer, the Item 4.02 8-K that concedes a prior financial statement is no longer reliable, the Item 1.05 8-K disclosing a material cybersecurity incident under the 2023 rule, the Form 4 filed two business days after a director's open-market purchase — passes through a single pipeline: the SEC's **Electronic Data Gathering, Analysis, and Retrieval** system, universally called **EDGAR**. EDGAR is the mechanical spine of the entire disclosure regime. A filing that lands correctly, on time, with the right submission type, credentials, iXBRL tagging, and exhibits satisfies the underlying statutory obligation; a filing that lands late, misclassified, or with a signature-block error converts the substantive disclosure event into its own enforcement, listing, and D&O-litigation exposure. The CFO who signs the Sarbanes-Oxley Section 302 certification on the first 10-Q is signing personally, not institutionally; the EDGAR choreography is what turns that substantive representation into a completed regulatory act.

The personal-liability exposure of a filing that goes wrong is not abstract. An Item 4.02(a) 8-K conceding non-reliance on previously issued financials triggers the restatement machinery, the Section 10D clawback under the SEC's 2023 final rule (chapter 4), and the securities-class-action complaint that follows within days. An Item 1.05 8-K filed on the fourth business day after the CFO's materiality determination sets the timeline the plaintiff-firm bar reads for a "should have been filed earlier" narrative. A missed Form 4 is an Item-405 proxy disclosure item under Reg S-K. A late 10-Q converts a filer in good standing into an issuer ineligible to use Form S-3 for the next twelve months, closing the shelf-registration door for follow-on offerings. The aftermarket consequence of a filing calendar the finance function cannot hold compounds into a specific access-to-capital cost that the CFO's cadence discipline exists to prevent.

> Reminder: this module is education, not legal / accounting / tax advice. Every EDGAR filing is executed under supervision of qualified securities counsel and the issuer's PCAOB-registered auditor, with financial-printer support handling submission, tagging, and access-code mechanics.

The core seat is the CFO / General Counsel / VP-Finance / Head of IR / Corporate Secretary running the EDGAR calendar as a live joint workstream — the same seat responsible for the SOX 302 certifications (chapter 4), the Rule 10A-3 audit committee governance (chapter 7), and the ongoing coordination with the financial printer and outside counsel.

## EDGAR — the system itself

EDGAR is the SEC-operated electronic filing system through which every registered issuer's public filings are submitted, indexed, and made publicly accessible on `sec.gov`. It has been the mandatory filing channel for domestic issuers since the mid-1990s and functions as both the intake queue for SEC staff review and the public-access layer for investors, analysts, plaintiff firms, and journalists. Every S-1, S-3, S-4, S-8, 10-K, 10-Q, 8-K, DEF 14A, Form 3 / 4 / 5, Schedule 13D / 13G, Form 144, and Form 12b-25 (notification of late filing) is an EDGAR submission with a specific submission type, a specific set of required exhibits, and a header block that identifies the filer.

The SEC has been running a multi-year modernisation programme known as **EDGAR Next**, replacing the legacy filer-authentication architecture with an identity layer built around individual-user accounts, dashboard-based filer administration, and API-based programmatic filing. <!-- needs-research: specific 2024/2025 EDGAR Next transition-milestone dates, adoption deadlines, and current status of legacy-credential deprecation --> The financial-printer relationship largely absorbs the transition, but the internal owner of EDGAR filer credentials should track SEC filer-manual updates so the access model in use is current.

## EDGAR filer credentials — CIK, CCC, PMAC, Form ID

An issuer that has never filed on EDGAR has no filer identity and cannot make a filing. Obtaining the credential set is a pre-filing workstream typically handled by the printer or counsel, but the CFO / GC should understand what is being obtained on the company's behalf.

**CIK — Central Index Key.** The 10-digit numeric identifier the SEC assigns to every EDGAR filer. The CIK is the primary retrieval key on `sec.gov` and never changes — a company that reorganises, changes its name, or is acquired retains its historical CIK across the filing record.

**CCC — CIK Confirmation Code.** An eight-character code (containing at least one letter, one digit, and one specified special character) that pairs with the CIK and authenticates every submission.

**PMAC — Password Modification Authorisation Code.** A separate code used to reset the CCC / password if compromised, held under strict access control.

**EDGAR access codes.** The authenticated login credentials for the EDGAR filing interface. Under EDGAR Next, the access model migrates toward individual-user identities linked to the filer entity rather than a single shared credential set.

**Form ID.** The SEC application used to obtain the initial CIK, CCC, PMAC, and access credentials. Form ID is filed via the EDGAR filer-management portal with notarised applicant authentication and processed by SEC staff. The practitioner timing rule: **file Form ID at least two weeks before the first anticipated submission**, because SEC processing is not instantaneous and a failed application (missing notarisation, mismatched entity name, wrong signature) requires resubmission and further delay. A company targeting a specific first-DRS date should have Form ID in-hand three weeks earlier as a working cushion.

**The filer-agent relationship.** The issuer rarely operates the EDGAR filing terminal directly. The filing act — logging in, uploading the submission package, entering the CIK and CCC, confirming acceptance, retrieving the accession number — is executed on the issuer's behalf by the financial printer (**Donnelley Financial Solutions / DFIN**, **Toppan Merrill**, **Workiva**) or by outside securities counsel operating under a filing authorisation. The CFO / GC should nonetheless know where the credentials live and who has access.

## The confidential DRS submission

The **confidential draft registration statement** privilege, available to emerging growth companies under **JOBS Act Section 6(e)** of the Securities Act and extended by SEC staff policy to all issuers for initial public offerings (chapter 5), is a filing-mechanic rather than a separate form. The issuer completes an S-1 (or F-1, for foreign private issuers) as it would for a public filing — same Reg S-K disclosure, same audited financials, same MD&A, same risk factors — but designates the submission for confidential treatment through the EDGAR submission type **DRS** rather than **S-1**. It enters SEC review through the Division of Corporation Finance intake queue but is not made publicly accessible on `sec.gov`.

Subsequent amendments during the confidential review cycle are submitted as **DRS/A**. When the issuer publicly files, the entire DRS submission history — the initial DRS plus every DRS/A — becomes part of the public record under Section 6(e). Every draft submitted, every amendment cleared, and (in redacted form for confidentially treated exhibits under Rule 406) every exhibit filed goes public. This is why DRS filings are drafted with the same care as public S-1 filings — the confidential-review phase is not a "safe to iterate loosely" period.

## The amendment cycle — S-1/A and DRS/A

After the initial DRS (or public S-1) is submitted, Division of Corporation Finance staff assigns the filing to a reviewer team — a legal reviewer for disclosure and legal-compliance items, an accounting reviewer for the financial statements, and (for specific industries) an industry-specialist reviewer. The first comment letter typically arrives approximately 30 days after initial submission — a practitioner rule of thumb rather than a statutory deadline. Subsequent comment letters after the issuer's response amendment are typically faster (10–20 days) because staff is reviewing a delta.

The issuer's response to each comment letter is filed as **S-1/A** (or **DRS/A**) — a full amended filing that replaces the prior version, accompanied by a **response letter** (drafted by outside counsel with auditor input on financial-statement comments) walking through each numbered comment and explaining how the amendment addresses it. Every comment must be answered — either with a specific disclosure change referenced by page number or with a specific explanation of why no change is required (with authority citation). Staff re-issues comments where the response is insufficient or issues new comments as disclosure evolves. A typical IPO S-1 goes through **three to five amendment cycles across four to eight months** before staff signals all substantive comments are cleared. The final "clean" amendment reflects all comment resolution and precedes the acceleration request below.

## The public filing "flip"

The confidential-to-public transition — universally called **the flip** — is executed by submitting the substantially final S-1 as a public submission type **S-1**, which displaces the DRS submission history in the public EDGAR record. On flip day, the entire DRS / DRS/A history becomes retroactively accessible on `sec.gov`. Under **JOBS Act Section 6(e)**, an EGC that used the confidential DRS accommodation must publicly file **at least 15 days before the road show** — specifically, before the first road-show meeting with prospective investors. Non-EGC issuers using the confidential-DRS accommodation follow a specific 15-day-before-road-show timing that mirrors the EGC rule under SEC staff guidance. The finance function's timeline works backward: pricing date → 15-day roadshow window → public S-1 flip at T-15 from roadshow start → clean amendment at or before the flip → cleared staff comments before the clean amendment → amendment cycle stretching four to eight months back to the initial DRS.

## The registration fee

**Section 6(b)** of the Securities Act imposes a registration fee calculated as a specified rate per dollar of registered securities. The rate adjusts annually by SEC fee-rate advisory. <!-- needs-research: specific current-year SEC registration-fee rate per million dollars of registered securities and effective date of the current rate --> The fee is paid electronically to the SEC through EDGAR fee-processing infrastructure at the time of filing the S-1 or any amendment that increases the registered dollar amount. Treasury coordinates with the printer to wire the fee on the correct filing date; a filing without the correct fee is not accepted and the submission is delayed.

## The Rule 424 prospectus

After the S-1 is declared effective and the deal is priced, the final prospectus — reflecting final offering size, price per share, syndicate allocations, use-of-proceeds table, capitalisation, and dilution — is filed under **Rule 424(b)** of the Securities Act. The standard IPO submission is **424(b)(4)**: a final prospectus reflecting substantive changes from the last effective prospectus in a deal priced pursuant to Rule 430A. The 424(b) filing must be made **within two business days of the earlier of first use or effectiveness**, and is the specific pricing-related public filing that captures the priced deal in the EDGAR record.

## Effectiveness and the acceleration request

An S-1 becomes effective under **Section 8 of the Securities Act** either (a) automatically 20 days after filing — rarely used in a marketed IPO because it forecloses staff comment work and provides no pricing-timing coordination — or (b) upon SEC staff declaration following an issuer-and-underwriter **acceleration request** under Section 8(a). The acceleration request is a written request signed by the issuer and the managing underwriters asking the SEC to accelerate the effective date to a specific date and time — typically **4:00 PM Eastern the day before the anticipated pricing date** — so pricing occurs after market close, allocations are made overnight, and trading opens the next morning.

Acceleration is not granted mechanically. Staff clears the request only after all substantive comments are resolved and specific conditions are satisfied: FINRA has issued a "no objections" letter to underwriting compensation under **FINRA Rule 5110**, the listing exchange has issued a listing-authorisation letter (chapter 7), the auditor has consented to use of its report (Exhibit 23), and underwriter and issuer counsel have delivered their opinions and negative-assurance letters. Staff historically signals readiness through a **"Bedford Falls" letter** or equivalent clearance communication. Effectiveness-day choreography: confirm staff clearance in the morning, submit the acceleration request via the printer, receive the SEC's order, and stand by for the market-close pricing meeting.

## Form 8-A — Exchange Act registration

The Securities Act registers a specific *offering*; the Exchange Act registers a specific *class* of securities for ongoing reporting and exchange listing. Every listed issuer must register its listed class under **Section 12(b)** of the Exchange Act by filing **Form 8-A** — a short-form registration that largely incorporates by reference the S-1 disclosure rather than repeating it. Form 8-A is filed on EDGAR contemporaneously with S-1 effectiveness so the Section 12(b) registration is in place before listing goes live. Without Section 12(b) registration, the class cannot list and the deal cannot trade.

Section 12(b) registration is what puts the issuer inside the full Exchange Act regime — **Section 13(a)** periodic reporting, **Section 14** proxy rules, **Section 16** insider reporting, Regulation FD, and the SOX Section 302 / 404 / 906 certifications. An issuer that registered under **Section 15(d)** alone (an S-1 without concurrent listing — rare in a traditional IPO) is subject to periodic reporting but not to Section 14 proxy rules or Section 16 insider reporting.

## The post-IPO disclosure cadence — the specific filings

For a US domestic issuer, the ongoing calendar is:

**Form 10-K.** Annual report, due **60 days after fiscal year-end for large accelerated filers** (defined under **Rule 12b-2** as issuers with $700M+ public float measured as of the last business day of the second fiscal quarter), **75 days for accelerated filers** ($75M–$700M public float), **90 days for non-accelerated filers and smaller reporting companies**. Contains audited annual financials, MD&A, risk factors, business description, executive compensation (Part III typically incorporated by reference from the proxy), and the full SOX 302 / 404 / 906 certifications.

**Form 10-Q.** Quarterly reports for Q1, Q2, and Q3 (no Q4 10-Q; the fourth quarter is captured in the 10-K), due **40 days after quarter-end for large accelerated / accelerated filers**, **45 days for others**. Contains unaudited quarterly financials (subject to auditor interim review under AS 4105), MD&A, and updated Part II disclosures.

**Form 8-K.** Current reports triggered by specific enumerated events (see next section), due **within four business days** of the triggering event. Certain Reg FD items must occur promptly and often before the four-day mark.

**DEF 14A — annual proxy statement.** Filed in advance of the annual meeting, containing director-election, executive-compensation, auditor-ratification, and other-matter disclosures required under Schedule 14A. Where the solicitation includes non-routine matters, a **preliminary PRE 14A** is filed first with a 10-day SEC review window before the definitive DEF 14A.

**Form 4.** Section 16(a) transaction reports by directors, designated Section 16 officers, and 10% beneficial owners — due **within two business days after the transaction**, measured from transaction date not trade-settlement date.

**Form 3.** The initial Section 16(a) filing on becoming an insider — due **within 10 days of becoming subject to Section 16**, which for directors and officers at the IPO means 10 days after the effective date of the Section 12 registration.

**Form 5.** Annual Section 16(a) report for transactions not required to be reported on Form 4 — due **within 45 days after fiscal year-end**.

**Schedule 13D / 13G.** Beneficial-ownership reports required from any person or group acquiring more than 5% of a class of registered voting equity. **Schedule 13D** is the active-investor form; **Schedule 13G** is the short-form passive-investor filing available to qualified institutional investors under **Rule 13d-1(b)** and to specific passive investors under **Rule 13d-1(c)**. <!-- needs-research: specific current Schedule 13D / 13G filing timelines under the SEC's 2023 amendments to Regulation 13D-G --> The 13D / 13G obligation runs against the holder, not the issuer — but the CFO / GC / IR head monitors filings closely because a new 13D filer is often the opening move of an activist campaign.

## Form 8-K item-by-item — the specific enumerated events

**Section 1 — Registrant's Business and Operations.** **Item 1.01** (Entry into a Material Definitive Agreement); **1.02** (Termination); **1.03** (Bankruptcy or Receivership); **1.05** (Material Cybersecurity Incident — the 2023 rule requiring disclosure within four business days of the issuer's materiality determination, subject to a narrow national-security-and-public-safety delay).

**Section 2 — Financial Information.** **2.01** (Completion of Acquisition or Disposition of Assets); **2.02** (Results of Operations and Financial Condition — the item under which quarterly earnings releases are furnished); **2.03** (Direct Financial Obligation or Off-Balance-Sheet Arrangement); **2.04** (Triggering Events Accelerating a Direct Financial Obligation); **2.05** (Costs Associated with Exit or Disposal Activities); **2.06** (Material Impairments).

**Section 3 — Securities and Trading Markets.** **3.01** (Notice of Delisting); **3.02** (Unregistered Sales of Equity Securities); **3.03** (Material Modification to Rights of Security Holders).

**Section 4 — Matters Related to Accountants and Financial Statements.** **4.01** (Changes in Certifying Accountant); **4.02** (Non-Reliance on Previously Issued Financial Statements or a Related Audit Report) — the item that concedes a restatement and triggers the Section 10D clawback (chapter 4) and the securities-class-action window.

**Section 5 — Corporate Governance and Management.** **5.01** (Changes in Control); **5.02** (Departure of Directors or Certain Officers; Election of Directors; Appointment of Certain Officers; Compensatory Arrangements) — a multi-sub-trigger item covering NEO departures, new-hire compensation arrangements, and long-term incentive grants; **5.03** (Amendments to Articles or Bylaws; Change in Fiscal Year); **5.05** (Amendments or Waivers to the Code of Ethics); **5.07** (Submission of Matters to a Vote — annual-meeting vote results, within four business days of the meeting); **5.08** (Shareholder Director Nominations).

**Section 7 — Regulation FD.** **7.01** (Regulation FD Disclosure) — content "furnished" rather than "filed," a specific Exchange Act distinction limiting Section 18 liability but not reducing Rule 10b-5 exposure.

**Section 8 — Other Events.** **8.01** (Other Events) — a discretionary item for information not required under any specific item but that the issuer chooses or is required by the anti-fraud discipline to disclose.

**Section 9 — Financial Statements and Exhibits.** **9.01** — the item under which specific exhibits required by any of the preceding items are indexed.

The CFO / GC / IR head maintains a live 8-K trigger monitor — every executive-team item that could be a trigger flows through counsel review with a specific "8-K required, yes / no, if yes what item" determination. The four-business-day clock runs unforgivingly; a filing missed by a day is a specific Item-405 disclosure item in the next proxy and a specific S-3 eligibility question.

## Regulation FD (Fair Disclosure)

**Regulation FD**, adopted in 2000 and codified at Rules 100–103, prohibits an issuer's selective disclosure of material non-public information to specified persons — **broker-dealers, investment advisers, institutional investment managers, and holders reasonably expected to trade on the basis of the information**. The rule requires that MNPI disclosed to a covered person be **simultaneously** disclosed publicly (for intentional selective disclosure) or **promptly thereafter** (for unintentional disclosure). Public disclosure is typically made through **Form 8-K Item 7.01** or through another broadly disseminating method (press release through a major wire, live-streamed public call with advance notice, filing under Item 2.02). A tweet from the CEO's personal account is not, by itself, a Reg FD-compliant channel unless pre-designated as a recognised disclosure channel with the market notified in advance (the 2013 Netflix / Reed Hastings guidance).

For unintentional disclosure, the "promptly" backstop is **the earlier of 24 hours or the commencement of the next day's trading on the New York Stock Exchange**. A Thursday-evening slip must be corrected by Friday's open; a Friday-evening slip by Monday's open. A functioning Reg FD programme requires (a) a written policy identifying covered persons and presumptively material information categories, (b) communications-team, IR, and executive training, (c) legal / IR pre-clearance of investor communications, (d) an incident-response protocol for the unintentional-disclosure scenario. The CFO / GC / IR head owns the programme jointly.

## Section 16 insider reporting

**Section 16(a)** requires directors, designated Section 16 officers, and 10% beneficial owners to report transactions on Form 3 (initial), Form 4 (transactions, two business days), and Form 5 (annual catch-up). The two-day Form 4 window is short; the practitioner apparatus (printer-managed Section 16 services, in-house filing agents, or outside counsel with a dedicated Section 16 practice) is what keeps filings on time.

**Section 16(b) short-swing profit recovery.** Requires disgorgement to the issuer of any "profit" from a matched purchase-and-sale (or sale-and-purchase) within a six-month window. Strict liability — no intent or MNPI possession required. Enforcement is typically by shareholder derivative demand or plaintiff-firm letter, and the issuer must claim the disgorgement. Every Section 16 trade runs through a written pre-clearance process (chapter 7 covers the insider trading policy) that tests for short-swing exposure against the insider's prior six-month history. Structured trading under **Rule 10b5-1** does not eliminate the Form 4 obligation; each plan trade is a Form 4 event. Personal 10b5-1 plan design is covered in mod-114 chapter 3.

## Sarbanes-Oxley Section 302 and 906 certifications

Every 10-K and 10-Q must be accompanied by two CEO and CFO certifications.

**Section 302** — the civil certification required by SOX Section 302 and Reg S-K Item 601(b)(31). The certifying officers represent that they have reviewed the report; that based on their knowledge the report does not contain any material misstatement or omission; that the financials fairly present financial condition and results; that they are responsible for establishing and maintaining disclosure controls and procedures and ICFR; that they have designed and evaluated those controls; that they have disclosed to the auditor and audit committee any significant deficiencies, material weaknesses, and any fraud (whether or not material) involving management or ICFR-role employees; and (for the 10-K) that they have indicated any material ICFR changes. The specific language is prescribed by SEC rule.

**Section 906** — the criminal certification required by SOX Section 906 and **18 U.S.C. §1350**. The officers represent that the report fully complies with Section 13(a) or 15(d) requirements and that the information fairly presents financial condition and results. Knowing violations carry criminal fines and imprisonment.

Both certifications are personal to the CEO and CFO (or equivalent principal executive and principal financial officer). They cannot be delegated. The sub-certification cascade — operating-unit and functional-owner sub-certifications supporting the CEO / CFO representation — is chapter 4's mechanism.

## XBRL / iXBRL tagging

**Regulation S-T Rule 405** requires most filings containing financial statements and specific cover-page data to be submitted in **Inline XBRL (iXBRL)** — a structured-data format tagging each numeric line item, textual disclosure block, and cover-page data element against a specific taxonomy element from the **Financial Reporting Taxonomy** maintained by the FASB (for GAAP filers). The tagging permits machine-readable analysis, comparability, and enforcement-analytics review. <!-- needs-research: specific current-year iXBRL tagging requirements by filer class and specific effective dates of the most recent taxonomy updates --> The inline-XBRL transition, moving tags from separately submitted exhibits to embedded HTML, was completed for most filer classes by the mid-2020s. Tagging is executed by the printer using tagging software (Workiva Wdesk, Certent, DFIN ActiveDisclosure, Toppan tagging tools) with the financial-reporting team's review of tag selection, extension elements (custom elements where a concept is not natively in the taxonomy), and calculation relationships.

## The financial printer relationship

Most issuers execute EDGAR filings through a financial printer that acts as filing agent, tagging services provider, working-group coordinator, and EDGAR-interface layer. The market is oligopolistic — the practitioner-standard printers are **DFIN**, **Toppan Merrill**, **Workiva** (a software-first entrant that has taken share in 10-K / 10-Q / proxy through Wdesk), and **Nasdaq** (through post-2022 disclosure-services acquisitions). Printer services include EDGAR-formatting (HTML with embedded iXBRL, exhibit management, TOC generation, hyperlinking), iXBRL tagging against the current taxonomy, filing execution on the CFO's timeline, working-group document management during S-1 or proxy drafting, and post-filing distribution.

The reason nearly every issuer uses a printer: EDGAR infrastructure is technical (submission-type-specific packaging, header conventions, exhibit-cross-referencing, iXBRL alignment), tagging is specialised, filing coordination requires 24-hour operational coverage during pricing week and quarter-end windows, and credential management (CIK / CCC / access-code security) is easier to outsource than to build. The CFO / GC / IR head owns the printer relationship as a vendor-management workstream and typically re-tenders every three to five years.

## The disclosure calendar as an IR / GC / CFO joint workstream

The quarterly cadence — earnings release under Item 2.02 → 10-Q within 40 days of quarter-end → earnings call the day of or day after release → management presentation for the next investor-conference cycle — is a joint workstream owned by the CFO (numbers and MD&A), the GC (legal review and 8-K item classification), and the IR head (call script, Q&A prep, sell-side outreach, buy-side conference calendar). The annual cadence — 10-K within the filer-class deadline → DEF 14A in the specific window before the annual meeting → annual meeting → Item 5.07 8-K within four business days → say-on-pay results disclosure — sits atop the quarterly cadence and requires coordination with the audit committee (for 10-K and audit-committee-report content), the compensation committee (for proxy compensation disclosure), and outside counsel (for proxy drafting and D&O questionnaire administration). Out-of-cycle 8-K triggers flow through weekly (or in high-activity periods, daily) counsel triage against the item taxonomy. The finance-function close discipline that supports this cadence is covered in `startup-finance-fundraising-curriculum` mod-111.

**The pre-earnings blackout.** The insider trading policy (chapter 7) typically prohibits trades by Section 16 insiders and other specified insider populations from a specific pre-quarter-end date (typically 15 or 30 days before quarter-end) through **two business days after earnings release**. The blackout interacts with Rule 10b5-1 plans (permitting trading during blackout under a pre-adopted plan, subject to the SEC's 2022 amendments — a mandatory cooling-off period of the later of 90 days or two business days after disclosure of results for the fiscal quarter of adoption, and 30 days for directors and officers) and with the Item 408 8-K disclosure of insider trading arrangements.

## The first year post-IPO — the specific transition milestones

**First earnings release and earnings call** approximately **35 days after the first full post-effective quarter-end**, coordinated so the release captures the first clean quarter (not the stub quarter partly pre-IPO) and precedes the first 10-Q. Underwriter research analysts typically initiate coverage during the specific post-quiet-period window.

**First 10-Q** approximately **40 days after the first full post-effective quarter-end** (large accelerated / accelerated) — the first exercise of SOX 302 / 906 certifications and the first ICFR representation.

**First DEF 14A and annual meeting** approximately **four months after fiscal year-end** — the first proxy season, with the first say-on-pay vote, first director-election vote under the public-company voting standard, and first auditor-ratification vote.

**First 10-K** at **60 / 75 / 90 days after fiscal year-end** depending on filer class — the first SOX 404 auditor attestation (chapter 4), the first full-year audited filing under the public regime, and the first Part III proxy-incorporation-by-reference exercise.

**Lock-up expiry** at approximately **180 days after pricing** (mod-108 chapter 6) — a specific liquidity-and-trading event with 10b5-1 and Section 16 implications.

**First S-3 shelf registration.** Once the issuer has 12 calendar months of reporting history and current filings under S-3 eligibility criteria, it may file a Form S-3 shelf enabling efficient follow-on offerings under Rule 415. Mod-108 covers the shelf mechanics.

## Withdrawal of a registration statement — SEC Rule 477

Where an offering is not proceeding — market window closes, pricing fails to produce an acceptable book, an M&A transaction supersedes the IPO under the dual-track (chapter 6), or the issuer chooses to withdraw — the issuer files an application to withdraw the registration statement under **SEC Rule 477** (submission type **RW** on EDGAR). The SEC consents unless a specific enforcement or investor-protection concern applies. Rule 477 preserves the confidential-treatment status of any DRS that was never publicly filed and returns the issuer to pre-registration status. A withdrawn S-1 does not preclude a future filing, but the withdrawn filing is a fact that will need to be addressed in the subsequent filing's disclosure and in the underwriter-diligence work on the re-attempt.

## Summary

EDGAR is the mechanical spine through which every substantive act of public-company disclosure is executed — the confidential DRS submission under JOBS Act Section 6(e) with its DRS/A amendment cycle over four to eight months, the public "flip" at least 15 days before the roadshow, the Section 6(b) registration fee, the Section 8(a) acceleration request typically for 4:00 PM Eastern the day before pricing, the Rule 424(b)(4) final prospectus within two business days of first use, the Form 8-A Section 12(b) registration that puts the class inside the reporting regime, and the ongoing 10-K / 10-Q / 8-K / DEF 14A / Form 3-4-5 / Schedule 13D-G cadence. The credential set — CIK, CCC, PMAC, access codes, obtained via Form ID at least two weeks in advance — is administered through the financial printer (DFIN, Toppan Merrill, Workiva) that executes filings with iXBRL tagging under Reg S-T Rule 405. The 8-K item taxonomy from 1.01 through 9.01 defines the four-business-day current-report triggers, including the 2023 Item 1.05 cybersecurity-incident item and the Item 4.02 non-reliance item that triggers the Section 10D clawback and securities-class-action window. Regulation FD prohibits selective disclosure to broker-dealers, advisers, institutional managers, and holders reasonably expected to trade, requiring simultaneous or 24-hour / next-open public disclosure. Section 16(a) requires Form 3 / 4 / 5 filings with a two-business-day Form 4 clock and a Section 16(b) short-swing-profit interaction. SOX Section 302 and 906 certifications accompany every 10-K and 10-Q with personal civil and criminal exposure. Rule 477 withdraws a registration statement not proceeding. The disclosure calendar is a joint IR / GC / CFO workstream anchored on the quarterly earnings-through-10-Q sequence and the annual 10-K / proxy / meeting sequence, with the pre-earnings blackout and Rule 10b5-1 interaction shaping insider trading. The finance function's cadence discipline is what keeps the calendar on time and S-3 shelf eligibility intact.
