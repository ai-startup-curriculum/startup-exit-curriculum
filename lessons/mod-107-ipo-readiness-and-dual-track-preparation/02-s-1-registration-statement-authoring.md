# S-1 Registration Statement Authoring — Reg S-K, Reg S-X, and the SEC Comment-Letter Cycle

## Why this matters

The S-1 is the document on which the entire IPO — and the entire post-IPO **Section 11** liability regime — rests. At effectiveness, every audited number, every risk factor, every MD&A trend disclosure, every executive-compensation table, and every exhibit becomes a document as to which the issuer, every signing director, every signing officer, every underwriter, every expert (the PCAOB-registered auditor most prominently), and every selling stockholder is potentially liable under **Section 11 of the Securities Act of 1933** for any material misstatement or omission. Section 11 is strict-liability for the issuer — no scienter, no reliance, no loss-causation in the plaintiff's prima facie case, only a negative-causation defence at trial. Directors, officers, underwriters, and experts have a **due-diligence defence** under Section 11(b) requiring a **reasonable investigation** appropriate to the portion for which they were responsible, with a lower "reasonable ground for belief" standard for the **expertised portions** (the auditor's report, any specific expert report). **Section 12(a)(2)** adds an anti-fraud regime for the prospectus itself; **Rule 10b-5** sits behind both. The plaintiff-bar practice model — Robbins Geller, Bernstein Litowitz, Kessler Topaz, Pomerantz, Bragar Eagel, and the firms that monitor every effectiveness for downstream stock drops — is calibrated exactly against this regime.

The practitioner discipline of S-1 authoring is therefore not "write the marketing document" and not even "write the disclosure document" — it is *construct the paper trail that supports every defendant's Section 11(b) due-diligence defence, while satisfying every line-item obligation of Regulation S-K, every accounting-presentation obligation of Regulation S-X, and every consistency test the SEC staff will apply during the comment-letter cycle*. The org meeting, drafting sessions, due-diligence Q&A, source-and-cite binders, printer proofs, and auditor comfort letters all exist to produce that paper trail. Chapter 1 covered whether to file; this chapter installs how the document is built.

> Reminder: this module is education, not legal / accounting / tax / investment advice. Every specific S-1 is authored under qualified issuer counsel with PCAOB-registered auditor sign-off and underwriter-counsel diligence, with each item-disclosure decision made against current SEC rule text and staff guidance.

## The Section 11 diligence backdrop that shapes drafting posture

The Section 11(a) defendants are: the issuer, every signatory, every director whether or not they signed, every underwriter, and every expert as to its expertised portion. **Item 512** pulls the principal executive officer, principal financial officer, principal accounting officer, and a majority of the board into the signature block. The auditor is an expert as to the audited financials; its Section 7 consent is filed as Item 601 exhibit 23.1. The **BarChris** framework (Escott v. BarChris Construction, still-taught practitioner canon) establishes that reasonable investigation is a facts-and-circumstances inquiry adjusted for each defendant's role, expertise, and access. Inside directors owe more than outside directors; audit-committee members owe specific attention to the financials; underwriters, as "watchdogs" for the investing public, owe an independent investigation beyond passive reliance on management representations.

The operational manifestation is a documented diligence process: multi-session **due-diligence Q&A** with management (2-4 hours per session, transcribed); a **source-and-cite binder** tracing every material claim to an underlying source (audited statements, internal SaaS-metrics workbooks, Gartner / IDC / Forrester reports, database queries); a **management representation letter** to underwriters; an **officers' certificate** at pricing; and the auditor's **comfort letters** at pricing and closing that update the audited-financial-statement diligence and tick-and-tie every prospectus number that traces to the accounting records. The plaintiff-firm pattern is that a 10%-plus post-IPO stock drop triggers PSLRA filings within days, alleging that some S-1 disclosure — a growth-rate representation, a customer-concentration statement, a moat claim, a risk-factor omission, a critical-accounting-estimate misstatement — was false or misleading at effectiveness. The source-and-cite binder is what defence counsel later uses to move to dismiss on the due-diligence defence.

## The drafting workflow — org meeting to effectiveness

**Org meeting.** Two to four weeks after the board's go-decision (chapter 1), the working group convenes for a full-day meeting hosted by issuer counsel. The group includes issuer management (CEO, CFO, GC, controller, head of IR); issuer counsel (a Wilson Sonsini, Cooley, Latham, Davis Polk, Skadden, Simpson Thacher, Kirkland, Fenwick, or Goodwin team of 4-8 lawyers); underwriter counsel (typically Cravath, Sullivan & Cromwell, Davis Polk, or Simpson Thacher for the syndicate); the lead-left ECM team, industry banker, and IPO-execution deal team; co-manager ECM leads; the PCAOB-registered auditor's engagement partner, senior manager, and technical-accounting specialist; and the financial printer's account executive (**DFIN** / Donnelley Financial Solutions; **Toppan Merrill**; **Workiva** for iXBRL-native drafting). The org meeting sets the timeline (target DRS submission, public flip, roadshow launch, pricing), assigns drafting responsibilities (issuer counsel drafts the front-of-book Business, Risk Factors, MD&A, Executive Compensation; auditor and management drive the financials; underwriter counsel drafts specific underwriter-facing sections), and issues underwriter counsel's due-diligence request list.

**Drafting sessions.** For 8-16 weeks the working group iterates through 2-3 all-hands sessions per week plus daily working-group calls. Controller and auditor tick-and-tie every number to the audited statements and internal-source workbooks. The banker's industry team owns comps and market-sizing input; the IR head reads for aftermarket narrative; the GC for risk-factor and litigation disclosures; underwriter counsel for defence posture and diligence-record consistency.

**DRS submission.** For an EGC — defined under Securities Act §2(a)(19) as an issuer with less than **$1.235B** in annual gross revenue in its most recently completed fiscal year (indexed periodically by Commission release), with EGC status running up to five years from IPO or until earlier loss on specified triggers — the initial submission is a **confidential Draft Registration Statement (DRS)** under Section 6(e). Since 2017 the SEC has extended DRS availability to all issuers regardless of EGC status; effectively every first-time IPO today uses DRS. The S-1 is not visible on EDGAR until the public flip, letting the working group work through initial comment rounds without market exposure. Auditor consent is not required at initial DRS but is required at the public flip.

**The SEC comment-letter cycle.** The Division of Corporation Finance staff issues a first comment letter typically 25-30 calendar days after submission. Comments range from specific accounting questions (staff often engages the Office of the Chief Accountant on technical revenue-recognition, business-combination, or stock-based-compensation questions), to disclosure requests (more specificity on a risk factor, more MD&A trend explanation, table reconciliations, non-GAAP reconciliations), to legal-disclosure questions. A first comment letter for an IPO typically contains 30-80 comments. Response is due within roughly 30 calendar days as a **response letter** (numbered response to each comment, citing the disclosure change or explaining why no change is required) plus a marked **amended S-1** filed via EDGAR. Three to five rounds are typical, each narrowing to remaining specific issues. The final round produces "no further comments" clearance, after which the issuer requests **acceleration of effectiveness** under Section 8(a), effective at a specific time (typically 4:00 PM ET the day before pricing).

**Public flip and printer.** Under JOBS Act §6(e)(1), an EGC using DRS must publicly file the S-1 at least **15 days** before the road show (the first meeting with prospective investors). The flip converts DRS to publicly-filed S-1, adds the auditor's Section 7 consent, and refreshes interim-period financials under Reg S-X Article 10. Coordination with the gun-jumping regime and TTW cooling-off is in mod-108 chapter 2. The financial printer handles EDGAR filing, iXBRL tagging under Rule 405 of Reg S-T, typesetting, and printer-proof coordination; printer-proof discipline becomes intensive in the final rounds when every character, footnote, and cross-reference must be exact.

## Business section — Item 101

**Regulation S-K Item 101** governs the business description. Required disclosures include a **general development of business** over the past five years (material changes, acquisitions and dispositions, reorganisations); a description of the business as currently conducted — principal products and services, principal markets, distribution methods, raw-material sources and availability, seasonality, working-capital practices, customer concentration (any single customer representing 10%+ of consolidated revenue triggers disclosure), backlog, government contracts, competitive conditions, R&D expenditures, environmental costs, and number of employees. The 2020 amendments made Item 101 more **principles-based** — the numeric five-year requirement softened to a materiality standard, and **human capital resources** disclosure was added as Item 101(c)(2)(ii).

The drafting discipline is threefold. First, the **business narrative** is drafted consistent with the TTW and roadshow decks (mod-108); every market-sizing number cites its source (Gartner, IDC, Forrester, or a bottom-up build with described methodology); every "market leadership" claim ties to a specific measurable with the source cited. Second, the **five-year history** is a factual chronology with material events flagged (funding rounds, key hires, product launches, acquisitions, litigation resolutions, key customer wins). Third, the **revenue disclosure** — by segment under **ASC 280** where the chief operating decision-maker reviews performance at segment level (with FASB ASU 2023-07 requiring expanded segment-expense disclosure), by geography where material, and by customer concentration — is a tick-and-tie exercise with the audited financials and internal reporting. A single-reportable-segment issuer says so explicitly and identifies the CODM.

## Risk Factors — Item 105

**Regulation S-K Item 105** governs risk factors. The 2020 amendments modernised Item 105 with a **principles-based** requirement to disclose the material risks known to the issuer that make an investment speculative or risky, a **15-page soft cap** (beyond which a two-page **summary of risk factors** must lead the section), **plain-English** drafting under **Rule 421**, and grouping under specific descriptive headings rather than generic factors.

The drafting discipline is specificity. "We operate in a competitive market" is useless; a risk factor naming specific competitors, the specific mechanism (hyperscaler bundling, marketplace platform dependency, regulator-approved substitute), and the customer-segment or margin impact is both useful and Section 11-defensive. The practitioner taxonomy:

- **Industry and market** — sector-structural risks (regulatory-regime dependency, technology-shift, industry-vertical concentration, platform-dependency on a hyperscaler, app store, or marketplace).
- **Business and operational** — revenue and customer concentration, key-personnel, product-quality and outage, data-privacy regulation (GDPR, CCPA, sectoral), international operations (FX, transfer pricing, sanctions).
- **Financial** — going-concern exposure where the auditor has expressed substantial doubt; revenue-recognition-estimate risk; goodwill-impairment risk for acquisitive issuers; interest-rate; dilution from equity compensation; non-GAAP-metric risk as a specific line item.
- **Regulatory and legal** — the specific regulatory regimes (financial services, healthcare, telecom, energy, transportation, gambling, cannabis, crypto), pending litigation, pending enforcement inquiries, material uncertain tax positions.
- **IP** — patent-infringement exposure, trademark disputes, licence dependency, open-source compliance, trade-secret posture.
- **Cyber** — attack-surface disclosure, past material incidents, and board-oversight and management-role disclosure required by the 2023 amendments (Item 106 governance and Form 8-K Item 1.05 material-incident disclosure post-IPO).
- **AI and model** — for AI-native issuers, training-data provenance, licensor exposure, hallucination and accuracy risk, emerging AI regulation (EU AI Act, US state AI laws, sectoral rules), and dependency on foundational-model providers.
- **Governance** — **controlled company** disclosures (where >50% voting power is held by a specific person, entity, or group, entitled to listing-rule exemptions from majority-independent-board and committee-composition requirements but subject to specific disclosure); **dual-class or multi-class capital structure** disclosures (voting concentration, sunset or lack thereof, FTSE and S&P index-inclusion penalties, fiduciary-duty overlay).
- **Common-stock** — volatility, no-dividend intent, charter and bylaw anti-takeover, DGCL §203 if not opted out, exclusive-forum provision.

The specificity discipline is what separates a Section 11-defensive risk section from a vulnerable one. Risk factors are drafted after (or with) the due-diligence Q&A; every known risk surfaced by management is either disclosed or explicitly addressed with the documented non-disclosure rationale.

## MD&A — Item 303

**Regulation S-K Item 303**, substantially revised in 2020 (effective 2021), governs Management's Discussion and Analysis. The revision eliminated the specific tabular contractual-obligations requirement, sharpened the forward-looking trend discussion, and codified **critical accounting estimates** disclosure. The current structure includes an **objective statement**, a **results-of-operations** discussion with the **known-trend and uncertainty** requirement of Item 303(b)(2)(ii), a **liquidity and capital resources** section, and **critical accounting estimates**.

The most consequential discipline is the trend disclosure. Item 303(b)(2)(ii) requires description of any known trends or uncertainties that have had or are **reasonably likely** to have a material favourable or unfavourable impact on net sales, revenues, or income from continuing operations. "Reasonably likely" is broader than "probable" and captures known trends beyond remote possibility. Every trend management is discussing in board meetings, operating reviews, or the internal forecast — slowing growth in a specific customer segment, a pricing-pressure dynamic, an input-cost trend, a regulatory-change trend — is evaluated against the standard and either disclosed or documented in the diligence file as a specific non-disclosure decision. The plaintiff-firm attack model on MD&A is precisely this: management knew a trend, did not disclose it, and the trend materialised post-IPO.

The results-of-operations discussion analyses *why* line items changed, not merely *that* they changed. A 40% revenue increase must be analysed by driver — new-logo count, expansion within existing base, pricing, mix, geography, product line — with the specific quantification required by Item 303(b)(1)(i) where two or more factors caused a material change. **Liquidity and capital resources** covers cash, debt facilities, expected use of IPO proceeds, and cash-covenant considerations, coordinated with the Use of Proceeds section. **Critical accounting estimates** covers the estimates materially affecting the statements — revenue recognition under **ASC 606** (variable consideration, standalone selling prices, contract-modification accounting), stock-based compensation valuation (especially pre-IPO grants scrutinised in **cheap-stock** SEC comments, where staff challenges historical 409A valuations against the anticipated IPO price and may require incremental compensation expense), business-combination purchase accounting, income taxes under **ASC 740** (uncertain tax positions, valuation allowances), and allowance for credit losses under **ASC 326**.

**Non-GAAP measures** are governed by **Regulation G** and **Item 10(e) of Regulation S-K**. Reg G requires any non-GAAP measure presented outside a filing to be accompanied by the most directly comparable GAAP measure and a reconciliation. Item 10(e), applying within SEC filings, requires the same reconciliation, requires the GAAP measure be presented with **equal or greater prominence** than the non-GAAP measure, prohibits certain presentations (adjusting for non-recurring items occurring within the past two years or reasonably likely to recur in the next two; adjusting for recurring cash operating expenses), and requires disclosure of why management believes the measure is useful. Every non-GAAP metric — Adjusted EBITDA, non-GAAP operating income, free cash flow, ARR, billings, contribution margin, magic number, Rule of 40 — has a defined-terms glossary, a reconciliation for every period presented, equal-prominence GAAP presentation, and a use-rationale disclosure. Non-GAAP prominence and cheap-stock are among the most frequent first-round SEC comment categories.

## Executive compensation — Item 402, and Items 404 and 407

**Regulation S-K Item 402** for a **non-EGC** requires the **Compensation Discussion and Analysis (CD&A)**; the **Summary Compensation Table** for three years covering the CEO, CFO, and next three most highly-compensated executive officers; the **Grants of Plan-Based Awards**, **Outstanding Equity Awards at Fiscal Year-End**, **Option Exercises and Stock Vested**, **Pension Benefits** (rare for tech), **Non-Qualified Deferred Compensation**, **Potential Payments Upon Termination or Change in Control**, and **Director Compensation** tables; and the **Pay Versus Performance** disclosure under Item 402(v), added by the SEC's 2022 rules implementing Dodd-Frank §953(a), reconciling reported compensation to "compensation actually paid" over five years alongside cumulative TSR, peer-group TSR, net income, and a company-selected financial performance measure.

An **EGC** benefits from scaled disclosure under JOBS Act Title I — may omit the CD&A entirely, may present only two years of Summary Compensation Table data, and may report only the CEO plus the next two most highly-compensated executive officers (not the four other NEOs required of a non-EGC). An EGC is also exempt from pay-versus-performance, the pay-ratio disclosure under Dodd-Frank §953(b), and say-on-pay. Chapter 5 covers the EGC elections in depth.

The drafting discipline is threefold. First, the compensation programme the S-1 describes must be the programme the company actually operates — a CD&A describing rigorous benchmarking, performance-based equity with specific hurdles, and clawback compliance under the 2023 rules (**Rule 10D-1** plus the NYSE / Nasdaq listing-standard clawbacks) must tie to actual committee minutes, grant documents, and clawback policies. Second, the cheap-stock analysis intersects with Item 402 — pre-IPO grant stock-compensation expense is a frequent comment focus, and disclosure must be internally consistent with the stock-compensation footnote and valuation-methodology description. Third, **Item 404 related-person transactions** disclosure covers transactions with executives, directors, 5%+ stockholders, and immediate family exceeding the current **$120,000** threshold — a frequent diligence-gap area covering founder loans, executive relocations, related-party services and investments, and pre-IPO secondary transactions with executives. **Item 407** requires governance disclosure — board-independence determinations, board leadership, board risk oversight, committee composition, and audit-committee financial-expert designation. Item 407 interlocks with listing-rule governance (chapter 7) and the SOX 404 ICFR representation (chapter 4).

## Use of Proceeds, Dilution, Principal and Selling Stockholders, Capital Stock, and Future Sale

**Item 504** requires description of the principal purposes for the net proceeds and the approximate amount for each; where no specific plans, the fact and reasons; where proceeds repay indebtedness, the specific terms and prior use. Drafting balances specificity against flexibility; the typical pattern mixes general corporate purposes, product-development and geographic-expansion investment, acquisition capacity (with the standard "no specific acquisition currently subject to a definitive agreement" language), and specific debt-repayment where applicable. Where the offering includes a secondary component, the disclosure notes the issuer receives no proceeds from it.

**Item 506** requires the dilution table — historical net tangible book value per share, increase from new investors' contributions, pro forma net tangible book value per share after the offering, and dilution per share to new investors. Mechanically simple but comments-relevant because it interacts with offering size, pricing range, greenshoe, and pre-IPO structure changes (option and warrant exercises, IPO-conversion of preferred to common).

**Item 403** requires beneficial-ownership disclosure by directors, officers, and 5%+ stockholders under Rule 13d-3 (including options, warrants, and convertibles exercisable within 60 days). **Item 507** adds selling-stockholder disclosure where the offering has a secondary component. Issuer and underwriter counsel reconcile beneficial ownership to the shareholder register, equity-plan administrator records, and the specific option-exercise, RSU-vesting, and warrant-exercise mechanics happening at or before pricing.

**Item 202** requires description of the securities — authorised and outstanding shares of each class; voting rights (with specific attention to dual-class or multi-class); dividend, liquidation, and conversion rights (including IPO-conversion of preferred); registration rights; anti-takeover provisions (blank-check preferred, classified board, supermajority requirements, advance-notice bylaws, exclusive-forum provisions); and state anti-takeover treatment (DGCL §203 for Delaware issuers). The **exclusive-forum provision** — designating the Delaware Court of Chancery for internal-affairs claims and, following the Delaware Supreme Court's **Salzberg v. Sciabacucchi** decision, often the federal district court for Securities Act claims — is a specific charter mechanism worth its own diligence attention because plaintiff-firm forum-shopping is a liability-exposure driver.

**Shares Eligible for Future Sale** describes shares freely tradable at effectiveness, shares subject to the underwriter **lock-up** (typically 180 days with customary release provisions and early-release triggers), the mechanics of **Rule 144** and **Rule 701** governing post-lock-up resales, projected equity-plan overhang, and the Form S-8 the issuer files shortly after IPO to register plan shares. The release choreography is an aftermarket-supply consideration the underwriters coordinate against the IR narrative post-IPO.

## Financial statements — Regulation S-X

**Regulation S-X** governs the form and content of the financials. The core articles for an operating-company IPO are **Article 1** (general), **Article 2** (auditor qualifications), **Articles 3-4** (general instructions and rules), **Article 5** (commercial and industrial companies — the line-item requirements for a typical operating-company issuer), **Article 8** (smaller reporting companies — a scaled framework parallel to EGC), **Article 10** (interim), plus industry-specific articles.

The audited-financial-statement footprint for a non-EGC is **three years of audited statements of operations, stockholders' equity, and cash flows, plus two years of audited balance sheets** — the "3-2" footprint. Under **Section 7(a)(2)(A) of the Securities Act**, added by the JOBS Act, an **EGC may present only two years** of the operations / equity / cash-flow statements — the "2-2" footprint (chapter 5). Interim periods under Article 10 require the most recent completed quarter with a comparative prior-year interim, subject to an auditor **SAS 100 review (AS 4105)** rather than a full audit.

**Age of financial statements.** Statements included in the S-1 must be no more "stale" than the periods specified in Rule 3-12. For a calendar-year issuer the typical pattern is that an S-1 filed between fiscal year-end and 45 days later may use the prior year's audited financials plus the third-quarter interim; after 45 days the new year's audited financials must be included. The specific staleness cliffs (45 / 90 / 135 days depending on filer status) drive the filing calendar — a mid-cycle staleness cliff forces an amendment adding new financials, which the working group plans against.

Financials and MD&A interlock. Every MD&A number ties to a financial-statement number, which ties to the audited working papers. Segment footnotes tie to the business-section revenue-by-segment disclosure. ASC 606 disaggregation ties to the MD&A driver analysis. Critical-accounting-estimate MD&A ties to the accounting-policy footnote. Chapter 3 develops the auditor's role in depth; for the S-1 the auditor delivers the audit report on each year of audited financials (expressed under PCAOB auditing standards, including critical audit matters under **AS 3101** for large filers, with EGC-specific CAM phase-in rules), the review report on any interim period, the **Section 7 consent** filed as Item 601 exhibit 23.1 for each amendment where the audit report is included, and the pricing-and-closing **comfort letters** to underwriters providing negative assurance on interim-period changes and tick-and-tie for every S-1 number that traces to the accounting records.

## Exhibits, signatures, and XBRL

**Item 601** governs exhibits — the underwriting agreement (Ex. 1.1); charter and bylaws as in effect at effectiveness (Ex. 3.1 / 3.2); specimen certificate or book-entry reference (Ex. 4.1); the validity-of-shares legal opinion (Ex. 5.1); material contracts (Ex. 10.x — equity-compensation plans, executive-employment agreements, key commercial contracts, material leases, credit agreements, registration-rights agreement); any tax opinion (Ex. 8.1); subsidiaries list (Ex. 21.1); auditor consent (Ex. 23.1); and any additional expert consents. Material contracts filed as exhibits are publicly available on EDGAR (chapter 8) and are subject to confidentiality-treatment analysis under **Rule 406** and the 2019 amendments permitting redaction of competitively-sensitive information without a confidential-treatment application.

**Item 512** governs undertakings and signatures — commitments to file post-effective amendments in specified circumstances, remove from registration securities not sold, and provide specific information to purchasers. The signature block requires the principal executive officer, principal financial officer, principal accounting officer, and a majority of the directors — each signature representing that signatory's Section 11 exposure.

**Rule 405 of Regulation S-T** requires financial-statement data to be tagged in **Inline XBRL (iXBRL)** — machine-readable XML embedded within the human-readable HTML on EDGAR. Cover-page data is separately tagged in iXBRL. Tagging runs against the FASB US-GAAP taxonomy for the financials and the SEC's DEI taxonomy for cover-page data. The financial printer or Workiva-style platform handles tagging with the accounting team, but taxonomy-element selection and extension-element creation require accounting-and-taxonomy judgement. Tagging errors are a specific comment category; the taxonomy is validated against the EDGAR Filer Manual and taxonomy-specific validation rules before submission.

## Summary

The S-1 creates and organises the Section 11 / Section 12(a)(2) / Rule 10b-5 liability regime the issuer, directors, officers, underwriters, and auditor operate against post-IPO. The drafting workflow — org meeting through DRS submission through the three-to-five-round comment-letter cycle through the 15-day EGC public flip through Section 8(a) acceleration and effectiveness — is a 4-9 month workstream coordinated across issuer counsel, underwriter counsel, the PCAOB-registered auditor, the lead-left ECM team, and the financial printer (DFIN / Toppan / Workiva), with the working group iterating under a paper-trail discipline that supports each defendant's Section 11(b) due-diligence defence. The specific Reg S-K obligations (Item 101 business and five-year history, Item 105 principles-based risk factors with 15-page cap and plain-English, Item 303 MD&A with reasonably-likely trend disclosure and critical accounting estimates, Item 402 executive compensation with the EGC scaling alternative and 2022 pay-versus-performance, Item 404 related-person transactions, Item 407 governance, Items 504 / 506 use of proceeds and dilution, Item 202 capital stock and anti-takeover, Items 403 / 507 principal and selling stockholders, Item 512 undertakings and signatures, Item 601 exhibits) combine with the Reg S-X financial-statement obligations (Article 5 line items, Article 8 SRC scaled financials, Article 10 interim, the 3-2 audited footprint or the 2-2 EGC footprint under Securities Act §7(a)(2)(A)), the SAS 100 / AS 4105 interim-review discipline, the ASC 606 / 280 / 740 accounting interlocks, the Reg G and Item 10(e) non-GAAP reconciliation-and-prominence discipline, and the Reg S-T Rule 405 iXBRL tagging requirement to produce the effective registration statement. Chapter 5's EGC elections reduce the footprint; chapters 3 and 4 supply the expertised-portion substrate; `startup-finance-fundraising-curriculum` mod-111 supplies the auditable numbers.

Chapter 3 turns to PCAOB auditor engagement — auditor selection, the PCAOB-standards audit process, the pre-IPO reaudit programme where the prior auditor was not PCAOB-registered or where the prior audit does not meet PCAOB scope, the auditor's role as the expertised-portion signatory under Section 11, the specific comfort-letter and consent mechanics at pricing and at closing, and the ongoing quarterly-review and annual-audit cadence the post-IPO company will operate against.
