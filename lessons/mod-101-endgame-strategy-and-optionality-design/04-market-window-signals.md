# Market-Window Signals — Reading the Exit Window

## Why this matters

The endgame ranking (chapter 2) and the buyer map (chapter 3) both change under external conditions the company does not control. A sector-multiple compression cuts the same acquisition offer nearly in half. An IPO window that was open in January is closed by August and reopens the following spring. A strategic acquirer that was net-acquisitive two years ago is cutting cost this year and has no M&A budget. The founders who read these signals reliably run different transactions than the founders who don't — sometimes waiting six months to pick up a 30% price lift, sometimes accelerating a process by ninety days to close inside a window that is visibly narrowing.

This chapter installs a market-signal literacy. It is not a promise that you can time the market. It is a small set of *reproducible* readings you can maintain quarterly that keep your endgame ranking honest across cycles.

## What you are trying to read

Four macro-signal categories matter for endgame timing. Each has a different mechanism, and each has canonical data sources you can subscribe to or pull from.

### 1. IPO-window open / closed

Whether the public markets are receptive to new issuers of your sector and shape.

The "IPO window" is not a single binary. It has been useful to think in three sub-windows:

- **Sector receptivity** — is the public market currently rewarding new listings in your sector at premium multiples? For software / SaaS, the readings from public-comp indexes (below) are your primary source. Cyclical sectors — biotech, consumer, fintech — have their own indexes and their own windows that open and close on different beats.
- **Growth-vs-profitability preference** — public markets rotate between rewarding growth-at-any-cost and rewarding rule-of-40 / profitability. Your operating model is either well-adapted to the current preference or not. The gap between the two determines whether you are pricing at a strong multiple or a punitive one.
- **Recent-cohort proof** — how are the last 3–6 IPOs of shape roughly like yours trading post-IPO? Broken IPOs (breaking issue price, first-year missed guidance, class-action filings) narrow the window for the next cohort even when the underlying macro is unchanged.

### 2. M&A market volume and character

Whether M&A activity in your sector is contracting or expanding, and what shape of deal is closing.

- **Aggregate deal volume** — total announced M&A dollar volume in your sector, quarter over quarter. Sources: PitchBook, LSEG (formerly Refinitiv) M&A league tables, Mergermarket, S&P Capital IQ.
- **Deal-size mix** — is the volume concentrated in mega-deals (transformative combinations) or spread across tuck-ins? A sector with high volume but concentrated in mega-deals is not a sector where sub-scale companies are getting acquired.
- **Structure mix** — cash vs. stock-heavy vs. mixed. Cash-heavy M&A markets are where financial buyers are active; stock-heavy markets are where strategics are using currency.
- **Multiple direction** — are recent announced deals in your sector closing at expanding or compressing revenue / EBITDA multiples? mod-102 develops the valuation-anchoring depth; this chapter uses the direction of the multiple as a market-window signal.

### 3. Sector-multiple compression / expansion

Where public-company comparable multiples for your sector are trading — and, especially, how volatile that reading has been in the past 90 days.

Multiple compression is often the earliest warning that both the M&A market and the IPO window are shifting. Private-market valuations lag public-market multiples by 6–18 months, so a sharp compression in public multiples now is a *leading* indicator that private-market pricing is about to reset. This is directly relevant to timing:

- If you are considering a sell-side process, a proximate compression event may argue for going now versus waiting.
- If you are considering a primary financing, a proximate expansion may argue for holding — waiting will get you a higher price.
- If you are considering an IPO, the *volatility* of the multiple matters as much as its level; you cannot IPO into a rapidly-moving market.

### 4. Strategic-buyer appetite

Whether the strategics on your buyer map are net-acquisitive this year.

- **Earnings-call M&A language.** Read the transcripts of every strategic-acquirer earnings call in your buyer map's top-tier segment. Categorise the CEO's / CFO's language into "actively net acquisitive," "opportunistic," "cost-focused" (translation: not acquiring), and "returning capital" (translation: definitely not acquiring). This changes quarter to quarter.
- **Acquisition-cadence patterns.** How many acquisitions has each strategic done in the last 4 / 8 / 12 quarters? A strategic doing 3–5 acquisitions per year is in "buy" mode; a strategic that hasn't announced a deal in 8 quarters is either not shopping or is priced out of most deals.
- **Executive changes at target acquirers.** New CEO / new head of corp-dev often carry a strategy reset. A strategic thesis that was live under the outgoing executive may be dead under the incoming one; the reverse also happens.
- **Regulatory posture.** Under some antitrust regimes, large strategics may be constrained from acquiring at all in specific segments. This is a regime-level signal that shifts on political and case-law time-scales.

## The canonical data sources

Public and industry-standard sources you will want to know by name. This is not an exhaustive list, but any serious market-window read draws on some subset of these.

### IPO-window / public comparables

- **Meritech Capital public-comparables tables** — Meritech publishes a set of public-company comparables tables (SaaS and adjacent) with sector-multiple readings updated regularly. Widely-referenced by growth-equity and IPO-track practitioners. <https://www.meritechcapital.com/public-comparables>
- **Bessemer Cloud Index (BVP Nasdaq Emerging Cloud Index, NQCLOUD)** — Bessemer's public-cloud index tracks a curated basket of cloud-software companies with historical multiple / growth / rule-of-40 data. <https://cloudindex.bvp.com/>
- **Renaissance Capital IPO Center** — IPO calendar, first-day performance data, aftermarket performance by cohort. <https://www.renaissancecapital.com/ipo-center/stats>
- **SEC EDGAR** — the source of truth for confidential and public S-1 filings, DRSes, and offering data. <https://www.sec.gov/edgar/search-and-access>
- **Sector-specific indexes** — biotech: XBI (S&P Biotech ETF); fintech: FINX; consumer: various. Choose the index that most closely tracks your sector.

### M&A volume and deal terms

- **PitchBook** — subscription. Deep private-market and M&A activity data, valuations, deal-terms studies. Commonly used inside corp-dev and growth-equity shops.
- **LSEG (Refinitiv) M&A League Tables** — quarterly M&A league tables by advisor and by sector, publicly available in press-release form. <https://www.lseg.com/en/data-analytics/products/deals-intelligence>
- **Mergermarket** — subscription. M&A intelligence with early-signal reporting on deals in progress.
- **S&P Capital IQ** — subscription. Public-comp screens, precedent-transaction screens, ratio analysis.
- **ABA Deal Points Study** — the American Bar Association's periodic Deal Points Studies (Private Target M&A, Public Target M&A, Strategic Buyer / Private Equity Buyer) publish empirical distributions of deal terms actually seen in the market. Used heavily in negotiation to benchmark specific clauses. <https://www.americanbar.org/groups/business_law/committees/ma/deal_points/>

### Strategic-buyer intelligence

- **10-K, 10-Q, 8-K, and earnings-call transcripts** for every strategic in your buyer map — SEC EDGAR and the acquirer's investor-relations site. Transcripts also on Seeking Alpha (limited free), Bloomberg, and Motley Fool.
- **Acquisition announcements via 8-K** — every material acquisition by a public strategic has an 8-K within 4 business days. EDGAR search on "Item 1.01" or "Item 2.01" filters to the material events.
- **Antitrust posture** — DOJ Antitrust Division public press releases, FTC public actions, EU Commission decisions. Sector-relevant antitrust posture is a live-and-changing signal.

### Post-2024 IPO-window reopening dynamics

The IPO window post-2024 has specific characteristics worth naming:

- Sustained multi-quarter drought (2022–2024) followed by a re-opening tied to interest-rate normalisation and specific issuer categories (AI infrastructure, cybersecurity, healthcare IT, defence tech) leading.
- Growth-plus-profitability preference — issuers pricing at strong multiples share a rule-of-40 or better shape, and issuers that only have growth without a credible path to profitability are pricing at a discount.
- Return of the confidential DRS submission as the default choreography for tech issuers — bookbuild timelines, cornerstone-investor participation, and staggered lock-up releases now standard.
- Increased attention to secondary-market pre-IPO pricing as a market-window signal — the emergence of Nasdaq Private Market's tender-offer data, Forge Global's private-market volatility index, and the general rise of secondary as an IPO-alternative pressure-release valve (mod-106).

You do not need to memorise the post-2024 details; you need to have the *habit* of reading a small set of sources every quarter and updating the frame.

## The 90-day dashboard

A pattern that works: build a 90-day dashboard, one page, updated at the start of every quarter, that turns the raw signal into a directional read.

A minimum-viable structure:

**Section 1 — IPO window**

- Bessemer Cloud Index level (or your sector's index): current, 90-day high, 90-day low, direction. One-sentence commentary.
- Meritech public-comp median multiple for your sector-slice: current, direction from prior quarter. One-sentence commentary.
- Recent-cohort proof: 3–5 recent IPOs in your sector; where they are trading vs. issue price; any broken-issue cohort effect.
- Direction call: **Open** / **Narrowing** / **Closed** / **Reopening**. One-sentence justification.

**Section 2 — M&A market**

- Sector M&A dollar volume, last two quarters: direction.
- Deal-size mix: mega-deal share.
- Announced-multiple direction (compressing / stable / expanding).
- Direction call: **Buyers active** / **Selective** / **Compressed** / **Quiet**. One-sentence justification.

**Section 3 — Strategic-buyer appetite**

- Top-tier buyers from your map who reported this quarter; their M&A language classified.
- Executive changes at any tier-1 entrant.
- Any adjacent-category acquisition that changes the map (e.g., a strategic loses a partnership → becomes a plausible acquirer of you).
- Direction call: **Net acquisitive** / **Mixed** / **Cost-focused**. One-sentence justification.

**Section 4 — What this means for the frame**

- One sentence per candidate endgame (from chapter 1's list). Does this quarter's read move the ranking up, down, or leave it unchanged?
- One or two operating implications for the coming quarter.

The whole thing fits on a page. Its value is in the *cadence*, not the beauty — a page-a-quarter is enough for the board to see how the signals are moving, and enough for you to notice when your own read has been unchanged for too long (which usually means you have stopped reading the sources).

## Common failure modes

Three traps to avoid.

- **Reading only your own multiple.** Founders who watch only their own 409A or their own last-round valuation miss the direction of the market. Watch the public comps first, then your own valuation as a lagging indicator.
- **Trusting a single source.** Different providers have different definitions of "sector," different methodologies for cohort selection, and different biases. Cross-check: Meritech vs. Bessemer vs. your own hand-pulled comp table.
- **Treating window-open as urgency.** An open IPO window today does not automatically mean IPO now. The right response to an open window is to accelerate readiness *while* preserving the alternative (dual-track — mod-107). Founders who treat "window open" as a decision often over-commit to a specific path and lose optionality.

## The interaction with the endgame frame

Chapter 2 introduced the ranking of the five candidate endgame outcomes. Chapter 4 tells you that the ranking flips under external conditions you do not control. The discipline is:

1. Rank the outcomes with the current market-window read explicit in the reasoning.
2. Name the specific market-condition change that would flip the ranking (compression event, buyer appetite shift, IPO-window closure).
3. Watch for that condition to arrive; do not wait for it to be undeniable before updating the ranking.

That last point matters. The founders who mishandle market-window shifts are almost always the ones who saw the signal three months before they acted on it — but wanted to believe the shift was temporary. The dashboard is your protection against your own reluctance to update.

## Summary

Reading the exit window is a discipline, not a prediction. Four macro-signal categories — IPO-window sector receptivity, M&A market volume and character, sector-multiple direction, and strategic-buyer appetite — combine to produce a directional read on which endgame outcomes are most live this quarter. The canonical sources (Meritech, Bessemer Cloud Index, PitchBook, LSEG, Mergermarket, ABA Deal Points, EDGAR, earnings-call transcripts) are the shared vocabulary. A quarterly one-page dashboard turns the raw readings into a directional call that updates the endgame ranking without over-reacting to noise. The rest of the module presumes this literacy — the operating-decision audit in chapter 5, and the memo in chapter 6, both draw on the current market-window read as an input.
