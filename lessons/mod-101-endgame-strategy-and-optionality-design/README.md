# mod-101 — Endgame Strategy & Optionality Design

**Estimated effort:** 18 hours
**Stage:** GROWTH (works at earlier stages too — the whole point of this module is that it becomes cheaper the earlier you install it)
**Seat:** founder-CEO, CFO, GC, SVP-Corp-Dev, board-observer

This module frames the endgame decision *before* you are forced to make it. The five candidate outcomes — acquisition, IPO, secondary liquidity, stay-independent, responsible shutdown — do not sit at the end of the operating loop. They sit *above* it, and they cast shadows backward onto the financing terms you sign, the buyer relationships you tend (or don't), the board seats you fill, and the change-of-control clauses you initial in a commercial contract without reading. The founder-CEOs who show up at a transaction with real optionality did that upstream work years earlier. The ones who do not have one option and negotiate against it from a position of forced choice.

The module owns the *decision frame*. It does not re-teach the startup-as-a-system model or the founder-numbers slice ([`startup-foundations`](https://github.com/ai-startup-curriculum/startup-foundations) owns those), and it does not re-teach the ongoing-company financial infrastructure or cap-table maintenance ([`startup-finance-fundraising-curriculum`](https://github.com/ai-startup-curriculum/startup-finance-fundraising-curriculum) owns those). It sits on top of both and reasons about *which endgame* the operating machine is being pointed at.

## Learning objectives

By the end of this module you should be able to:

- Name the five candidate endgame outcomes for a specific company and defend the trade-offs among them across control, capital, time-to-outcome, employee outcomes, personal-brand impact, and investor return distribution.
- Maintain a rolling buyer map — strategic acquirers by adjacency and thesis, financial acquirers by mandate and fund vintage, market-structure players who might become acquirers — that the board can review quarterly, not a one-time slide.
- Read market-window signals — IPO-window indicators from public-comp indexes, M&A-market volume from the standard databases, sector-multiple compression or expansion, and strategic-buyer appetite shifts — and translate them into endgame-timing recommendations.
- Audit the operating decisions that pre-close endgame optionality — venture-debt covenants, warrant coverage, secondary restrictions, preference-stack overhang, ROFR / co-sale / drag-along mechanics, board composition, and change-of-control clauses in commercial contracts — and design them to keep options live rather than pre-closed.
- Author an endgame-optionality memo for a specific company: 3–5 candidate paths, trigger conditions that would move the company toward each, and the operating changes needed to preserve or open each path.

## Ownership boundary

This module frames the endgame decision. It does *not* re-teach:

- the **startup-as-a-system model, the stage ladder, or the founder-numbers slice** — `startup-foundations` owns those. When this module refers to *stage*, *runway*, *default alive vs. default dead*, or the *founder's operating loop*, it is calling upward into Foundations vocabulary.
- the **ongoing-company financial infrastructure** — three-statement model, cohort unit economics, cap-table *maintenance*, priced-round preferred-stock economics, board packs, monthly close — `startup-finance-fundraising-curriculum` owns those. This module uses those numbers as inputs into the endgame frame; it does not teach how to build them.
- the **deep valuation methodology** (Berkus, VC method, revenue multiples, LBO math) — that lives in `startup-finance-fundraising-curriculum` mod-106 and is used as an input here. mod-102 (M&A Market Structure) is where sell-side valuation-anchoring becomes an operating skill.

What this module *adds* on top: the decision frame that sits above the operating loop, the rolling-buyer-map discipline, the market-window signal literacy, the optionality-preserving-operating-decisions audit, and the endgame-optionality memo as a durable artefact.

## How to work through this module

Pick a real (or realistic hypothetical) growth-stage startup with a specific ARR / burn / cap-table / customer-concentration shape and carry it through every exercise. The module is worth exactly as much as the concreteness of the company you point it at. If you use a hypothetical, freeze the numbers in a short profile at the start (chapter 1's exercise leans on it) and don't let them drift.

Read a chapter, then do the matching exercise while the frame is fresh. Chapters 1–2 set the frame; chapters 3–5 install the three operating disciplines (buyer map, market-window read, optionality-preserving audit); chapter 6 pulls it all into the memo. The lab (published separately) then asks you to publish that memo for review.
