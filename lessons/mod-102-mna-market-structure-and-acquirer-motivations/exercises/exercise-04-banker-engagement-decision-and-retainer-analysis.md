# exercise-04: Banker Engagement Decision and Retainer Analysis

**Estimated effort:** 3 hours

## Objective

Construct the banker-engagement memo for your company's plausible sell-side process. Decide sole-mandate vs. dual-mandate vs. no-banker; if a banker, decide bulge-bracket vs. sector boutique vs. Big-Four vs. specialist; select 3 candidate firms and run a paper bake-off across them; then work through the fee-stack negotiation — retainer credit mechanics, success-fee percentages, definition of "transaction value," expense cap, tail structure, and conflict-disclosure obligations. By the end you should be able to defend the choice to your board and to sit across from a bank on the engagement-letter redline knowing which lines to hold and which to concede.

## Background

This exercise covers material from:

- [Chapter 4 — The Investment-Bank Engagement Decision](../04-investment-bank-engagement-decision.md)

Chapters 3 (buyer map) and 5 (pre-marketing document set) are supporting inputs — the buyer-set shape from exercise-03 is the primary driver of which firms fit, and the pre-marketing document work in exercise-05 is what the banker's associate team will scaffold once engaged.

## Prerequisites

- The company profile you have carried through mod-101 and earlier mod-102 exercises.
- Your completed buyer map from exercise-03 (the top-tier composition strongly shapes which banker fits).
- Access to the candidate banks' public materials: recent M&A league-table positions (LSEG / Refinitiv Deals Intelligence quarterly summaries are publicly cited), transaction announcements they have been named on in the last 24 months (available on their firm websites and in press releases), and (where available) their public partner-and-managing-director bios.
- Access to the ABA Business Law Section's Deal Points Studies for context on market fee ranges (see resources.md).
- Optional: 1–2 conversations with founders or CFOs who have run a sell-side with a banker in the last 3 years. These are the highest-signal source for what specific firms are like as a client.

## Tasks

### 1. Set the deal parameters

Fix a set of parameters for the plausible transaction. You will use these throughout the exercise; changing them mid-way invalidates the fee-math analysis.

- **Plausible-transaction-value band.** Conservative / midpoint / optimistic (three-point range). Use the shape classification from exercise-02 and any valuation input from `startup-finance-fundraising-curriculum` mod-106 to set it.
- **Consideration mix expectation.** Cash-heavy / stock-heavy / mixed, with rough percentages, tied to the acquirer sub-types most likely in your top tier.
- **Buyer-set shape.** How many strategics in the top tier, how many financials, geographic spread, relationship-depth distribution.
- **Timeline.** From banker mandate signing to plausible signing — typically 6–9 months for a well-run process. Note earlier if a specific market-window constraint pulls it in.
- **Sector-complexity tag.** High / medium / low sector-specificity (chapter 4's frame).
- **Process-complexity tag.** Simple / standard / complex (chapter 4's frame).
- **Founder / CFO bandwidth self-assessment.** Honest — what fraction of a founder-CEO and a CFO week can be dedicated to sell-side work over the 6–9 month window?

Write these on one page. This is the fact-set every subsequent decision references.

### 2. Make the sole-vs-dual-vs-no-banker decision

Work through chapter 4's six-variable framework:

1. Deal-size band.
2. Buyer-set shape.
3. Sector complexity.
4. Process-complexity requirements.
5. Founder / CFO bandwidth.
6. Board and investor preferences.

Write a 1–2 page decision memo naming:

- The choice — sole mandate, dual mandate, or no banker.
- The reasoning grounded in the six variables. Be specific: "dual mandate is not warranted here because our top-tier buyer set is dominated by strategics where a single sector-boutique can carry both financial and strategic buyer coverage" is defensible; "sole mandate feels right" is not.
- The specific counter-considerations you weighed and rejected. If you considered dual mandate and rejected it, name why. If you considered no-banker and rejected it, name why.
- The board-and-investor-preference check. Note any preferences you know of and how they influenced the choice.

If your choice is no-banker, jump to task 6 (no-banker process design) and skip tasks 3–5. If your choice is sole or dual mandate, continue.

### 3. Shortlist 3 candidate firms

For the sole-mandate case: shortlist 3 firms across at least 2 of the four categories (bulge-bracket / sector boutique / Big-Four / specialist). For the dual-mandate case: shortlist 4 firms across the coverage split you intend to run.

For each firm, produce a one-page profile:

- **Category and positioning.** Where do they sit in the market? Firm size, deal-flow scale, sector focus.
- **Recent league-table position.** Their public standing in tech M&A (or your sector) over the last 4–8 quarters. LSEG / Refinitiv league tables are the shared reference; boutique-specific rankings (e.g., Qatalyst's tech-M&A track record) may be published in the trade press.
- **Recent relevant transactions.** 3–5 transactions the firm has led (or been named on) in the last 24 months that are structurally comparable to yours — same shape, similar buyer type, similar deal size. Note the transaction, the client, the buyer, the announcement date, and (where public) the reported outcome.
- **Named team.** Who would run the mandate? Senior partner, managing director, deal-team lead. Include tenure and prior deals.
- **Buyer coverage.** Their strength in the specific top-tier and second-tier acquirers on your buyer map. Note any specific relationships you know about — a partner who worked at a top-tier acquirer, a prior deal with a top-tier acquirer, publicly-cited relationships.
- **Strengths for your specific process.** 2–3 specific things this firm brings that would matter for your deal.
- **Weaknesses / risks.** 2–3 specific concerns — attention level given your deal size, gaps in buyer coverage, senior-team-vs-junior-team attention ratio, potential conflicts.

### 4. Run the paper bake-off

Score each of the 3 shortlisted firms across at least 5 dimensions:

| Dimension | Weight | Firm A | Firm B | Firm C |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Buyer coverage for your top-tier set | | | | |
| Sector expertise | | | | |
| Senior-partner attention (fit vs. their attention economics) | | | | |
| Process management / infrastructure | | | | |
| Fee expectations relative to value delivered | | | | |
| Cultural fit with your team | | | | |
| Board / investor comfort with the firm | | | | |

Score each on 1–5. Explicitly weight the dimensions to reflect what actually matters for your deal (buyer coverage often dominates for a competitive process; sector expertise dominates for a narrow-buyer process; process management dominates when founder-CEO / CFO bandwidth is tight).

Pick the winner and write a 1-paragraph selection defense grounded in the scores.

### 5. Negotiate the engagement letter

Assume the winning firm has sent you their first-draft engagement letter. It leans toward the bank on every negotiable point (chapter 4's realistic assumption). Work through the specific line items:

**5.1 — Retainer**
- **Their proposed structure.** A plausible first-draft: $75k upfront + $50k / month for a 12-month engagement, non-refundable.
- **Your target.** Reduce non-refundable portion; credit 100% against success fee; cap the monthly if the process extends. Write your specific counter-proposal.

**5.2 — Success-fee structure**
- **Their proposed structure.** A plausible first-draft: modified Lehman at the small-deal end, or a tiered percentage at the mid-to-large end (e.g., 1.25% on the first $500M, 1% on the next $500M, 0.75% above). Include a minimum fee (e.g., $5M) that binds at the small end.
- **Your target.** Negotiate the percentage curve, the minimum, and — critically — the definition of "transaction value" (see 5.3). Consider whether to introduce a threshold-and-kicker structure that pays the bank an incremental percentage above a defined threshold (aligns the bank with pushing on price; but poorly-designed kickers create perverse incentives to hold out for a higher price when a lower certain deal is available).

**5.3 — Definition of "transaction value"**
- Walk through each component and take a specific position:
  - Rollover equity — included at face value, present value, or excluded entirely?
  - Assumed debt — included or excluded?
  - Cash retention pool for post-close team retention — included in the base or carved out?
  - CVRs and contingent consideration — face value, present value at signing, or only-when-earned?
  - Earn-outs — same question as CVRs.
  - Escrow / holdback — included at face or present-value discounted for expected forfeiture?
  - Working-capital adjustment — how does the base react to positive vs. negative working-capital adjustments?
  - What happens if the deal is renegotiated post-signing at a lower value?

For each, write your target position and the fallback you would accept.

**5.4 — Expense structure and cap**
- **Their proposed structure.** At-cost pass-through with no cap. Small administrative fee on top (e.g., 3%).
- **Your target.** Cap total expenses at a specific dollar amount (e.g., $250k–$500k depending on deal complexity); require pre-approval above a threshold (e.g., $25k); remove the administrative fee.

**5.5 — Tail / trailing rights**
- **Their proposed structure.** 24-month tail on any buyer they contacted during the mandate.
- **Your target.** 12-month tail; include a named-list schedule attached to the engagement letter distinguishing pre-existing contacts (not covered) from banker-introduced (covered); include a written definition of "contacted" or "introduced" that ties to a documented outreach rather than a passing mention.

**5.6 — Termination and term**
- **Their proposed structure.** 18-month term with limited termination rights for the client.
- **Your target.** 12-month term; termination-for-cause provision that allows exit if performance is materially deficient (defined objectively where possible — e.g., failure to secure a threshold number of qualifying bids by a defined date).

**5.7 — Indemnification and conflict disclosure**
- **Their proposed structure.** Broad client indemnification of the bank; passive conflict-disclosure.
- **Your target.** Retain the standard indemnification but negotiate the fraud / gross-negligence / wilful-misconduct carve-out. Require written conflict disclosure at engagement covering: prior advisory relationships with any prospective bidder, prior transactions with any prospective bidder in the last 24 months, any personal / family / financial relationship between banker personnel and bidder personnel, and any staple-financing arrangements. Require ongoing update throughout the mandate. Treat undisclosed conflicts as engagement-terminating events.

**5.8 — Buyer-list schedule**
- Draft the actual buyer-list schedule you would attach: for each name on your top-tier and second-tier buyer map, identify whether the acquirer is a "pre-existing contact" of yours (a specific counterpart, a specific prior conversation you can point to) or a banker-introduction candidate. This schedule is what the tail-rights language attaches to; it is worth authoring specifically.

Produce a **redlined engagement letter summary** (2–3 pages) that names each line item, the bank's proposed language, your target, and your fallback. This is the artefact you would hand to outside counsel for the actual redline.

### 6. If no-banker: install the process muscle

If you chose no-banker in task 2, this section replaces tasks 3–5. Design the machinery you will install instead:

- **Outside sell-side legal counsel.** Name a specific firm and named partner (Cooley, Wilson Sonsini, Gunderson Dettmer, Fenwick, Latham, DLA Piper, Orrick, or others with tech-M&A depth). Note their recent sell-side experience.
- **Specialist advisor engagement (optional).** An ex-banker acting in an advisory rather than mandate capacity. Note the specific gaps they would cover, the fee structure (fixed / hourly, not success), and a named-candidate profile if you have one.
- **Finance-team readiness.** Named owner (CFO or Head of Finance), their sell-side experience, and the specific workstreams they will run (Q-of-E preparation, working-capital model, three-statement model roll-up, historical financials audit or review confirmation).
- **Process cadence.** Draft the process calendar — CIM (or equivalent) authoring window, buyer-outreach cadence, indication-of-interest deadlines, management-presentation slots, definitive-round diligence, signing target.
- **Buyer outreach owner.** Who runs each of the top-tier outreach touches? Founder-CEO for peer-CEO conversations; who for corp-dev outreach where a banker would normally own it?
- **Board discipline.** What board-visibility cadence and what documented board-process protocol replaces the fiduciary-defensibility-by-banker-mandate that a banker engagement would provide? mod-109 is the depth reference; for this exercise, name the discipline.
- **What you sacrifice.** Be honest — brand signal to acquirer boards, process-management scale, presumption of process discipline in fiduciary review. Note how you propose to compensate.

### 7. Fee-stack forecast

Build a small fee model that shows the total banker economics under three scenarios: conservative (deal value at the low end of your range), midpoint (at the midpoint), optimistic (at the high end). Include:

- Total retainer paid over the assumed timeline.
- Success fee at each scenario, applying your negotiated percentage curve and minimum.
- Total expenses at cap.
- Total banker economics (all of the above), and the effective percentage of deal value that represents.

If dual mandate: repeat for both banks and show the aggregate.

Contrast this against the "value delivered" back-of-the-envelope: at the midpoint scenario, what is the plausible deal-value uplift attributable to running with this specific banker (buyer coverage, competitive-tension creation, negotiation) versus running with the second-place banker or no banker? You will not have precision here, but a defensible estimate (with reasoning) is worth writing down; it is what your board wants to hear.

### 8. Board memo

Draft a 1-page board memo capturing the recommendation and the rationale:

- The choice (sole / dual / no banker, and if banker, which firm).
- The rationale grounded in the six-variable framework.
- Fee expectations at each scenario.
- Conflict-disclosure discipline.
- Alternative considered and rejected (the runner-up firm, or the no-banker option).
- Next steps and timeline.

## Starter guidance

Four anti-patterns to watch out for:

- **Brand chasing.** Selecting a bulge-bracket bank because the brand is recognisable, even though your deal is well below their attention threshold. You will get a junior team and generic process; you will pay tier-1 fees for tier-3 attention. If your deal size does not warrant the bulge-bracket, the sector boutique or Big-Four with senior attention is a better choice on almost every axis.
- **Fee-percentage tunnel vision.** Optimising the success-fee percentage while conceding on the transaction-value definition. The definition can move total banker economics by 20%+ on the same nominal deal value; get it right first, then negotiate the percentages.
- **Under-specified tail rights.** Accepting a 24-month tail on "all buyers contacted" without a named-list schedule. This makes the second-attempt sell-side (if the first attempt fails) hostage to the original banker. The named-list schedule is the specific protection.
- **Conflict-disclosure passivity.** Signing an engagement letter without a written conflict disclosure. Especially for boutiques with narrow sector focus, conflicts are common — the bank has usually advised at least one plausible bidder on something in the last 24 months, and staple-financing conflicts (in financial-buyer contexts) are worth surfacing early rather than at the surprise-during-diligence moment.

## Acceptance criteria

You can demonstrate that:

- Deal parameters are set as a one-page fact-set.
- Sole-vs-dual-vs-no-banker decision is a written memo grounded in the six-variable framework.
- If banker: 3 shortlisted firms each have a one-page profile, and the paper bake-off table is scored with weighted dimensions.
- Winning firm is selected with a defended selection paragraph.
- Engagement-letter redline covers retainer, success-fee curve, transaction-value definition (all sub-components), expenses, tail (with buyer-list schedule), termination, indemnification, and conflict disclosure — each with the bank's proposed language, your target, and your fallback.
- If no-banker: alternative process machinery is designed with named owners, cadence, and sacrifice acknowledgement.
- Fee-stack forecast covers conservative / midpoint / optimistic scenarios with total banker economics and an effective-percentage-of-deal-value read.
- Board memo (one page) is drafted and would be presentable at the next board meeting.

## Reflection

Add a short reflection:

1. Which of the three shortlisted firms would you have selected without doing the bake-off, and did the bake-off change your view? Where specifically did the paper bake-off surface something you would have otherwise missed?
2. Which line in the engagement letter is the one you would most reluctantly concede in a redline round? What does that tell you about your priorities?
3. If your deal ends up 30% below the midpoint scenario, do the fee economics still make sense for both sides? What would you do differently at engagement to protect that downside?
4. What is the single biggest process-quality risk if you had chosen the runner-up firm (or the no-banker option) instead?

## Stretch goals

- **Ex-client outreach.** For each of the 3 shortlisted firms, try to schedule a 20-minute call with a founder / CFO who used them for a sell-side in the last 3 years. Ask specifically: (a) did the senior team stay engaged through the mandate, (b) how did they handle the final-round bidding, (c) what would you have negotiated differently in the engagement letter with hindsight, (d) would you use them again? Update your shortlist profiles with what you learn.
- **Conflict-disclosure walk-through.** For your winning firm, do the public-record work to identify any known conflicts — advisory relationships with acquirers on your top-tier list (Bloomberg / Reuters / press-release archives), M&A league-table positions on prior deals involving those acquirers, staple-financing arrangements they run in the buyout-PE space. Note what you would specifically raise at the engagement kickoff.
- **Fee-tier comparison across firms.** Where possible (published league-table data, industry reporting), compare the winning firm's typical fee tiers against the runner-up. Note where the fee difference is small enough that value-delivered dominates and where the fee difference is large enough that the runner-up's economics deserve reconsideration.
- **Rural Metro / Del Monte study.** Read (or re-read) the *In re Rural Metro Corp. Stockholders Litigation* (Del. Ch. 2014) and *In re Del Monte Foods Co. Shareholders Litigation* (Del. Ch. 2011) opinions with attention to what specific banker conduct the court criticised. Translate the findings into 3–5 specific conflict-disclosure or process-discipline items you would add to your engagement-letter language.
