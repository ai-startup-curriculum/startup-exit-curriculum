# Resources for mod-102-mna-market-structure-and-acquirer-motivations

Primary source documentation, practitioner canon, data providers, empirical deal-terms studies, and legal-and-regulatory references used by this module. Prefer these over secondary write-ups: they are authoritative, and — for the empirical studies and league-table sources especially — the sources you cite in a board memo or a banker discussion need to be sources the other side can re-check.

## Sell-side M&A practitioner canon

The specific frames in chapters 1–6 draw on this set of writing. All of it is worth reading directly.

- **Joshua Rosenbaum and Joshua Pearl — *Investment Banking: Valuation, LBOs, M&A, and IPOs* (3rd edition, Wiley)** — the standard sell-side reference used in banker training programmes. The comparable-companies and precedent-transactions chapters live in `startup-finance-fundraising-curriculum` mod-106 (per ownership boundary); the sell-side-process chapters (Chapter 6 in the 3rd edition, and the merger-consequences analysis) are the primary references for this module's process depth. <https://www.wiley.com/en-us/Investment+Banking%3A+Valuation%2C+LBOs%2C+M%26A%2C+and+IPOs-p-9781119706182>
- **Alexandra Reed Lajoux — *The Art of M&A* (5th edition, McGraw-Hill)** — the practitioner encyclopedia across the deal lifecycle. The sell-side process, buyer identification, and pre-marketing chapters supplement this module's frame. <https://www.mhprofessional.com/9781260121780-usa-the-art-of-ma-fifth-edition-a-merger-acquisition-buyout-guide>
- **Justin Roberts (ed.) — *The Practical M&A Manual*** — practitioner reference for the sell-side workflow. Useful as a cross-reference on the CIM structure and management-presentation choreography.

## Delaware fiduciary framework — sell-side specific

Delaware case law on sell-side process defensibility, banker conflicts, and superior-proposal handling. mod-109 owns the full depth; the specific cases named in chapters 4 and 6 are here for direct reference.

- **In re Rural Metro Corp. Stockholders Litigation (Del. Ch. 2014)** — the seminal case on sell-side banker liability for aiding and abetting board breach of fiduciary duty, arising from undisclosed banker conflicts and process manipulation. Referenced in chapter 4's banker-conflict discipline. Opinion: <https://courts.delaware.gov/opinions/> (search "Rural Metro").
- **In re Del Monte Foods Co. Shareholders Litigation (Del. Ch. 2011)** — leading case on staple-financing conflicts in sell-side context. Referenced in chapter 4. <https://courts.delaware.gov/opinions/> (search "Del Monte").
- **Revlon v. MacAndrews & Forbes Holdings (Del. 1986)** — the change-of-control fiduciary standard that governs sell-side process when the board's Revlon duties are triggered. Referenced in chapter 6 for the pre-marketing fiduciary-out discussion. <https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/1573024/revlon-inc-v-macandrews-forbes-holdings-inc/>
- **Corwin v. KKR Financial Holdings (Del. 2015)** — the doctrine that a fully informed uncoerced stockholder vote can cleanse (some) fiduciary breaches, materially shaping the post-signing communication discipline. mod-109 develops the full framework. <https://courts.delaware.gov/opinions/> (search "Corwin").
- **Kahn v. M&F Worldwide Corp. (Del. 2014) — MFW dual protection** — the standard for controlled-transaction fiduciary review, including the special-committee and majority-of-minority-stockholder-vote requirements. Relevant to take-private-consortium scenarios from chapter 1. <https://courts.delaware.gov/opinions/> (search "M&F Worldwide").
- **Weinberger v. UOP (Del. 1983)** — the entire-fairness standard for conflict transactions and the fair-price / fair-dealing framework. <https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/1465816/weinberger-v-uop-inc/>
- **Delaware Court of Chancery — opinions archive** — the primary source for reading current M&A opinions in full. <https://courts.delaware.gov/chancery/>
- **Delaware General Corporation Law (DGCL)** — the underlying corporate law framework. <https://delcode.delaware.gov/title8/c001/index.shtml>
- **DGCL §262 — appraisal rights** — statutory right that shapes fiduciary and process discipline. <https://delcode.delaware.gov/title8/c001/sc09/index.shtml>

## Filings and regulatory sources

- **SEC EDGAR** — the source of truth for 8-K filings (Item 2.01 — Completion of Acquisition or Disposition; Item 1.01 — Entry into a Material Definitive Agreement; Item 5.02 — leadership changes tied to transactions), DEF 14A merger-vote proxies, and tender-offer materials. The primary source for acquirer M&A history in chapter 3's buyer-map evidence sub-diligences. <https://www.sec.gov/edgar/search-and-access>
- **SEC — Regulation M-A (Item 1000 series)** — the disclosure requirements for M&A transactions, tender offers, and going-private transactions. <https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-17/chapter-II/part-229/subject-group-ECFR3f2f7b8a2f8f8b1>
- **SEC — Rule 14e-3** — the tender-offer insider-trading rule that governs pre-announcement M&A discussions in public-company contexts. <https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/17/240.14e-3>
- **SEC — Regulation FD** — the fair-disclosure rule that shapes public-company M&A communications. <https://www.sec.gov/rules/final/33-7881.htm>
- **FINRA — Rule 5150** — the fairness-opinion disclosure rule for FINRA-member firms. Referenced in chapter 4's banker-engagement discussion. <https://www.finra.org/rules-guidance/rulebooks/finra-rules/5150>
- **Hart-Scott-Rodino (HSR) Antitrust Improvements Act — Premerger Notification Program (FTC)** — the reporting thresholds, filing-fee tiers, and waiting-period mechanics that shape transaction timing. Thresholds are updated annually. <https://www.ftc.gov/enforcement/premerger-notification-program>
- **CFIUS — Committee on Foreign Investment in the United States** — national-security review for transactions involving foreign acquirers. Relevant when acquirer sub-type analysis includes foreign strategics or foreign LPs in a financial buyer's fund. <https://home.treasury.gov/policy-issues/international/the-committee-on-foreign-investment-in-the-united-states-cfius>
- **DOJ Antitrust Division and FTC Bureau of Competition — press releases and enforcement actions** — for tracking sector-relevant enforcement posture that affects acquirer appetite. DOJ: <https://www.justice.gov/atr> FTC: <https://www.ftc.gov/about-ftc/bureaus-offices/bureau-competition>

## Ownership boundary — where the depth lives

This module owns the market-structure and pre-marketing layer of sell-side M&A. It does not re-teach the underlying mechanics. When a specific mechanic is needed:

- **Valuation methodology at Rosenbaum-Pearl comparable-and-precedent depth (Berkus, VC method, revenue multiples, LBO math, DCF, precedent-transactions screens)** → `startup-finance-fundraising-curriculum` mod-106. This module uses valuation as an *input* to buyer conversations and as the framing for the CIM's financial section; it does not re-teach how the numbers are built.
- **Rolling buyer-map maintenance as an ongoing-company discipline (the standing stage-0 muscle before pre-marketing begins)** → mod-101 in this track. This module extends the map to sell-side-execution depth; it does not re-teach the ongoing-company hygiene.
- **Endgame decision framing (the five candidate outcomes, market-window signals, optionality-preserving operating decisions)** → mod-101 in this track. Scenario E in exercise-06 explicitly points back to the mod-101 chapter 6 endgame-optionality memo discipline.
- **Deal structure and consideration design (asset vs. stock vs. reverse-triangular, earn-out mechanics, escrow, R&W insurance design, tax under IRC §368 / §338(h)(10) / §280G / §409A / §1202, HSR / CFIUS clearance mechanics)** → mod-103 in this track. When this module refers to consideration mix, it is at the choose-the-shape level, not the draft-the-mechanics level.
- **LOI and definitive-agreement authoring, no-shop / fiduciary-out mechanics, MAC / MAE clauses, indemnification structure, R&W insurance policy interaction with the SPA** → mod-104 in this track.
- **Sell-side data-room construction and diligence-workstream response (folder structure, permissions, VDR selection, Q&A management, diligence-request response cadence)** → mod-105 in this track. This module coordinates *with* the data-room build so the CIM narrative and the data-room contents are consistent (chapter 5's narrative-consistency matrix and exercise-05's task 5 make this coordination concrete); it does not teach how to construct the room.
- **Secondary transactions (founder secondary sizing, tender-offer design, forward-contract secondaries, ROFR choreography)** → mod-106 in this track.
- **IPO-readiness and execution (S-1 authoring, PCAOB audit, SOX ICFR, JOBS Act EGC, bookbuild, direct listing, SPAC alternatives), including the M&A-vs-IPO dual-track pattern** → mod-107 and mod-108 in this track. Scenario C in exercise-06 (fundraise-vs-sale) is the smaller-scale analog; the IPO-vs-M&A dual-track has its own choreography developed there.
- **Transaction governance and Delaware fiduciary-duty analysis at deal-scale (Revlon triggering, Corwin cleansing, MFW dual protection, Weinberger entire fairness, special-committee construction, fairness-opinion procurement, ISS / Glass Lewis engagement, Rural Metro / Del Monte banker-conflict depth)** → mod-109 in this track. This module surfaces the pre-marketing choices that later become fiduciary-duty questions; it does not adjudicate them.
- **Transaction communications and stakeholder management (employees, customers, partners, press, regulators, activists)** → mod-110 in this track.
- **Post-close integration mechanics (100-day plan, retention plan execution, cultural integration)** → mod-111 in this track. This module uses integration-appetite as a buyer-map dimension; it does not re-teach how to execute integration.
- **Wind-down mechanics (ABC, Ch 7 vs. Ch 11, Section 363, WARN Act, officer personal-liability watch)** → mod-113 in this track.

> Data-provider URLs, empirical-study release cadences, and specific numeric thresholds (HSR filing thresholds, league-table cutoffs, fee percentages at market) update at least annually and sometimes mid-year. When an exercise or memo cites a specific value, follow the link, snapshot the URL and date in your notes, and use that; do not rely on numbers that may be stale in this document. Bulge-bracket and boutique firms' recent-transaction lists on their own websites are the most reliable source for firm-specific league-table position at any given moment.
