# mod-108 — IPO Execution: Roadshow, Pricing, Greenshoe, Lock-Ups & IPO Alternatives

**Estimated effort:** 26 hours
**Stage:** MATURE
**Seat:** founder-CEO, CFO, GC, SVP-Corp-Dev, VP-IR, investment-banker

This module picks up where mod-107 leaves off. The company has cleared the readiness bar (chapter 1 of mod-107), the S-1 is drafted and cleared through the SEC comment cycle (mod-107 chapter 2), the PCAOB audit is complete (mod-107 chapter 3), SOX 404 is in flight (mod-107 chapter 4), the EGC / Rule 10A-3 / listing-exchange decisions are made (mod-107 chapters 5–7), and the org meeting has kicked off the workstream (mod-107 chapter 8). What remains is the *transaction execution* itself — choosing the underwriter syndicate, marketing the deal to institutional investors under the JOBS Act's testing-the-waters privilege and the SEC's gun-jumping rules, choreographing the roadshow, pricing and allocating the offering, running Regulation M stabilisation and the greenshoe in the aftermarket, structuring the lock-up architecture, and — for issuers where the traditional IPO is the wrong instrument — choosing among the direct-listing, SPAC / de-SPAC, DPO, and Reg A+ alternatives.

Every one of these workstreams is intensely rule-bound. The gun-jumping prohibition in Section 5 of the Securities Act sits behind the JOBS Act's testing-the-waters privilege; Regulation M sits behind the greenshoe and the syndicate's aftermarket stabilisation; SEC Rule 5315 / NYSE Rule 102.01B sit behind the direct listing; the 2024 SEC SPAC rules sit behind the de-SPAC. A founder-CEO / CFO / GC who reads a marketing calendar without reading the rulebook behind it is a founder who signs a comment letter later.

> Reminder: this module is education, not legal / tax / accounting / investment advice. A live IPO engages SEC-registered underwriters, PCAOB-registered auditors, qualified securities counsel, and specialist tax and communications advisors. The Reg-M / Section 5 / Rule 10b-5 exposures are personal-liability exposures for officers and directors, and they attach whether or not the founder read the rule.

## Learning objectives

By the end of this module you should be able to:

- Run an underwriter bake-off, choose the lead-left and joint-lead-left banks, construct the co-manager syndicate, negotiate the gross-spread economics, and defend the syndicate construction to the board and to the pre-IPO investor base.
- Author testing-the-waters communications under the JOBS Act EGC / Section 5(d) framework — the QIB / IAI audience limit, the SEC gun-jumping compliance perimeter, the FAQ / Q&A discipline that keeps messaging consistent with the S-1 and the confidential DRS.
- Choreograph a roadshow — the T-14 to T-1 pricing week, pilot fishing, cornerstone conversations, mega-fund one-on-ones, analyst-day mechanics, and the daily book-building read that reprices the range.
- Price the offering and allocate — the initial-range convention, the range-revision decision, the pricing meeting, the allocation methodology across long-only / crossover / hedge-fund / cornerstone / employee-directed-share-programme / friends-and-family / retail buckets.
- Apply Regulation M (Rules 101 and 102) and manage the 15% greenshoe over-allotment — stabilising bids, syndicate covering, naked shorts in hot deals, penalty bids, and the lead-left's syndicate-manager role in the first 30 days.
- Design the lock-up architecture — the 180-day standard, the underwriter's early-release discretion, earnings-window and material-event carve-outs, staggered releases, and the aftermarket-pricing consequence at expiry.
- Choose among IPO alternatives — direct listing (Spotify / Slack / Coinbase / Warby Parker pattern under SEC Rule 5315 and NYSE Rule 102.01B, with the 2020 amendment authorising primary-offering component), SPAC / de-SPAC (post-2024 SEC-rules co-registrant liability, PSLRA safe-harbour removal, sponsor-comp disclosure, minimum-book-value test), Direct Public Offering (small-issuer alternative), Reg A+ Tier 2 (up to $75M / 12 months, mini-IPO consumer-brand alternative).

## How to work through this module

Pick a specific pre-IPO company (real, in-flight, or a realistic hypothetical you carry through the module) with a specific sector, ARR, growth rate, cap-table shape, and geographic footprint. Every exercise is worth what the specificity of the company is worth. Freeze the profile at the start of exercise 01 and carry it through.

Read a chapter, then do the matching exercise while the frame is fresh. Chapters 1–2 install the pre-marketing regime; chapters 3–4 install the live-marketing regime; chapters 5–6 install the post-pricing aftermarket regime; chapter 7 installs the alternatives frame. Exercise 07 and 08 apply the alternatives frame to a company for which the traditional IPO may not be the right instrument.
