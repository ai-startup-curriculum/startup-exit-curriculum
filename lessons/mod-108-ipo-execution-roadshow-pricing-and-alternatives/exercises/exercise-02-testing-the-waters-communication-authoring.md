# exercise-02: Testing-the-Waters Communication Authoring

**Estimated effort:** 3 hours

## Objective

Author a complete, counsel-reviewable testing-the-waters (TTW) packet for the pre-IPO company you froze at the start of exercise-01, grounded in the JOBS Act Section 5(d) privilege and the SEC gun-jumping perimeter. The packet should be the artefact you would hand to the lead-left and to issuer counsel as the starting point of a TTW-review working-group call: a QIB / IAI target list, a TTW slide deck with counsel-ready talking points, a meeting-log template, an FAQ / Q&A master, a publicity policy, and a Rule 135 pre-filing announcement draft. By the end of the exercise you should be able to defend every piece of content against the specific statute and rule that governs it.

## Background

This exercise covers material from:

- [Chapter 2 — Testing the Waters and Gun-Jumping Compliance under the JOBS Act](../02-testing-the-waters-and-gun-jumping.md)

The gun-jumping perimeter is a *liability* perimeter, not a style guide. Section 5(c) prohibits any offer before filing; Section 5(d) opens a narrow carve-out for QIB / IAI conversations; the Rule 163A / 168 / 169 / 135 / 134 regime defines what else can be said and when. This exercise forces you to author real documents and defend each piece of content against the specific rule.

## Prerequisites

- The same frozen company profile from exercise-01 (sector, ARR, growth rate, cap-table, target offering size, listing-exchange target, pre-IPO investor base).
- The lead-left and joint-book-runner selection from exercise-01's selection memo (you will coordinate the TTW packet with them in a real deal).
- Access to SEC EDGAR, Rule 144A, Rule 501, the SEC's JOBS Act materials, and specific comparable-deal S-1 risk-factor sets (freely available). A recent comparable issuer's `EX-99` filings often include post-roadshow materials that can serve as a shape-reference.

## Tasks

### 1. The QIB / IAI target list

Draft a target list of 40-80 institutional accounts that meet the Rule 144A QIB definition or the Rule 501(a) IAI definition. Organise the list by category:

- **Long-only mega-funds.** T. Rowe Price, Fidelity, Wellington, Capital Group, BlackRock, Vanguard, State Street, Franklin Templeton, Baillie Gifford, and specific sector-sleeves relevant to your sector.
- **Crossover and growth investors.** D1, Coatue, Tiger Global, Dragoneer, General Atlantic, TCV, Light Street, Lone Pine, Whale Rock, Durable Capital.
- **Sovereign-wealth and strategic-institutional accounts.** GIC, Temasek, ADIA, Mubadala, PIF, Future Fund, and specific pension / endowment accounts where relevant.
- **Hedge funds with specific IPO participation history** in your sector (named).
- **Specific pre-IPO existing-investor conversion into post-IPO conversations.** Where appropriate — the specific insider accounts your lead-left expects to re-up on the deal.

For each listed account, record: the account name, the Rule 144A QIB classification (bank, insurance company, registered investment company, employee benefit plan with assets ≥ $5M under common management, broker-dealer under the Rule 144A specific thresholds, or qualifying entity under the Rule 144A $100M-in-non-affiliate-securities test) or Rule 501(a) IAI classification; the senior PM or sector head on the account; the specific prior comparable-deal participation history; and the lead-left-sales-force-relationship status (strong / moderate / needs-development).

Mark accounts you believe are IAI but not QIB as such — the Section 5(d) privilege reaches both, but your counsel documentation should distinguish them. Do NOT include retail accounts, individual-accredited-investor accounts (non-institutional), or any account that would not qualify under Rule 144A or Rule 501(a). If you cannot confirm a specific account's classification, mark `<!-- needs-research: QIB/IAI qualification for <account> -->` rather than guessing.

### 2. The TTW slide deck

Draft a 12-18 slide TTW deck covering (do NOT include a price target, a projected valuation, a projected offering size, or any specific-price-conditioning content — these are prohibited under Section 5(d) and the S-1-consistency rule):

- **Cover slide.** Company name, "testing-the-waters materials under Section 5(d) of the Securities Act" footer, specific confidentiality legend, and the SEC-mandated-equivalent notice that these materials are not an offer to sell and any offer would be made only through the registration statement.
- **The business.** Company overview, product, customer value proposition, GTM motion.
- **The market.** Addressable-market framing (TAM / SAM / SOM) with specific-source citations — every market-sizing number is reproducible in the S-1 Item 101 business section.
- **The financial model.** Historical revenue, growth, gross margin, NRR, Rule-of-40 — every number reconciles to the S-1 financials.
- **Unit economics and cohort data** where specific-reproducibility against the S-1 is given.
- **Competitive positioning.** Named competitors; specific differentiation; specific moats.
- **Management team.** Named executives and board; specific prior-career credentials — every claim reproducible in the S-1 Item 401 directors / officers section.
- **The ask.** Specific framing: "we are considering an IPO over the next 6–12 months; we would appreciate your view on our positioning against the sector, on the specific disclosure content that would help you underwrite the security, and on the specific pricing-anchor comps you weigh most heavily." Do NOT include a price target, a specific valuation ask, or a specific-offering-amount commitment.
- **The specific-S-1-content preview.** Risk factors you expect to disclose; MD&A themes; critical-accounting-estimate flags.
- **Appendix.** The specific-prior-period P&L / balance-sheet / cash-flow extracts that back the specific claims in the deck; detailed cohort data; detailed unit economics.

Every specific numeric claim in the deck should carry a specific-source footnote identifying the paragraph of the S-1 draft (by Reg S-K Item or Reg S-X Article) in which the same number appears. Where a specific claim cannot be reproduced in the current S-1 draft, flag `<!-- needs-research: reconcile <claim> with S-1 draft paragraph -->`.

### 3. The meeting-log template

Author a specific meeting-log template that counsel can audit. Each entry should capture:

- Date, time, duration.
- Meeting mode (in-person, phone, video).
- Issuer attendees (named).
- Underwriter sales-force attendees (named).
- Investor attendees (named).
- Investor firm, Rule 144A QIB / Rule 501(a) IAI classification, and the specific documentation of that classification (lead-left-sales-force-confirmation form, counsel-reviewed classification letter, or specific prior-deal participation record).
- Materials shared (deck version, appendix version, specific hand-outs).
- Topics covered (bulleted summary).
- Specific-questions asked by the investor (verbatim where possible).
- Specific-follow-ups committed to (and by whom).
- Any specific-content outside the TTW deck that was shared and the counsel-review status.

The template should be usable as a tracker across the whole TTW period. Include a specific-field for "underwriter-counsel review date" and "issuer-counsel review date" per entry.

### 4. The FAQ / Q&A master

Draft 15-25 specific FAQ / Q&A pairs covering the specific questions you expect from employees, customers, partners, press, and board-adjacent stakeholders during the pre-filing / post-filing / pre-effective period. Each Q&A should:

- Have a specific question in employee / customer / partner / press language.
- Have a specific answer that is consistent with the S-1 draft, approved by counsel, and defensible under the Rule 168 / 169 ordinary-course business-communications safe harbour where relevant.
- Flag the categories of questions that *must* be deferred ("we cannot comment on the offering during the offering window; please refer to our public filings on SEC EDGAR").

Include at least:

- 3 employee-focused FAQs (timing, equity treatment, trading-window posture).
- 3 customer-focused FAQs (continuity, product roadmap, pricing).
- 3 partner-focused FAQs (continuity of the partnership, go-to-market impact).
- 3 press-focused FAQs (the "will you comment on your IPO" variant, specific-product-launch inquiries, specific-industry-event inquiries).
- 3 board-adjacent FAQs covering the specific pre-IPO-investor LP-communications your sponsors will field.
- 3 specific hard-question variants from the gun-jumping-risk-high category — social-media-generated speculation, employee all-hands questions, specific-industry-conference inquiries.

### 5. The publicity policy

Draft a 2-3 page publicity policy covering the offering window. The policy should:

- Define the offering window (pre-filing, post-filing / pre-effective, post-effective) with the specific dates for your deal.
- Enumerate specific prohibited communications (press releases beyond the Rule 135 / 134 content list, executive interviews with financial press on the offering, investor-conference participation outside the syndicate agenda, offering-related social-media posts).
- Enumerate specific permitted communications (ordinary-course product-and-customer announcements subject to specific content review, regular-cadence employee communications, regulatory-required filings, TTW meetings authorised by counsel with the paper-trail discipline from task 3).
- Specify the specific review-and-sign-off workflow (who drafts, who reviews, who signs off, where the record is maintained).
- Specify the specific social-media policy for executive-level accounts (Twitter/X, LinkedIn, Medium, YouTube, TikTok, Bluesky, specific-industry-forum posts).
- Specify the specific training programme the policy requires (who is trained, when, and what written acknowledgement is maintained).

### 6. The Rule 135 pre-filing announcement

Draft the specific Rule 135 pre-filing announcement your company would issue at the moment of confidential DRS submission (if an EGC) or at the moment of initial public filing. The announcement must stay strictly within the Rule 135 permitted-content list:

- Issuer name.
- Type of securities to be offered.
- Anticipated offering amount (narrow range if any; specific-amount-not-fixed is permitted).
- Anticipated timing (narrow window).
- Anticipated exchange listing.
- Specific SEC-required disclaimers (announcement is not an offer; any offer will be made only through the registration statement; the registration has been filed but is not yet effective; etc.).

Do NOT include projected valuation, projected pricing, specific-business-commentary, use-of-proceeds narrative, product-roadmap commentary, named-underwriter commentary (named-underwriter identification is permitted post-filing under Rule 134, not under Rule 135 pre-filing).

### 7. Counsel-review red-flag log

Produce a red-flag log of at least 10 specific drafts / pieces of content / communications you expect counsel to push back on across the TTW period, and the specific-rule-based reason:

- A specific slide / claim from your TTW deck where the Section 5(d) consistency-with-S-1 rule is close to the line.
- A specific FAQ answer where the Rule 169 ordinary-course business-information safe harbour is weak.
- A specific social-media post you have seen a founder-type person publish in a prior deal that would create specific gun-jumping exposure.
- A specific press inbound you expect during the window and the specific holding statement you would deploy.
- A specific product-launch moment during the window and the specific-content-review discipline you would require.
- A specific industry-conference participation decision you would defer.
- A specific-executive-interview-request from a specific financial-press publication and the counsel-counselled response.
- A specific-partnership-announcement that would otherwise occur in the window and the specific deferral decision.
- A specific-employee-all-hands moment where the Q&A discipline is likely to be tested.
- A specific-cornerstone-conversation that risks drifting into pricing conversation and must be re-scoped.

Each entry: specific-piece-of-content, specific rule or statute that governs, specific proposed resolution, specific-fallback-if-resolution-is-rejected.

## Starter guidance

Three anti-patterns to avoid:

- **The "we won't say anything, so we're safe" fallacy.** Pre-filing silence does not immunise the issuer — Rule 169 protects factually consistent ordinary-course business communications, and total silence can distort customer and partner relationships. The practitioner discipline is *specific-content-review discipline*, not *total silence*.
- **The pitch-book-copy TTW deck.** The TTW deck is NOT the pitch book. Pitch-book content frequently contains forward-looking commercial projections that are not reproducible in the S-1 under the Reg S-X / Item 303 standards. Starting from the pitch book and iterating down is a specific anti-pattern; start from the S-1 draft and iterate up.
- **The retail-investor TTW slip.** A single TTW conversation with a retail-individual-accredited investor (not an *institutional* accredited investor) is a Section 5(c) offer and a gun-jumping event. Underwriter-counsel documentation of QIB / IAI classification per account is not a formality.

## Acceptance criteria

You can demonstrate that:

- The QIB / IAI target list has 40-80 accounts with Rule 144A / Rule 501(a) classification per account.
- The TTW deck has 12-18 slides, zero price / valuation / offering-amount commitments, and every numeric claim traced to a specific S-1 paragraph.
- The meeting-log template captures counsel-reviewable paper trail.
- The FAQ / Q&A has at least 15 specific entries across employee / customer / partner / press / board-adjacent / hard-question categories.
- The publicity policy covers prohibited, permitted, workflow, social-media, and training sections.
- The Rule 135 announcement is strictly within the permitted-content list.
- The red-flag log has 10+ entries each with a specific-rule citation and a specific proposed resolution.
- A critical reader (issuer counsel, lead-left-counsel, specific plaintiff-firm-adversary-simulator) could find at most one line in your packet that would plausibly cross the gun-jumping perimeter.

## Reflection

Add a short reflection (½ page):

1. Which TTW deck slide was hardest to constrain to the Section 5(d) consistency-with-S-1 rule, and why?
2. If a specific mega-fund insists on a specific valuation conversation during the TTW meeting, how do you redirect without closing the door on the account's eventual roadshow participation?
3. Which FAQ answer are you most concerned a specific executive would improvise around under pressure, and how would you reinforce the discipline?

## Stretch goals

- **Comparable-deal reverse-engineering.** Pick a specific recent IPO in your sector that was priced in the past 24 months. Reconstruct, from the public S-1, the specific TTW content you believe was used. What do you infer the TTW materials said, and what do you infer the SEC comment letter addressed about the TTW materials?
- **Mock SEC comment letter.** Draft a 1-page mock SEC comment letter in the voice of SEC Corp-Fin staff that would be issued if your TTW packet were produced in response to a comment-letter request. What specific items would be flagged, and what specific issuer-response language would you deploy?
- **Social-media incident simulation.** Simulate a specific "founder-tweets-something-marginal" moment at T-5 days before pricing. Draft the specific-containment response: the specific-counsel-call, the specific-tweet-takedown-decision, the specific-disclosure-amendment-if-any, the specific board-committee-communication.
