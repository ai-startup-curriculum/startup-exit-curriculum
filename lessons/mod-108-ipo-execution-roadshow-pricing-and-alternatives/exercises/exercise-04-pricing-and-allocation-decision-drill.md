# exercise-04: Pricing and Allocation Decision Drill

**Estimated effort:** 4 hours

## Objective

Run the full T-1 pricing meeting end-to-end against your frozen company — set an initial range, author the range-revision decisions across two scenarios (hot deal and soft deal), make the final price / size / greenshoe decision, author the allocation methodology, run the specific FINRA Rule 5130 / 5131 eligibility review across the allocation book, and produce the specific closing-package artefacts (pricing-committee consent, Rule 424(b) prospectus cover-page draft, allocation memo, allocation record extract). By the end of the exercise you should be able to defend each decision to the pricing committee, to issuer counsel, to underwriter counsel, and to a specific adversarial reader simulating a FINRA / SEC retrospective audit.

## Background

This exercise covers material from:

- [Chapter 4 — Pricing the Offering and Allocating the Book](../04-pricing-and-allocation.md)

It builds on exercise-03's daily book-building model — the specific scenario-level book shapes are the input to this exercise's pricing and allocation decisions.

## Prerequisites

- Frozen company profile from exercise-01, syndicate from exercise-01, TTW target list and FAQ from exercise-02, roadshow calendar / one-on-one map / book-building model from exercise-03.
- Access to specific comparable-deal Rule 424(b) pricing prospectuses on SEC EDGAR for shape-reference (e.g., recent SaaS, fintech, or healthtech IPOs in your sector).
- Access to the FINRA Rule 5130 / 5131 rulebook (freely available on FINRA.org).
- Access to SEC Rule 430A / 424(b) rule text (freely available at the eCFR).

## Tasks

### 1. The initial-range filing

Author the specific initial-range filing for the pre-effective S-1 amendment:

- Anticipated pricing range per share (e.g., $18.00 to $22.00) — justify the specific range width.
- Anticipated base offering size (shares) — specify the primary vs. secondary split.
- Anticipated greenshoe size (shares, as 15% of base).
- Specific "anticipated pricing" (the lead-left-and-CFO's internal target that sits above the range in the typical 15-20%-below-anticipated-pricing convention). Document this internal number — it is not disclosed, but it anchors the range.
- Specific pricing-anchor comps (3-5 named recent comparable IPOs with pricing-date, pricing-vs-range outcome, and current aftermarket price vs. offering price). This is the price-anchor evidence that the syndicate desk used to set the range.

### 2. The two-scenario range-revision drill

Run the daily book-building scenarios from exercise-03 (hot and soft) through the range-revision decision framework:

**Scenario A — the hot deal.**

- Book moves from 1.5× (T-9) to 8× (T-1) at the top of range.
- Decide: at what T-day do you file a range up-revision?
- What is the specific new range you file?
- Does the up-revision stay within the Rule 430A 20% spread (relative to the midpoint of the last-filed range), or does it require re-circulation under Rule 424(b)?
- What is the specific procedural choreography — amendment filing, red-herring re-circulation (or not), buy-side-indication-reconfirmation discipline, specific-dropouts expected?
- Document the specific decision in a 1-page syndicate-committee memo.

**Scenario B — the soft deal.**

- Book moves from 0.8× (T-9) to 1.1× (T-1) at the range midpoint.
- Decide: do you (a) hold the range and price at the low end, (b) down-revise the range, (c) postpone the pricing?
- What specific signals in the composition and quality reading drive the decision?
- For each option, specify the specific T-day on which you commit, the specific procedural choreography, and the specific-aftermarket-signal consequence.
- Document the specific decision in a 1-page syndicate-committee memo.

### 3. The pricing-meeting decision package

For each scenario (hot and soft), author the specific pricing-meeting decision package:

- **Book snapshot.** The specific coverage ratio, composition, and quality snapshot at book-close (from exercise-03's model).
- **Price-and-size recommendation.** The specific recommended final price per share and the specific recommended final offering size, with specific rationale drawn from the demand curve and the composition.
- **Greenshoe recommendation.** The specific recommended greenshoe size (default: 15% of base; justify any variance).
- **Primary / secondary final mix.** The specific split; justify any deviation from the S-1-amendment disclosure.
- **Specific-trade-off commentary.** What specifically is traded off at this price-and-size — e.g., in the hot-deal case, the specific-expected first-day-pop percentage and the specific-money-left-on-the-table concern; in the soft-deal case, the specific-underclearing-risk and the specific aftermarket-support cost the syndicate expects.
- **Specific-alternative price.** The specific-alternative-price the pricing committee could reasonably select (e.g., $0.50-$2.00 higher or lower than the recommendation) and the specific-consequence of that alternative.

### 4. The allocation methodology memo

Author the specific allocation methodology memo (4-6 pages) covering:

**Bucket targets.**

- Long-only mutual funds: X% target, Y% range of acceptable deviation.
- Crossover hedge funds: X% target.
- Pure hedge funds: X% target.
- Cornerstone allocations: X% honoured per pre-commitment.
- EDSP: X% carved out per pre-IPO board decision.
- F&F: X% carved out per pre-IPO board decision.
- Retail: X% target (if the deal uses a retail-access channel — Robinhood IPO Access, SoFi IPO Investing, Schwab / TD retail-access, or similar).

**Within-bucket weighting discipline.**

For long-only: specific weighting toward anchor accounts (T. Rowe Price, Fidelity, Wellington, Capital Group, BlackRock, Vanguard, State Street, Franklin Templeton, Baillie Gifford, D1, Coatue, Tiger — subject to classification), with specific weighting criteria (prior-deal participation, prior-aftermarket-hold-pattern, specific-strategic-relationship-building intent).

For crossover and hedge funds: specific weighting toward accounts with specific-prior-hold-not-flip pattern, specific-conviction-thesis signals (e.g., account PM asked specific-deep-sector-questions at the one-on-one), specific-size-of-prior-institutional-position in comparable issuers.

For cornerstones: specific pre-committed-amount honoured; specific-tracking that the cornerstone shares are subject to the specific lock-up disclosed in the prospectus.

For EDSP: specific eligibility criteria (all employees, tenured subset, or specific long-tenure subset), specific per-employee cap, specific board-decision reference.

For F&F: specific FINRA Rule 5130 restricted-persons review discipline, specific per-participant cap, specific documentation.

For retail (if used): specific retail-access-platform integration, specific per-retail-participant cap, specific prospectus-delivery discipline.

**Specific-trade-off articulation.** Why specifically the above distribution — what aftermarket-quality objective is being targeted, what demand-clearing concern is being resolved, what strategic-relationship-building is being prioritised.

### 5. The FINRA Rule 5130 / 5131 eligibility review

Produce a specific eligibility-review checklist that the syndicate desk would run against every account on the allocation list:

- **Rule 5130 restricted-persons test.** For each account, the specific eligibility certification (institutional vs. restricted-person-category, broker-dealer-affiliation-screen, portfolio-manager-of-institutional-account carve-out, specific-family-member-of-restricted-person screen for F&F).
- **Rule 5131 spinning prohibition.** For each account, the specific "is-this-an-executive-officer-or-director-of-an-investment-banking-client" screen; the specific "is-the-allocation-a-quid-pro-quo" screen; the specific-documentation of the rationale.
- **The paper trail.** Where the specific-certification documents are maintained (syndicate-desk-folder, underwriter-counsel-filing-system, specific-FINRA-review-ready repository).

Simulate a specific "FINRA retrospective audit" scenario: your deal closed, and six months later FINRA requests the specific allocation-rationale documentation for the top-30 allocations. Can you produce it? What specific gaps would the audit surface, and how would you remediate?

### 6. The closing-package artefact set

Draft (or outline, where drafting is premature) the specific closing-package artefacts:

- **The pricing-committee consent.** A 1-2 page written consent executed by the pricing committee members approving the specific price, size, greenshoe, and allocation methodology. Reference the underwriting agreement, the FINRA Rule 5110 filing, and the specific board resolutions that delegated the authority.
- **The Rule 424(b) pricing-prospectus cover page.** The specific cover-page content with the final price, size, greenshoe terms, use-of-proceeds summary, and the specific SEC-required disclosure legends.
- **The allocation-record extract.** A specific-format record identifying each allocated account, allocation size, allocation price, and Rule 5130 / 5131 eligibility confirmation.
- **The comfort-letter and legal-opinion schedule.** The specific list of deliverables (auditor comfort letter at pricing and at closing, issuer-counsel opinion, underwriter-counsel opinion, bringdown certificates) with the specific responsibility matrix.

### 7. The first-day-of-trading forecast

For your pricing recommendation in each scenario, produce a specific first-day-of-trading forecast:

- Expected opening-trade-price range.
- Expected first-day closing-price range.
- Expected first-day volume (as a percentage of offering size).
- Expected composition of first-day sell-pressure (hedge-fund flippers, specific-weighted-allocations-that-you-expect-to-flip).
- Expected need for syndicate stabilisation and expected greenshoe-exercise pattern (preview of exercise-05).
- The specific "well-priced-deal pop" target (practitioner-known 15-25% range) and the specific-signals-you-are-targeting.

## Starter guidance

Three anti-patterns to avoid:

- **The "price for the pop" trap.** Pricing below where the book clears to produce a specific first-day pop over 50% leaves specific money on the table and transfers wealth from the issuer to the day-one allocated accounts. The 15-25% pop is the practitioner-known sweet spot; above that is specific under-pricing.
- **The "oversubscribed means price higher" fallacy.** A 10× oversubscribed book composed of hedge-fund flippers at the range midpoint is a specific first-week-selling-pressure signal — pricing at the top of the range in that case produces a break-of-issue after the hedge funds flip at the open. The specific discipline is to read the composition and quality, not just the coverage.
- **The "allocation-methodology-as-afterthought" error.** Allocation is the aftermarket-quality lever. A specific-methodology-undocumented allocation surfaces as a FINRA Rule 5131 issue in retrospective audit. The specific discipline is to document the rationale ex-ante.

## Acceptance criteria

You can demonstrate that:

- The initial-range filing has a specific range, specific base/size, specific greenshoe, specific internal-anchor-price, and 3-5 named comparable-pricing anchors.
- Both scenarios have specific range-revision decisions with Rule 430A procedural choreography.
- The pricing-meeting decision package covers price, size, greenshoe, primary/secondary mix, trade-off commentary, and specific-alternative-consequence.
- The allocation methodology memo has specific bucket targets, within-bucket weighting discipline, and the specific-trade-off articulation.
- The FINRA Rule 5130 / 5131 eligibility review is specific and would survive a retrospective-audit stress test.
- The closing-package artefact set is specific and reproducible.
- The first-day-of-trading forecast is specific and quantified.
- A critical reader (pricing-committee chair, FINRA retrospective-auditor, outside-counsel) can test each decision and find it defensible.

## Reflection

Add a short reflection (½ page):

1. In the hot-deal scenario, how much of the specific "money left on the table" (the first-day-pop percentage above 15-25%) is a legitimate aftermarket-quality cost versus specific under-pricing?
2. In the soft-deal scenario, what specific allocation move could you make to improve the aftermarket-quality signal without hurting the demand-clearing?
3. Which specific account on your allocation list is the one you are most concerned about from a FINRA Rule 5131 spinning-prohibition perspective, and how would you document the specific-independent-rationale that defends the allocation?

## Stretch goals

How does the comp move change the range-revision decision, and how do you re-anchor the pricing committee?
