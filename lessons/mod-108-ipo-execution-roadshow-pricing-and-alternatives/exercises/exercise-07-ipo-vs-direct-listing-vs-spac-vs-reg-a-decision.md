# exercise-07: IPO vs. Direct Listing vs. SPAC vs. Reg A+ Decision

**Estimated effort:** 3 hours

## Objective

Author a specific board-ready decision memo that evaluates the five viable going-public paths — traditional bookbuilt IPO, direct listing (secondary-only), direct listing with primary-offering component (post-2020 Rule 102.01B / Rule 5315 form), de-SPAC (post-2024 SEC rules), and Reg A+ Tier 2 — against a *specific* issuer. Use your frozen company from exercise-01 as the primary case; also run the decision through two contrasting hypothetical issuers (a consumer brand with a retail-affinity base, and a strong-brand-mature-SaaS-company with no urgent primary-capital need) to force the specific-issuer-fit decision discipline. By the end of the exercise you should be able to defend a specific path recommendation to the board, and defend the specific rejection of the four alternatives.

## Background

This exercise covers material from:

- [Chapter 7 — IPO Alternatives — Direct Listing, SPAC / de-SPAC, DPO, and Reg A+](../07-ipo-alternatives.md)

Note that chapter 7 also discusses DPO briefly; the exercise does not require a DPO fit analysis unless one of your hypothetical issuers (specifically the consumer brand) actually fits — in which case include it as an option.

## Prerequisites

- Frozen company profile from exercise-01.
- Two hypothetical contrasting issuers you will author at the start of the exercise:
  - **Hypothetical A — Consumer brand with retail-affinity base.** $25M revenue, 60% growth, large customer community (e.g., 2M+ engaged customers), passionate retail investor following, raising $30M-$50M, strong-brand consumer DTC or specialty-foods / specialty-apparel / beauty profile.
  - **Hypothetical B — Mature strong-brand SaaS.** $800M ARR, 25% growth, 125% NRR, mature institutional-investor existing shareholder base (10+ secondary rounds, broad employee ownership), balance sheet strong, no immediate primary-capital need.
- Access to the SEC's 2024 SPAC rules release, NYSE Rule 102.01B, Nasdaq Rule 5315, Regulation A text, and specific comparable-recent-transaction S-1 / F-4 / 1-A filings on SEC EDGAR.

## Tasks

### 1. The three issuer profiles

For each of the three profiles (your frozen primary company, Hypothetical A, Hypothetical B), produce a specific profile sheet with:

- Business overview (2-3 sentences).
- Revenue / ARR, growth rate, margin profile, Rule of 40.
- Pre-money valuation target.
- Primary-capital need (specific-dollar-amount with specific-use-of-proceeds breakdown).
- Existing shareholder base composition (VC concentration, founder concentration, employee concentration, specific-institutional-crossover concentration, specific-secondary-market participation history).
- Brand recognition and specific-affinity-investor-base presence (if any).
- Specific-sector classification and specific-sector-comparable-recent-transaction patterns.

### 2. The decision-dimension matrix

For each of the three issuer profiles, score the five going-public paths (bookbuilt IPO, direct listing secondary-only, direct listing primary-component, de-SPAC post-2024, Reg A+ Tier 2) across the eight decision dimensions from chapter 7:

1. **Capital-raise need.** Score fit (0-5) for each path given the specific primary-capital need.
2. **Brand and shareholder-base strength.** Score fit (0-5) for each path given the specific brand strength and existing shareholder base.
3. **Sector fit.** Score fit (0-5) for each path given the specific sector's historical alternative-fit patterns.
4. **Timeline.** Score fit (0-5) for each path given the specific timeline urgency and the specific-path-typical-duration.
5. **Cost.** Score fit (0-5) for each path given the specific cost structure (bookbuilt-IPO gross spread 6-7%, direct-listing financial-advisor fees 1-2%, de-SPAC sponsor-promote + PIPE fees, Reg A+ offering-platform fees 5-10%).
6. **Ongoing regime.** Score fit (0-5) for each path given the specific-reporting-regime burden.
7. **Liability posture.** Score fit (0-5) for each path given the specific Section 11 / 12(a)(2) / Rule 10b-5 exposure and specific-2024-SPAC-rule-impact for de-SPAC.
8. **Investor-base composition.** Score fit (0-5) for each path given the specific target investor base.

Present the matrix as a specific 5-paths × 8-dimensions table per issuer profile, with specific-scores and specific-paragraph-justification per cell. Compute a specific weighted-score per path per issuer, with the specific weights justified (e.g., capital-raise need weighted 2× for Hypothetical A, brand strength weighted 2× for Hypothetical B).

### 3. The decision memo per issuer

For each of the three issuer profiles, author a specific 3-4-page board decision memo covering:

- **Recommendation.** Specific recommended path.
- **Rationale.** 3-5 specific reasons the recommended path beats each specific alternative, drawn from the decision-dimension matrix.
- **Specific risk-and-mitigation.** The 2-3 specific risks of the recommended path and the specific-mitigation plans.
- **Specific-alternative-fallback.** If the recommended path fails (market-window-collapse, SEC comment-letter friction, specific-counterparty-collapse for de-SPAC), what is the specific fallback path?
- **Specific-cost estimate.** Specific-dollar-cost estimate for the recommended path (gross-spread or advisory fees + legal + accounting + exchange-listing + specific-platform fees for Reg A+ + specific-sponsor-promote for de-SPAC).
- **Specific-timeline.** Specific month-by-month timeline from board decision to pricing / listing / de-SPAC close.
- **Specific-governance-and-fiduciary considerations.** Specific-handoff to mod-109 for the specific governance frame (if applicable — e.g., controlled-company direct listing with specific-board-committee considerations, de-SPAC-with-sponsor-conflict considerations).

### 4. The post-2024 de-SPAC specific-feasibility analysis

For the one issuer among the three (likely your frozen primary case if it is pre-revenue or early-revenue specific-technology, or Hypothetical A if the specific-consumer-brand fits a specific SPAC-sponsor-relationship) for which de-SPAC is at least conceivable, author a specific feasibility analysis covering:

- Specific potential SPAC-sponsor universe (named sponsors with specific-recent-SPAC-track-record, specific-sector-focus).
- Specific PIPE-anchor requirements and specific PIPE-market read.
- Specific co-registrant liability analysis — what does the specific target private company take on by signing as co-registrant on the Form S-4 / F-4?
- Specific PSLRA-safe-harbour-removal consequence for forward-looking projections — what projections can the target provide in the de-SPAC proxy / registration statement under the 2024 rules?
- Specific sponsor-promote economics (typically 20% of post-SPAC-IPO equity for nominal price) and specific-dilution-impact on the target shareholders.
- Specific sponsor-conflicts-of-interest-disclosure obligations under the 2024 rules.
- Specific minimum-book-value test if applicable.
- Specific decision — is de-SPAC a defensible choice given the specific-rule-change-impact or is it dominated by traditional IPO?

Even if the specific issuer does not ultimately fit de-SPAC, the analysis should articulate the specific 2024-rules-impact in enough depth to defend the specific "we are not pursuing de-SPAC because [specific-rule-impact]" position to the board.

### 5. The direct-listing specific-execution analysis

For the one issuer among the three for which direct listing is a plausible path (likely Hypothetical B — strong-brand mature SaaS with no urgent primary-capital need), author a specific direct-listing execution analysis covering:

- Specific pre-2020 vs. post-2020 form (secondary-only vs. primary-component) decision.
- Specific financial-advisor selection (likely a subset of your exercise-01 syndicate — direct listings use the same tier-1 banks but as financial advisors not underwriters) and specific-fee-structure.
- Specific exchange choice (NYSE under Rule 102.01B or Nasdaq under Rule 5315) and specific-opening-auction mechanics.
- Specific investor-education event design (analogous to the roadshow but structured differently — public presentation, often open to retail, with specific-institutional-focus-session).
- Specific pre-listing shareholder-contact outreach — which pre-IPO holders need to be contacted about selling into the opening, which have contractual lock-ups that constrain selling into the opening, how is the specific opening-liquidity base built?
- Specific aftermarket-dynamic expectations — opening-auction-price-discovery mechanics, specific first-day-volume expectations, specific first-week price-volatility expectations.
- Specific absent-syndicate-stabilisation consequence — the specific aftermarket does not have the lead-left syndicate-manager role from exercise-05; what specific IR / market-maker-coordination substitutes?
- Specific lock-up architecture — direct listings often do not have a 180-day standard lock-up (though pre-existing contractual lock-ups from prior financings may apply). How do you think about the specific supply dynamics without an underwriter-negotiated lock-up?

## Starter guidance

Three anti-patterns to avoid:

- **The "default to bookbuilt IPO" error.** Chapter 7's framing is that the alternatives should be explicitly considered, not implicitly dismissed. A specific issuer-fit analysis against each alternative is the discipline, not a specific-"IPO-first-with-alternatives-as-fallback" default.
- **The "pre-2024 de-SPAC analysis" out-of-date error.** The 2024 SEC rules materially changed de-SPAC economics and specific-liability-posture. Any de-SPAC feasibility analysis grounded in pre-2024 practice is specifically out-of-date and misleading. Ground the analysis in the specific 2024-rules framework.
- **The "direct listing-is-just-cheaper-IPO" simplification.** Direct listings are a specific-different mechanic — the specific opening-auction-pricing mechanic, the specific absence of syndicate stabilisation, the specific often-reduced-lock-up architecture, and the specific pre-listing investor-education substitute for the roadshow. The specific execution differences shape the specific fit.

## Acceptance criteria

You can demonstrate that:

- The three issuer profiles are specific and distinct.
- The decision-dimension matrix is specific per issuer with weighted-scores and specific-cell-justifications.
- Each decision memo recommends a specific path with specific rationale, risk, fallback, cost, timeline, and governance considerations.
- The de-SPAC feasibility analysis is grounded in the 2024 SEC rules.
- The direct-listing execution analysis addresses the specific-opening-auction and specific-absence-of-syndicate-stabilisation.
- The Reg A+ Tier 2 feasibility analysis addresses the specific-$75M cap, specific-ongoing-regime, and specific-investor-base outcome.
- The comparative synthesis articulates the specific-decision-criteria that produce the specific-different-paths.
- A critical reader (board director, outside counsel, specific-financial-advisor-peer) can test each recommendation and find it defensible.
