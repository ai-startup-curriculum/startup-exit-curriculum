# exercise-03: Roadshow Choreography and Book-Building Drill

**Estimated effort:** 4 hours

## Objective

Choreograph a complete two-week roadshow for the frozen company profile, build the daily book-building read model, and rehearse the CEO / CFO through the specific hard-question set. By the end of the exercise you should be able to defend the city-by-city calendar, the account-by-account one-on-one map, the group-lunch schedule, the analyst-day decision, and the daily-coverage-read model to the lead-left syndicate desk and the pricing committee — and you should have a rehearsed CEO / CFO Q&A playbook that survives the specific murder-board stress test.

## Background

This exercise covers material from:

- [Chapter 3 — Roadshow Choreography — Pilot Fishing to Pricing Week](../03-roadshow-choreography.md)

It also builds on your prior exercise outputs — the lead-left and co-manager slate from exercise-01, the TTW target list and FAQ / Q&A from exercise-02. Do not re-pick the lead-left here; carry it forward.

- Frozen company profile from exercise-01 (sector, ARR, growth rate, cap-table, target offering size, listing-exchange target).
- The underwriter syndicate from exercise-01.
- The TTW target list and FAQ / Q&A master from exercise-02.
- Access to specific recent-comparable-deal SEC EDGAR filings (preliminary prospectuses, free-writing prospectuses, and specific pricing-amendment filings) to shape the roadshow-deck comparables and the specific comparable-pricing anchor.

## Tasks

### 1. The T-14 to T-1 marketing calendar

Author the day-by-day calendar from T-30 (end of pilot-fishing window) through T+1 (first day of trading). For each day, specify:

- Date (anchor to a specific hypothetical pricing date 60–90 days in the future).
- Which cities are visited (New York / Boston / Baltimore / SF / LA / Chicago / Denver / Kansas City / Edinburgh / London / Frankfurt / Amsterdam / Zurich / Singapore / Hong Kong — pick the specific subset for your deal).
- Which executives are in which city (CEO / CFO / optional COO or CRO), with the specific-travel-split that lets the CEO and CFO cover different accounts in different cities when the calendar is dense.
- How many one-on-one meetings per day per executive (typical heavy day: 8–12 per executive).
- Group-lunch locations and host-bank per day.
- Specific analyst-day timing (T-14 / T-12 / T-10 or deferred to the virtual-marketing phase) and host-bank.
- Any mandatory specific stops (Edinburgh for a global-growth deal targeting Baillie Gifford, Singapore / Hong Kong for a book targeting GIC / Temasek).
- Virtual-meeting slots (post-COVID practice retains a meaningful virtual portion — mark which accounts are virtual vs. in-person and why).

### 2. The account-by-account one-on-one map

From your TTW target list (exercise-02), build the specific roadshow one-on-one map. For each targeted account, specify:

- Account name, firm, city, PM / sector-head attendees.
- Meeting mode (in-person one-on-one, virtual one-on-one, group lunch, no meeting).
- Meeting day and time slot.
- Which executive leads (CEO vs. CFO).
- Account "anchor-tier" classification:
  - **Tier A** — anchor targets expected to deliver >5% of the deal (typically 5–10 named accounts: T. Rowe Price, Fidelity, Wellington, Capital Group, GIC, Temasek, Baillie Gifford, specific crossover anchor).
  - **Tier B** — core long-only / crossover demand builders.
  - **Tier C** — tail-of-the-book accounts, specific sector specialists, specific hedge-fund flippers.
- Pre-meeting prep brief (3–5 bullets): PM's known thesis / portfolio holdings in comparable issuers, pilot-fishing feedback (if any), the specific-hard-question this PM is expected to ask, the specific-angle to lean into for this PM.

The map should cover 60-150 accounts. Flag any account for which classification data cannot be verified as `<!-- needs-research: ... -->`.

### 3. The analyst-day decision

Document a specific analyst-day decision: hold it or skip it, and justify. The analyst-day decision factors:

- Deal size (analyst days are more common for $300M+ deals; smaller deals compress into the pre-launch rehearsal window).
- Sector-analyst-density in the sub-sector (an analyst-day is more valuable when there are 6+ initiating-coverage analysts).
- Specific competitor behaviour (did the recent comparable issuer hold one? What did it produce?).
- Specific FINRA Rule 2242 constraints you need to be aware of.
- Specific-management-time cost (an analyst-day burns a half-day of CEO / CFO rehearsal preparation time).

If you decide to hold an analyst day, specify: the host bank, the city, the date (T-14 or T-12), the specific list of analysts invited (named analysts at syndicate banks and at non-participating broker-dealers), the specific agenda, the specific-structured-Q&A format, the specific-content treatment that keeps the materials consistent with the S-1 and the Rule 2242 research-quiet-period discipline.

### 4. The daily book-building read model

Build a spreadsheet or table that simulates the daily book-building read across the T-9 to T-1 marketing days. For each day, model:

- The running total indications of interest (IOIs) by account, with the specific-price condition for each (at the range midpoint, at the top of the range, at the bottom of the range, above the range, below the range, "price-insensitive").
- Running coverage ratio (total IOIs / offering size) at the range midpoint, at the top, and at the bottom.
- Composition — percentage long-only, crossover hedge fund, hedge fund, cornerstone, retail / EDSP, by day.
- Geographic composition — US / Europe / APAC — by day.
- Quality signal — percentage of the book from accounts with specific-prior-deal-aftermarket-hold-pattern (fundamental holders) vs. specific-prior-deal-flip-pattern (hedge-fund flippers).

Design the model such that a day-by-day update plausibly reflects a real roadshow:

- T-9 and T-8: book opens with pilot-fishing anchors.
- T-7 and T-6: east-coast NY / Boston meetings drive demand; book moves to 1.0–1.5× coverage.
- T-5 and T-4: west-coast and Baltimore meetings add demand; book moves to 2.0–3.5× coverage in a healthy deal.
- T-3 and T-2: international and tail accounts add; book finalises in the 3–8× oversubscribed range in a hot deal, 1–2× in a soft deal.
- T-1: book closes.

Run two scenarios through the model:

- **Scenario A — the hot deal.** Book moves from 1.5× (T-9) to 8× (T-1) at the top of range. Model the range up-revision decision point (which day, under what Rule 430A constraint).
- **Scenario B — the soft deal.** Book moves from 0.8× (T-9) to 1.1× (T-1) at the range midpoint. Model the range down-revision decision point (which day) and the postponement-vs-continue decision.

### 5. The roadshow-deck outline

Produce a specific slide-by-slide outline for the 30-45-slide roadshow deck (do NOT draft every slide; this is the structural outline the deck author would hand to graphics / design). For each slide, specify:

- Slide title.
- Core content (one sentence of the point the slide makes).
- Numeric claims on the slide and the specific S-1 paragraph (Reg S-K Item or Reg S-X Article location) in which each number appears.
- Rule 433 FWP legend placement.

Include the specific transaction-summary slide, the executive-summary slide, the market-opportunity slide, the product / platform slide, the GTM-traction slide with specific ARR / growth / NRR / Rule-of-40 reference, the competitive-positioning slide, the financial-model slide (historical with S-1-consistent projections for the EGC-permitted horizon), the capital-structure-and-use-of-proceeds slide, the leadership slide, and the transaction-summary close.

### 6. The CEO / CFO Q&A playbook

Author a specific Q&A playbook covering at least 25 anticipated hard questions. For each question, provide:

- The question in investor language (specific, verbatim where you have reference material).
- The counsel-reviewed answer (specific, S-1-consistent, with specific-paragraph-reference where appropriate).
- The specific-deflection-fallback if the answer touches a disclosure-sensitive topic ("we address that in the risk-factor section of our S-1; the specific position is [X]").
- The specific-forbidden-topics boundary (do not answer, defer to prospectus).

Minimum coverage:

- Why now / timing-of-the-deal.
- Growth-durability (cohort retention and expansion).
- Unit economics (payback, LTV / CAC, magic number).
- Gross-margin trajectory.
- Competitive positioning versus named competitors (at least 3).
- Profitability path and specific-timeline-to-FCF.
- M&A appetite and use-of-proceeds specifics.
- Capital-allocation framework.
- Customer concentration (if specific to the issuer).
- Pricing-power and discounting-behaviour.
- Macro-sensitivity (specific-exposure to downturn / cyclical dynamics).
- Specific-regulatory-risks and the risk-factor-section mitigation.
- Dual-class share structure defence (if the issuer has one).
- Insider-selling disclosure and signal.
- Specific AI / cybersecurity / ESG posture (if the issuer has adopted the Reg S-K Item 106 or Item 402(v) specific disclosures referenced in mod-107 chapter 7).

### 7. The post-pricing hand-off checklist

Draft the specific post-pricing hand-off checklist for the moment the pricing committee adjourns:

- Who signs the underwriting agreement, when, and who is in the room.
- Who executes the specific Rule 462(b) effectiveness-amendment if the deal uses one.
- Who notifies the exchange (NYSE / Nasdaq listing specialist) of the final price and size.
- Who activates the IR function for first-day-of-trading press and buy-side-communications.
- Who starts the 180-day lock-up clock (chapter 6) and ensures the lock-up-covered-persons list is final.
- Who hands off to the syndicate stabilisation desk (chapter 5) for the aftermarket.
- Who commences the FINRA Rule 2242 research-quiet-period discipline for syndicate research analysts.
- Who initiates the specific Form 8-A exchange registration (if not previously filed).

Each entry: named role (lead-left syndicate head, issuer counsel, underwriter counsel, IR head, CFO, GC), specific action, specific deliverable.

## Starter guidance

Three anti-patterns to avoid:

- **The CEO-does-everything calendar.** A CEO booked for 12 meetings per day for 9 straight days without CFO or COO coverage of specific accounts is a CEO who is unrehearsed and running on cortisol by T-3. The specific discipline is to split the calendar between CEO and CFO where the CFO has credibility, and to protect specific days for rest and specific murder-board rehearsal.
- **The pilot-fishing-equals-roadshow confusion.** Pilot fishing is TTW under Section 5(d); it is not the roadshow. The specific separation — pilot-fishing conversations are documented and bounded by the TTW discipline from chapter 2; roadshow conversations run post-filing under the Rule 433 FWP regime — is what counsel enforces.
- **The "oversubscribed book is a good book" fallacy.** A 20× oversubscribed book composed of hedge-fund flippers who intend to sell at the open is a specific aftermarket risk. The specific discipline is to read the book for *composition* and *quality*, not just coverage.

## Acceptance criteria

You can demonstrate that:

- The T-14 to T-1 calendar is specific, with executive-split, cities, group-lunch and analyst-day events, and travel logistics.
- The one-on-one map has 60-150 accounts with tier classification and pre-meeting brief per account.
- The analyst-day decision is justified and specific.
- The daily-book-building model runs both scenarios (hot and soft) with specific Rule 430A range-revision decision points.
- The roadshow-deck outline specifies 30-45 slides with the S-1-paragraph-reference per numeric claim.
- The Q&A playbook has 25+ hard-question / answer / deflection entries.
- The post-pricing hand-off checklist is specific and role-anchored.
- A critical reader (lead-left-syndicate-head, pricing-committee chair, mock buy-side PM) could stress-test the choreography and find it defensible.
