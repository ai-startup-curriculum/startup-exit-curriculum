# exercise-01: Underwriter Bake-Off and Selection Drill

**Estimated effort:** 3 hours

## Objective

Take a specific pre-IPO company (real or a realistic hypothetical you carry through the module) and produce a defensible underwriter-selection deliverable — a long-list, a short-list, a diligence packet for each short-list bank across the six diligence dimensions, a selection memo with lead-left / joint-book-runner / co-manager slate, and an engagement-letter-negotiation punch list. By the end you should be able to defend the syndicate construction to the board, to the pre-IPO investor base, and to a critical outside reader.

## Background

This exercise covers material from:

- [Chapter 1 — Underwriter Bake-Off, Syndicate Construction, and Gross-Spread Economics](../01-underwriter-bake-off-and-syndicate-construction.md)

You do not yet need the TTW regime (chapter 2), the roadshow choreography (chapter 3), the pricing and allocation (chapter 4), the Reg M / greenshoe (chapter 5), the lock-up architecture (chapter 6), or the alternatives frame (chapter 7). Those come in later exercises. The point of this exercise is to install the specific discipline of running a structured underwriter selection rather than defaulting to the specific bank the founder's board director introduced.

## Prerequisites

- A specific pre-IPO company profile with: sector, ARR / revenue, growth rate, gross margin, NRR, target offering size, target listing exchange, target investor-base composition, geographic footprint, and specific cap-table composition (VC investor concentration, founder concentration, employee-equity concentration).
- The specific readiness memo from mod-107 (if you have completed that module) or the specific board-authorised transaction-thesis (if you are working from a real deal).
- Access to SEC EDGAR and to specific recent-comparable-deal S-1 filings (freely available). Access to Renaissance Capital, PitchBook, or specific league-table sources if available (subscription); a specific alternative is to use the specific quarterly league-table press releases from LSEG / Refinitiv (publicly available).

## Tasks

### 1. Long-list construction

Draft the long list of 6-10 potential lead-left candidates. The long list should include:

- At least 4 tier-1 bulge-bracket banks (drawn from Goldman Sachs, Morgan Stanley, JPMorgan, Bank of America, Citi, Barclays).
- At least 1-2 sector boutiques with specific sub-sector coverage relevant to your company (e.g., for a SaaS company: Jefferies, Guggenheim Securities, William Blair, Evercore ISI, Piper Sandler; for biotech: Piper Sandler, Stifel, TD Cowen legacy, Leerink Partners; for financial services: Stifel, Piper Sandler, Raymond James, Baird; for industrials: William Blair, Baird, Raymond James).
- At least 1 specific specialist bank chosen for a specific fit — e.g., Allen & Company for media, a specific mid-market firm for a smaller offering, a specific analyst-first firm for research-quality emphasis.

For each long-list bank, note:

- The specific reason it is on the list (league-table strength, sector-analyst rank, specific prior-deal proof point, board-director recommendation).
- The specific concern or reservation (specific-bank-issue if any, specific-sector-coverage-gap, specific-recent-deal-performance issue).

### 2. Short-list to 4-6 banks with RFP diligence

Short-list to 4-6 banks. For each short-list bank, prepare a specific diligence packet covering the six dimensions from chapter 1:

- **Industry expertise.** Named senior banker; years of sub-sector coverage; specific prior-deal count in the sub-sector.
- **Comparable-transaction credentials.** List of 3-5 specific recent comparable IPOs the bank has led or co-led in the sub-sector (with pricing date, offering size, first-day performance, and current-day aftermarket price vs. offering price).
- **Research-analyst reputation and reach.** Named analyst; Institutional Investor All-America ranking (or equivalent); specific analyst-published notes on comparable issuers in the past 12 months.
- **Sales-force distribution.** Institutional sales-force headcount by geography; specific-account-relationship strength for your target investor list; specific-non-US-reach if relevant.
- **Aftermarket-support commitment.** Specific commitments on research-initiation timing, market-making, post-IPO conferences, non-deal roadshows.
- **Cornerstone / EDSP / F&F participation history.** Specific prior-deal experience with the ancillary programmes you intend to run.

If any specific field cannot be filled with evidence (e.g., you cannot access specific analyst rankings, or specific sales-force headcount is not public), mark it `<!-- needs-research: ... -->` rather than inventing.

### 3. Reference-call script

Draft a specific reference-call script you would use with 3-5 CFOs of companies each short-list bank took public in the prior 24 months. The script should cover:

- Did the specific senior banker deliver the specific coverage promised in the pitch?
- Did the specific analyst deliver the specific coverage promised — timing of initiation, price target, coverage depth?
- Did the sales-force work the book — specific account penetration, specific institutional-relationship follow-through?
- Did the stabilisation-and-greenshoe execute cleanly?
- Did the syndicate hold together in a specific market-moment (e.g., a specific down-day during marketing, a specific narrative-challenge, a specific pricing-week volatility)?
- Were the post-IPO relationships (follow-on execution, convert issuance, M&A advisory) offered on competitive terms?
- Would the CFO hire the bank again? For what specific work?
- What one thing would the CFO change if they were doing the deal again?

The script is what you would actually use in a 20-30-minute reference call.

### 4. Selection memo

Draft a 3-5-page selection memo for the transaction committee (board committee) covering:

- **Recommendation.** Named lead-left, named joint-book-runners (if any), initial co-manager slate.
- **Rationale.** Specific reasons the lead-left was selected against the specific alternatives, drawn from the six-dimension diligence.
- **Syndicate construction rationale.** Why these specific co-managers — specific diversity, specific regional reach, specific growth-investor relationship, specific sub-sector coverage the lead-left does not provide.
- **Gross-spread negotiation position.** Target gross-spread percentage with rationale, target reimbursable-expense structure, target fee-credit-if-any structure.
- **Trade-offs.** What you gave up by not selecting the runner-up, and why the trade was worth it.
- **Losers-call plan.** Specific banks you will call as losers within 24 hours; specific banks you will offer co-manager slots to.

### 5. Engagement-letter-negotiation punch list

Draft a specific punch list of 8-15 items you would negotiate in the engagement-letter drafting with issuer counsel and underwriter counsel. Each item is specific, drawn from chapter 1's engagement-letter treatment:

- Gross-spread percentage and specific commitments on further reduction if specific conditions are met.
- Reimbursable-expense caps (counsel, travel, marketing) with specific per-line-item and aggregate caps.
- Specific fee-credit provisions (e.g., prior-M&A-advisory-work credits, financing-commitment credits).
- Exclusivity period and specific termination-triggers.
- Indemnification-and-contribution provisions with specific-carve-outs.
- Specific commitments on aftermarket support (specific-cadence research updates, specific-conference invitations, specific non-deal-roadshow opportunities).
- Specific commitments on syndicate-construction changes (if any).
- Specific commitments on ancillary services (custody, transfer-agent introductions, comfort-letter-workstream discipline).
- Any specific fee-cap on the greenshoe economics.
- Any specific-facts provisions relevant to your company (e.g., specific pre-IPO investor considerations, specific cornerstone-programme mechanics).

Each item includes a specific fallback position — what you will accept if you cannot get the target.

## Starter guidance

Three anti-patterns to avoid:

- **The tier-1-default.** Selecting Goldman + Morgan Stanley without doing the sector-boutique-comparison work is a specific under-diligence. The sector-boutique may have a specific analyst franchise materially stronger for your specific sub-sector than the bulge-bracket alternatives; the six-dimension diligence tells you whether that is the case.
- **The pitch-book acceptance.** Every pitch book is a marketing document. Test every specific claim against specific evidence — SEC EDGAR filings, Institutional Investor rankings, Renaissance Capital IPO-tracker data. Do not accept specific claims without evidence.
- **The economics-under-negotiation-avoidance.** The gross-spread convention is 6-7% but every basis point below 7% flows to the balance sheet. A CFO who accepts the first-draft economics leaves specific money on the table.

## Acceptance criteria

You can demonstrate that:

- The company profile is filled in with specific numbers.
- The long list has 6-10 banks with a specific reason for each.
- The short list has 4-6 banks with the six-dimension diligence completed (each field filled with evidence or specific `<!-- needs-research: -->` marker).
- The reference-call script has 6-10 specific questions.
- The selection memo names a specific lead-left, joint-book-runners (if any), and co-manager slate with rationale.
- The engagement-letter punch list has 8-15 items each with a target and a fallback.
- A critical reader (board director, outside counsel, peer CFO) could disagree with a specific choice and see the specific evidence you used.

## Reflection

Add a short reflection (½ page):

1. Which of the six diligence dimensions surfaced the biggest gap between the pitch-book claim and the evidence?
2. If you had to make the selection with only the short-list diligence and no reference calls, which lead-left would you have chosen and why?
3. Which specific engagement-letter-punch-list item is the one you are most confident you can win in negotiation, and which is the one you expect to lose?

## Stretch goals

- **Two-syndicate comparison.** Construct a second alternative syndicate (different lead-left, different co-manager slate) and write a 1-page comparison memo. What does the alternative buy you that the primary syndicate does not? What does it cost?
- **Board conversation preparation.** Prepare the specific 5-slide deck you would present to the transaction committee summarising the selection recommendation. What is the specific slide the board director is most likely to push back on, and what is your response?
- **FINRA Rule 5110 pre-review.** Ask a peer or advisor familiar with the specific FINRA Rule 5110 filing to review your target gross-spread and specific reimbursable-expense structure against the specific Rule 5110 fairness and reasonableness framework. Note what you would change.
