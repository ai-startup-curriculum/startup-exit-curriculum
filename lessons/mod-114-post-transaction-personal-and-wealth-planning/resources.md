# Resources for mod-114-post-transaction-personal-and-wealth-planning

Primary source documentation, practitioner canon, data and research providers, and vendor references used by this module. Prefer the primary sources over secondary write-ups — for the §1202, Rule 10b5-1, §664, and §501(c)(3) materials especially, the private-client-tax attorney, estate attorney, CPA, and securities counsel who will actually implement the plan will cite the sources below in their engagement letters and opinions.

> **Reminder: this module is education, not personal tax, investment, legal, or estate advice.** Every citation below is a statute, regulation, release, practitioner reference, or vendor description that *supports* a live engagement with qualified counsel — it does not substitute for one. Statutes and regulations change (§1202 has been amended repeatedly; the Rule 10b5-1 amendments took effect in 2023; state conformity rules change year to year); verify the current status before relying on any specific citation.

## Internal Revenue Code and Treasury Regulations

The core statutory anatomy for the module.

- **IRC §1202 — Partial exclusion for gain from certain small business stock** — the Qualified Small Business Stock exclusion, including the five qualifying-stock requirements, the per-issuer-per-shareholder cap, the gift-carryover rule under §1202(h)(2), the §368-reorg carryover under §1202(h)(4), the active-business requirement, and the §1202(e)(3) excluded-category list. <https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/26/1202>
- **IRC §1045 — Rollover of gain from qualified small business stock to another qualified small business stock** — the 60-day replacement-QSBS rollover mechanism for pre-5-year sales. <https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/26/1045>
- **IRC §1(h) — Maximum capital gains rate** — the capital-gains rate structure including the §1(h)(4)(A)(ii) 28% maximum for 50%-vintage §1202 gain. <https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/26/1>
- **IRC §1411 — Net investment income tax** — the 3.8% NIIT; note that §1202-excluded gain is excluded from NIIT. <https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/26/1411>
- **IRC §57(a)(7) — AMT preference items** — the AMT preference for 75% and 50% §1202-exclusion gain. <https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/26/57>
- **IRC §351 — Transfer to corporation controlled by transferor** — relevant for QSBS packing (contributing property for stock). <https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/26/351>
- **IRC §§671–679 — Grantors and others treated as substantial owners** — the grantor-trust triggers that must be avoided for a non-grantor-trust QSBS stacking vehicle. <https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/26/subtitle-A/chapter-1/subchapter-J/part-I/subpart-E>
- **IRC §643(f) — Treatment of multiple trusts** — the aggregation rule that constrains multi-trust stacking designs. <https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/26/643>
- **IRC §664 — Charitable remainder trusts** — the CRT structure including CRAT and CRUT variants, the 10% minimum-remainder requirement, and the four-tier distribution ordering rule. <https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/26/664>
- **IRC §170 — Charitable, etc., contributions and gifts** — the §170(b) AGI percentage limits (60% cash to public charity, 30% appreciated property to public charity, 30% cash to private foundation, 20% appreciated property to private foundation), the §170(e)(5) qualified-appreciated-stock exception, and the §170(f)(11) qualified-appraisal requirement. <https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/26/170>
- **IRC §501(c)(3) — Charitable, religious, educational, etc. organizations** — the tax-exempt status underlying DAFs, CRTs (remainder beneficiaries), and private foundations. <https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/26/501>
- **IRC §§4940–4945 — Private foundation excise taxes** — the net-investment-income tax (§4940), self-dealing (§4941), minimum-distribution requirement (§4942), excess-business-holdings (§4943), jeopardising-investments (§4944), and taxable-expenditure (§4945) rules. <https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/26/subtitle-D/chapter-42>
- **IRC §4958 — Taxes on excess benefit transactions** — intermediate sanctions on public charities (including DAF sponsors) for excess-benefit transactions. <https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/26/4958>
- **IRC §§2501–2524 — Gift tax** — the federal gift-tax rules including the annual exclusion (§2503), lifetime unified exemption (§2505), spousal gift-splitting (§2513), and the §2522 charitable-contribution deduction. <https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/26/subtitle-B/chapter-12>
- **IRC §§2601–2663 — Generation-skipping transfer tax** — the GST rules relevant for grandchild-generation trust planning. <https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/26/subtitle-B/chapter-13>
- **IRC §1041 — Transfers of property between spouses or incident to divorce** — the inter-spousal transfer rule relevant for spousal QSBS planning. <https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/26/1041>
- **IRC §1259 — Constructive sales treatment for appreciated financial positions** — the rule triggering sale treatment for certain hedging transactions (collars, PVFs) that eliminate substantially all risk. <https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/26/1259>
- **IRC §1092 — Straddles** — the straddle rules relevant for options-collar structuring and holding-period preservation. <https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/26/1092>
- **IRC §1234 — Options to buy or sell** — the tax treatment of option premiums for collar structures. <https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/26/1234>
- **IRC §721 — Nonrecognition of gain or loss on contribution** — the partnership-contribution non-recognition rule underlying exchange-fund tax treatment. <https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/26/721>
- **IRC §1091 — Loss from wash sales of stock or securities** — the wash-sale rule relevant for tax-loss harvesting around concentrated-position management. <https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/26/1091>
- **26 CFR (Treasury Regulations)** — the Treasury-issued regulations implementing the above statutes. Access via <https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-26>. Note: Treasury regulations under §1202 are limited; much of the practitioner interpretation relies on the statute, legislative history, and selected Revenue Rulings and Private Letter Rulings.
- **Rev. Rul. 2003-7** — IRS ruling on the tax treatment of prepaid variable forward contracts as open transactions. <https://www.irs.gov/pub/irs-drop/rr-03-07.pdf>

## IRS forms and instructions

The specific tax-filing-and-reporting forms the plan will produce.

- **Form 706 — US Estate (and Generation-Skipping Transfer) Tax Return** — required for estates above the lifetime unified exemption; also used for spousal portability election under §2010(c)(5). <https://www.irs.gov/forms-pubs/about-form-706>
- **Form 1041 — US Income Tax Return for Estates and Trusts** — the trust-income-tax return filed by non-grantor trusts. <https://www.irs.gov/forms-pubs/about-form-1041>
- **Form 5227 — Split-Interest Trust Information Return** — the CRT (and CLT) annual reporting. <https://www.irs.gov/forms-pubs/about-form-5227>
- **Form 1023 and Form 1023-EZ — Application for Recognition of Exemption Under §501(c)(3)** — the private-foundation (and other §501(c)(3)) application forms. <https://www.irs.gov/forms-pubs/about-form-1023>

- **17 CFR §230.144 — Rule 144 safe harbour for sales of restricted and control securities** — the holding period, current-public-information, volume-limitation, manner-of-sale, and Form 144 filing requirements. <https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-17/chapter-II/part-230/subject-group-ECFRfed24d2ed5bdd36/section-230.144>

- **SEC Regulation Best Interest (2019, Release No. 34-86031)** — the broker-dealer best-interest standard for retail customers (relevant to the fiduciary-vs-broker distinction in chapter 7). <https://www.sec.gov/rules/final/2019/34-86031.pdf>

- **Tax Policy Center (Brookings and Urban Institute)** — non-partisan tax-policy analysis. <https://www.taxpolicycenter.org/>
- **Tax Foundation** — tax-policy research. <https://taxfoundation.org/>
- **Senate Finance Committee** and **House Ways and Means Committee** — primary legislative sources for pending tax changes. <https://www.finance.senate.gov/> and <https://waysandmeans.house.gov/>

## Cross-module interactions

- **mod-103 — M&A deal structures and consideration** — chapter 08 covers QSBS preservation through the M&A transaction structure (asset vs. stock, §368 reorg, §338(h)(10) implications). This module runs after the transaction structure has been chosen.
- **mod-104, mod-105 — LOI negotiation and diligence** — the transaction mechanics that lead to the specific sale structure.
- **mod-106 — Secondary transactions and pre-exit liquidity** — pre-IPO structured secondaries and tenders; §1202 and §1045 analysis of secondary-tender participation.
- **mod-107, mod-108 — IPO readiness and execution** — the IPO process and lock-up architecture that this module's 10b5-1 and diversification plans sit on top of.
- **mod-109 — Transaction governance and fiduciary duty** — the fiduciary-duty framework for insiders and board directors.
- **mod-110 — Transaction communications and stakeholder management** — the communications discipline around personal-liquidity events.
- **mod-111 — Post-close integration and 100-day plan** — the operational post-close arc that overlays with the personal-planning arc.
- **mod-113 — Winding down shutdown mechanics** — the mirror-image shutdown-personal-tax material (§165 / §1244 worthless-security deductions, loss-of-QSBS-eligibility).
- **startup-finance-fundraising-curriculum mod-104 / mod-106** — cap-table waterfall math and preferred-stock economics that produce the gross-realisation input to this module.
- **startup-operations-governance-curriculum** — the ongoing corporate finance-and-governance function including the issuer-level insider-trading-policy administration.
