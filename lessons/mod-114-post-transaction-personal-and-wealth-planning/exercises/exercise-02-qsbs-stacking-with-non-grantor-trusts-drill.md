# exercise-02: QSBS Stacking With Non-Grantor Trusts Drill

**Estimated effort:** 3 hours

## Objective

Design a specific, defensible QSBS stacking plan for the frozen personal fact pattern you established in exercise-01, culminating in a specific stacking-design memo you could hand to a private-client tax attorney and estate attorney as the opening document of the engagement. By the end of the exercise you should know how many separate $10M caps your fact pattern can defensibly support, which specific non-grantor trusts (or family-member recipients) would hold which specific blocks of QSBS, how the gift-tax lifetime exemption is consumed by the gifts, how the §1045 rollover (if applicable) fits into the plan, and what the specific advance-timing calendar needs to be to make the plan defensible.

## Background

This exercise covers material from:

- [Chapter 2 — QSBS Stacking Mechanics](../02-qsbs-stacking-mechanics.md)
- Dependencies on [Chapter 1 — IRC §1202 QSBS Fundamentals](../01-irc-1202-qsbs-fundamentals.md) and the exercise-01 tranche inventory.

Skip this exercise only if your exercise-01 cap-arithmetic result shows your entire aggregate gain fits comfortably within a single $10M cap (and your fact pattern does not credibly expand above the cap through planned future appreciation or §1045 rollover reinvestment). For founders with aggregate gain above $10M, this exercise is essential.

## Prerequisites

- The frozen personal fact-pattern profile from exercise-01.
- The tranche-by-tranche inventory and cap-arithmetic result from exercise-01.
- Access to primary sources: IRC §1202(h) (gift-carryover treatment), §1202(h)(4) (§368 reorg carryover), §1045 (replacement QSBS rollover), §643(f) (multiple-trust aggregation), §§671–679 (grantor-trust triggers), §§2501–2524 (gift tax), §§2601–2663 (GST tax), the current federal gift-tax lifetime unified exemption amount, and the state-residency-of-trust rules for your state of residence.
- A rough estimate of your current lifetime-gift-tax-exemption usage (prior gifts that have consumed exemption; any prior Form 709 filings).
- A planning horizon for the exit transaction — you will be designing a plan that assumes a specific 12–24+ month advance-timing window.

## Tasks

### 1. The expansion thesis — what the stacking is solving

State explicitly, at the top of your working document, the specific arithmetic problem the stacking is solving:

- Current pre-planning scenario (from exercise-01): aggregate QSBS gain, applicable cap ($10M or 10× basis), excluded portion, taxable portion, federal tax, state tax.
- Target post-planning scenario: aggregate QSBS gain remains the same, applicable aggregate caps across multiple $10M caps (across founder plus trusts plus family-member recipients), excluded portion, taxable portion, federal tax, state tax.
- The specific dollar delta the plan is intended to capture (the reduction in taxable portion × the applicable blended tax rate).

If the delta is less than roughly $500K, consider whether the complexity and ongoing-administration cost of the stacking plan is justified. For deltas of $1M+ the stacking plan is typically self-funding against its own setup and administration cost.

### 2. The family-structure census and recipient inventory

Build a specific inventory of potential recipients for QSBS gifts. For each recipient, note:

- **Direct family-member recipients.** Spouse (if applicable); adult children (age 18+ for most purposes, age 24+ for kiddie-tax-free status), with specific ages; adult siblings; parents; any other adult family members with whom you have a close relationship and whose receipt of QSBS would be plausible as an estate-planning gift.
- **Trust recipients.** For each candidate trust, note the specific primary beneficiary(ies), the trust type (non-grantor trust is the stacking vehicle), the proposed trust situs (Delaware, South Dakota, Nevada, Wyoming are the common favourable-state choices; your own state of residence if a state-residency-of-trust rule applies), the proposed trustee (independent trust company, individual trustee not related to the grantor), and the trust's distribution standard (HEMS — Health, Education, Maintenance, Support — is the common standard).
- **Existing structures.** Any existing non-grantor trusts, GRATs, SLATs, foundations, or DAFs that could receive QSBS. Note which are grantor trusts (do not expand the cap — flow through to grantor) and which are non-grantor (expand the cap).

Produce a candidate-recipient table with the specific count of defensible $10M caps your fact pattern can support. A conservative count: founder (1 cap) + spouse-owned portion (1 cap, if community property or spousal gift supports it) + one non-grantor trust per *distinct primary beneficiary* with distinct HEMS provisions, properly-independent trustees, and plausible non-tax purposes.

### 3. The stacking-design decision

Design the specific stacking structure. For each proposed cap, specify:

- **Cap-holder identity.** Founder personally, spouse personally, specific non-grantor trust (name), specific adult family-member recipient (name).
- **QSBS block to transfer to this cap-holder.** Specific tranches (from exercise-01's inventory) and specific share counts, with specific aggregate adjusted basis and specific expected gain.
- **Timing of the transfer.** Specific target date, chosen to maximise the advance-timing window before any contemplated transaction. For a 12–24 month advance window, target gift completion no later than 12–18 months before the anticipated transaction.
- **Mechanic of the transfer.** For trust transfers: completed gift to the non-grantor trust under §2501, basis carryover under §1015, QSBS characteristics carryover under §1202(h)(2). For family-member transfers: same mechanic. For spousal transfers: §1041 inter-spousal transfer with carryover basis, QSBS characteristics carryover.
- **Gift-tax consequences.** Fair-market-value of the transferred stock at the gift date (requires qualified appraisal under §170(f)(11) for non-publicly-traded stock exceeding $10K — plan the appraisal timing). Lifetime-exemption consumption against the current federal exemption amount. Any gift tax owed above the exemption (40% rate on exceedances).
- **GST allocation.** For trusts with grandchild-generation or more-remote beneficiaries, specific GST-exemption allocation plan on the Form 709 for the year of the gift.

### 4. The §643(f) and substance-over-form defence

For each pair of proposed trusts, run the §643(f) aggregation-risk analysis:

- **Grantor identity.** Are the trusts' grantors "substantially the same"? Design around this by using distinct grantors — spouse A funds Trust 1, spouse B funds Trust 2, non-spouse family members fund their own trusts if the family-member's wealth supports it.
- **Primary-beneficiary identity.** Are the trusts' primary beneficiaries "substantially the same"? Design around this by using distinct primary beneficiaries — Trust 1 for Child A, Trust 2 for Child B, Trust 3 for grandchildren pot, Trust 4 for sibling.
- **Principal-purpose-of-tax-avoidance analysis.** Document the specific non-tax purposes each trust serves: beneficiary's education and health support, inheritance protection against creditors and divorce, multigenerational-wealth-transfer discipline, specific state-residency-of-trust tax benefits, specific-GST-planning benefits. The absence of a specific non-tax purpose is a §643(f) and substance-over-form red flag.

For each proposed trust, document the plausible non-tax purposes in writing. If a trust's only plausible purpose is QSBS stacking, consider restructuring the beneficiary profile or dropping the trust from the plan.

### 5. The §1045 rollover decision

For tranches that will *not* reach 5 years by the transaction date (from exercise-01's holding-period analysis), decide whether §1045 rollover is operationally viable:

- **Replacement-QSBS identification.** Where would you reinvest the proceeds within the 60-day window? Candidate replacement investments: a new company you are founding, an angel-stage investment in a qualifying startup, a specific portfolio of early-stage angel investments through an SPV. Note that the replacement corporation must satisfy all the §1202 requirements at the replacement-stock-issuance date.
- **Rollover amount.** The deferral applies to the portion of proceeds reinvested in replacement QSBS. Non-reinvested proceeds are taxable in the sale year. Decide the specific target rollover amount vs. retained-liquidity amount.
- **Form 8949 and specific election mechanics.** The §1045 election is made on the tax return for the year of the original sale, with specific reporting on Form 8949. Plan the specific election preparation with your CPA.
- **Aggregate-holding-period tacking.** After rollover, the replacement stock's holding period includes the original stock's holding period. Project the specific date on which the aggregate holding period reaches 5 years and the replacement stock becomes §1202-eligible.

For founders whose entire gain is QSBS-eligible at the transaction date (full 5-year hold on all tranches), §1045 is not applicable to this exit; it may still be a planning tool for future positions.

### 6. The gift-tax and lifetime-exemption calendar

Build a specific gift-tax calendar covering the year of the gifts and the subsequent planning horizon:

- **Lifetime-exemption consumption.** For each proposed gift, document the FMV, the exemption consumed, and the cumulative-exemption-remaining after the gift.
- **Form 709 filing calendar.** Form 709 is due April 15 of the year following the gift. Note the specific filing dates for each gift year.
- **Annual-exclusion planning.** The $19K-per-donee-per-year (for 2026; verify current amount) annual exclusion is small relative to QSBS gift sizes but can incrementally reduce the lifetime-exemption consumption. For spousal gift-splitting, document the specific election.
- **Spousal portability.** For a married couple, the deceased-spouse's unused lifetime exemption can be ported to the surviving spouse via Form 706. Note the specific portability-election dependency if the plan assumes it.
- **Sunset-timing exposure.** The federal lifetime unified exemption may change in the future depending on legislative action; large stacking plans that depend on the current exemption level should capture the exemption consumption while the current rules are in effect, with an understanding that legislative changes could reduce the available exemption in later years. Note any legislative-timing considerations that affect your plan.

### 7. The trust-situs and administration plan

For each proposed non-grantor trust, specify:

- **Trust situs.** Delaware, South Dakota, Nevada, Wyoming, Alaska for the common favourable-state choices. For California-resident grantors with California-resident beneficiaries, note that California's throwback-tax rules under Cal. Rev. & Tax Code §17742 can reach the trust's income even if the trust is sited outside California — specific review with California-tax counsel is essential.
- **Trustee selection.** Independent trust company (Northern Trust, Bessemer Trust, US Bank Private Wealth Management, BNY Mellon Wealth Management, South Dakota Trust Company, Delaware Trust Company, or similar), individual trustee not related to the grantor, or a co-trustee arrangement. The trustee must have genuine independent discretion for the non-grantor-trust classification to hold.
- **Trustee fees and administration cost.** Trust-administration fees typically 0.4–1.0% of trust assets annually, with minimum annual fees of $5K–$25K per trust. Model the aggregate ongoing cost across all proposed trusts.
- **Distribution standard.** HEMS or a specific-defined distribution-standard. Discretionary standards are stronger for the §643(f) non-tax-purpose defence but require the trustee to exercise genuine discretion.
- **Investment policy.** The trust will hold the QSBS; after sale, the trust holds cash and reinvests. Specify a draft investment-policy statement at the trust level.
- **Ongoing reporting.** Form 1041 annually, Schedule K-1 to beneficiaries for any distributions. Plan the trust-tax-preparation engagement with the CPA.

### 8. The advance-timing calendar

Build a specific calendar from the current date through the planned transaction date. Include:

- **Trust formation and drafting.** 4–12 weeks with estate counsel per trust. Earlier is better.
- **Trustee engagement.** 2–8 weeks for independent trust company selection and onboarding.
- **Qualified appraisal procurement.** 4–8 weeks for non-publicly-traded stock. Must be complete before the gift is finalised.
- **Gift completion.** Specific target date for each gift. Target 12–24+ months before the transaction.
- **Form 709 preparation and filing.** Due April 15 of the year following the gift. Involves the CPA.
- **Trust-administration establishment.** Bank accounts, EIN, initial trustee actions. Not a one-week task.
- **Trust-level 10b5-1 planning (if applicable).** For trusts holding Section-16-attributable stock in a public issuer, specific trust-level 10b5-1 planning is required. See exercise 03.
- **Transaction-close planning.** Coordinate the stacking plan with the broader transaction-structure choice (chapter 2's interaction with mod-103).

A plan with trust-formation 18 months before the transaction, specific-independent-trustee engagement in month 1, qualified-appraisal in months 2–3, gift completion in month 4, Form 709 filing in month 16, and transaction close in month 18, is qualitatively different from a plan where everything compresses into the final 8 weeks.

### 9. The stacking-design memo

Produce a 2–3 page stacking-design memo you would hand to a private-client tax attorney and estate attorney at the first engagement meeting. The memo should include:

- The expansion thesis (specific dollar delta the stacking is capturing).
- The proposed cap inventory (founder, spouse, trusts, family-member recipients; specific count of caps).
- The proposed QSBS-block transfers to each cap-holder (specific tranches, share counts, basis, FMV).
- The §643(f) and substance-over-form defence summary (distinct grantors, distinct primary beneficiaries, documented non-tax purposes, advance-timing calendar).
- The §1045 rollover plan (if applicable).
- The gift-tax and lifetime-exemption calendar.
- The trust-situs and trustee-engagement plan.
- The specific unresolved questions for counsel (one bullet per question): e.g., "Confirm whether a Delaware non-grantor trust with a California-resident primary beneficiary avoids California's throwback-tax under §17742 — specific California-tax-counsel review required"; "Confirm whether the proposed spousal gift under §1041 and §1202(h)(2) establishes a second cap for the spouse or is aggregated with the founder's cap in community-property California".

## Starter guidance

Three anti-patterns to avoid:

- **The "set up 10 trusts the week before signing" compression failure.** The single largest determinant of stacking defensibility is advance timing. A plan completed 18 months before the transaction, with genuine trust administration and independent trustees, is qualitatively different from one completed 3 weeks before signing an LOI. If the compressed-timing plan is the only plan available, consider dropping the stacking and running direct-sale-with-cap only — the stacking's expected value is heavily discounted by the increased collapse risk.
- **The "same primary beneficiary across all trusts" §643(f) failure.** Four trusts whose primary beneficiary is "my children in equal shares" are much easier to aggregate under §643(f) than four trusts with distinct named primary beneficiaries. Design the trusts with distinct primary-beneficiary allocation.
- **The "retained-control" grantor-trust failure.** A trust that looks non-grantor on paper but is administered as the founder's personal account — founder-controlled trustee, distributions solely at founder's direction, no genuine independent oversight — can be attacked as a grantor trust under §§674 or 675, collapsing the stacking design. Independent trustees exercising genuine discretion is essential.

## Acceptance criteria

You can demonstrate that:

- The expansion thesis is specific in dollars and specific in the resulting cap arithmetic.
- The proposed cap count is defensible under §643(f) and substance-over-form, with specific documentation of distinct grantors, distinct primary beneficiaries, and non-tax purposes.
- The proposed QSBS-block allocation among cap-holders is specific per tranche.
- The §1045 rollover plan (if applicable) specifies the replacement-QSBS target, the 60-day window, and the Form-8949 election mechanics.
- The gift-tax calendar accounts for lifetime-exemption consumption, Form 709 filings, and spousal-portability considerations.
- The trust-situs plan specifies state, trustee, administration cost, and distribution standard.
- The advance-timing calendar runs from the current date through the transaction with specific milestones and durations.
- The stacking-design memo is specific enough that a private-client tax attorney and estate attorney would be able to walk into the first engagement meeting with a specific agenda — including specific-trust drafting assignments, specific appraisal RFPs, and specific state-tax-counsel referrals.
- A critical reader (a private-client tax attorney, an estate attorney, a sophisticated CFO who has worked on another founder's stacking plan) can test each design decision and find it defensible or specifically-questioned.

## Reflection

Add a short reflection (½ page):

1. If your advance-timing window is less than 12 months before the transaction, which specific elements of the plan are at highest risk and how would you compress them (or drop them)?
2. If the federal lifetime exemption is reduced before your gifts are completed, how does the plan change? What is the specific trigger for pulling the gift completion forward?
3. For founders in California, Pennsylvania, or other non-conforming states, what specific state-tax strategies interact with the stacking plan (trust situs selection, trustee selection, state-residency-change timing)?

## Stretch goals

- **Alternative stacking configurations.** Design two alternative stacking plans — one more aggressive (more trusts, more caps) and one more conservative (fewer trusts, lower collapse risk) — and specify the trade-off between expected-value tax savings and collapse-probability.
- **Multi-generation stacking.** For founders with substantial expected future appreciation beyond the current transaction (e.g., stock in the acquirer post-§368-reorg, or retained-position post-IPO), design a longer-horizon stacking plan that captures the §1202 exclusion on future appreciation.
- **State-residency-change-integrated plan.** For founders considering a pre-close residency change, design the integrated plan — residency change, trust formation in the new residence state, and stacking plan — with specific timing and documentation for the Franchise-Tax-Board-audit-defensible residency change.
