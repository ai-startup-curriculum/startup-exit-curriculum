# exercise-05: CRT / DAF / Private-Foundation Structuring Drill

**Estimated effort:** 3 hours

## Objective

Design the specific charitable-vehicle structure for your frozen fact pattern — selecting among Charitable Remainder Trust (CRT), Donor-Advised Fund (DAF), and private foundation (and combinations), choosing specific sponsors or trustees, specifying the pre-close contribution amounts and timing, and producing a decision memo you could hand to a private-client tax attorney, estate attorney, and (for larger foundation structures) a foundation specialist as the opening document of the engagement. By the end of the exercise you should be able to defend the specific vehicle mix against your philanthropic intent, the specific pre-close-timing against the anticipatory-assignment-of-income doctrine, and each vehicle's governance and successor structure against the family's long-term giving arc.

## Background

This exercise covers material from:

- [Chapter 5 — Charitable Vehicles](../05-charitable-vehicles.md)
- Dependencies on [Chapter 1 — QSBS Fundamentals](../01-irc-1202-qsbs-fundamentals.md), [Chapter 2 — Stacking](../02-qsbs-stacking-mechanics.md), [Chapter 4 — Post-Lockup Diversification](../04-post-lockup-diversification.md), and preview of [Chapter 6 — Family Office Setup](../06-family-office-setup.md).

Even if your philanthropic intent at this exercise is small, the exercise is worth running to document the specific intent and to confirm that the small-scale structure (likely a DAF) is the right fit.

## Prerequisites

- The QSBS tranche inventory and cap-arithmetic from exercise-01.
- The stacking plan (if applicable) from exercise-02.

## Tasks

### 1. The philanthropic-intent profile

Author a one-page philanthropic-intent profile at the top of your working document. Include:

- **Dollar magnitude.** Total aggregate philanthropic commitment over the planning horizon — specific number. If uncertain, specify a range (e.g., "$3M–$10M over the next 20 years, depending on specific decision triggers").
- **Recipient-category breakdown.** For each major category you intend to support (e.g., education, health, religious, climate, specific-cause), the approximate percentage of the aggregate commitment.
- **Deployment horizon.** When you intend to actually deploy the funds — immediate (0–3 years), medium-term (3–10 years), long-term (10+ years), multigenerational (across your lifetime and your descendants'), permanent (foundation that outlives the family).
- **Governance preference.** Who should participate in grant decisions — you alone, you and spouse, you and adult children, extended family, non-family advisors or board. Current preference may evolve.
- **Legacy orientation.** Do you intend to build a long-term philanthropic institution that bears the family name across generations, or do you intend to deploy the funds within your lifetime and let the vehicles conclude at some point?
- **Specific-named-charities list.** Any specific charities you already know you want to support, including specific grant-amount targets and timing if specific.

### 2. The pre-close vs. post-close contribution decision

Decide which portion of your charitable commitment should be funded pre-close with appreciated stock vs. post-close with cash:

**Pre-close appreciated-stock contribution.**

- Advantages: avoids capital-gains tax on the appreciation (the vehicle sells the stock without triggering donor-level tax), produces a §170 deduction at FMV. Combined benefit roughly 25–40% of the stock's value depending on vintage, cap, and state.
- Timing requirement: must be complete before the sale becomes effectively certain. Practitioner consensus: before the LOI is signed is safely within the window; after the definitive agreement is signed is risky; at or after closing is typically not effective.
- Appraisal requirement: for non-publicly-traded stock exceeding $10K, a qualified appraisal under §170(f)(11) is required. Plan the appraisal timing — 4–8 weeks typical.

**Post-close cash contribution.**

- Advantages: simpler mechanics, no appraisal requirement, no anticipatory-assignment-of-income concern.
- Disadvantages: loses the capital-gains-tax-avoidance benefit; the §170 deduction applies to the after-tax cash contributed rather than to the full FMV of what the pre-close contribution would have given.

**Decision.** Specify the specific portion of the charitable commitment to fund pre-close and the specific portion to fund post-close. For founders with substantial above-cap gain, large pre-close contributions are often the single largest tax lever available.

### 3. The vehicle-mix selection

Design the specific vehicle mix. For each vehicle, specify:

**DAF (recommended for most founders' flexible-granting component):**

- Contribution amount (pre-close appreciated-stock portion).
- Sponsor selection (Fidelity Charitable, Schwab Charitable, Vanguard Charitable, Impact Assets, National Philanthropic Trust, specific community foundations). Compare sponsors on: investment options, fee structure, grant flexibility, minimum requirements, successor-advisor terms, sponsor's governance posture, and sponsor's track record with similar donor profiles.
- Successor-advisor plan — specific individuals (spouse, adult children, grandchildren as they reach adulthood) with specific advisor roles and expected transition timing.
- Investment strategy within the DAF — specific allocation among the sponsor's available investment pools, with specific rationale for the equity/fixed-income/alternatives mix.
- Granting cadence — specific target annual grant amount, specific granting discipline (e.g., 5% minimum annual distribution to force deployment, higher for specific immediate priorities).

**CRT (recommended for founders needing lifetime income from the contributed assets):**

- Contribution amount.
- Variant selection: CRAT (fixed annual payment), CRUT (variable), NIMCRUT (net-income-with-makeup), Flip-CRUT (NICRUT until a trigger event, then SCRUT). Choose and justify based on your income needs and the contributed-asset's expected cash-flow profile.
- Payout rate — specific percentage (typically 5–8%). Note that §664 requires the projected charitable remainder to be at least 10% of the initial contribution value, which constrains the maximum payout rate given the term and §7520 rate.
- Term — life of donor, joint lives (donor and spouse), defined term up to 20 years. Choose and justify.
- Named charitable remainder beneficiary(ies) — specific charities or specific class of charities. For founders with a parallel private foundation, the CRT remainder can be designated to the foundation.
- Trustee selection — independent trust company or qualified individual trustee.
- Investment strategy within the CRT — specific allocation that supports the required payout cadence.
- Form 5227 filing coordination with the CPA.

**Private foundation (recommended for founders with substantial philanthropic commitment and multigenerational-institutional intent):**

- Contribution amount (note: AGI limits are tighter for private foundations — 30% of AGI for cash, 20% for appreciated property, with deduction generally limited to basis for non-qualified-appreciated-stock contributions).
- Formation jurisdiction — Delaware nonprofit corporation (common), the family's home state, or a specific trust jurisdiction. Note the ongoing state-registration-and-reporting obligations.
- §501(c)(3) application — Form 1023 (full) or 1023-EZ (smaller foundations). 6–18 month application window.
- Board composition — specific individuals (founder, spouse, adult children, non-family board members) with specific roles.
- Grant-strategy outline — specific program areas, specific grant-making discipline, specific grant-amount ranges.
- Administrative plan — in-house staff (if any), external foundation-counsel engagement, CPA engagement for Form 990-PF, foundation-platform selection if applicable (Benevity, FoundationSource, Grants Management Systems).
- §4942 minimum-distribution planning — 5% annual distribution baseline; specific-grant-pipeline development to meet the requirement.
- §4941 self-dealing review — specific review of any transactions between the foundation and disqualified persons (founder, family, substantial contributors, related entities). Note any planned leases, loans, or other transactions and resolve before formation.
- §4943 excess-business-holdings review — if the foundation will hold stock in the founder's operating company (common pattern), confirm the aggregate holdings (foundation + disqualified persons) do not exceed 20% of voting stock.

### 4. The AGI-limit and multi-year-deduction calendar

Build a specific multi-year AGI-and-deduction calendar:

- **AGI projection by year.** Years T-1, T (transaction year), T+1 through T+5. Include wages, business income, capital-gains income (QSBS-non-excluded portion, non-QSBS gains, other), investment income, and other.
- **Charitable-deduction limit by year.** Compute the applicable §170 AGI percentage limits by vehicle type:
  - Cash to public charity (including DAF): 60% of AGI.
  - Appreciated long-term property to public charity (including DAF, CRT): 30% of AGI (for FMV deduction).
  - Cash to private foundation: 30% of AGI.
  - Appreciated long-term property to private foundation: 20% of AGI (and generally limited to basis for non-qualified-appreciated-stock).
- **5-year carryforward.** Deductions exceeding the current-year limit carry forward up to 5 years, subject to the same AGI percentage limits in each carryforward year.
- **Multi-vehicle coordination.** For plans using multiple vehicles in the same year, note the ordering rule — the §170 limits apply in a specific sequence that practitioners work through carefully (public-charity cash first, public-charity appreciated-property next, private-foundation cash next, private-foundation appreciated-property last, subject to the applicable AGI percentages). CPA review required for the specific ordering.

### 5. The pre-close-contribution timing calendar

For each pre-close contribution, build a specific timing calendar:

- **Vehicle formation.** DAF account opening (1–4 weeks). CRT drafting and trustee engagement (4–12 weeks). Private foundation formation and §501(c)(3) application (3–18 months). Begin earliest for the slowest vehicle.
- **Qualified appraisal procurement.** For pre-IPO or private-company stock, 4–8 weeks for a defensible qualified appraisal under §170(f)(11). The appraiser must meet specific IRS criteria and the appraisal must be attached to the donor's return via Form 8283.
- **Stock-transfer execution.** Specific transfer mechanics — stock-power execution, cap-table update, broker-and-transfer-agent coordination, specific contribution date documented.
- **Transaction-structure coordination.** For a stock-for-stock merger, the vehicle will receive acquirer stock in the merger; confirm this is desirable and that the vehicle can hold the acquirer stock.
- **Anticipatory-assignment-of-income analysis.** Document the contribution date relative to the LOI, definitive agreement, and closing date. For contributions after the definitive agreement is signed, counsel should specifically review the recharacterisation risk.
- **Form 709 (gift tax) filing coordination.** Charitable contributions are not gift-tax-triggering (charitable contributions to §501(c)(3) organisations are deductible for gift-tax purposes under §2522), but any related non-charitable gifts (e.g., successor-advisor appointments or specific-individual beneficiary designations) may have gift-tax implications. CPA review of Form 709 for the gift year.
- **Form 8283 filing.** For non-cash contributions exceeding $500, Form 8283 is filed with the tax return. For non-cash contributions exceeding $5,000, Section B of Form 8283 is filed with the qualified appraisal.

### 6. The successor and legacy structure

For each vehicle, specify the successor and legacy plan:

**DAF successor advisors.**

- Specific named individuals (spouse, adult children, grandchildren as they reach the sponsor's permitted age) with specific-generation sequencing.
- Sponsor's rules on successor-advisor generation limits (typically 2–3 generations; varies by sponsor).
- Terminal-distribution plan — on the end of the successor-advisor chain, what does the sponsor do with the remaining DAF balance (specific family-nominated charities, sponsor's own donor-advised-funds-pool, specific terminal-distribution plan).

**CRT remainder beneficiary.**

- Specific named charities or specific class of charities to receive the remainder at the end of the income term.
- Alternative: remainder to the family's private foundation (if applicable).
- Specific trust-document provisions on remainder-beneficiary modification (typically limited).

**Private foundation governance across generations.**

- Specific board-succession plan — current board, expected next-generation board transition timing, next-next-generation preparation.
- Specific governance documents (bylaws, mission statement, grant-making-policy) that will guide future generations.
- Specific family-council or family-advisor-committee integration with the broader family-office governance (preview of chapter 6).
- Specific termination or spend-down plan if the foundation is not intended to be permanent — some founders design foundations with defined sunset dates (e.g., spending-down over 25–50 years) rather than permanent-institution structures.

### 7. The specialist-engagement plan

Specify the specific specialist engagements:

- **Private-client tax attorney** — overall charitable-planning strategy, §1202 and §170 integration, anticipatory-assignment-of-income analysis.
- **Estate attorney** — CRT drafting, trustee selection, foundation formation, bylaws, successor-structure design.
- **CPA (private-client / high-net-worth)** — AGI projections, multi-year deduction modelling, Form 1040, Form 5227 (CRT), Form 990-PF (foundation), Form 8283 (non-cash contributions).
- **Foundation specialist** (for substantial foundations) — program design, grant due-diligence, foundation-counsel for compliance questions.
- **Qualified appraiser** (for non-publicly-traded stock) — Form 8283 Section B qualified appraisal.
- **Wealth manager / fiduciary RIA** — investment strategy within each vehicle (DAF investment-pool selection, CRT trust-asset management, foundation endowment management).
- **DAF sponsor relationship manager** — ongoing DAF account administration, granting workflow, successor-advisor activation.

For each engagement, specify the specific scope, fee structure, and coordination mechanism (chapter 7's engagement-letter and coordinator discipline).

### 8. The charitable-vehicle decision memo

Produce a 2–3 page decision memo you would hand to the private-client tax attorney and estate attorney at the first engagement meeting. The memo should include:

- The philanthropic-intent profile summary (dollars, categories, deployment horizon, governance preference, legacy orientation).
- The pre-close-vs-post-close contribution decision with specific amounts.
- The vehicle-mix selection with specific per-vehicle details.
- The AGI-limit and multi-year-deduction calendar.
- The pre-close-contribution timing calendar.
- The successor and legacy structure summary.
- The specialist-engagement plan.
- The specific unresolved questions for counsel (one bullet per question): e.g., "Confirm whether the planned pre-IPO stock contribution to the private foundation is 'qualified appreciated stock' under §170(e)(5), permitting FMV deduction rather than basis deduction"; "Confirm the specific §501(c)(3) application timeline such that the foundation is tax-exempt by the planned pre-close contribution date"; "Confirm the specific Flip-CRUT trigger-event drafting for a pre-IPO stock contribution with a flip at the first-sale-of-contributed-stock date".

## Starter guidance

Three anti-patterns to avoid:

- **The "contribute after the sale" mistake.** The single largest tax lever is pre-close appreciated-stock contribution. A founder who sells first, pays the tax, and contributes the after-tax cash has given up roughly 20–25% of the intended charitable capital's value. If the pre-close window is still open, use it.
- **The "private foundation for pre-IPO stock" mistake.** Contributions of pre-IPO or other non-publicly-traded stock to a private foundation generally produce only a basis-level deduction (not FMV), because pre-IPO stock is not "qualified appreciated stock" under §170(e)(5). The same contribution to a DAF or CRT (both public-charity structures) produces a full-FMV deduction. For pre-IPO contributions, DAF or CRT is almost always the better vehicle.
- **The "over-fund the vehicle" mistake.** Charitable-vehicle contributions are irrevocable. A founder who contributes more than they genuinely intend to give to charity cannot reclaim the excess for personal use. Err on the side of smaller initial contributions with the option to add later, rather than large upfront commitments that may not match actual philanthropic intent.

## Acceptance criteria

You can demonstrate that:

- The philanthropic-intent profile is specific in dollars, categories, deployment horizon, governance preference, and legacy orientation.
- The pre-close-vs-post-close contribution decision is specific with amounts and specific anticipatory-assignment-of-income analysis.
- The vehicle-mix selection is specific per-vehicle with specific sponsor/trustee/formation details.
- The AGI-limit and multi-year-deduction calendar is specific by year, with correct per-vehicle percentage limits applied.
- The pre-close-contribution timing calendar is specific on vehicle formation, appraisal procurement, stock transfer, transaction coordination, and tax filings.
- The successor and legacy structure is specific on named individuals and specific generation-sequencing.
- The specialist-engagement plan is specific on counsel, CPA, foundation specialist (if applicable), qualified appraiser, wealth manager, and DAF sponsor contact.
- The decision memo is specific enough that a private-client tax attorney and estate attorney would be able to open a specific first-meeting agenda without preliminary scoping.
- A critical reader (a private-client tax attorney with charitable-planning practice, a foundation-counsel partner, a CPA with foundation-tax practice) can test each decision and find it defensible.

1. If your charitable commitment is substantially smaller than the exercise contemplates (e.g., $100K–$500K rather than $5M+), is a vehicle even the right structure? What alternative (direct giving from after-tax proceeds without a vehicle) would be simpler and nearly as efficient?
2. If your family's long-term philanthropic engagement is uncertain (adult children's interest in philanthropy is unknown, grandchild generation not yet old enough to assess), how does the successor-structure design adjust for the uncertainty? Which vehicle types are more flexible under this uncertainty?

## Stretch goals

- **CRT-remainder-to-foundation combination.** Design the specific integrated plan where the CRT's charitable remainder is designated to the family's private foundation. Specify the trust-document provisions, the remainder-valuation mechanics, and the long-term foundation-funding implications.
- **Program-related-investment (PRI) exploration.** For founders with impact-investing interest, explore how a private foundation could use PRIs under §4944(c) to deploy capital into mission-aligned investments that would otherwise be jeopardising. Specify specific candidate PRI structures and the self-dealing-and-counsel-review requirements.
- **Multi-generation grant-strategy roadmap.** For a founder with substantial long-term philanthropic commitment, author a specific 25-year grant-strategy roadmap — specific program areas, specific grant-amount ranges by year, specific family-governance milestones for next-generation involvement, specific foundation-staffing growth plan.
