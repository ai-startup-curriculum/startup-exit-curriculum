# mod-114 — Post-Transaction Personal & Wealth Planning (QSBS, 10b5-1, Diversification, Family Office)

**Estimated effort:** 16 hours
**Stage:** MATURE (typically the 6–18-month window on either side of a founder / early-employee liquidity event — the sale close, the IPO pricing, the secondary tender, the milestone-payment cliff)
**Seat:** founder-CEO, founder-CTO, founder-CPO, C-level early employee, first-employee cohort with material QSBS, board director with QSBS through a directorship, angel with a concentrated QSBS position — read *with* a personal-tax attorney, a wealth manager, a CPA, and (for the trust and family-office work) an estate attorney

This module installs the **post-transaction personal-and-wealth-planning overlay** — the discipline that lets a founder or early employee whose exit is imminent (or has just landed) convert a paper liquidity event into a defensible personal-tax, concentration-risk, philanthropic, and family-office plan without missing the specific statutory windows that make the plan cheaper by seven or eight figures. Every other module in this track has treated the transaction as a corporate act with corporate consequences; this module treats it as a *personal* event with *personal* consequences that have their own statutory scaffold — IRC §1202 QSBS and §1045 rollover, the SEC 2023 amendments to Rule 10b5-1, IRC §664 charitable remainder trusts and §170 charitable-deduction limits, and the family-office construction that carries the outcome forward for a generation or more.

The frame is *personal-tax and personal-wealth discipline that respects statutory deadlines and delegates to specialists correctly*, not "how to be your own private-client tax attorney." The practitioner canon here — the IRC and Treasury Regulations on §1202 / §1045 / §664 / §170 / §529 / §2503, the SEC's 2023 Rule 10b5-1 amendments, the trust-and-estate treatises (Ferguson-Freeland-Ascher on trust design, Restatement Third of Trusts), the Carta / Holloway / AngelList post-liquidity education libraries, the private-client-tax practitioner writing (WilmerHale, Withers, Kozusko Harris Duncan, McDermott Will & Emery, Morgan Lewis private-client, Perkins Coie private-client), the family-office research (UBS Global Family Office Report, EY / Deloitte / KPMG family-office surveys, Campden Wealth research), the wealth-manager writing (Iconiq Capital, Aspiriant, Bessemer Trust, Northern Trust, JP Morgan Private Bank, Goldman Sachs Private Wealth Management), and the technology-vendor documentation (Addepar, Masttro, Eton Solutions, Ledgex) — converges on a small number of concrete disciplines this module installs. The point is not to substitute for a private-client tax attorney or wealth manager; the point is to arrive at those advisors *already understanding the shape of the decisions on the table* so their time is spent on judgment rather than education, and to know which decisions have statutory deadlines that pre-close planning cannot recover post-close.

> **Reminder: this module is education, not personal tax, investment, legal, or estate advice.** Every claim in this module refers to statutes, regulations, or practitioner materials that change (§1202 has been amended repeatedly; the Rule 10b5-1 amendments took effect in 2023 and are still being litigated in enforcement; state-conformity rules change year to year). Every personal fact pattern (your holding period, your basis, your state of residence, your family structure, your existing trust arrangements) determines which of the frames applies. A live plan is authored by a personal-tax attorney (private-client), a CPA, an estate attorney, a wealth manager, and — for the private-foundation and family-office layers — potentially by counsel and consultants specialised in those disciplines. This module is what you read *before* you engage them so that when you do their time goes to judgment, not education.

## Learning objectives

By the end of this module you should be able to:

- Apply the **IRC §1202 Qualified Small Business Stock exclusion** to your specific position — the 5-year holding requirement, the original-issuance requirement (stock acquired directly from the issuer, not on a secondary), the C-corp-at-issuance requirement, the $50M gross-asset test at and after issuance, the active-business requirement with its excluded-business list, the $10M / 10x-basis per-issuer per-shareholder cap, the 100% federal exclusion for post-September-27-2010 stock vs. 75% for stock acquired between February 18, 2009 and September 27, 2010 vs. 50% for earlier vintages, the state-conformity variation (California and Pennsylvania do not conform; Massachusetts conforms; most other states conform with variations), and the AMT / net-investment-income-tax coordination.
- Structure **QSBS stacking** to multiply the $10M cap — multiple non-grantor trusts each with their own $10M cap, gifting to trusts before the 5-year window closes so the trust receives QSBS characteristics under §1202(h), gifting to family members with independent QSBS caps, spousal-portability planning that respects the joint-vs-separate cap analysis, generation-skipping tax (GST) coordination, and the IRC §1045 rollover into replacement QSBS to preserve the holding period for a pre-5-year sale.
- Author a **Rule 10b5-1 trading plan** for a newly-public founder that survives an SEC enforcement inquiry — the SEC 2023 amendments (90-day cooling-off for insiders, 30-day cooling-off for issuers, the up-to-120-day cap on the officer / director cooling-off in specific scenarios, insider self-certification of good faith and no material-nonpublic-information at plan adoption, the no-overlapping-plans restriction with narrow exceptions, the single-trade-plan-per-12-months restriction), the plan design (regular-cadence sales, staggered releases, price-condition triggers, event-triggered pauses for blackout periods), the disclosure-and-audit-committee-approval discipline, and the interaction with the IPO lock-up expiry and staggered-release windows.
- Design a **post-lockup diversification strategy** that manages concentration risk without triggering avoidable tax — the concentration-risk quantification (percentage of net worth in single-issuer stock, correlation with human-capital exposure at the same issuer), the staged-sale plan (sell into strength across 12–36 months under a 10b5-1 plan), the exchange-fund alternative (Eaton Vance, Cache Financial, Long Angle, Ithan Creek — pool contribution into a diversified fund for the 7-year required holding period without triggering a taxable sale), the options-collar (buy protective puts / sell covered calls to bracket a range), the prepaid variable forward contract (borrow against the stock at a haircut with a defined payoff structure).
- Structure a **charitable-remainder-trust (CRT) / donor-advised-fund (DAF) / private-foundation** strategy — the tax-deduction-vs-donation-vs-legacy trade-off, the CRT annuity-vs-unitrust variants (CRAT, CRUT, NIMCRUT, flip-CRUT), the DAF sponsor selection (Fidelity Charitable, Schwab Charitable, Vanguard Charitable, Impact Assets, National Philanthropic Trust), and the private-foundation structure (§501(c)(3), §4940 net-investment-income tax, §4942 minimum-distribution rule, §4943 excess-business-holdings, §4944 jeopardising-investment, §4945 taxable-expenditure) that pairs a founder wealth event with a multigenerational giving vehicle.
- Set up a **family office** — the direct-family-office (SFO) vs. multi-family-office (MFO) vs. outsourced-CIO decision, the staffing pattern (Chief Investment Officer, Chief Financial Officer / controller, tax specialist, lifestyle-and-security lead, general counsel), the technology stack (Addepar, Masttro, Eton Solutions, Ledgex, Sage Intacct-based multi-entity accounting), the governance model (family-council, next-generation-development programme, philanthropic-strategy integration, investment-policy statement discipline).
- Recognise the **ownership boundary** — this module delivers education, not personal tax, investment, or legal advice; it hands off to qualified counsel (private-client tax attorney, wealth manager, CPA, estate attorney, and — for the specific structures — trust attorney, foundation counsel, family-office consultant) and does not attempt to replace personal advice.

## How to work through this module

Pick a specific personal fact pattern — real (your own upcoming liquidity event, or a scenario you are helping a co-founder / peer through) or a realistic hypothetical you build for this module — and carry it through every exercise. Freeze the profile at the top of exercise-01 (state of residence, filing status, family structure including any minors and next-generation beneficiaries, existing trust arrangements, your role at the company, the specific tranches of QSBS-eligible stock you hold with acquisition dates and adjusted basis, the transaction type and expected close date, any existing 10b5-1 plan or lack thereof) and do not let it drift. The module is worth exactly what the concreteness of your scenario is worth; a wealth plan built against vague numbers produces a vague plan.

Read chapter 1 and do exercise-01 first — the QSBS analysis is the analytic spine every subsequent chapter depends on. Read chapter 2 and do exercise-02 next only if your fact pattern includes gain that exceeds a single $10M cap (or credibly will after §1045 rollover reinvestment); if your entire gain fits comfortably under one $10M cap, chapter 2 is optional context. Read chapters 3 and 4 in parallel if your exit is an IPO — the 10b5-1 plan and the post-lockup diversification plan are the same document family, and exercises 03 and 04 are designed to be executed as one pair against the same fact pattern. If your exit is an M&A cash-out or a secondary, chapter 3 is optional (10b5-1 applies only to public-company insiders); chapter 4 still applies (with the diversification tools that do not require public-company mechanics: staged sale, exchange fund, options collar for the acquirer's public stock if the consideration includes it). Read chapter 5 and do exercise-05 in the same window as chapters 3–4; the CRT / DAF / private-foundation planning has specific pre-close windows (contributing appreciated stock *before* the sale is materially different from contributing cash *after*) that must be captured in the same planning cycle. Read chapter 6 last; the family-office decision is downstream of the wealth-event size and shape, and running it before you know the size produces a family-office design that does not fit. Read chapter 7 as the synthesis; the hand-off discipline determines whether the module's output survives contact with the specialists who will actually implement it.
