# mod-106 — Secondary Transactions & Pre-Exit Liquidity

**Estimated effort:** 22 hours
**Stage:** GROWTH / MATURE (founder secondaries appear at Series C and beyond; employee tenders become recurring workforce infrastructure at Series D and later; structured secondaries and platform-cleared resales are exercised whenever a bounded liquidity path is needed before an exit)
**Seat:** founder-CEO, CFO, GC, VP-People / CPO, compensation-committee chair, securities counsel, tender-offer administrator (Carta / NPM / Shareworks), 409A appraiser, tax counsel

This module owns the *transaction-execution* apparatus for pre-exit liquidity events. mod-101 through mod-105 have installed the sell-side M&A arc — endgame framing, buyer typology, deal structure, LOI and definitive agreements, diligence and data-room operations. Not every liquidity event is a full company sale, though; long before an exit clears, a growth-stage private company is running a related but distinct family of transactions that let founders, employees, and (sometimes) early investors realise bounded liquidity while the company remains private. Those transactions are the founder secondary, the employee tender offer, the structured secondary (collateralised loan or forward contract), the secondary-market-platform-cleared resale, and the tender-in-connection-with-financing — each with its own governance mechanics, its own pricing methodology, its own tax and securities-law regime, and its own signalling implications.

The module covers each shape end-to-end: the design decisions (pricing, eligibility, participation cap, buyer identity), the choreography (ROFR / co-sale notice, board approval, participant education, tender administration), the tax and waterfall analysis (preference-stack-adjusted common price, four tax-treatment categories, §1202 QSBS interaction, withholding mechanics), the platform and regulatory landscape (Nasdaq Private Market, Forge Global, EquityZen, Carta X, PARAGON Prime, Section 4(a)(7), Rule 144 / 144A, FINRA Notice 09-05, blue-sky compliance), and the ongoing-compliance interactions (409A refresh triggers, Rule 701 aggregate-value monitoring). Do these badly and the founder secondary signals exit-intent to the acquirer universe, the tender leaves participating employees with tax bills they did not anticipate, the forward contract triggers a §1259 constructive-sale event nobody planned for, the ROFR notice window lapses and a preferred holder sues for breach, or the 409A refresh drives ISO strike prices materially higher for the next hiring class. Do them well and each transaction closes cleanly, on time, within compliance, with a workforce that trusts the process.

## Learning objectives

By the end of this module you should be able to:

- Design a founder secondary transaction — the rules-of-the-road, the 10–20% common-holding sale ceiling, the alignment-of-interest optics that keep the lead investor comfortable, the board-approval choreography, the tax treatment (long-term-capital-gains vs. §1202 QSBS interaction, holding-period preservation), and the timing rules that keep a founder secondary from signalling exit-intent to acquirers.
- Structure an employee tender offer — pricing methodology (last-round preferred vs. common-share equivalent vs. secondary-market clearing), participation caps (per-employee cap, aggregate-round cap, level-based caps), eligibility rules (tenure requirement, exclusion of executive-team, exclusion of pre-vest shares), waterfall analysis across the preference stack, tax withholding across the four categories (already-held common, same-day cashless NSO exercise, ISO qualifying disposition, RSU settle-and-tender), and the operational choreography (365-day rolling tender vs. one-off, Carta-administered vs. transfer-agent-administered).
- Structure forward-contract and collateralised-loan structured secondaries — the advance rate and haircut trade-off, the §1259 constructive-sale exposure envelope, the counterparty landscape (Setter Capital, ClearList, Rainmaker Securities, DBO Partners, specialist private-securities-lending arms of large financial-services firms), and the counterparty-credit risk the direct-sale mechanics do not carry.
- Execute on secondary-market platforms — Nasdaq Private Market (issuer-organised tender administration), Forge Global (secondary-market clearing venue), EquityZen (buyer-and-seller matching), Carta X (Carta's own venue), PARAGON Prime (specialist broker-dealer), the FINRA Notice 09-05 broker-dealer conduct framework, the Section 4(a)(7) resale exemption, the Rule 144 / 144A restricted-security resale mechanics, and blue-sky compliance across state jurisdictions.
- Manage the ROFR (Right of First Refusal) and co-sale choreography — the standard NVCA-voting-agreement pattern (company first, preferred second, other holders third), the transfer-notice mechanics, the waiver mechanics (board decision, preferred-holder circulation, "silence is a waiver" provisions), and the frequently-overlooked co-sale (tag-along) right that lets minority holders participate proportionally.
- Design tender-offer-in-connection-with-a-financing patterns — the increasingly common combined transaction that closes alongside a new preferred round, the pricing negotiation (secondary at a defined discount to primary), the participation-cap and waterfall analysis, and the interaction with the primary-round cap-table shuffle owned by `startup-finance-fundraising-curriculum` mod-104.
- Manage the interaction with the 409A refresh cycle — the material-event trigger for a fresh 409A, the appraiser-selection and methodology-selection decisions, the impact on subsequent ISO strike prices, and the Rule 701 aggregate-value monitoring that shares the compliance surface.
- Recognise the ownership boundary — equity-comp *policy* (grant guidelines, refresh cadence, compensation-committee governance) is owned by `startup-operations-governance-curriculum`; equity *economics* (waterfall math at scale, 409A methodology, Rule 701 aggregate-value monitoring at ongoing-company depth) is owned by `startup-finance-fundraising-curriculum` mod-104; this module owns the transaction-execution mechanics of the secondary event itself.

## Ownership boundary

This module owns the transaction-execution apparatus for pre-exit liquidity events. It does *not* re-teach:

- **Equity-compensation *policy*** — grant guidelines (how much equity a level-5 engineer receives), refresh cadence (annual, biannual, event-driven), promotion-refresh discipline, new-hire-grant standards, the compensation-committee charter and governance protocols, and the equity-plan document itself — [`startup-operations-governance-curriculum`](https://github.com/ai-startup-curriculum/startup-operations-governance-curriculum) owns those. When a tender's design decisions cross into policy territory (should executives be excluded from the tender? what is the tender's target cadence? does executive participation merit a separate committee approval?), those decisions defer to the compensation-committee governance owned by the operations-governance track.
- **Equity *economics*** — waterfall math at scale (the general mathematical framework, not the specific transaction's application), preference-stack modelling under different scenarios, dilution modelling under primary-round financings, 409A appraisal methodology (OPM vs. PWERM vs. hybrid) at technical depth, Rule 701 aggregate-value monitoring at ongoing-company depth, and cap-table maintenance at Carta / Pulley / Shareworks configuration depth — [`startup-finance-fundraising-curriculum`](https://github.com/ai-startup-curriculum/startup-finance-fundraising-curriculum) mod-104 owns those. This module *uses* those disciplines as inputs; it does not re-teach them.
- **The general M&A execution arc** — endgame framing (mod-101), buyer typology (mod-102), deal structure (mod-103), LOI and definitive agreements (mod-104), diligence and data-room operations (mod-105), and transaction governance / fiduciary duty at deal-scale (mod-109). This module runs *alongside* the M&A arc — a founder secondary at the same time as an inbound sell-side conversation, a tender-in-connection-with-financing at the same time as a Series E primary round — but does not re-teach the M&A execution apparatus.
- **Personal-tax and personal-wealth planning at seller depth** — the individual founder's or executive's personal-tax planning (§1202 stacking, gifting-and-trust strategies, state-residence planning), estate-and-wealth planning, and post-transaction diversification — [mod-114](../mod-114-post-transaction-personal-and-wealth-planning) in this track owns those. This module installs the transaction-execution seat's tax vocabulary (waterfall analysis, withholding categories, §1202 preservation at the transaction seat); the personal-tax specifics for any individual seller defer to their personal tax advisor and to mod-114.
- **The AI / ML / GPAI-related diligence content** that also touches secondary transactions in AI-heavy targets — the AI-governance content owned by [`chief-ai-officer-learning`](https://github.com/ai-governance-curriculum/chief-ai-officer-learning) and [`head-of-ai-governance-learning`](https://github.com/ai-governance-curriculum/head-of-ai-governance-learning). This module treats secondary transactions as transactions in a security regardless of the target's technology posture; the AI-specific diligence considerations belong to the AI-governance tracks.

What this module *adds* on top: the transaction-execution mechanics for the five pre-exit liquidity transaction shapes (founder secondary, employee tender, structured secondary, platform-cleared resale, tender-in-connection-with-financing), the specific tax and waterfall analysis at the transaction-execution seat, the ROFR / co-sale choreography that runs under every share transfer, the platform and counterparty landscape a modern practitioner must know, and the coordination discipline across the compensation-policy, equity-economics, and transaction-execution tracks.

## How to work through this module

Carry a specific hypothetical target through the module — ideally the same target you have been running from mod-101 forward. Chapters 1 through 3 build the founder-secondary and employee-tender apparatus; chapter 4 adds the structured-secondary instruments that expand the shareholder's option set; chapter 5 covers the platform-and-regulatory framework that governs every private-company-security transfer; chapter 6 covers the ROFR / co-sale choreography that runs under all of the above; chapter 7 covers the combined primary-plus-secondary pattern that has become common at growth stage; chapter 8 closes with the 409A refresh, Rule 701 interaction, and ownership-boundary discipline.

Read a chapter, then do the matching exercise while the frame is fresh. The exercises deliberately overlap on the target's fact pattern — the founder secondary sized in exercise-01, the tender designed in exercise-02, the structured-secondary evaluated in exercise-03, and the tender-in-connection-with-financing modelled in exercise-06 are all set against the same hypothetical target's cap table, preference stack, and 409A cycle. That overlap is intentional: it lets you observe how a single company's liquidity architecture composes across transaction shapes rather than treating each shape as a standalone puzzle.
