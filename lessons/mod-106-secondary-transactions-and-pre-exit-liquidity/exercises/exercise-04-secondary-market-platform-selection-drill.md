# exercise-04: Secondary Market Platform Selection Drill

**Estimated effort:** 3 hours

## Objective

Select the right secondary-market platform for four distinct transaction fact patterns, defend each selection against the platform's operational, pricing, and regulatory profile, and author the target-company platform-relationship posture (what information the target agrees to provide, which platforms are on the target's approved-counterparty list, which are affirmatively declined). Produce (a) a platform-selection matrix (2–4 pages) comparing the five dominant platforms against a common set of dimensions, (b) four platform-selection memos (1–2 pages each) covering the four fact patterns, and (c) a target-company platform-relationship posture document (3–5 pages) that the target's CFO / GC could adopt as standing policy.

## Background

This exercise covers material from:

- [Chapter 5 — Secondary-Market Platforms and the FINRA / Rule 144 / Section 4(a)(7) Regulatory Framework](../05-secondary-market-platforms-and-regulation.md) — the exemption stack (Section 4(a)(1) / 4(a)(2) / 4(a)(7), Rule 144, Rule 144A), the FINRA Notice 09-05 broker-dealer conduct framework, the blue-sky compliance surface, and the five dominant platforms (NPM, Forge Global, EquityZen, Carta X, PARAGON Prime).

Supporting references:

- [Chapter 6 — ROFR and Co-Sale Choreography](../06-rofr-and-co-sale-choreography.md) — every platform-cleared transaction runs through the target's ROFR / co-sale apparatus.
- [Chapter 8 — 409A Refresh Cycles, Rule 701 Interaction, and the Ownership Boundary](../08-409a-refresh-and-ownership-boundary.md) — platform-cleared secondary volume at prices materially different from the current 409A may trigger a refresh.

## Prerequisites

- Familiarity with the current platform landscape at chapter 5's depth. Platform product features change; verify the current-generation feature set against each platform's own documentation before finalising the analysis.
- Access to the FINRA Notice 09-05 (available on FINRA's website), the SEC's rules on Section 4(a)(7) (Section 4 of the Securities Act as amended by the FAST Act), Rule 144, Rule 144A, and Regulation D Rule 501.
- Access to a spreadsheet for the platform-comparison matrix.

## Tasks

### 1. Set up the four fact patterns

Freeze four distinct transaction fact patterns as the drill's inputs. Suggested profiles:

- **Fact Pattern A — Large late-stage issuer-organised tender.** Target is a growth-stage AI-infrastructure company, Series E, $5B implied valuation, 1,200 employees, planning a $400M tender for eligible employees at a defined common-share equivalent price. Buyer identity: a syndicate of existing preferred investors and one new secondary-market buyer. Target company is highly organised, has securities counsel engaged, and expects the tender to close in 90 days.
- **Fact Pattern B — Standing secondary market for a growth-stage target.** Target is a growth-stage vertical-SaaS company, Series D, $2B implied valuation, 500 employees. The target is not running an issuer-organised tender; former-employee shareholders and current-employee shareholders holding meaningful vested equity want an ongoing path to bounded liquidity. Target is willing to participate in a platform's standing-market programme if the operational fit is reasonable.
- **Fact Pattern C — Individual founder secondary through a bilateral broker.** Founder of a mid-stage private company (Series C, $800M implied) wants to sell 5% of common holdings to a specific institutional buyer (a family office not currently on the target's cap table). The target is willing to consent to the transfer subject to the ROFR / co-sale process. Volume is $10M; buyer identity is defined.
- **Fact Pattern D — Employee-shareholder wanting to sell in a company without an active tender programme.** Long-tenured (7 years) senior IC at a growth-stage target that has not run a tender in the last 3 years. The IC has $2M in vested common they want to sell. Target is neutral-to-supportive but has no in-house liquidity apparatus.

If your carried-through target from prior exercises maps to one of these, use it; otherwise the profiles above stand as the exercise inputs.

### 2. Build the platform comparison matrix

Produce a 2–4 page comparison matrix covering the five dominant platforms — Nasdaq Private Market (NPM), Forge Global, EquityZen, Carta X, PARAGON Prime — plus a "bilateral FINRA broker" column (representing specialist broker-dealers like Rainmaker Securities running bilateral negotiated transactions).

Dimensions to include (at minimum):

- **Primary product** — issuer-organised tender administration vs. standing marketplace vs. bilateral matching vs. block-broker.
- **Buyer universe** — accredited-investor pool vs. QIB pool vs. institutional-only vs. individual-accredited plus institutional.
- **Regulatory-exemption operationalisation** — how the platform administers Section 4(a)(7), Rule 144, or Rule 144A resales.
- **FINRA Notice 09-05 compliance posture** — the platform's broker-dealer arm's information-provision and suitability discipline.
- **Blue-sky compliance** — whether the platform runs state-by-state analysis and filings on the target's behalf.
- **Issuer-consent mechanic** — whether the platform requires the target's cooperation for its transactions, and how the ROFR / co-sale process is administered.
- **Pricing transparency** — whether the platform publishes transaction data or aggregate market indicators.
- **Fee structure** — transaction-fee percentage, minimums, admin fees.
- **Tender-offer administration capability** — for platforms that offer it (NPM, Carta X), the specific workflow (participant portal, election workflow, proration mechanics, settlement).
- **Information-provision relationship** — the specific programme the platform offers target companies for standing-market participation.
- **Reputation and market position** — the platform's practitioner-familiar reputation and market share for the specific transaction shape.

### 3. Select a platform for each fact pattern

For each of the four fact patterns, write a 1–2 page platform-selection memo covering:

- **Recommended platform** — the specific platform (or bilateral broker) chosen.
- **Regulatory-exemption analysis** — which exemption applies (typically Section 4(a)(7) for platform-cleared transactions with accredited-investor buyers, Rule 144 for long-tenured non-affiliate resales, or Rule 144A for QIB-only transactions), and how the platform administers it.
- **FINRA Notice 09-05 posture** — the information-provision expectation the platform imposes and whether the target can and will meet it.
- **Blue-sky compliance** — the state-by-state analysis and whether the platform handles it.
- **ROFR / co-sale integration** — how the platform's workflow accommodates the target's ROFR notice-and-waiver mechanics (chapter 6). Some platforms have institutionalised this; others push the mechanics back to the target's counsel.
- **Pricing and fees** — the platform's pricing model and the specific fee applied to the transaction.
- **Timeline** — the expected time-to-close from initiation.
- **Rejected alternatives** — at least two other platforms considered and why they were rejected.

### 4. Author the target-company platform-relationship posture

Produce a 3–5 page policy document that a target company's CFO / GC could adopt as standing policy for how the target engages with secondary-market platforms. Cover:

- **Approved-platform list** — the specific platforms the target is willing to work with for standing-market and tender-administration purposes.
- **Declined-platform list** — the platforms the target is affirmatively declining and the reasons (regulatory posture, buyer-universe fit, information-provision expectations, market reputation).
- **Information-provision policy** — what information the target agrees to provide to approved platforms on a standing basis. Typical menu: quarterly aggregate financial summary, cap-table summary, current 409A common price, key business developments. Note the practitioner tension: platforms need information to satisfy FINRA Notice 09-05; targets typically want to limit information leakage.
- **Blue-sky compliance policy** — whether the target expects the platform to run state-by-state analysis and filings, and where the target's counsel picks up any residual work.
- **ROFR / co-sale integration** — the target's expectation for how platform-cleared transactions run through the ROFR notice-and-waiver process, including any bulk-notice or aggregated-batch mechanics.
- **Pricing-observation policy** — how the target's finance function monitors platform-cleared secondary volume and pricing, and the threshold at which the volume becomes a §409A material event triggering a fresh refresh.
- **Employee-communications policy** — what the target communicates to employees about platform-cleared secondary options, the timing of any communications, and the boundaries between company-endorsement and neutral-information-sharing.
- **Bilateral-broker policy** — the target's posture toward bilateral broker-dealer transactions (Rainmaker Securities and comparable) that operate outside the electronic-platform infrastructure.
- **Governance sign-off** — which target-side function owns the platform-relationship posture (typically the CFO for pricing / financial monitoring, the GC for regulatory posture, the board or a specific committee for policy approval).

### 5. Draft the platform-approval memo template

Draft a 1-page template that the target company would use to approve a specific transaction through a specific platform. Cover:

- Transaction identifier (platform, buyer, seller, share count, price).
- Regulatory exemption confirmation (which exemption applies, counsel sign-off).
- ROFR / co-sale process status (notice delivered, waiver received, or exception category applies).
- 409A materiality flag (does the transaction contribute to a materiality threshold that would trigger a refresh?).
- Blue-sky compliance confirmation.
- Sign-off (CFO, GC, or delegate).

The template is the operational tool the target would use to keep the platform-relationship posture defensible on a per-transaction basis.

## Starter guidance

Common platform-selection errors to avoid:

- **Choosing the platform by brand rather than by fit.** NPM is dominant in tender administration but not necessarily the right platform for standing-market activity for a small-cap issuer; Forge is dominant in standing-market for well-known targets but does not administer issuer-organised tenders in the way NPM does. The fit-by-transaction-shape discipline matters.
- **Assuming the platform handles ROFR mechanics automatically.** Some platforms have institutionalised the ROFR notice-and-waiver process; others push the mechanics back to the target's counsel. The specific ROFR integration is the operational reality that determines whether platform-cleared transactions close on schedule or stall.
- **Underestimating the FINRA Notice 09-05 information-provision expectation.** A target that refuses to provide the basic information a broker-dealer must convey to its customer effectively cannot participate in the platform. The target must decide, at the policy level, what information it is willing to provide and to which platforms.
- **Ignoring the 409A materiality implication of standing-market activity.** Repeated platform-cleared transactions at prices materially different from the current 409A create observable data the target's 409A appraiser will consider at the next refresh. The finance function should be monitoring platform volume as a compliance signal.
- **Treating bilateral broker-dealers as equivalent to platforms.** Bilateral brokers (Rainmaker Securities and comparable) run specific negotiated transactions; they are useful for one-off block trades but do not provide the standing-market infrastructure that platforms do. The choice between them is transaction-shape driven.
- **Neglecting the employee-communications boundary.** Endorsing a specific platform to employees can be read as investment advice; neutral information-sharing (e.g., "several platforms operate in the secondary market for private-company shares; employees should consult their own advisors") is typically the appropriate posture.
- **Approving platforms without checking blue-sky posture across the shareholder base.** A shareholder resident in California, for example, may face specific state-registration or exemption requirements not covered by the platform's default blue-sky package.

## Acceptance criteria

You can demonstrate that:

- Platform comparison matrix covers the five dominant platforms plus a bilateral-broker column across at least ten dimensions.
- Four fact-pattern platform selections are made, each with a defended rationale, exemption analysis, FINRA Notice 09-05 posture, ROFR integration analysis, and rejected alternatives.
- Target-company platform-relationship posture document is authored at 3–5 pages and covers approved / declined platforms, information-provision policy, blue-sky compliance, ROFR integration, 409A materiality monitoring, employee-communications policy, bilateral-broker policy, and governance sign-off.
- Platform-approval memo template is drafted at 1 page and suitable for per-transaction operational use.
- The overall analysis defensibly distinguishes among Section 4(a)(7), Rule 144, and Rule 144A across the four fact patterns.

## Reflection

Add a short reflection:

1. Which of the four fact patterns produced the closest platform-selection decision, and what was the tie-breaking dimension?
2. If the target company were unwilling to provide the FINRA Notice 09-05 information-provision expectation, what specific transaction shapes would remain available to shareholders, and at what operational cost?
3. If the platform-cleared secondary volume in your target's stock reached $50M in a rolling 12-month window at prices 40% above the current 409A common, would you trigger a fresh 409A refresh or wait until the next scheduled refresh? What are the fact-specific inputs to the decision?
4. Which of the five dominant platforms would you *not* want your target company to work with under any circumstance, and what specific concern drives the decision?

## Stretch goals

- **Live-platform demo walkthrough.** Sign up for a demo or free-tier access on Forge, EquityZen, or Carta X and walk through the standing-market or tender-administration workflow. Note the operational realities that differ from the paper analysis.
- **FINRA Notice 09-05 depth-analysis.** Read the full text of Notice 09-05 and adjacent FINRA guidance and draft the specific broker-dealer conduct standards the target should expect the platform's broker-dealer arm to satisfy.
- **State-by-state blue-sky matrix.** Build a specific state-by-state blue-sky compliance matrix for a target whose shareholder base is concentrated in a specific set of states (California, New York, Washington, Texas, Massachusetts). Identify the state-specific exemption analyses required and the platforms that operationalise them.
- **Comparable-issuer benchmarking.** Assemble the platform-relationship posture of 3–5 public examples (companies whose approved-platform relationships are publicly known — Palantir, Databricks, Stripe, Discord, and others) and compare their posture against the policy you drafted.
- **Section 4(a)(7) mechanics walk-through.** Draft the specific documentation package a Section 4(a)(7) resale requires (issuer information delivery, seller attestation, buyer accredited-investor confirmation) and identify the specific artefacts each platform administers.
- **§409A materiality-threshold sensitivity analysis.** Model the platform-cleared secondary volume-and-pricing thresholds at which a fresh 409A refresh would be triggered, and produce a monitoring dashboard specification for the target's finance function.
