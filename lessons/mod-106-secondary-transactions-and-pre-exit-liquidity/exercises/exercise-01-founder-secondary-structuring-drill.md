# exercise-01: Founder Secondary Structuring Drill

**Estimated effort:** 3 hours

## Objective

Design a defensible founder secondary transaction for a specific hypothetical target and founder fact pattern. Produce a board-ready memo (6–10 pages) that names the transaction's shape, sizes it against the 10–20% common-holding ceiling, defends it against the four alignment-of-interest questions the lead investor will ask, lays out the board-approval choreography, works the tax treatment (including §1202 QSBS holding-period interaction), and defends the timing against the exit-intent signalling failure mode. At the end you should be able to walk into a lead-investor pre-brief with the full analysis already in hand and close the discussion in one meeting.

## Prerequisites

- The hypothetical target you carried through mod-101–mod-105 (or a comparable growth-stage venture-backed profile). At minimum: sector, ARR, headcount, cap-table shape (founder + 3–5 institutional investors + employee pool), most-recent primary-round headline and per-share preferred price, prior 409A common price, and founding date (to anchor §1202 holding-period math).
- Familiarity with the founder's grant record — founding-grant date and share count, any secondary purchases, any refresh grants, vesting posture. If constructing a hypothetical, freeze a plausible profile before starting (e.g., 8-year-old company, founder holds 15% of common with 100% vesting, founding grant issued at incorporation with §1202-qualifying facts).
- Access to the NVCA Model Legal Documents (charter, voting agreement, ROFR / co-sale agreement) to anchor the notice-and-waiver mechanics.
- The current 26 U.S.C. §1202 statute and IRS guidance on QSBS.

## Tasks

### 1. Set the target-and-founder fact baseline

Write a 1-page fact baseline covering:

- **Target profile** — legal name, jurisdiction of incorporation (Delaware C-corp typical), sector, ARR, growth rate, cash position, most-recent primary round (date, per-share preferred price, headline valuation, lead investor).
- **Cap-table shape** — total shares outstanding (common vs. each preferred series), option pool position, fully-diluted share count, current 409A common price.
- **Founder profile** — role (CEO / CTO / other co-founder), current common holdings (share count, fully-vested vs. unvested breakdown, projected-vest position at 12 and 24 months), founding-grant date, any prior secondary sales, personal residence (state — for state-tax analysis), any prior 83(b) elections on file.
- **QSBS posture** — is the founding grant §1202-qualifying? What is the current holding period (years and months since acquisition)? Is the 5-year mark reached, and if not, when? What is the founder's basis, and what §1202 exclusion cap applies (greater of $10M or 10× basis)?
- **Transaction context** — is a primary round proximate, has a standalone secondary buyer surfaced, or is an employee tender being planned? What is the transaction's proximate trigger?

### 2. Choose the transaction shape and defend the choice

From chapter 1's three canonical shapes:

- **Shape A** — secondary sleeve on a primary round.
- **Shape B** — standalone off-cycle secondary.
- **Shape C** — founder participation in an employee tender.

Choose the shape that fits the fact pattern and write a half-page defence covering:

- Why this shape fits the target's context.
- What signalling implication it carries (mild for A; material for B; depends-on-tender-communications for C).
- What alternate shape you rejected and why.
- What downstream governance mechanic it triggers (ROFR / co-sale, board approval, disclosure).

### 3. Size the secondary against the 10–20% ceiling

Produce a specific size for the transaction and a defensible sizing argument:

- **Denominator** — the founder's fully-vested position (not total common outstanding).
- **Proportion** — the specific percentage of the founder's holdings proposed for sale.
- **Retained position** — the founder's equity value post-secondary (shares × current 409A) and remaining-vesting book (unvested shares × projected value).
- **Ratio framing** — "founder selling X% of vested position, retaining Y%, continuing to vest into Z shares over the next 24 months."

Where the proportion is above 10%, add a paragraph explaining what special context justifies the higher number (e.g., 8-year tenure with no prior secondary, primary-round-only prior liquidity, specific documented household-liquidity requirement). Where the proportion is at or above 20%, note that this is a *material* transaction that will require a specific board discussion and consider whether the size should be reduced.

### 4. Answer the four alignment-of-interest questions

Draft the founder's answers to the four questions the lead investor will ask (chapter 1):

1. **What is the founder's remaining exposure?** Retained equity value ÷ pre-secondary equity value, as a percentage and as an absolute dollar figure at the current 409A.
2. **What is the founder's remaining unvested-equity book?** Unvested share count and projected value at typical exit valuation ranges (e.g., 2×, 3×, 5× current implied valuation).
3. **What is the use of proceeds?** A general framing (not an audit) that positions the transaction as household stabilisation rather than as exit-intent. Include one or two of: mortgage payoff, education funding, portfolio diversification, secondary residence, healthcare, family-financial-obligation coverage.
4. **What is the founder's post-secondary commitment posture?** Whether the transaction includes a founder-recommitment component — re-affirmation of assignment-of-inventions, re-affirmation of full-time exclusive commitment, or a specific continued-employment term (2–4 years typical) with a specific severance / clawback structure. Choose whether the transaction includes this component and defend the choice.

Each answer should be a concise paragraph (not a bullet list) that reads as if the founder were delivering it in a pre-brief with the lead investor. The written form is the artefact; the spoken delivery is the practitioner test.

### 5. Draft the board-approval choreography

Produce a specific sequence of governance steps from pre-brief through close, covering:

- **Pre-brief the lead** — who, when, materials, and expected outcome.
- **Board notification** — how the transaction is surfaced to the full board, when, and in what form (email pre-read, informal call, formal board memo).
- **ROFR / co-sale notice** — the notice contents, delivery mechanism, and windows against the target's specific ROFR / co-sale agreement. Reference chapter 6 for the mechanics.
- **Board resolution** — the specific resolution to waive the company's ROFR, the specific votes required (typically majority of the board with the founder abstaining if they are a director), and the documentation format (unanimous written consent vs. meeting-and-minutes).
- **Preferred-holder waiver circulation** — the mechanics for circulating the waiver to preferred holders, the response window, and the default-to-waive convention.
- **Co-sale calculation** — whether the co-sale right applies and, if so, what the co-sale calculation looks like against the target's preferred base.
- **Documentation and close** — the secondary-purchase-agreement (or the primary-round SPA's secondary component), the funds flow, the transfer-agent update, and the disclosure filings (Form D update or blue-sky notice, as applicable).
- **Post-close disclosure** — how the transaction is communicated to the workforce (typically not disclosed proactively beyond the executive team and cap-table holders; disclosed at any future employee-tender or all-hands where transparency serves a specific purpose).

### 6. Work the tax treatment

Draft a 2-page tax-treatment analysis covering:

- **Federal capital-gains treatment** — long-term-capital-gains (holding period met) at 20% + 3.8% NIIT for high-income founders. Include the specific per-share basis, sale price, gain, and federal tax at the transaction date.
- **§1202 QSBS analysis** — is the founding grant §1202-qualifying? Has the 5-year holding period been met? If yes, apply the exclusion (greater of $10M or 10× basis) to the federal LTCG and compute the residual federal tax. If not, identify how many months short of the 5-year mark and whether the transaction should be sequenced to preserve the exclusion (delay the sale to preserve, or structure as a forward contract per chapter 4 that preserves the holding period).
- **State tax** — the founder's state-residence treatment of capital gains. California (13.3% top rate, no §1202 conformity), New York (varies), Washington (no state income tax), Texas (no state income tax), etc. Compute the state tax at the transaction date.
- **Withholding** — none for capital gains on already-held stock; the founder is responsible for their own estimated-tax payments.
- **Net-to-founder** — the specific net cash the founder receives after federal + state tax and any transaction expenses. Compare against the gross transaction size.

### 7. Defend the timing against the exit-intent signalling failure mode

Draft a half-page timing defence covering:

- **Distance from any current inbound M&A conversation** — a founder secondary within 3 months of receiving a specific unsolicited inbound is a governance issue and typically should be paused; a secondary well outside any inbound window is neutral.
- **Distance from a planned sell-side process** — a founder secondary within 12 months of a plausible sell-side launch will be read by future acquirers as pre-marketing behaviour and can suppress bidder valuation; if the founder anticipates a sell-side in the next 12–18 months, the secondary should either close now (well ahead of the process) or wait until the process is complete.
- **Distance from a planned IPO** — SEC and underwriter conventions typically restrict founder-and-executive secondary activity in the pre-IPO quiet period and through the lockup; the timing must be defended against IPO planning.
- **Communication choreography** — how the transaction is documented internally and how, if at all, it is discussed with any current external counterparties (banker, corp-dev inbound, board observer).

### 8. Draft the one-page executive summary

Author a 1-page executive summary suitable as the cover to the board memo. Cover:

- Transaction shape and headline size (dollars and % of founder's position).
- Retained position and vesting posture.
- Use of proceeds framing.
- Board-approval choreography summary.
- Tax treatment summary (federal + state, QSBS status).
- Timing defence one-liner.
- Recommended approval action (waive company ROFR, circulate preferred waiver, close on target date).

## Starter guidance

Common founder-secondary-design errors to avoid:

- **Sizing on the dollar number rather than the proportion.** Boards react to proportion; presenting a $15M cash-out without stating the ratio to the founder's remaining position turns a one-meeting discussion into a three-week negotiation.
- **Skipping the pre-brief with the lead.** A founder who arrives at the full-board meeting with a proposed secondary the lead has not been separately briefed on is a founder whose secondary is going to be resized (downward) or delayed.
- **Ignoring the §1202 holding-period math.** A founder who is 4 years and 8 months into the QSBS holding period and sells now forfeits an exclusion that could be worth $2M–$4M in federal tax. The math should always be run before the transaction is committed.
- **Treating the ROFR waiver as automatic.** The board must formally decide; the preferred holders must formally circulate. A transaction that treats the mechanics as ministerial can be voided if a preferred holder later argues they were cut out of the notice window.
- **Assuming the tax is straightforward.** State-residence-change planning, §1202 stacking, non-grantor-trust gifting, and other advanced-tax strategies can materially change the after-tax result. The exercise here is at the transaction-execution seat's depth; the founder's personal tax advisor drives the specific execution.
- **Timing the transaction near an inbound.** A secondary announced in the same quarter as a corp-dev inbound signals a specific readiness to sell that the founder does not want to broadcast; the timing discipline is worth the friction.

## Acceptance criteria

You can demonstrate that:

- Target-and-founder fact baseline is written with cap-table shape, founder's holdings and vesting posture, and QSBS status.
- Transaction shape is chosen from the three canonical shapes with a defended rationale.
- Sizing is presented as a proportion of the founder's fully-vested position (not as a dollar number in isolation), with a defence against the 10–20% ceiling.
- The four alignment-of-interest questions are each answered in a concise paragraph suitable for delivery in a lead-investor pre-brief.
- Board-approval choreography is sequenced from pre-brief through post-close disclosure, with ROFR / co-sale mechanics grounded in chapter 6.
- Tax treatment is worked with federal LTCG, §1202 QSBS analysis, state tax, and net-to-founder computation.
- Timing is defended against inbound M&A, sell-side, and IPO exit-intent signalling risks.
- One-page executive summary is drafted at cover-memo depth.

## Reflection

Add a short reflection:

1. Which of the four alignment questions turned out to be the most difficult to answer defensibly, and what does that tell you about the founder's actual alignment posture?
2. If the founder's proportion had been 25% instead of the number you sized, what specific board-and-lead-investor conversation would you have needed to run, and would the transaction have proceeded?
3. If §1202 QSBS had not yet reached the 5-year mark, what structural alternative (delay the sale, structure as a forward contract that preserves the holding period, size the transaction to non-QSBS shares only) would you have recommended, and why?
4. What is the single sentence you would deliver at the front of the lead-investor pre-brief that lands the entire alignment argument?

## Stretch goals

- **Live NVCA-document redline.** Pull the NVCA Model Voting Agreement and the Model ROFR / Co-Sale Agreement and mark the specific sections that govern the transaction. Draft the specific transfer-notice document that would be delivered under those agreements.
- **§1202 stacking analysis.** Analyse whether the founder can structure the secondary to preserve §1202 exclusion for specific share lots (e.g., sell the non-QSBS shares first, hold the QSBS shares) and quantify the tax savings from the sequencing.
- **State-residence-change modelling.** Model the after-tax result if the founder relocated to a no-state-income-tax jurisdiction (Washington, Texas, Florida, Nevada) 12 months before the transaction. Include the residency-establishment mechanics and the timing constraints.
- **Post-close employee-morale simulation.** Draft the specific internal-communications plan for how the transaction is (or is not) disclosed to the workforce, and stress-test it against three failure modes (leak to press, all-hands question, glassdoor post).
- **Comparable-transaction benchmarking.** Assemble 3–5 publicly-observable founder-secondary transactions from the past 24 months (SEC filings, press coverage) and compare their sizing, timing, and market reception against your proposed transaction.
