# Resources for mod-106-secondary-transactions-and-pre-exit-liquidity

Primary-source regulatory and statutory anchors, NVCA model documents, platform documentation, specialist counterparty references, practitioner legal-writing, and cross-track pointers. Prefer these over secondary write-ups: for the securities-law, tax, and NVCA references especially, board memos and counsel discussions require sources the other side can re-check. Regulatory rules and platform product features change; verify current-generation text and features at the linked source before citing.

## NVCA model legal documents

The National Venture Capital Association publishes the industry-standard preferred-stock financing document set. The Model Voting Agreement and the Model Right of First Refusal and Co-Sale Agreement are the practitioner baseline for the ROFR / co-sale mechanics that every secondary transaction runs through.

- **NVCA — Model Legal Documents portal** — index of the current-generation model set (Certificate of Incorporation, Stock Purchase Agreement, Investor Rights Agreement, Voting Agreement, Right of First Refusal and Co-Sale Agreement, Management Rights Letter, Indemnification Agreement, and legal-opinion templates). Referenced throughout chapter 6 and used as the drafting baseline for exercise-05. <https://nvca.org/model-legal-documents/>
- **NVCA — Model Right of First Refusal and Co-Sale Agreement** — the specific document governing the three-level ROFR cascade (company / preferred / other) and the co-sale participation right. Read the entire document — the notice, waiver, and co-sale mechanics are all in the text.
- **NVCA — Model Voting Agreement** — contains the drag-along right and, in some model configurations, additional ROFR provisions. Referenced in chapter 6 and exercise-05 stretch goals for the drag-along / ROFR interaction.
- **NVCA — Yearbook** — annual data on venture-backed private-company activity, including secondary-market-related data where reported. <https://nvca.org/nvca-yearbook/>

## Securities law — federal exemption stack

The Section 5 registration requirement, and the specific exemptions private-company secondary transactions rely on, are the regulatory frame for every transaction in this module.

- **Securities Act of 1933 — full text** (15 U.S.C. §77a et seq.) — the statutory frame for the entire securities-law-exemption analysis in chapter 5. <https://www.sec.gov/about/laws/sa33.pdf>
- **Section 4(a)(7) of the Securities Act** (added by the FAST Act of 2015) — the modern practitioner exemption for private-company secondary resales. Text at 15 U.S.C. §77d(a)(7). Referenced throughout chapter 5 and used in exercise-04 for platform-selection analysis. <https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/15/77d>
- **SEC — Rule 144** (17 C.F.R. §230.144) — the safe-harbour for resales with holding-period, current-information, and (for affiliates) volume-limitation requirements. The non-affiliate 12-month holding period is the primary alternative to Section 4(a)(7) for long-tenured private-company shareholders. <https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-17/chapter-II/part-230/subject-group-ECFR85e40b40deca7bf/section-230.144>
- **SEC — Rule 144A** (17 C.F.R. §230.144A) — the QIB-only resale exemption. Rarely used for individual-shareholder private-company secondaries; occasionally relevant for institutional-only transactions. <https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-17/chapter-II/part-230/subject-group-ECFR85e40b40deca7bf/section-230.144a>
- **SEC — Regulation D, Rule 501 — accredited investor definition** — the buyer-side eligibility filter for Section 4(a)(7). Verify the current-generation definition; the SEC has periodically updated the accredited-investor definition. <https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-17/chapter-II/part-230/subject-group-ECFR2c8f45e6ffdb8b7/section-230.501>
- **SEC — Fast Act Modernization and Simplification of Regulation S-K** and related fact sheets on the FAST Act's Section 4(a)(7) provision. <https://www.sec.gov/> (search "FAST Act Section 4(a)(7)")
- **SEC — Small Business Compliance Guide on private-company secondary transactions** — periodic SEC staff guidance on private-company-security resale mechanics. <https://www.sec.gov/education/smallbusiness>

## FINRA — broker-dealer conduct for private-company secondaries

- **FINRA Regulatory Notice 09-05** — Unregistered Resales of Restricted Securities. The seminal guidance on FINRA-member broker-dealer obligations in private-secondary transactions. Referenced throughout chapter 5. <https://www.finra.org/rules-guidance/notices/09-05>
- **FINRA — Rules pertaining to private-company secondaries** — Rules 2010 (Standards of Commercial Honor), 2111 (Suitability), 5310 (Best Execution), and related conduct rules. <https://www.finra.org/rules-guidance/rulebooks>
- **FINRA — Investor Alerts on pre-IPO share offerings** — periodic alerts warning investors about fraud in private-company-share secondary transactions. Referenced in exercise-04 for the platform-relationship-posture analysis. <https://www.finra.org/investors/insights>
- **FINRA — Restricted Securities Notice 07-04** and related earlier guidance on Rule 144 mechanics. <https://www.finra.org/rules-guidance/notices>

## Tax — IRC provisions governing secondary-transaction tax treatment

Primary sources for the tax analyses in chapters 1, 3, 4, and 8.

- **IRC §1202 — Partial exclusion for gain from certain small business stock** (26 U.S.C. §1202) — the QSBS exclusion referenced throughout the module. Referenced in mod-103 chapter 8 for the substantive QSBS analysis; referenced here for the holding-period-preservation discipline in structured secondaries. <https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/26/1202>
- **IRC §1259 — Constructive sales treatment for appreciated financial positions** (26 U.S.C. §1259) — the anti-tax-deferral statute that restricts forward-sale planning on appreciated positions. Central to chapter 4's constructive-sale analysis and to exercise-03. <https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/26/1259>
- **IRC §1001 — Determination of amount of and recognition of gain or loss** (26 U.S.C. §1001) — the sale-or-exchange doctrine that applies to high-advance non-recourse loans potentially recharacterised as sales. <https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/26/1001>
- **IRC §409A — Inclusion in gross income of deferred compensation under nonqualified deferred compensation plans** (26 U.S.C. §409A) — the tax regime governing deferred-compensation instruments, including the fair-market-value discipline for stock-option strike prices. Central to chapter 8. <https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/26/409A>
- **Treasury Regulations §1.409A-1 through §1.409A-6** — the implementing regulations for §409A, including the specific safe-harbour language for the independent-appraisal presumption used by 409A appraisers. <https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-26/chapter-I/subchapter-A/part-1/subject-group-ECFR2064c6d90d0d15b/section-1.409A-1>
- **IRC §83 — Property transferred in connection with performance of services** (26 U.S.C. §83) — the framework for ordinary-compensation treatment of NSO exercise, RSU settlement, and related equity-compensation events. Central to chapter 3's tax-treatment categories. <https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/26/83>
- **IRC §421 through §424 — Statutory stock options** (26 U.S.C. §§421–424) — the ISO qualifying-disposition framework used in chapter 3's Category-3 analysis. <https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/26/subtitle-A/chapter-1/subchapter-D/part-II/subpart-A>
- **IRC §163(d) — Investment interest** (26 U.S.C. §163(d)) — the limitation on deductibility of interest on collateralised-loan structures used in chapter 4. <https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/26/163>
- **IRS Publication 550 — Investment Income and Expenses** — the general reference for individual-investor tax treatment. <https://www.irs.gov/publications/p550>
- **IRS Publication 525 — Taxable and Nontaxable Income** — including guidance on employee stock-option and RSU taxation. <https://www.irs.gov/publications/p525>
- **IRS Revenue Ruling 2003-98 and related guidance on the tax treatment of stock options and RSUs** — practitioner-familiar rulings on the specific timing and character of equity-compensation events. <https://www.irs.gov/pub/irs-drop/rr-03-98.pdf>

## SEC Rule 701 — compensatory-arrangement exemption

- **SEC — Rule 701** (17 C.F.R. §230.701) — the exemption from Section 5 registration for securities issued under compensatory arrangements. The aggregate-value cap and the $10M disclosure-trigger discipline are central to chapter 8. <https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-17/chapter-II/part-230/subject-group-ECFR85e40b40deca7bf/section-230.701>
- **SEC — 2018 Amendments to Rule 701** — raised the disclosure-trigger threshold from $5M to $10M. <https://www.sec.gov/rules/final/2018/33-10529.pdf>
- **SEC — Concept Release on Compensatory Securities Offerings and Sales (Rule 701 and Form S-8)** — periodic SEC review of the exemption. <https://www.sec.gov/rules/proposed>

## Tender-offer regulatory framework

- **SEC — Rule 13e-4** (17 C.F.R. §240.13e-4) — the issuer-tender-offer rule for public companies. Private-company tenders are not literally subject to this rule but are typically structured to satisfy its analogous procedural discipline. Referenced in chapter 2. <https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-17/chapter-II/part-240/subject-group-ECFR5c8f6db43b9c74b/section-240.13e-4>
- **SEC — Rule 14e-1** (17 C.F.R. §240.14e-1) — the 20-business-day minimum tender-offer duration. The private-company analogue observed by chapter 2. <https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-17/chapter-II/part-240/subject-group-ECFR5c8f6db43b9c74c/section-240.14e-1>
- **SEC — Regulation 14E and Regulation 14D** — the broader tender-offer rules that inform private-tender procedural design. <https://www.sec.gov/rules/regulation-14e>
- **SEC — No-action letters on private-company tender offers** — periodic SEC staff no-action letters that shape private-tender structural practice. Search the SEC's no-action-letter database for specific issuers or dates. <https://www.sec.gov/divisions/corpfin/cf-noaction.shtml>

## SEC and IRS enforcement / disciplinary actions

For understanding the specific failure modes that regulators have targeted.

- **SEC — Enforcement actions on private-company-securities fraud** — periodic SEC enforcement actions against unregistered broker-dealers, platform-level frauds, and pre-IPO share offering schemes. Search the SEC's Litigation Releases database. <https://www.sec.gov/litigation.shtml>
- **SEC — Case No. 3-16045 (In the Matter of Sand Hill Exchange)** — a specific historical enforcement action against a private-secondary trading platform that operated without proper registration. Referenced for the operational discipline required in the platform-selection analysis. Search SEC ALJ decisions.
- **FINRA — Disciplinary actions against broker-dealers in private-company-secondary transactions** — <https://www.finra.org/rules-guidance/enforcement/monthly-disciplinary-actions>
