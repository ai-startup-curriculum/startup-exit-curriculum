# Retention Monitoring Against the Pre-Close Plan

## Why this matters

Retention is the specific metric the deal thesis is most likely to be silently missing. Every material integration has a retention plan (mod-110 chapter 2) authored pre-close, a retention-package overlay for the top-20-quartile individuals (mod-110 chapter 5, MIP and retention agreements), and a specific set of retention checkpoints in the definitive agreements (typically at 6 / 12 / 18 / 24 months for the retention-bonus payments). What most integrations *do not* have is a specific *monitoring discipline* that measures actual retention behaviour against the plan at a specific cadence, surfaces hidden retention risk before it becomes a specific departure, and triggers specific interventions before the specific retention-loss is irreversible.

The empirical pattern: acquired-employee attrition is *higher* than ordinary-course attrition for a specific window (typically 12–18 months post-close), *concentrated* in specific cohorts (top-performers, longer-tenured employees, employees with acquirer-side alternatives), and *predictable* if the specific monitoring signals are attended to (specific engagement signals, specific 1:1 patterns, specific participation patterns in specific meetings, specific internal-mobility signals). The specific difference between an integration that hits its retention plan and one that misses is not the specific quality of the retention overlay — it is the specific discipline of monitoring and intervening.

This chapter installs that monitoring discipline. The specific practices — the 30 / 60 / 90-day retention checkpoints, the top-quartile retention-risk list refresh, the retention-bonus payment triggers, and the specific hidden-retention-risk surfacing patterns (skip-level meetings, engagement surveys, one-on-one manager notes) — are what turn the pre-close retention plan into an executed retention outcome.

> Reminder: this module is education, not people-analytics / HR-technology / employment-law advice. Every specific retention monitoring implementation engages specific tooling (Culture Amp, Lattice, Peakon, engagement-survey vendors, HRIS-based reporting) and specific counsel (employment, tax, executive-compensation) whose specific requirements shape the specific execution.

## The 30 / 60 / 90-day retention checkpoints

**The specific framework.** Retention monitoring is structured around specific checkpoints at day 30, day 60, and day 90 (with continuing cadence at day 180 and day 365), each with specific *content* and specific *decisions* the checkpoint produces.

### Day-30 retention checkpoint

**The specific content.** The first structured review of acquired-team retention behaviour, 30 days after close. The specific metrics reviewed:

- **Actual acquired-team headcount vs. plan.** Number of acquired employees still with the acquirer at day 30 vs. the specific pre-close headcount. Any specific unplanned departures (i.e., not part of a specific planned reduction) are individually reviewed.
- **Voluntary attrition rate vs. plan.** The specific per-cent of acquired employees who have voluntarily resigned in the first 30 days vs. the specific plan-of-record. Compared to the acquiree's specific pre-close voluntary attrition rate and to the acquirer's specific ordinary-course attrition rate for equivalent cohorts.
- **Top-quartile retention list status.** Each of the specific top-20-quartile individuals identified pre-close (mod-110 chapter 2) is specifically named and their specific status reviewed: still with acquirer, in retention conversation, resigned, other status.
- **Retention-bonus payment status.** For any specific retention bonuses scheduled to pay at 30 days post-close (typically the specific first tranche of the retention overlay), the specific payment status is confirmed.
- **Retention-conversation completion.** The specific 1:1 conversations with each top-quartile individual (mod-110 chapter 2) — were they held on schedule, what were the specific outcomes, are there specific follow-ups.

**The specific decisions.**

- **Individual-level retention interventions.** For any specific top-quartile individual whose retention status is at risk (specific signals: disengagement in the retention conversation, expressed concerns, specific manager-observation of behavioural change), a specific intervention is planned — typically an escalation to the executive sponsor for a specific follow-up conversation.
- **Retention-list refresh.** New individuals who have emerged in the first 30 days as specific retention risks (either because pre-close identification missed them, or because their status has changed) are added to the specific list.
- **Cross-workstream escalation.** Retention-loss patterns that trace to specific workstream issues (e.g., a specific pattern of engineering-team resignations linked to a specific systems-cutover decision that the engineers found frustrating) are escalated to the specific workstream lead for review.

### Day-60 retention checkpoint

**The specific content.** The specific mid-point review, 60 days after close. All of the day-30 metrics, plus:

- **First engagement-survey read.** A specific engagement survey is typically fielded at day 30–60 (see engagement-survey section below) and the results are reviewed at the day-60 checkpoint.
- **First promotion / performance-management interaction outcomes.** Any specific promotion decisions, performance-management conversations, or comp-adjustment decisions that have happened in the first 60 days are reviewed for their specific retention consequences.
- **Skip-level-meeting findings.** The specific skip-level meetings (see skip-level section below) held in the first 60 days are reviewed for specific concerns surfaced.
- **Manager-1:1 note themes.** The specific themes from acquired-employee 1:1 notes (aggregated by the People-workstream lead with acquired-team-manager cooperation) are reviewed for retention-relevant patterns.

**The specific decisions.**

- **Adjustments to the day-90 organisational-integration plan.** If the retention monitoring surfaces specific concerns about the specific comp-and-leveling harmonisation planned for day 90, the specific plan is adjusted.
- **Additional retention-package escalation.** For specific individuals whose retention risk has escalated, an additional retention-package increment may be authorised (a specific supplemental cash payment, a specific role-scope-expansion commitment, a specific additional equity grant).
- **Culture-integration adjustments.** Specific findings from the engagement survey or skip-level meetings that indicate specific culture-integration issues are surfaced to the culture-integration workstream (chapter 5) for specific response.

### Day-90 retention checkpoint

**The specific content.** The specific pre-org-integration-milestone review, 90 days after close. All of the day-30 and day-60 metrics, plus:

- **Comp-and-leveling harmonisation reception.** The specific reception of the day-90 comp-and-leveling communications (chapter 2, chapter 5) is measured — via specific manager reports, specific employee response, specific attendance patterns at post-communication forums.
- **Second engagement-survey read.** A specific second engagement survey at day 90 provides trend data against the day-60 survey.
- **Retention-bonus payment status through day 90.** Any specific retention bonuses paid at 90 days post-close (specific tranche schedules vary) are confirmed.
- **Second-quartile retention monitoring.** The specific retention list is expanded from the top-20-quartile to include the second quartile (typically an additional 30-50 individuals depending on organisation size), with specific check-ins scheduled with each.

**The specific decisions.**

- **Day-100 review readiness.** The specific retention data feeds directly into the day-100 review's decision on whether to proceed with deeper integration (chapter 2).
- **Retention-plan revision for the next 90 days.** The specific retention plan for days 90–180 is revised based on the specific findings.
- **Individual-level departure planning.** For specific individuals who have clearly signalled departure (specific resignation notice, specific decline of retention offers, specific silent-departure patterns detected), the specific replacement / knowledge-transfer / customer-handover planning is initiated.

### Ongoing cadence — day 180 and beyond

Beyond day 90, the retention monitoring cadence typically continues at day 180, day 270, and day 365, with the specific frequency and specific depth aligning to the specific retention-bonus payment schedule (which typically has payments at 6 / 12 / 18 / 24 months). The specific pattern: the day 180 checkpoint is a specific structured review; the day 270 and day 365 checkpoints are integrated into the ongoing People-function operations.

## Top-quartile retention-risk list refresh

**The specific principle.** The pre-close retention-risk list (mod-110 chapter 2) is a specific snapshot at signing. It becomes stale in specific predictable ways: individuals whose situations change, individuals whose specific concerns surface post-close that were not anticipated pre-close, individuals who become retention risks due to specific post-close experiences (a specific bad interaction with an acquirer-side executive, a specific manager change, a specific role change). The specific list must be refreshed at a specific cadence to remain useful.

**The specific refresh cadence.** The specific list is reviewed at every retention checkpoint (day 30, day 60, day 90, day 180) and refreshed by the acquirer-side CHRO / People-workstream lead with acquiree-side manager input.

**The specific refresh criteria.**

- **Additions to the list.** Individuals who have emerged in the specific window as new retention risks:
    - Individuals identified in specific skip-level meetings or engagement surveys as at-risk that were not on the pre-close list.
    - Individuals whose specific role or manager has changed and whose retention picture has changed.
    - Individuals who have been approached by specific external recruiters or have specific external opportunities.
    - Individuals whose specific personal circumstances have changed (relocation demands, life-events, family considerations).
- **Removals from the list.** Individuals whose retention risk has resolved:
    - Individuals who have signed extended retention agreements.
    - Individuals whose specific concerns have been addressed and who now show specific engagement signals.
    - Individuals who have departed (removed for a different reason — no longer at-risk because they are gone).
- **Escalation on the list.** Individuals whose retention risk has increased and who require additional attention:
    - Move from "monitoring" to "active retention conversation" status.
    - Move from "active retention conversation" to "at-immediate-risk" status.
    - Move from "at-immediate-risk" to "departure planning" status.

**The specific artefact.** A specific retention-risk register maintained by the People-workstream lead, reviewed at each checkpoint, with per-individual named executive sponsor, specific retention actions taken, and specific status.

## Retention-bonus payment triggers

**The specific principle.** Retention bonuses (typically structured as cash payments at 6 / 12 / 18 / 24 months post-close, or as specific equity grants that vest on similar schedules per mod-110 chapter 5) have specific triggering events that must occur for the specific payment to be earned. The specific monitoring of these triggers is a specific finance / people / legal cross-workstream discipline.

**The specific typical triggers.**

- **Continued employment on the specific date.** The specific most-common trigger — the employee must be actively employed by the acquirer (or by the acquiree operating as a wholly-owned subsidiary) on the specific vesting date. Involuntary termination without cause typically accelerates the specific payment; voluntary resignation typically forfeits; termination for cause typically forfeits.
- **Specific performance conditions.** Some retention bonuses have specific performance conditions attached (specific business-unit revenue thresholds, specific product-milestone achievement, specific customer-retention thresholds). These are less common than pure time-based retention but exist in specific structures.
- **Specific role-continuity conditions.** Some retention bonuses require the employee to remain in a specific role or in a specific reporting relationship for the specific payment to be earned. These are also less common than pure time-based retention.

**The specific monitoring discipline.**

- The specific list of all retention-bonus recipients with their specific vesting schedules is maintained by the People-workstream lead in coordination with the Finance-workstream lead and Legal-workstream lead.
- At each specific vesting date, the specific eligibility is confirmed (typically 30–60 days in advance of the payment): is the employee still employed, are the specific performance conditions met, are the specific role-continuity conditions met.
- The specific payment is executed on the specific date in accordance with the specific employment agreement and applicable tax withholding (subject to IRC §409A payment-timing constraints — see mod-110 chapter 5 for the underlying framework).
- Specific communications with each retention-bonus recipient at the vesting event — the specific confirmation of payment, the specific tax treatment, the specific next-tranche schedule.
- Specific forfeiture events (resignation, termination for cause, failure of performance / role-continuity conditions) trigger specific documentation of the forfeiture and specific communication with the employee.

**The specific coordination with 409A.** Retention bonuses that are structured as deferred compensation are subject to IRC §409A payment-timing constraints — the specific payment date cannot be changed once the specific arrangement is documented, and specific acceleration provisions are limited to specific 409A-permitted events. The specific 409A discipline is owned by executive-compensation counsel; the specific monitoring is that the specific payment schedule is executed on the specific dates without informal modifications.

## Hidden retention-risk surfacing — the practices that catch what the individual conversations miss

**The specific principle.** The specific retention conversations with the top-quartile individuals surface *known* retention risks. The specific harder-to-catch retention risks are the ones that are not being surfaced in the specific 1:1 with the executive sponsor — the specific quiet-quit patterns, the specific silent-disengagement patterns, the specific "I'm interviewing but I haven't told anyone" patterns. The specific practices below are designed to catch these specific hidden risks.

### Skip-level meetings

**The specific practice.** The acquirer-side executive sponsor and (separately) the acquiree-side executive sponsor conduct specific skip-level meetings with acquired employees — the executive sponsor meets with individuals 2–3 levels below them, without the individual's direct manager present, to surface specific concerns that would not surface through the direct-manager channel.

**The specific cadence.** Typically weekly for the first 30 days, then bi-weekly through day 90, then monthly through day 180. Each skip-level covers 3–5 individuals in either 1:1 or small-group formats.

**The specific content.** Not a specific performance conversation and not a specific "let me sell you on the acquirer" conversation — a specific listening conversation. The specific questions:

- What is going well for you in the integration?
- What is not going well?
- What are you hearing from your peers that you think we should know?
- What specific concerns do you have that you haven't raised with your manager?
- What would make the specific difference to your experience in the next 30 days?

**The specific handling of findings.** Skip-level findings are aggregated (with specific individual attribution protected) and reviewed at the retention checkpoint. Specific concerns that appear from multiple individuals are surfaced as specific patterns. Specific concerns that appear from one individual but are consequential are surfaced individually with the specific individual's permission.

**The specific pattern that fails.** Skip-level meetings that become a specific "the executive sponsor complained about people's managers" backchannel. The specific defence: the executive sponsor does not act unilaterally on findings that would undermine the specific manager relationship — findings are surfaced to the specific manager, or (if the specific manager is the specific concern) to the specific escalation channel.

### Engagement surveys

**The specific practice.** Specific engagement surveys are fielded to the acquired workforce at specific cadence — typically at day 30–60 (first read), day 90 (second read), day 180 (third read), and quarterly thereafter. The specific survey is designed to measure the specific components of belonging (chapter 5) and to surface specific retention risks.

**The specific typical questions.**

- **Belonging.** "I feel like a valued member of the combined company." "I understand how my work contributes to the combined company's goals." "The combined culture is a place where I want to work."
- **Manager relationship.** "My manager provides me with the support I need to succeed." "My manager understands what I need from them." "I trust my manager."
- **Compensation and career.** "I understand my compensation and how it compares to market." "I see a clear career path for myself in the combined company." "I am recognised for my contributions."
- **Integration experience.** "The integration has been well-managed." "I have the information I need to be effective in the integration." "The specific changes to my role are ones I understand and support."
- **Retention.** "I plan to be with the combined company 12 months from now." (This specific question is a specific direct retention-signal measure.)

**The specific handling of results.**

- The specific survey results are analysed by specific cohort (function, tenure, level, geography) to surface specific patterns.
- The specific results are shared back with the acquired workforce — the specific principle that survey findings are not just for management is a specific trust-building signal.
- The specific findings are used to inform specific interventions — culture-integration adjustments, comp-and-leveling communication improvements, retention-package escalations.
- The specific trend across surveys is monitored — a specific declining trend in belonging or in the specific 12-month-retention-intent question is a specific leading indicator of specific departures.

**The specific pattern that fails.** Surveys that are fielded but not shared back with the workforce and not visibly acted upon. The specific pattern signals to acquired employees that the surveys are performative rather than substantive.

### One-on-one manager notes

**The specific practice.** The acquired-team managers keep specific notes from their specific weekly 1:1s with each direct report, and the People-workstream lead reviews aggregated themes across the specific notes at each retention checkpoint.

**The specific typical themes to watch.**

- **Explicit expressions of dissatisfaction.** Specific comments about the integration, the acquirer, the specific role changes, the specific compensation, that the specific manager captures.
- **Specific behavioural change.** Specific patterns the specific manager notes — decreased engagement in specific meetings, decreased quality of work product, specific patterns of arriving late / leaving early / taking specific unusual PTO.
- **Specific external-mobility signals.** Specific mentions of external opportunities, specific requests for LinkedIn recommendations from unusual sources, specific patterns of taking meetings at unusual times.
- **Specific personal-circumstance changes.** Specific life events (relocation, family, health) that affect the specific individual's retention picture.

**The specific handling.** Manager-notes findings are shared with the People-workstream lead in aggregate (protecting specific individual privacy where appropriate). Specific patterns that indicate retention risk trigger specific per-individual review at the retention checkpoint.

**The specific pattern that fails.** Manager notes that are not kept, or are kept but not shared with the People-workstream lead. The specific defence: specific manager-training on the specific importance of notes and specific tooling that makes the specific note-keeping easy (Lattice, 15Five, specific HRIS-integrated tools).

### Internal-mobility signals

**The specific practice.** The People-workstream lead monitors specific internal-mobility signals from acquired employees — specific applications to internal roles, specific participation in internal talent-marketplace programmes, specific interest in specific development programmes, specific patterns of internal-recruiter contact.

**The specific pattern.** Some internal-mobility activity from acquired employees is a specific *retention-positive* signal — the specific employee sees a specific future in the acquirer and is exploring it. Other patterns are specific *retention-negative* signals — the specific employee is signalling dissatisfaction with their current role and would leave if the internal move does not work out. The specific pattern determines the specific intervention.
