# mod-111 — Post-Close Integration & 100-Day Plan: IMO, Systems, Culture, Retention, Earn-Outs

**Estimated effort:** 22 hours
**Stage:** GROWTH / MATURE (any deal that closes — sell-side sold company, buy-side of a strategic tuck-in, PE take-private)
**Seat:** founder-CEO (now inside acquirer), CFO, GC / chief legal officer, CHRO / VP-People, CTO / VP-Engineering, SVP-Corp-Dev / integration lead, acquirer-side executive sponsor

This module installs the **post-close integration overlay** that sits on top of a closed transaction and converts a signed and funded deal into a realised outcome — an acquired business that runs, an acquired team that stays, an escrow that releases without dispute, and an earn-out that pays as intended. It is the workstream that determines whether the deal thesis actually gets executed — whether the promised revenue synergy materialises, whether the top-quartile engineering leaders are still there at month twelve, whether the day-30 finance close in the acquirer's ledger produces trustworthy numbers, whether the earn-out that the seller negotiated into the SPA pays out or is disputed into a two-year arbitration. The Sirower / McKinsey / BCG / Bain / Big-Four post-merger-integration (PMI) canon is consistent on the empirical shape: **most acquisitions destroy value at the acquirer's stock-price level**, and the specific determinant that separates the deals that create value from the deals that destroy it is not deal-structure sophistication — it is the discipline and pace of the first 100 days.

The frame is *choreography of consequential decisions*, not synergy-tracking spreadsheets. An **Integration Management Office (IMO)** with a named executive sponsor on each side, a full-time integration lead, dated workstream leads across finance / people / systems / product / GTM / legal / IT / brand / customer-success / partner-ecosystem, and a specific cadence (daily standup for the first 30 days, weekly steering, monthly board update) — this is the machine. A **day-1 readiness checklist** — payroll cutover, email-and-identity cutover, benefits transition, org-chart publication, town-hall calendar — is executed to the hour. A **day-30 systems-cutover milestone** — finance close in acquirer's ledger, HR system cutover, security-perimeter integration — is executed to the day. A **day-90 organisational-integration milestone** — comp-and-leveling harmonisation, promotion-cycle alignment, performance-management alignment — is executed to the week. A **day-100 review** produces an explicit go / no-go on deeper integration. Around all of it, the retention plan designed in mod-110 is monitored against actual behaviour, the technology-and-systems integration decisions are made with an eye to preserved optionality via API-parity, the culture is integrated on the belonging axis rather than the compensation axis, and the escrow / working-capital-true-up / earn-out choreography is executed against the specific SPA language mod-104 negotiated.

The founder-inside-acquirer 100-day plan gets its own chapter because the founder-CEO's seat inside the acquirer is a specific — and specifically fragile — leadership situation. Reporting-line clarity, decision-rights division, integration-of-reports (do the founder's previous direct reports still report through the founder, or do they report laterally into the acquirer?), alumni-network handoff patterns, and — critically — the honest-signal pattern for a founder who realises the seat is not working: all of these are specific to the post-close founder situation and are the difference between a founder who executes a productive two-year integration and one who resigns in month five to the surprise of everyone including themselves.

> Reminder: this module is education, not legal / tax / accounting / employment / executive-search advice. Every live integration engages qualified counsel (corporate, tax, employment, IP), the acquirer's audit firm for post-close finance-consolidation questions, HR-specialist advisors for benefits and comp harmonisation, and — for the escrow / earn-out workstream — the specific escrow agent and (if relevant) the specific R&W underwriter named in the closing documents. Follow the counsel-imposed regime; this module describes the operational discipline that sits underneath it.

## Learning objectives

By the end of this module you should be able to:

- Construct an **Integration Management Office (IMO)** — the executive-sponsor pairing (acquirer-side and acquiree-side co-leads), the integration-lead selection decision (dedicated full-time integration executive vs. business-line owner), the workstream-lead roster (finance, people, systems, product, GTM, legal, IT, brand, customer-success, partner-ecosystem), the cadence design (daily standup, weekly steering, monthly board update), the risk-and-issue register (RAID log), and the decision-log discipline that lets you defend day-90 decisions in a day-500 dispute.
- Design the **first-100-day plan for the acquired company** — the day-1 readiness checklist (payroll cutover, email-and-identity cutover, benefits transition, org-chart publication, town-hall calendar), the day-30 systems-cutover milestones (finance close in acquirer's ledger, HR system cutover, security-perimeter integration), the day-90 organisational-integration milestones (comp-and-leveling harmonisation, promotion-cycle alignment, performance-management alignment), and the day-100 review with an explicit go / no-go on deeper integration.
- Design the **first-100-day plan for the founder-inside-acquirer seat** — reporting-line clarity (into whom, on which cadence), decision-rights division (what the founder still decides vs. escalates), integration-of-reports (previous direct reports report through the founder vs. laterally into acquirer), alumni-network handoff pattern for the founder's talent pipeline, and the honest-signal pattern for a founder who realises the seat is not working.
- Make **technology-and-systems integration decisions** — consolidate-vs-preserve product (immediate consolidation, phased sunset, indefinite preservation), engineering-org merge or preserve, data-and-infrastructure migration approach (lift-and-shift, refactor, rebuild), security-perimeter consolidation (single-tenant to multi-tenant, IdP consolidation, security-tool consolidation), and the pattern of preserving optionality via API-parity during transition.
- Design **culture integration** — values-mapping and reconciliation, comp-and-leveling harmonisation (the source of most day-90 discontent), benefits-harmonisation, offsite / all-hands rhythm, ritual-preservation (the small things that make the acquired team feel acquired-not-erased), and the retention-through-belonging pattern that outperforms retention-through-compensation.
- Monitor **retention** against the retention-plan design from mod-110 — the 30 / 60 / 90-day retention checkpoints, the top-20-quartile retention-risk list refresh, the retention-bonus payment triggers, and the pattern of surfacing hidden retention risk (skip-level meetings, engagement surveys, one-on-one manager notes).
- Manage **escrow release and earn-out achievement** — the 12–24-month general-escrow release choreography, the specific-indemnity-claim escrow choreography, the working-capital true-up execution, earn-out achievement monitoring (revenue vs. gross-profit vs. product-milestone), and the earn-out-dispute pattern and its avoidance through clear control-and-oversight rights.

## Ownership boundary

- **Ongoing-company operations-function design** — the standing IMO-equivalent (transformation office), the ongoing HRIS / ERP / IdP administration function, the ordinary-course engineering-and-product organisation design, the ordinary-course culture and rituals maintenance, and the standing security-and-compliance function — is owned by `startup-operations-governance-curriculum` (level 50). This module never re-teaches how to run an ordinary-course finance-close or how to design an ordinary-course promotion cycle. It owns the *transaction-specific overlay* — the post-close integration workstream that stands up temporarily to move the acquired business from its pre-close state into the acquirer's ongoing-operations envelope.
- **Deal-structure design and SPA-language authoring** (asset vs. stock, earn-out clause design, escrow mechanics, R&W insurance, working-capital adjustment mechanics) are owned by mod-103 and mod-104. This module assumes the definitive agreements are signed and the escrow-and-earn-out language is fixed, and reasons about how to *execute* against that language post-close.
- **Pre-signing and announcement-day employee communications choreography, MIP design, WARN Act workforce actions** are owned by mod-110. This module picks up on **day 2** after the announcement-day handoff mod-110 defines — the day-1 all-hands is chapter 2 of mod-110; everything from day 2 forward is this module.
- **Transaction governance and fiduciary duty for the deal decision itself** (Revlon, Corwin, MFW, special-committee construction, fairness opinions) is owned by mod-109. This module owns the post-close *decision-log discipline* that lets a day-500 stockholder-litigation defence or an escrow dispute reconstruct why a specific integration decision was made.
- **Buy-side pre-close diligence** (financial, legal, tax, commercial, technology, IP, HR, privacy, security, AI-model, open-source) is owned by mod-105. This module assumes diligence is done, integration-diligence findings are documented, and the integration plan attaches to those findings.
- **Founder-CEO succession into a professional CEO, board-composition graduation, and executive-team bench design** are owned by mod-112. This module owns the founder's *first-100-day* seat inside the acquirer; the deeper succession-decision architecture (including the case where the founder-CEO is being replaced at the acquirer post-close) is mod-112's territory.
- **Post-transaction personal-wealth planning** (QSBS, 10b5-1, exchange funds, diversification) is owned by mod-114 and never re-taught here.

## How to work through this module

Pick a specific closed-or-closing transaction — a strategic acquirer buying a mid-size private target, a PE take-private of a public company, a large-strategic acquiring a founder-led product-line acquisition — with a specific headcount, specific site footprint, specific product surface, specific top-customer concentration, specific escrow and earn-out terms, and specific founder-post-close-role. Freeze the profile at the start of exercise 01 and carry it through all six exercises. The module is worth exactly what the concreteness of your target profile is worth; a hypothetical with hand-wave numbers produces a hand-wave integration plan.

Read chapter 1 and do exercise 01 first — the IMO is the machine that everything else runs through, and the charter document you draft in exercise 01 is what the day-1 through day-100 milestones in exercise 02 attach to. Read chapter 2 next and do exercise 02 — the acquiree 100-day plan is the operational spine and forms the plan-of-record every subsequent chapter tunes. Chapter 3 and exercise 03 install the founder-inside-acquirer overlay; do them before chapter 5 (culture) because the founder's early behaviour is the single most consequential cultural signal in the first 90 days. Chapter 4 and exercise 04 make the technology-and-systems decisions that shape most of the day-30 milestones; the exercise deliberately forces you to name what you consolidate vs. preserve and defend the choice. Chapter 5 and exercise 05 install the culture-integration overlay; do this exercise with awareness that the comp-and-leveling harmonisation you design here creates most of your day-90 discontent. Chapter 6 introduces the retention monitoring discipline that runs continuously through the 100 days; there is no separate exercise for chapter 6 — the retention-monitoring cadence attaches to the acquiree 100-day plan in exercise 02 and to the escrow-and-earn-out drill in exercise 06. Chapter 7 and exercise 06 close the loop on escrow release, working-capital true-up, and earn-out achievement — the workstream that finally answers whether the deal thesis got executed.
