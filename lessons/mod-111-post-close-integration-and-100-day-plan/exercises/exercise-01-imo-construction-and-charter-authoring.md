# exercise-01: IMO Construction and Charter Authoring

**Estimated effort:** 4 hours

## Objective

Author the specific **Integration Management Office (IMO) charter** and the specific **operating cadence** that will run the first 100 days of post-close integration for a specific closed-or-closing transaction you freeze at the start of this exercise. By the end of the exercise, you have the specific document the acquirer-side executive sponsor, the acquiree-side executive sponsor, the integration lead, and every workstream lead reads on day 1 to know what the IMO is, who is on it, what it decides, how it meets, and when it dissolves. The charter is the single-source-of-truth artefact every subsequent exercise in this module references and attaches to.

## Tasks

### 1. Author the specific IMO charter document (4–6 pages)

Produce the specific IMO charter as a single document that opens with the specific mandate, specific scope, specific governance, specific decision rights, and specific dissolution criteria.

- **Mandate (½ page).** The specific one-paragraph statement of what the IMO exists to accomplish for the specific transaction. Specifically reference the specific deal thesis the IMO is executing against (specifically from the specific SPA recitals or the specific board-approval memo). Specifically call out that the IMO is a *decision-and-coordination venue*, not an operating team, and that it operates *above* both companies' ordinary-course leadership for the duration of integration.
- **Scope (½ page).** The specific list of workstreams the IMO covers for this specific transaction. Default list: finance / people / systems / product / GTM / legal / IT / brand / customer-success / partner-ecosystem / integration-planning (meta). Specifically call out whether specific additional workstreams are required for the specific transaction (e.g., for a specific regulated-industry target: regulatory / compliance; for a specific manufacturing target: operations / supply-chain; for a specific international target: cross-border / tax / works-council). Specifically call out workstreams that are *not* within IMO scope (e.g., ordinary-course product roadmap, ordinary-course customer-success operations) and explain how the boundary is drawn.
- **Executive-sponsor pairing (½ page).** Name the specific acquirer-side executive sponsor (specifically typically the acquirer's CEO or COO for a specific transformational deal, specifically the acquirer's business-unit GM or Corp Dev lead for a specific tuck-in) and the specific acquiree-side executive sponsor (specifically typically the acquiree's CEO or founder, specifically carrying forward from mod-110's announcement choreography). Specifically describe the specific co-leadership operating model — specifically how disagreements between the sponsors escalate (typically to the acquirer's CEO), specifically how the sponsors engage with the acquirer's board (typically at a monthly cadence), specifically how the sponsors are expected to show up at the specific IMO meetings.
- **Integration-lead selection (½ page).** Name the specific integration lead and specifically defend the specific selection decision. Explicitly resolve the "dedicated full-time integration executive vs. business-line owner" trade-off — if the specific integration is specifically large (specifically transformational, specifically cross-border, specifically multi-site), specifically default to the specific dedicated-full-time integration executive; if the specific integration is specifically modest (specifically product-tuck-in, specifically single-site), specifically default to the specific business-line owner with specific commitment-to-cadence from the specific executive sponsors. Specifically call out the specific percentage of time the specific integration lead will spend on the specific integration (specifically 100% for a specific dedicated executive, specifically 50–70% for a specific business-line-owner-also-integration-lead).
- **Workstream-lead roster (1 page).** The specific named workstream leads for each specific workstream, specifically paired (acquirer-side lead and acquiree-side counterpart for each) where the specific workstream covers both sides. For each specific workstream lead, specifically identify the specific percentage of time the specific lead will dedicate to the specific integration, the specific ordinary-course responsibilities that will be specifically delegated or deferred, and the specific scope of the specific lead's specific decision authority within the specific workstream. Specifically call out the "retention-is-dedicated-not-add-on" principle — the people workstream lead is specifically full-time on integration for the first 60 days.
- **Governance and decision rights (½ page).** The specific decision-rights framework — specifically what the specific IMO decides vs. what individual workstreams decide vs. what gets escalated to the executive sponsors vs. what gets escalated to the acquirer's CEO or board. Specifically use a RACI-style framework for the specific major decision categories (org-structure decisions, systems-cutover decisions, comp-and-leveling decisions, product-portfolio decisions, brand decisions, retention-package decisions, escrow-and-earn-out operational decisions). Specifically call out the specific authority limits on specific spending (e.g., any specific decision with specific financial impact over $X requires executive-sponsor approval).
- **Cadence (½ page).** The specific meeting cadence — specifically a specific daily standup (15 minutes) for the first 30 days, a specific weekly steering committee (90 minutes) through day 100, a specific monthly board update, specific workstream-lead syncs (specific per-workstream cadence), and specific executive-sponsor pairs (weekly or bi-weekly). Specifically call out the specific "decision-ready" discipline — specifically every item on the specific steering-committee agenda specifically has a specific decision frame (specific proposal, specific alternatives considered, specific recommendation, specific dependencies, specific owner, specific due date).
- **RAID log and decision log (½ page).** The specific tooling and specific discipline for the specific RAID log (risks, assumptions, issues, dependencies) and the specific decision log. Specifically name the specific tool (Confluence page, Notion database, Smartsheet, specific PMI-tooling-vendor platform), the specific owner of the log, the specific cadence of specific review at the specific steering committee, and the specific retention-and-archival discipline (specifically — the decision log is preserved for a specific minimum of 7 years for specific escrow-dispute and specific stockholder-litigation reconstruction purposes).
- **Dissolution criteria (¼ page).** The specific gate at which the IMO specifically dissolves — specifically a specific date (typically day-100 for a specific minor integration, day-365 for a specific full integration) or a specific milestone-based trigger (specifically "deeper integration go/no-go review complete and specific decisions handed off to specific ongoing-operations functions"). Specifically call out the specific hand-off mechanics — specifically how the specific open items at specific dissolution are specifically assigned to specific ongoing-operations functions (specifically which items go to the acquirer's VP-People, which to the controller, which to the CTO).
- **Specific accountability section (¼ page).** Specifically who approves this charter (specifically typically the specific acquirer CEO and the specific acquiree CEO before close, or the specific acquirer CEO and the specific acquiree-side executive sponsor just after close), the specific effective date, and the specific amendment procedure.

### 2. Build the specific workstream-lead roster as a specific table (1–2 pages)

Produce the specific roster as a specific table (or specific set of tables) with specific named individuals, specific role titles, specific ordinary-course responsibilities, specific integration responsibilities, specific percentage-of-time commitment, and specific escalation contacts.

- Specifically cover each specific workstream named in the scope section.
- Specifically identify, for each specific workstream, the specific acquirer-side lead *and* the specific acquiree-side counterpart. Where the specific workstream has no specific acquiree-side counterpart (e.g., the specific "integrating-the-brand-into-the-acquirer's-marketing-stack" workstream has only an acquirer-side lead), specifically note that explicitly.
- Specifically identify the specific "deputies" or "specific second-chair" individuals — the specific pattern where the specific workstream lead's specific ordinary-course responsibilities are specifically backfilled by a specific designated deputy during the specific integration window.
- Specifically identify any specific external-advisor engagements for the specific workstream — specifically which specific external advisors are engaged (specific audit firm, specific HR consultancy, specific IT-integration vendor, specific executive-compensation advisor), the specific scope of the specific engagement, and the specific primary acquirer-side contact for the specific advisor.

### 3. Author the specific first-30-day cadence calendar (1 page)

Produce the specific calendar of all specific IMO meetings in the first 30 days post-close as a specific dated schedule. Include:

- The specific day-1 standup (specifically starting at a specific time — typically 8:00am local time of the specific target headquarters — for the specific first 30 days).
- The specific first steering-committee meeting (specifically typically day 2 or day 3, 90 minutes).
- The specific first workstream deep-dives (specifically the finance workstream deep-dive, the people workstream deep-dive, the systems workstream deep-dive — specifically scheduled in the first 5 days).
- The specific "first retention-conversation review" (specifically typically day 7, aligned with mod-110's day-7 retention-conversation completion milestone).
- The specific first monthly board update to the acquirer board (specifically typically week 4).
- The specific day-30 review meeting (specifically the specific checkpoint that reviews the specific day-30 milestones from chapter 2 — specifically the finance-close in acquirer's ledger, the HR-system cutover, the security-perimeter integration).

Specifically identify for each meeting: the specific attendees (specifically mandatory vs. specific optional), the specific agenda (specifically pre-authored, not improvised), the specific pre-reads expected, the specific specific-decisions expected to be made at the specific meeting, and the specific meeting-notes owner.

### 4. Author the specific decision-log template (½ page) and the specific first entries (1 page)

Produce the specific template that every specific decision-log entry follows:

- **Decision ID** (sequential).
- **Date decided**.
- **Workstream**.
- **Decision statement** (specifically what was decided, in specific-and-defensible language).
- **Decision-maker** (specifically named individual with specific role).
- **Alternatives considered** (specifically what other options were specifically evaluated).
- **Rationale** (specifically why the specific decided option was specifically selected over the specific alternatives).
- **Related decisions** (specifically prior decisions this one specifically depends on or specifically modifies).
- **Dependencies** (specifically what specific other decisions or specific operational actions this specifically requires).
- **Reviewer** (specifically who specifically reviewed this before it became final — specifically executive sponsor, specific GC, specific external advisor).

Then produce the specific first 5–10 decision-log entries that the IMO specifically expects to make in the first 5 days — the specific "do we continue payroll on the acquiree's provider through 90 days" decision, the specific "do we preserve the acquiree's email domain through 180 days" decision, the specific "which specific acquirer executive attends the specific acquiree day-1 all-hands" decision, the specific "which specific acquirer executive gets paired with the specific acquiree founder for the first 30 days" decision. Each entry is specifically filled in using the specific template.

### 5. Author the specific RAID-log template (½ page) and the specific first entries (1 page)

Produce the specific RAID-log template and the specific first 10–15 entries. The specific RAID categories:

- **Risks** (specifically identified events that specifically might happen and specifically affect the integration — specifically with specific likelihood, specific impact, specific mitigation plan, specific owner).
- **Assumptions** (specifically working assumptions the specific plan specifically rests on — specifically with specific validation plan and specific owner).
- **Issues** (specifically events that specifically have happened and specifically require specific resolution — specifically with specific resolution plan, specific owner, specific due date).
- **Dependencies** (specifically cross-workstream or specific external dependencies the specific plan specifically rests on — specifically with specific contact, specific status, specific owner).

Populate with the specific first entries that the specific transaction specifically faces at close — specifically the specific "top-5 engineering leader has not countersigned the retention agreement" risk, the specific "payroll-provider assignment-on-change-of-control clause is being negotiated" issue, the specific "customer-contract change-of-control notices are tracking against a 30-day customer-review window" dependency.

### 6. Author the specific hand-off memo to the acquirer's board (1 page)

Draft the specific one-page memo the acquirer-side executive sponsor sends to the acquirer's board at the end of week 1, describing: the specific IMO status (specifically stood up, specific workstreams staffed, specific cadence running), the specific day-30 milestones expected, the specific day-100 go/no-go framework the IMO will execute, and the specific board-reporting cadence the IMO will maintain. The specific memo is the specific "first signal" to the specific board that the specific integration machine is specifically operational.

## Starter guidance

Three anti-patterns to avoid:

- **"The CFO can run the finance workstream as an add-on to the quarterly close."** This specifically fails during the quarter-end week when the specific CFO specifically has to choose between the specific earnings call and the specific day-30 finance-cutover review. The specific defence is specific second-chair backfill — specifically a specific VP-controller or specific Director-FP&A specifically takes the specific ordinary-course quarterly-close lead for the specific integration window.
- **"We'll decide the dissolution criteria later."** Specifically unterminated IMOs become specific permanent "transformation offices" that specifically consume specific executive bandwidth specifically forever and specifically never specifically hand off to specific ongoing-operations functions. The specific defence is a specific explicit dissolution gate specifically written into the specific charter on day 1.
- **"The decision log is overhead; we'll document decisions in meeting notes."** Specifically meeting notes are specifically searchable only by specific people who were specifically in the specific meeting. The specific decision log is specifically what the specific day-500 escrow dispute specifically reads, and specific scattered meeting notes specifically cannot be specifically reconstructed from specifically after the fact. The specific defence is specific decision-log discipline from day 1 — every specific decision at every specific IMO meeting ends with "add a decision-log entry for this; here is the entry."

## Acceptance criteria

You can demonstrate that:

- The charter is specifically specific to the specific transaction — specifically naming specific individuals, specific workstreams, specific escalation paths, and specific authority limits — rather than specifically generic PMI-template boilerplate.
- The workstream-lead roster covers every specific workstream from chapter 1 and specifically names a specific individual (acquirer-side and acquiree-side where applicable) for each.
- The first-30-day cadence calendar is specifically dated and specifically covers every specific meeting type from chapter 1.
- The decision-log and RAID-log templates are specifically operational — a reader could specifically pick them up and start specifically running them on day 1.
- The first entries in the decision log and RAID log are specifically grounded in the specific transaction profile — specific decisions and specific risks that specifically arise from the specific target headcount, specific site footprint, specific product surface, and specific SPA terms.
- A critical reader (an acquirer-side CFO who has run integrations before, an acquiree-side founder who has sold a company before, an external PMI advisor) could specifically disagree with a specific decision in the charter and specifically see the specific judgement call that specifically produced it — rather than being specifically unable to specifically engage because the charter is specifically generic.
