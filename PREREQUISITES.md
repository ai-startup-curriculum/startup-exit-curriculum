# Prerequisites — Startup Exit & Endgame track

**Role level:** 60 (deepest and latest-stage functional pillar — Startup family)
**Track:** `startup-exit-curriculum`

This track is the **deepest and latest-stage functional pillar** in the Startup family — the transaction-execution-and-endgame-decision apparatus for founder-CEO / CFO / GC / SVP-Corp-Dev / IB-MD seats. It assumes the learner already has the Foundations mental model installed, the ongoing-company financial infrastructure installed from `startup-finance-fundraising-curriculum`, and the ongoing-company governance / people / legal / ops infrastructure installed from `startup-operations-governance-curriculum`. The fourteen modules move an endgame owner from framing the exit decision (mod-101) through sell-side M&A execution (mod-102–105), secondary transactions (mod-106), IPO readiness and execution (mod-107–108), transaction governance and stakeholder management (mod-109–110), post-close integration (mod-111), leadership succession (mod-112), responsible wind-down (mod-113), and the post-transaction personal-wealth close (mod-114).

## Assumed lower-level curriculum

- **[`startup-foundations`](https://github.com/ai-startup-curriculum/startup-foundations)** (level 10) — required. Foundations owns the *system* (what a startup is), the *graph* (customer-facing / corporate-structure / economics branches), the *stages* (IDEA → PRE-SEED → SEED → SERIES-A → GROWTH → MATURE), the founder's operating loop (decide → allocate → communicate → measure), and the founder-numbers slice (runway, burn, growth read, default alive / default dead, North-Star metric selection). This track *never* re-teaches those. Most of the material only becomes actionable when the company is at or approaching GROWTH / MATURE — start Foundations first if you have not already.
- **[`startup-finance-fundraising-curriculum`](https://github.com/ai-startup-curriculum/startup-finance-fundraising-curriculum)** (level 40) — strongly recommended before mod-102 onward. Owns the ongoing-company financial infrastructure: three-statement model, cohort unit economics, cap-table maintenance, priced-round preferred-stock economics, board packs, monthly close, finance-org design. Also owns equity *economics* (waterfall math under different preference stacks, 409A methodology, Rule 701 aggregate-value caps). Every module in this track presumes that infrastructure is installed — mod-105's buy-side financial diligence, mod-106's tender-offer waterfall, mod-107's S-1 financial statements and PCAOB audit history, mod-108's public-comp pricing anchors, and mod-111's earn-out achievement management all sit on top.
- **[`startup-operations-governance-curriculum`](https://github.com/ai-startup-curriculum/startup-operations-governance-curriculum)** (level 50) — strongly recommended before mod-104 onward. Owns the ongoing-company governance / people / legal / ops infrastructure: bylaws, board composition trajectory, committee construction, corporate-secretary machinery, D&O programme, employment-contract suite, commercial-contracts library, privacy programme, insider-trading policy, code of ethics. Every module in this track presumes that infrastructure is installed and layers transaction-specific overlays on top — mod-104's SPA drafting sits on top of the commercial-contract library, mod-109's special-committee construction sits on top of the ongoing board and committee machinery, mod-110's WARN Act compliance sits on top of the ongoing workforce-communications capability, mod-111's culture integration sits on top of the ongoing values / handbook / DEI programme.

## Assumed working knowledge

Even after Foundations, Finance, and Operations-Governance, you should be practically comfortable with:

- **Reading a term sheet, an SPA, and an S-1 at the clause level** — mod-104 (LOI and definitive agreements), mod-107 (S-1 authoring), mod-108 (underwriter documents), mod-109 (proxy statements) all require reading long-form legal documents and reasoning about clause-level implications for cap table, waterfall, indemnification exposure, disclosure liability, and stockholder-vote outcomes. You do not need to be a lawyer — that is what corporate counsel is for — but you need to be able to red-line a document and defend a position with your legal advisor rather than delegate the reasoning entirely.
- **Reading Delaware Court of Chancery case law and NVCA / ABA practitioner materials** — mod-109's transaction-governance depth (Revlon, Corwin, MFW, Blasius, Van Gorkom, Weinberger, Rural Metro, Del Monte, El Paso) requires you to be comfortable reading judicial opinions and pulling the operating rule out of the fact pattern. The ABA Deal Points Studies, NVCA Model Documents, and Latham Book of Jargon are the shared vocabulary anchors — plan to spend time in each.
- **Reading SEC filings — S-1, 10-K, 10-Q, 8-K, proxy, DEF 14A, tender-offer 14D-9** — mod-107 (IPO readiness), mod-108 (IPO execution), mod-109 (transaction governance), mod-114 (10b5-1 plans) all require pulling structure and disclosure norms out of comparable public filings. If you have never read an S-1, pick 2–3 recent IPOs in your target sector and read them cover-to-cover before starting mod-107.
- **Financial-model fluency at CFO grade** — mod-103 (deal structures), mod-105 (Q of E and price renegotiation), mod-106 (waterfall analysis), mod-107 (S-1 financial statements), mod-108 (pricing / allocation) require reading and reasoning about financial models at the level installed by `startup-finance-fundraising-curriculum`. If you have not built a three-statement driver-based model, start there.
- **Ability to write executive-audience memos and slide-decks** — every module ships written or slide artifacts (endgame memos, buyer maps, CIMs, LOI red-lines, transaction-governance memos, communications plans, integration plans, wind-down playbooks). If you have never authored a board pack, an investor letter, or a management-presentation deck, plan to spend extra time on mod-102 (CIM authoring) and mod-110 (transaction-communications authoring).
- **Comfort with tax vocabulary at CFO / GC scale** — mod-103's tax structuring (IRC §368, §338(h)(10), §280G, §409A, §1202) and mod-114's personal-wealth planning (QSBS, QSBS stacking, 10b5-1) require reading tax-code sections and reasoning about basis, holding period, character (LTCG vs. ordinary), timing, and interaction with regulatory constraints. You do not need to be a tax attorney or CPA — that is what a Big Four tax practice or a private-client tax attorney is for — but you need to be able to reason about a tax structure with your advisor.
- **Working knowledge of the venture capital-markets / M&A vocabulary** — round stages, preferred-stock preferences, exit multiples, banker retainers and success fees, MOIC / IRR / DPI, public-comp multiples (EV/Revenue, EV/EBITDA, P/S, PEG), the IPO-window language, and the M&A-market-conditions language. Foundations installs the venture vocabulary; this track uses the M&A / capital-markets vocabulary as shorthand.
- **Willingness to work against a real (or realistic hypothetical) growth-stage startup with a specific ARR / burn / cap-table / customer-concentration shape** — every module has exercises and every project has a deliverable that only becomes concrete when applied to one company at the sell-side / IPO / secondary / succession / wind-down moment. Pick one before starting mod-101 and use it end-to-end.

## Not assumed, taught here

You do **not** need prior experience with:

- Any specific endgame-decision framework — the outcome-comparison, buyer-map maintenance, market-window signalling, and optionality-preserving operating decisions covered in mod-101.
- Any specific sell-side M&A execution flow — banker selection, buyer mapping at scoring depth, CIM / teaser / management-presentation authoring, pre-marketing choreography covered in mod-102.
- Any specific M&A deal-structure decision — asset vs. stock vs. reverse-triangular merger, consideration mix design, earn-out mechanics, escrow / holdback / working-capital adjustment, and the tax-structuring elements (IRC §368 A/B/C/F reorg, §338(h)(10) election, §280G parachute, §409A payment-timing, §1202 QSBS) covered in mod-103.
- Any specific LOI or definitive-agreement drafting pattern — no-shop / fiduciary-out / standstill / expense-reimbursement / break-up fee, reps-and-warranties layering, MAC / MAE definition, indemnification package design (survival, cap, basket, tipping, de-minimis), R&W insurance mechanics — all covered in mod-104.
- Any specific diligence workstream — sell-side data-room construction, quality-of-earnings preparation, buy-side workstream design across finance / legal / tax / commercial / technology / IP / HR / privacy / security / AI-model, diligence-findings-based price renegotiation, R&W underwriter scope negotiation — covered in mod-105.
- Any specific secondary-transaction mechanic — founder secondary sizing and choreography, employee tender-offer design (pricing, participation caps, waterfall, tax withholding), forward-contract structured secondaries, secondary-market platform execution, ROFR choreography, tender-in-connection-with-financing — covered in mod-106.
- Any specific IPO-readiness workstream — going-public decision framework, S-1 authoring, PCAOB auditor engagement, 2-year audit history preparation, SOX 404 readiness, JOBS Act EGC election, NYSE / Nasdaq listing-standard governance graduation, EDGAR filing workflow — covered in mod-107.
- Any specific IPO-execution mechanic — underwriter bake-off, TTW / gun-jumping compliance, roadshow choreography, pricing and allocation, Reg M stabilisation, greenshoe, lock-up architecture, direct listing / SPAC / Reg A+ alternatives — covered in mod-108.
- Any specific transaction-governance concept — Revlon change-of-control trigger, Corwin business-judgement gate, MFW dual-protection framework, Blasius franchise-interference test, Van Gorkom informed-decision standard, Weinberger entire-fairness test, special-committee construction, fairness-opinion procurement with banker-conflict analysis, appraisal-rights exposure, ISS / Glass Lewis engagement, stockholder-litigation defence — covered in mod-109.
- Any specific transaction-communications choreography — pre-and-post-signing employee waves, customer / partner / channel communications, press strategy, quiet-period compliance, MIP design at IRC 280G / 409A guardrails, WARN Act transaction-compliance, works-council notifications — covered in mod-110.
- Any specific post-close integration pattern — IMO construction, first-100-day plan for the acquiree and for the founder-inside-acquirer, technology / systems / culture integration decisions, retention monitoring, escrow-release and earn-out management — covered in mod-111.
- Any specific succession-planning pattern — founder-CEO succession readiness signals, board-conversation choreography, executive-search partner selection, incoming-CEO onboarding, founder-transition role scoping, board-chair transitions, executive-team bench design, interim-CEO playbook, board-composition graduation — covered in mod-112.
- Any specific wind-down mechanic — shutdown-vs-ABC-vs-Ch-7-vs-Ch-11 decision framework, ABC execution (Sherwood / DSI / SierraConstellation), WARN Act shutdown compliance, employee final-pay / PTO / COBRA / severance, IP disposition, D&O tail (6-year run-off), officer personal-liability watch under IRC §6672 / PAGA / WARN / FLSA / ERISA — covered in mod-113.
- Any specific post-transaction personal-wealth-planning pattern — IRC §1202 QSBS analysis and stacking with non-grantor trusts, Rule 10b5-1 trading-plan authoring, post-lockup diversification (staged sale, exchange fund, options collar, prepaid variable forward), CRT / DAF / private-foundation structuring, family-office setup — covered in mod-114 as education (not personal tax / investment / legal advice).

## Recommended reading before starting

- **Endgame practitioner canon:** [Peter Thiel — *Zero to One* Chapter 14 (The Founder's Paradox)](https://zerotoonebook.com/), [Ben Horowitz — *The Hard Thing About Hard Things* (Sell chapter, Founder-CEO succession chapter, Wartime CEO chapter)](https://www.benhorowitz.com/the-hard-thing-about-hard-things), [Elad Gil — *High Growth Handbook*](https://growth.eladgil.com/) (endgame + board chapters), and the [a16z Podcast](https://a16z.com/podcast/) / [First Round Review](https://review.firstround.com/) endgame essays — before mod-101.
- **M&A practitioner canon:** [Reed / Lajoux / Nesvold — *The Art of M&A* (5th ed.)](https://www.mheducation.com/highered/product/art-m-reed-lajoux/M9781260121780.html), Freund — *Anatomy of a Merger*, [Cooley GO M&A materials](https://www.cooleygo.com/documents/), and [Latham Book of Jargon (M&A)](https://www.lw.com/en/book-of-jargon-series) — skim before mod-102 through mod-105.
- **NVCA + Delaware canon:** [NVCA Model Legal Documents](https://nvca.org/model-legal-documents/) — skim the drag-along and preferred-stock protective-provisions clauses before mod-104 and mod-109. [Delaware Court of Chancery](https://courts.delaware.gov/chancery/) recent M&A opinions (Corwin, MFW, Rural Metro, Del Monte, In re Trados) — read at least two before mod-109.
- **IPO canon:** [Davis Polk Capital Markets Practice Materials](https://www.davispolk.com/insights) or [Latham US IPO Guide](https://www.lw.com/en/insights/us-ipo-guide-2024), plus a recent tech / SaaS S-1 (Databricks, Rubrik, Snowflake, Airbnb, Snap, Rivian, or a current-year IPO) — read cover-to-cover before mod-107.
- **SEC framework:** [SEC Division of Corporation Finance](https://www.sec.gov/divisions/corpfin) reference materials and the [JOBS Act EGC framework](https://www.sec.gov/spotlight/jobs-act.shtml) — skim before mod-107 and mod-108.
- **Tax framework:** [IRC §368](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/26/368), [§280G](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/26/280G), [§409A](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/26/409A), [§1202](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/26/1202) — read once before mod-103 and mod-114 to have the vocabulary in mind (you will not memorise them, but you should have read them once).
- **Integration + succession canon:** [Mark Sirower — *The Synergy Solution*](https://www.hbs.edu/faculty/Pages/item.aspx?num=61787) or [*The Synergy Trap*](https://www.hbs.edu/faculty/Pages/item.aspx?num=1998), plus [Bolster](https://bolster.com/insights) and [HBR CEO Succession research (Charan)](https://hbr.org/topic/succession-planning) — skim before mod-111 and mod-112.
- **Wind-down canon:** [NVCA + Cooley — Guide to Winding Down Your Startup](https://nvca.org/resource/nvca-model-legal-documents/), [Sherwood Partners ABC materials](https://www.sherwoodpartners.com/), and the [WARN Act (DOL)](https://www.dol.gov/agencies/eta/layoffs/warn) framework — before mod-113.
- **Personal-wealth canon:** [Carta Post-Liquidity Planning](https://carta.com/learn/personal-finance/) and [Holloway Guide to Equity Compensation — Equity in an Exit chapter](https://www.holloway.com/g/equity-compensation) — before mod-114.

## Not required, useful to have

- **Prior sell-side or buy-side M&A experience** — a first-time founder-CEO closing a strategic sale, a first-time CFO leading an acquisition, a first-time GC drafting an SPA, a first-time IB VP running a deal team, a first-time PE Principal leading a take-private. This track does not require it but the exercises are richer against a real context.
- **Prior IPO-adjacent experience** — a first-time CFO running an S-1 workstream, a first-time GC managing SEC comments, a first-time CEO running a roadshow, a first-time head of IR onboarding pre-IPO. mod-107 and mod-108 assume zero exposure but the pattern-recognition transfers.
- **Prior board-facing experience during a transaction** — first quarterly board pack in a sell-side context, first special-committee meeting, first fairness-opinion presentation, first ISS engagement. mod-109 and mod-110 assume zero exposure but the choreography transfers.
- **Prior integration or wind-down experience** — first post-close integration lead, first ABC as founder or advisor, first WARN-triggered layoff, first D&O tail purchase. mod-111 and mod-113 assume zero exposure but the pattern-recognition transfers.
- **Familiarity with a virtual data-room tool** — Intralinks, Datasite, Firmex, Ansarada, DealRoom. mod-105 uses one as the sell-side and buy-side artifact.
- **Familiarity with an M&A / IPO practitioner form library** — Cooley GO, Orrick Total Access, NVCA Model Documents, ABA Deal Points Studies. Skim the reference libraries before mod-104 to be comfortable searching them.
- **Legal / paralegal experience with corporate-finance documents** — makes mod-104, mod-107, and mod-109 faster; the term-sheet-teardown and S-1-outline drills lean on being comfortable in long-form legal text.
